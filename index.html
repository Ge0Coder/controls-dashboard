Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PID controller wasn’t invented for industry — it came from ship steering. Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analyzing how helmsmen on the USS New Mexico kept course, observing that good helmsmen unconsciously applied proportional, integral, and derivative action. Industrial process control adopted it decades later, which is why the math of a modern reactor temperature loop traces directly back to a Navy battleship’s rudder.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The Watt centrifugal governor — the brass spinning-ball icon of the steam age — is the reason we have the word “feedback” and arguably the reason control theory exists as a discipline. In 1868 James Clerk Maxwell wrote a paper titled “On Governors” to explain why some governors hunted (oscillated) instead of settling, and in doing so he produced the first rigorous stability analysis of a dynamic system, decades before Nyquist, Bode, or Routh. Every root-locus plot you’ve ever drawn descends intellectually from Maxwell trying to figure out why a piece of Victorian brass wouldn’t sit still.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from trade-show demo to plant-floor reality. The pitch is compelling: a single deterministic Ethernet fabric carrying everything from motion-control cyclic data (sub-millisecond, bounded jitter) up through MES-level information modeling, replacing the historical zoo of Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos, and Ethernet/IP CIP Motion. The IEEE 802.1 TSN suite provides the determinism (802.1Qbv scheduled traffic, 802.1AS time sync, 802.1CB frame replication), while OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) provides the semantic and information-modeling layer on top. The practical reality in 2026 is messier. TSN-capable switches from Cisco, Hirschmann, Siemens, and Moxa exist, but interoperability between vendors’ TSN configuration models (often via NETCONF/YANG with vendor-specific extensions) is still painful. Most greenfield deployments are still single-vendor islands. Controller-side, Beckhoff, Siemens, B&R, and Rockwell all have TSN-ready CPUs, but mixing brands on the same TSN domain is rare in production. For a controls engineer, the honest near-term value is converged diagnostics and gateway-free MES integration — not yet replacing your motion bus. Expect another two to three years before multi-vendor TSN feels as boring and reliable as Profinet does today.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle) remain the most consequential parts of the standard for engineers specifying systems in 2026. Increasingly, EU regulators implementing the NIS2 Directive and the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) point to 62443-4-1 certification as the de facto evidence of “secure by design” for industrial products. If you’re writing technical specifications for new PLC, RTU, or HMI procurement, requiring 62443-4-1 process certification (from the vendor) plus 62443-4-2 component certification (for the device family) is becoming the baseline expectation across European utilities and increasingly in North American critical infrastructure RFPs. Worth noting: ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 was substantially revised in 2024 and is now the operational counterpart — covering the asset owner’s cybersecurity management system. It maps cleanly onto NIST CSF 2.0’s Govern function, which means a single set of OT policies and procedures can often satisfy both frameworks if structured carefully. For engineers, the practical takeaway is that risk assessment documentation under 62443-3-2 (zones, conduits, target SL) is increasingly demanded as a deliverable on capital projects — start treating it as engineering documentation on par with P&IDs and network diagrams, not as a security afterthought.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, where the Cyber Av3ngers group (Iran-linked) compromised an internet-exposed Unitronics Vision-series PLC at a booster pump station. The attackers defaced the PLC’s HMI screen with anti-Israel messaging and forced the station offline; operators reverted to manual control while replacements were sourced. No water-quality impact occurred, but the symbolic and operational disruption rippled across dozens of US water utilities running the same product family. The root cause was depressingly simple: the Unitronics PLC was reachable from the public internet on TCP/20256 (the PCOM protocol port) and was using the default password “1111.” There was no firewall, no VPN, and no network segmentation between the PLC and the open internet. The attackers’ tooling appears to have been a simple Shodan-driven scanner that fingerprinted Unitronics devices and attempted the default credential. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, default passwords are an unforced error — every commissioning checklist needs a credential-rotation step that someone signs. Second, no PLC should ever be directly internet-reachable; if remote access is required, it goes through a hardened VPN or a vendor-mediated cloud gateway with MFA, full stop. Third, asset inventory matters: many utilities only discovered they owned vulnerable Unitronics devices because CISA published an advisory naming the product line — a sobering reflection of how little visibility many small utilities have into their own OT fleet.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a notable cluster of ABB advisories today, with vulnerabilities disclosed across B&R Automation Runtime, Automation Studio, B&R industrial PCs, and the Terra AC Wallbox EV charger. The Automation Runtime and Studio issues center on browser/session takeover and outdated third-party components, while the B&R PC advisory describes more serious network-exploitable flaws enabling remote code execution, DoS, and DNS cache poisoning. The Terra AC Wallbox issue is the most concerning from a physical-impact standpoint: heap memory corruption could allow firmware modification, which in a fleet of networked EV chargers could translate to grid-edge manipulation. Engineers running B&R-based machine control or any ABB-managed building/charging assets should pull the CSAF documents and plan patch windows now. Also advised today is Hitachi Energy GMS600, still carrying the long-tail OpenSSL CVE-2022-4304 (the Bleichenbacher timing oracle). While exploitation is non-trivial, GMS600 is a generator management relay used in power generation, so the advisory deserves attention from protection engineers. Kieback & Peter DDC building controllers (DDC4002/4100/4200/4400 families up to firmware 1.12.14) received an advisory for a browser-takeover flaw — relevant to facilities and BMS integrators across European real-estate portfolios. On the IT-side news that bleeds into OT, Trend Micro patched an Apex One on-prem zero-day (CVE-2026-34926) already in CISA KEV, and Drupal disclosed CVE-2026-9082 with mass exploitation attempts within hours — many plant HMIs, asset registries, and engineering portals run on Drupal-based intranet stacks. SecurityWeek also flagged ongoing industrial router exploitation and a Huawei router flaw blamed for a telecom blackout, reinforcing that edge cellular routers in remote substations and pump stations remain the soft underbelly. The FBI’s takedown of the “First VPN” cybercrime service and the arrest of the Kimwolf botnet operator are positive but won’t meaningfully reduce ransomware reconnaissance pressure on OT networks. Grafana confirmed source code theft via the TanStack supply-chain compromise — a useful reminder that observability stacks increasingly used for plant KPI dashboards are themselves a supply-chain target. If you’ve embedded Grafana in your historian or MES visualization layer, rotate tokens and review repo access now.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

This week’s KEV additions skew heavily IT but several have direct OT relevance. CVE-2026-0300 in Palo Alto PAN-OS (User-ID Captive Portal, unauthenticated root RCE) is the standout — many sites use PAN-OS firewalls as the IT/OT boundary, and an exploitable Captive Portal on a Purdue Level 3.5 firewall is essentially a free pass into the DMZ. Patch or disable Captive Portal immediately if exposed. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN (CVE-2026-20182) auth bypass is similarly serious for distributed sites using SD-WAN to backhaul remote facility traffic. Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) matters for any organization managing ruggedized handhelds or operator tablets through EPMM. CISA also re-added a batch of legacy Microsoft and Adobe CVEs from 2008–2010 (Conficker-era MS08-067, DirectShow, old Acrobat, IE use-after-frees). These look anachronistic but the re-listing usually signals fresh observed exploitation against unpatched, end-of-life systems — exactly the profile of many engineering workstations, historian servers, and HMI PCs still running Windows XP/7 embedded in OT. If you maintain a legacy island for a vendor-locked application, verify network isolation, application whitelisting, and removable-media controls; these CVEs are now actively scanned for. Finally, the Drupal SQLi (CVE-2026-9082) and Trend Micro Apex One directory traversal (CVE-2026-34926) round out the high-priority items. Apex One in particular is widely deployed on OT-adjacent endpoints; the on-prem variant is the affected one, so check your patch posture against Trend Micro’s advisory.Read more →
