Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PLC — Modicon's 084, built by Dick Morley's team in 1969 — was designed specifically so that General Motors maintenance staff could troubleshoot it in ladder logic, the same notation they already used to document relay panels. The deliberate choice to mimic existing electrical drawings, rather than invent a new programming paradigm, is arguably the single biggest reason PLCs displaced relay logic so quickly, and it's why ladder diagrams still dominate nearly 60 years later despite IEC 61131-3 offering four alternatives.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely — Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico and noticing they instinctively applied proportional, integral, and derivative action based on current heading error, accumulated drift, and rate of swing. His paper formalised what good helmsmen had been doing by feel for centuries, and the resulting mathematics went on to govern everything from refinery reactors to spacecraft attitude control. In other words, the single most-deployed control algorithm in industrial history was reverse-engineered from human intuition, not derived from first principles.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) is slowly becoming real in brownfield plants. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 standards that layer bounded latency, time synchronisation (via 802.1AS), and traffic scheduling (802.1Qbv) on top of standard Ethernet, allowing hard real-time traffic to share the same wire as video, OPC UA, and general IT traffic without jitter. For controls engineers this means you can finally collapse the parallel fieldbus and Ethernet runs that have cluttered panels since the early 2000s — EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, and CIP Motion can increasingly coexist on a converged TSN backbone. The practical implication is cabling and switch cost reduction, plus much easier diagnostic access from engineering workstations. The challenge is still vendor ecosystem maturity: TSN-capable switches from Cisco, Hirschmann, Siemens Scalance, and Moxa are shipping, but configuration tooling varies wildly, and the CNC (Centralised Network Configuration) model required for fully scheduled traffic is not yet plug-and-play. Most early deployments are in automotive body shops and semiconductor fabs where motion control density justifies the complexity. If you're specifying a new line in 2026-2027, ask your automation vendor specifically which TSN sub-standards (802.1Qbv, Qbu, Qci) are supported in both the controller and the switch firmware — many claim 'TSN-ready' while only implementing time sync. Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2, the risk assessment and system design standard, continues to be the quietly influential document shaping how zones and conduits get specified in new EPC projects. If you haven't seen it recently, it formalises the process of partitioning your plant into security zones based on consequence of compromise, then defining the conduits (network paths) that cross between them with a required Security Level (SL-T). The 2020 edition remains current, but ISA99 working groups are actively drafting technical reports around applying 62443 to safety instrumented systems and to cloud-connected OT, both of which are long-standing grey areas. NIST's SP 800-82 Revision 3, finalised in 2023, is still the most practical free reference for ICS security and maps cleanly onto 62443 if your organisation needs to speak both languages. For engineers writing security requirements into procurement specs, calling out 62443-4-2 component capability levels (SL-C) alongside functional specs is becoming the norm — asking a PLC vendor to demonstrate SL-C 2 compliance, for example, is a much more precise request than 'must be cyber-secure'. Worth watching: the IEC TC65 work on asset administration shells and the convergence with 62443 for digital-twin security. As OPC UA companion specs proliferate, the question of how a twin inherits the security posture of its physical asset is becoming a real engineering problem rather than a theoretical one.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment incident, where an attacker remotely accessed a plant HMI via TeamViewer and briefly altered the sodium hydroxide setpoint from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm. An operator noticed the mouse cursor moving on-screen and reverted the change within minutes, well before any physical effect could propagate. Subsequent investigation suggested the entry point was shared TeamViewer credentials, a flat network, and Windows 7 workstations with internet access. The incident is instructive precisely because nothing exotic happened — no zero-day, no APT, no custom ICS malware. It was commodity remote-access abuse against a facility whose segmentation and credential hygiene had drifted. The physical safety margin (chemical dosing limits, downstream pH monitoring, operator vigilance) is what prevented harm, not the cybersecurity posture. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, audit every remote-access tool actually in use on your plant floor — not what the policy says, but what's installed. Second, assume process safety layers are your last line of defence against cyber-induced manipulation, and verify they are independent of the compromised controller. Third, if you must allow remote HMI access, use session recording and MFA on a dedicated jump host, not shared credentials on the engineering workstation itself.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's headlines are dominated by two actively exploited perimeter-device vulnerabilities that often sit directly between corporate IT and plant networks. Palo Alto Networks has patched a PAN-OS zero-day enabling authenticated remote code execution with root privileges, and limited in-the-wild exploitation has already been confirmed. Cisco separately warned of an unauthenticated path-traversal flaw in its Secure Email Gateway that allows arbitrary file write and root-level command execution. Both vendors have published IoCs, and both appear on CISA's KEV list as of 14 October. Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday was unusually large, with 1,417 vulnerabilities addressed, 76 rated critical, and at least one already being exploited. For OT environments running Windows-based HMIs, engineering workstations, or historian servers, this is a sizeable patch cycle that will require careful staging through test benches before production rollout. The pre-disclosed and exploited items should be prioritised; everything else can follow the normal change window. On the threat-actor side, SecurityWeek reports two new RATs — CikaBika, which abuses Discord for command-and-control, and Hirudo, an Android trojan that swaps legitimate banking apps for malicious copies. Neither targets ICS directly, but both are relevant for engineers who use mobile devices for remote plant access or 2FA, and for anyone whose site allows Discord traffic to egress unrestricted. The CenterPoint Energy breach exposing data on one million Texans is a reminder that utility sector targeting continues, even where the compromise is on the corporate rather than operational side. SANS ISC flagged an interesting niche item: orphaned VBA projects inside AutoCAD DWG files, which can act as a stealthy delivery vector for macros in engineering environments where DWGs flow between EPCs, vendors, and site teams. Worth a reminder to your drafting and projects teams.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added four fresh entries on 14 October: the Palo Alto PAN-OS code injection (CVE-2026-0322), the Cisco Secure Email Gateway path traversal (CVE-2026-76519), and a pair of chainable Ahsay AhsayCBS flaws (CVE-2026-105133/105134) enabling authentication bypass plus command injection. The Palo Alto and Cisco entries are the priority for OT-adjacent networks — both are commonly deployed as the IT/OT demarcation device, and both now have confirmed exploitation. Patch, rotate credentials, and hunt against the published IoCs. The previous day brought Ivanti Connect Secure (CVE-2026-104935) authentication bypass and a Microsoft Windows Shell command injection (CVE-2026-96951). Ivanti VPNs are a frequent remote-access path for third-party integrators and SIs touching plant systems, so verify whether any site tunnels still rely on it. Earlier KEV additions this month also include Fortinet FortiMail, Citrix NetScaler (three CVEs), MikroTik RouterOS, and Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager — collectively, nearly every major perimeter and edge-routing vendor used around OT networks has a KEV entry this cycle. Practical remediation guidance: treat perimeter firewalls, VPN concentrators, and email gateways as compromise-assumed until patched and audited. For plant sites that cannot patch immediately, restrict management interfaces to jump-host-only access, verify no management plane is reachable from the process network, and increase logging to your SIEM for the next two weeks.Read more →
