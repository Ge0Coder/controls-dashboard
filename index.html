Today's Knowledge Bite

The GENESIS/ICONICS codebase being patched today traces back to software originally written in the 1980s for Windows-based process visualization, and still ships with COM/DCOM interop layers underneath its modern HTML5 front end. That decades-long backward compatibility is exactly why a single advisory can span GENESIS32, GENESIS64, and the brand-new GENESIS product line simultaneously — shared ancestry means shared bugs.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalized not in a chemical plant or power station but on US Navy ships in the 1920s. Nicolas Minorsky worked out the mathematics of three-term control while studying how to automatically steer the USS New Mexico, publishing his analysis in 1922 after observing how a skilled helmsman anticipated the ship's yaw — essentially performing derivative action in his head. Every temperature loop you tune today owes its structure to a human quartermaster's intuition about an 32,000-ton battleship.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from standards documents into real plants. The idea is simple on paper: combine OPC UA's semantic, secure, vendor-neutral data model with deterministic Ethernet from IEEE 802.1 TSN, so you can run motion control, safety, and SCADA data on the same wire with guaranteed latency. In practice it replaces the fragmented world of PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos, and CC-Link IE with a converged network — at least in theory. The practical implication for controls engineers is that your next greenfield line could genuinely use one physical network from the drive to the cloud, with QoS classes carrying 1 ms cyclic motion data alongside historian trends and video. Siemens, Beckhoff, B&R, and Rockwell all have shipping TSN-capable hardware now, and the OPC Foundation's FLC (Field Level Communications) spec is maturing. Adoption challenges are real: TSN requires end-to-end switch support with synchronized clocks (IEEE 802.1AS), configuration tooling is still vendor-specific despite the open standards, and troubleshooting a converged network when a servo drops out is genuinely harder than diagnosing a dedicated fieldbus. Most sites are piloting TSN on isolated cells rather than ripping out working PROFINET backbones — and that caution is probably correct for the next two to three years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues to be the center of gravity for OT cybersecurity standards, and the recent wave of ICS advisories is a reminder of why the Security Level (SL) targeting process in 62443-3-2 matters. The Siemens Industrial Edge App Publisher RCE chain — improper certificate validation combined with unescaped backend data — is a textbook case for applying 62443-4-1 secure development lifecycle requirements and 62443-4-2 component requirements around authentication and session integrity. If you're specifying new systems, requiring vendor evidence of 62443-4-1 certification (not just product-level 4-2) is becoming the practical bar. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the go-to guidance document for OT security program design, and increasingly it's being referenced in insurance questionnaires and regulatory audits rather than just internal policy. The CISA Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are also showing up in vendor contracts — particularly the requirements around unique credentials, MFA on remote access, and asset inventory. Worth noting: the CPGs explicitly call out eliminating default passwords on OT devices, which is still shockingly common in practice. For engineers writing specs this quarter, the most immediately useful move is referencing ISA/IEC 62443-2-4 for integrator and service provider requirements. It gives you concrete contractual language to require patch management SLAs, secure remote access controls, and incident notification from your SIs — exactly the gaps that today's Cisco and BeyondTrust KEV entries exploit.Read more →

Incident of the Week

The Volt Typhoon 'Silent Train' revelation this week is worth dwelling on. Lumen and Cisco Talos together described how China-nexus actors used a Cisco SD-WAN zero-day to steal cryptographic keys, then used that access to maintain persistent, low-noise footholds in networks connecting enterprise IT to operational environments. This is the same actor group CISA and NSA warned in 2023 and 2024 was pre-positioning in US water, energy, and transportation networks — not to steal data, but to enable disruptive attacks during a geopolitical crisis. What makes Silent Train notable is the tradecraft: compromise edge network gear (SD-WAN, firewalls, routers), extract keys and certificates, then blend into legitimate encrypted traffic between sites. For a controls engineer this means the attacker may already be inside the WAN tunnel between your corporate data center and plant DMZ, with no malware on any Windows host to detect. Three lessons: first, your Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ is only as strong as the network device terminating the WAN on either side — patch SD-WAN and firewall appliances with the same urgency you'd give a PLC in a safety loop. Second, assume keys and certificates on edge devices can be stolen and rotate them after any suspected compromise, not just patch the CVE. Third, monitor east-west traffic between IT and OT for anomalies in protocol and timing, not just signatures — Volt Typhoon's whole point is that they look like normal admin traffic.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA released a dense batch of ICS advisories today, dominated by Siemens and Mitsubishi Electric. The Mitsubishi advisory covers the entire GENESIS/ICONICS family — GENESIS64, GENESIS32, Hyper Historian, AnalytiX, MobileHMI, IoTWorX, MC Works64 and BizViz — with authentication bypass (CVE-2026-16436/16437) and arbitrary operation flaws (CVE-2026-16438) that can lead to tampering, data destruction, or DoS. If you run ICONICS-based HMIs or historians anywhere in your plant, treat this as priority patching. Siemens has three separate advisories: a DLL search-path flaw in Siemens Software Center (local privilege escalation), a serious RCE chain in Industrial Edge App Publisher via improper certificate validation plus Node.js integration, and an out-of-bounds read in Capital Embedded AR Classic affecting AUTOSAR Classic deployments in automotive/embedded control stacks. EPICS Base ≤7.0.10 received an advisory covering four new CVEs ranging from memory corruption to possible RCE — relevant to anyone running experimental physics, accelerator, or large-scale distributed control systems. The Kiloview E3 NDI Encoder advisory lists nine CVEs that together allow full device compromise; while NDI encoders are more commonly found in broadcast, they also appear in control room video walls and remote monitoring setups. On the IT side with direct OT impact: Cisco is dealing with two actively exploited zero-days. A Remote Access SSL VPN flaw in ASA/FTD lets unauthenticated attackers force firewalls to reload — the second ASA zero-day since August. Separately, Talos has linked an SD-WAN key-stealing campaign to infrastructure overlapping Volt Typhoon's 'Silent Train' network, the China-nexus actor repeatedly tied to pre-positioning in US critical infrastructure. If Cisco ASA, FTD, or SD-WAN sits between your enterprise and plant networks, assume active targeting and verify patch status urgently. Adobe, Veeam, and Juniper also pushed critical patches this week — Veeam in particular matters because many plants rely on it for engineering workstation and historian backups, and a compromised backup server is a pivot point into OT.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added a steady stream of entries to the KEV catalog over the last two weeks. Of direct OT relevance, CVE-2026-110936 (Siemens Software Center DLL hijacking) is now listed as actively exploited — this is an engineering-workstation-class problem, since Software Center is how many sites manage TIA Portal and other Siemens tool installations. The Broadcom VMware ESX logging flaw (CVE-2026-41709) and the Linux kernel issue (CVE-2026-46300) affect the virtualization layer under many SCADA, historian, and MES deployments; the ESX bug lets admin-level actors operate without audit trail, which is a nightmare for incident forensics in regulated environments. Two PHP unauthenticated RCE flaws (CVE-2026-109618 and 109619) and the Brotli memory corruption bug (CVE-2026-111497) have broad reach into any web-facing OT dashboards, vendor portals, or jump hosts. Fortinet path traversal (CVE-2025-61624) and the BeyondTrust Remote Support authentication bypass (CVE-2026-102764) are both likely vectors into OT environments via remote vendor access — audit any BeyondTrust appliances used for third-party integrator support immediately. Practical remediation: prioritize patching anything internet-exposed first, then engineering workstations and jump hosts, then virtualization hosts. For Siemens Software Center, restrict local admin on EWS and audit DLL load paths. For BeyondTrust, consider pulling appliances offline until patched if used for vendor OT access.Read more →
