Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PLC — the Modicon 084, built by Dick Morley's team in 1969 — was specified by GM to replace relay panels that had to be rewired every model-year changeover. Morley reportedly came up with the ladder logic concept after falling down the stairs, deciding the new controller had to be programmable by electricians who already read ladder diagrams. More than half a century later, IEC 61131-3 ladder is still the dominant language on the plant floor, outlasting dozens of 'next-generation' alternatives.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely. The first mathematically rigorous PID implementation was Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 analysis of automatic ship steering for the US Navy, in which he derived the three-term control law by watching how a human helmsman responded to heading error, its rate of change, and its accumulation. His controller was pneumatic, used on the USS New Mexico, and worked so well the Navy almost didn't believe the results. Every tuning rule you've ever argued about on a control loop traces back to a man watching a sailor steer a battleship.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is quietly becoming one of the most consequential standards for controls engineers in the next five years. It extends OPC UA down from the SCADA/MES layer into controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communications, with a defined companion specification for motion, safety, and I/O. Combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet, it promises what Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, and Sercos each solved in proprietary silos — deterministic, synchronized, vendor-neutral fieldbus communication. The practical implication is significant: in a mature OPC UA FX world, a Siemens PLC, a Rockwell drive, and a Beckhoff safety controller could exchange cyclic data on the same physical network, with the same semantic model, without protocol gateways. Early demonstrations at Hannover Messe and SPS have shown this working across six or more vendors simultaneously. Adoption is the challenge. TSN requires managed switches with IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduling throughout the path, controller firmware support is still uneven, and configuration tooling is immature compared to established fieldbuses. Most greenfield projects today still default to Profinet or EtherNet/IP because the engineering ecosystem is proven. Expect OPC UA FX to appear first in machine-building and semiconductor fabs, where vendor neutrality has direct commercial value, before trickling into process industries.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the most frequently cited part of the family in procurement specifications this year, now that asset owners are routinely demanding zone-and-conduit diagrams and target security levels (SL-T) as part of FEED deliverables. If you haven't yet been asked to produce one, you likely will be in 2027 — particularly for any project with insurance or regulatory scrutiny. ISA/IEC 62443-2-1, the asset-owner security program standard, was substantially revised and republished in 2024, and uptake into corporate OT security programs is accelerating. It now aligns much more closely with NIST CSF 2.0's Govern function, giving controls engineers a cleaner mapping when their IT counterparts ask 'how does your OT program map to CSF?' The practical upside is less duplicate paperwork. On the NIST side, SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the de facto reference for US critical infrastructure OT security, and the NIST OT Cybersecurity program has signalled interest in a focused supplement on secure remote access — an area where controls engineers daily juggle vendor VPNs, jump servers, and increasingly, zero-trust brokers. Watch for draft activity in early 2027.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's Azure API Management exploitation against Norwegian energy firms is a textbook example of how cloud-side flaws now reach OT. According to Microsoft's disclosure, attackers abused an authorization flaw in Azure API Management to extract secrets — API keys, connection strings, and service principal credentials — from tenants used by several energy sector customers. While the immediate target was cloud-stored secrets, the downstream risk is that those secrets often broker access to on-premises historians, remote-access portals, and cloud-connected edge gateways sitting at Purdue Level 3.5. The attack path did not require any OT-specific knowledge: a cloud misconfiguration plus a platform flaw was enough to reach credentials that unlock OT-adjacent systems. This mirrors the pattern seen in the 2023 Dragos and 2024 Schneider cloud-portal incidents — the OT boundary is increasingly defined by whichever cloud identity can reach your edge device. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, inventory every cloud service that holds credentials capable of reaching OT, and treat those credential stores as Level 3.5 assets regardless of where they live. Second, rotate service principal and API keys on a defined cadence, not just after an incident. Third, insist that edge gateways and remote-access brokers support short-lived, certificate-based authentication rather than long-lived shared secrets — most major vendors now support this, but it is rarely enabled by default.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a cluster of ICS advisories today with direct relevance to plant-floor and OEM engineers. Autonomy Logic's OpenPLC Runtime (<=4.2.4) has a race condition (CVE-2026-126088, CVSS 8.1) that lets unauthenticated attackers create multiple admin accounts — a serious concern for anyone using OpenPLC in lab, educational, or small production settings. Siemens Mendix Studio Pro has multiple MCP Server flaws permitting remote browser-driven attacks, and two large Siemens advisories cover the 'Node2Shell' Brotli decoder flaw (CVE-2026-111497) that ripples across their Industrial Software and Industrial Automation Software product lines; patches are available. The libumqtt advisory (CVE-2026-125132, CVSS 8.6) affects an MQTT client library widely embedded in IoT/edge gateways, so check vendor SBOMs. On the safety side, CISA flagged Elecbrakes EB2, VM1, and ELBC2000 electric trailer brake controllers with vulnerabilities that could allow an attacker to take control of braking, disable brake lights, or modify firmware — a rare and sobering reminder that 'ICS' now extends to consumer-grade mobile actuators sharing the roads with us. SecurityWeek reports Microsoft patched an Azure API Management flaw actively exploited to steal secrets from Norwegian energy companies, among other sectors. For OT teams whose historians, remote-access brokers, or cloud twin services run on Azure, review API Management configurations and rotate service principal credentials. Separately, GL.iNet routers and KVM-over-IP devices were compromised via the GoodCloud remote management service — cleanup requires firmware reflash. These dual-use devices are common in engineering remote-access kits. Dragos revealed that three AI inference providers suffered undetected intrusions involving nearly two million prompts, raising questions for any OT shop experimenting with cloud LLMs for alarm triage or procedure generation. Finally, SANS ISC noted that despite a flurry of 'xxx2Shell' deserialization bugs (phpAfterShell, Java4Shell, NGINX2Shell), they are holding at Infocon Green — but the pattern suggests classic deserialization flaws are having a renaissance worth watching in any Java/PHP-based SCADA web interface.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks are heavy on enterprise and perimeter infrastructure that typically sits adjacent to OT networks. Cisco Expressway (CVE-2026-76466) has hard-coded credentials allowing remote root execution — relevant because Expressway is often the collaboration gateway used by vendors for remote support. Broadcom VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890) has an auth-bypass in the Directory Service; most OT virtualization stacks still run vCenter, so patch urgently. Check Point gateways (CVE-2026-123039) and Barracuda ESG/WAF/ADC (CVE-2026-111560, CVE-2026-116606) round out the perimeter concerns. For OT-adjacent application servers, SAP NetWeaver (CVE-2025-42884) and Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448) both have unauthenticated RCE paths — SAP in particular is often the business-to-plant integration layer. Perforce Puppet Enterprise (CVE-2026-115731) deserialization is notable for any site using Puppet to manage engineering workstations or Linux-based historians. OpenSSH on Linux (CVE-2026-123158) auth bypass is worth tracking across every jump host in your DMZ. Practical priority for a controls engineer this week: inventory Internet-reachable vCenter, Expressway, Check Point, and Barracuda assets touching your Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ; verify Siemens patch status for the Node2Shell advisories; and confirm no OpenPLC instances are reachable beyond the engineering VLAN until 4.2.5 is deployed.Read more →
