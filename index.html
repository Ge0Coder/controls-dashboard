Treat your Fortinet perimeter as compromised until proven otherwise — FortiBleed has exposed credentials from roughly 86,000 internet-facing FortiGate and FortiVPN devices, which is the most common IT/OT boundary firewall in the field. Compound that with an actively exploited unauthenticated RCE in Splunk Enterprise (CVE-2026-20253) and you have both the perimeter and the detection layer under pressure in the same week. Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Modbus protocol, designed by Modicon in 1979 for the 984 PLC, allocated only seven bits for the function code and uses a single byte for the unit identifier — meaning the entire protocol's command vocabulary was architected to fit comfortably inside a 256-byte serial frame on an RS-485 line running at 9,600 baud. Nearly half a century later, that same frame structure is what your TCP/502 traffic still carries, padded by the MBAP header. It is one of the longest-lived wire formats in industry, and its lack of authentication is a direct consequence of an era when 'the network' meant a twisted pair inside a locked cabinet.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics: the first formal three-term controller was implemented pneumatically in the 1930s by the Taylor Instrument Companies, with proportional, integral and derivative action realised through bellows, restrictors and nozzles working on instrument air at roughly 3 to 15 psi. Nicholas Minorsky had derived the mathematics even earlier, in 1922, while working on automatic steering for the USS New Mexico — he obtained the integral and derivative terms by watching helmsmen and observing that good ones anticipated the ship's swing rather than just reacting to heading error. The 3-15 psi signal range survived the transition to electronics as the 4-20 mA loop, which is why your modern HART transmitter still echoes a pneumatic convention from a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the controller-to-controller and controller-to-field-device profile of OPC UA that has been progressively rolling out since the joint announcement by the OPC Foundation, ODVA, PI and others. Where classic OPC UA targeted vertical communication from PLC to SCADA or MES, OPC UA FX defines the semantics, information models and PubSub mappings needed for horizontal, deterministic exchange between PLCs and even down to drives and I/O — running over standard Ethernet and, optionally, Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) for hard real-time performance. For a controls engineer, the practical promise is a single, vendor-neutral way to do peer-to-peer data exchange between, say, a Siemens line controller and a Rockwell packaging cell without writing custom gateway logic in a protocol converter. The information-model approach also means a motor or valve appears as a typed object with consistent attributes regardless of vendor, which dramatically reduces tag mapping work during integration. Adoption challenges remain real, however. TSN switching hardware is still expensive and configuration tooling is immature; conformance certification for FX-capable devices is only just emerging from major vendors; and most installed bases still rely on EtherNet/IP, PROFINET or EtherCAT for the fieldbus layer. Expect to see FX first in greenfield automotive and battery plants where the cost of protocol heterogeneity is highest, and only later in retrofits.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle requirements) continue to be the standards drawing the most procurement activity, particularly as European operators prepare for full NIS2 enforcement and US asset owners reference them in TSA pipeline directives. Increasingly, RFQs from end users now require vendors to provide an IEC 62443-4-1 certification or a documented gap assessment, and to map their product capabilities to 62443-4-2 security levels SL1 through SL4. If you are specifying PLCs, RTUs or HMIs this year, asking for the SL-Capability statement is becoming a baseline expectation rather than a stretch goal. On the framework side, NIST is continuing work on SP 800-82 Revision 3, which aligns OT guidance with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function and adds more explicit treatment of cloud-connected OT, remote access, and supply chain considerations. ISA's TR84.00.09 on cybersecurity related to safety instrumented systems also remains the go-to reference for any engineer wrestling with how to keep SIS logic solvers segregated from BPCS in a way that satisfies both functional safety and cyber auditors — particularly relevant given how many of this month's KEV entries involve VPN and firewall products that often sit between BPCS and SIS zones in practice.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's reflection is on the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where attackers affiliated with the CyberAv3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station and defaced its HMI with a political message. The intrusion was straightforward: the PLC was exposed directly to the internet on its default port (TCP 20256) with the factory-default password '1111' still in place. No zero-day, no malware — just a reachable controller with no authentication hardening. What made the event significant was not the technical sophistication but the scale of similar exposure it exposed. CISA's follow-up advisory found hundreds of similar Unitronics PLCs reachable from the internet across US water and wastewater utilities, many in small municipalities without dedicated OT security staff. The booster station kept running on manual control with no process upset, but it could easily have gone differently. Three lessons remain directly actionable. First, no controller should ever have its programming port exposed to the public internet — use a VPN or a properly configured DMZ jump host with MFA. Second, change every default credential on every device, including 'unimportant' auxiliary controllers, because attackers do not distinguish. Third, build the manual-operations muscle: Aliquippa stayed safe because operators knew how to run the station without the HMI, and that capability needs to be drilled, not assumed.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline event for OT teams this week is FortiBleed, a large-scale credential theft campaign that has compromised roughly 86,000 internet-accessible Fortinet firewalls and VPN concentrators — about half of all such devices reachable from the public internet. Because Fortinet appliances are extremely common as the IT/OT boundary firewall and as remote-access termination points for vendors and engineers, the practical assumption today is that any Fortinet device with a publicly reachable management or SSL-VPN interface should be considered compromised until proven otherwise. Rotate local accounts and pre-shared keys, force MFA re-enrolment, and review VPN session logs for the past several weeks for unfamiliar source ASNs. In parallel, Splunk Enterprise CVE-2026-20253 — an unauthenticated RCE via the PostgreSQL sidecar endpoint — is being exploited in the wild within days of disclosure, and CISA gave federal agencies only three days to patch. Many process plants now run Splunk as the central SIEM for OT log aggregation, so a compromise there is effectively a compromise of your detection layer. If your Splunk indexers or search heads sit in a shared IT/OT zone, prioritise patching ahead of normal change windows or pull them off the network until updated. A Klue supply-chain compromise has exfiltrated Salesforce data from several major security vendors, and Cisco announced an acquisition of WideField to bolster Splunk's 'agentic SOC' identity context — a reminder that the trend toward AI-driven, identity-centric SOC tooling is accelerating, and these tools will increasingly reach down into OT telemetry. Operation Endgame also took down 106 SocGholish command-and-control servers and cleaned 15,000 compromised WordPress sites, modestly reducing the drive-by download risk for engineering laptops used for vendor portal access. Finally, a new strain called CryptoBandits is notable because it bundles a SOCKS5-over-Tor backdoor with its data-theft functionality. The combination of covert command-and-control and remote code execution on the same victim host is precisely the toolset that has been used in past OT pivot attacks, where an infected engineering workstation becomes a long-term foothold into the control network.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

This month's KEV additions are heavy with network-edge and remote-access products that sit in or adjacent to OT DMZs. Beyond the Splunk and Fortinet items, CISA added Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-20262 path traversal and CVE-2026-20245 root command execution), Arista EOS tunnel decapsulation flaw CVE-2026-7473, Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 authentication bypass CVE-2026-50751 (known ransomware use), Palo Alto PAN-OS VPN auth bypass CVE-2026-0257, and an Ivanti Sentry unauthenticated root RCE (CVE-2026-10520). Collectively these affect the exact device classes used to segment plant networks and to terminate vendor and engineer VPNs. For controls engineers, the practical takeaway is to treat the IT/OT firewall, the jump-host VPN, and the SD-WAN edge as a single patch-priority block this cycle. Confirm with your IT counterparts that any of these products in your environment are at fixed firmware levels, and verify that IKEv1 is disabled where IKEv2 is supported — the Check Point flaw specifically bypasses authentication during IKEv1 negotiation. Two enterprise-side items also matter for plants: Oracle PeopleSoft (CVE-2026-35273, ransomware confirmed) and Oracle WebLogic (CVE-2024-21182) are commonly used as the backbone for MES, CMMS and historian web frontends, so check whether any plant-facing Oracle middleware is exposed.Read more →
