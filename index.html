Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PID tuning rules Ziegler and Nichols published in 1942 were derived empirically on pneumatic controllers using water-filled bellows and mercury manometers, not electronics. Their ultimate-gain method deliberately drives a loop into sustained oscillation — a technique that today would trigger safety alarms on most modern DCS systems, yet the resulting tuning constants are still the starting point taught in virtually every controls curriculum worldwide.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The centrifugal flyball governor that James Watt popularised in 1788 to regulate steam engines is widely credited as the first industrial feedback controller — but it was James Clerk Maxwell's 1868 paper "On Governors" that first mathematically analysed why some governors hunted and oscillated while others settled smoothly. That paper is now regarded as the founding document of control theory, predating Nyquist and Bode by more than sixty years. Maxwell, better known for electromagnetism, essentially invented stability analysis as a side quest because engineers kept building governors that shook their engines apart.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is quietly becoming one of the more consequential developments in industrial networking. It extends the OPC UA information model down to controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication, traditionally the domain of EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, or vendor-proprietary protocols. Combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) at the Ethernet layer, OPC UA FX promises deterministic, sub-millisecond cyclic data exchange between devices from different vendors over the same converged network — the long-promised death of the protocol zoo in the control cabinet. For plant-floor engineers, the practical implication is that over the next 3–5 years you'll start seeing drives, remote I/O, and even safety devices advertising OPC UA FX support alongside their traditional fieldbus. The appeal is obvious: one engineering tool, one diagnostic protocol, one security model from the sensor to the cloud. Adoption challenges are real though — TSN requires managed switches with hardware timestamping, your existing network engineers probably haven't configured 802.1Qbv scheduling before, and the first-generation devices will carry a price premium. Most sites will see FX arrive piecemeal, starting with new greenfield cells, not retrofits.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to gain traction as the de-facto method asset owners are being asked to apply during FEED and detailed design phases. The key practical artifact it demands is a documented zone and conduit model with target security levels (SL-T) justified by risk — something many brownfield sites still don't have formally recorded. If your organisation is bidding on new capital projects, expect this to appear in the technical specifications, often referenced alongside NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3. ISA is also progressing work on 62443-4-2 conformance testing through ISASecure's CSA certification scheme, which is increasingly cited in procurement specifications for PLCs, RTUs and gateways. The practical takeaway for engineers specifying new hardware: ask vendors for the specific SL-C (capability security level) their device is certified to, not just marketing claims of "62443 compliant." A controller certified to SL-C 2 under ISASecure CSA is a measurable thing; a datasheet claim of "supports 62443" is not. This distinction is starting to matter during audits and insurance renewals.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, where the CyberAv3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers didn't exploit a sophisticated vulnerability — the PLC was internet-exposed on its default port 20256 with the default password "1111" still in place. They defaced the HMI with a political message and forced the station into manual operation; crucially, water quality was never affected because operators had physical fallback procedures. The incident is instructive precisely because it was so unsophisticated. Shodan at the time showed thousands of Unitronics PLCs similarly exposed worldwide, and CISA issued an advisory noting the same attacker was targeting the entire product line opportunistically. Three lessons remain evergreen: first, no PLC should ever have a management interface reachable from the public internet, full stop — if remote access is required, it goes through an authenticated VPN with MFA. Second, default credentials must be changed as part of commissioning sign-off, with a verification step in the FAT checklist. Third, manual fallback procedures are not legacy baggage — they are the control of last resort, and the Aliquippa operators' ability to run the station manually is the reason this incident is a case study rather than a public-health emergency.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline today is CVE-2026-21589, a critical arbitrary file access vulnerability in self-hosted Atlassian Data Center products. Atlassian pushed patches on October 5th, and SANS ISC confirmed scanning and exploitation attempts began within hours of PoC publication. Jira, Confluence and related tooling frequently sit in engineering networks as documentation hubs for P&IDs, control narratives, FAT/SAT records and change tickets — a successful read-primitive here can expose network diagrams, credentials in attachments, and PLC source archives. If your plant still runs self-hosted Atlassian behind the firewall, treat patching as urgent and audit what sensitive engineering content is actually stored there. SonicWall and Splunk released fixes for critical authentication-bypass and RCE issues. SonicWall gateways are commonly used as the DMZ boundary for remote vendor access into OT, and Splunk is increasingly deployed as an OT SIEM or historian analytics layer. Both deserve priority patching in change windows. Meanwhile, TP-Link is facing state-level lawsuits over ISP router flaws — a reminder that consumer-grade networking gear sometimes creeps into remote pump stations, small sites, and contractor-managed cells where it should never live. On the threat-actor front, the US has placed a $10M bounty on an alleged Hafnium operator, Zhang Yu, tied to the Chinese group that historically targeted Exchange and later pivoted to critical infrastructure reconnaissance. SANS also continues to document abuse of legitimate RMM tools (ScreenConnect and others) by threat actors — directly relevant to OT, where vendor remote-support tools are often whitelisted and unmonitored. Review which RMM agents are installed on engineering workstations and HMIs, and disable any that aren't contractually required. Finally, a cautionary business story: a ransomware "recovery" firm was caught quietly paying ransoms and reselling the decryption keys at an $11M markup. Worth remembering when evaluating incident-response retainers for your site.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the last two weeks are dominated by network-edge and identity-adjacent gear that typically fronts OT environments. Citrix NetScaler received three separate KEV entries (CVE-2026-88779, 88772, 88771) covering DoS, RCE and unauthenticated command execution. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-76504), Fortinet FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286), F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127), Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952), and both Check Point management and gateway products (CVE-2026-93616, 85102) round out a very ugly month for perimeter infrastructure. If any of these sit between your enterprise and plant networks — or terminate vendor VPNs into OT — patch cycles need to be compressed. Two entries deserve special OT attention. MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-67279) is widely deployed in low-cost industrial and remote-site deployments; unauthenticated exec access on a RouterOS device sitting at a wellhead or substation is a direct pivot into Purdue Level 2. Zyxel GS1900 switches (CVE-2026-7273) are frequently found in small automation cabinets — stack overflow via crafted HTTP from the LAN side means anything on the control VLAN can take the switch over. Inventory these devices, disable web management where possible, and segment management interfaces onto a dedicated VLAN that engineering workstations cannot reach directly.Read more →
