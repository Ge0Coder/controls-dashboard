Today's Knowledge Bite

The RADIUS protocol vulnerability (Blast-RADIUS) that's now showing up in Schneider's Modicon switches has its roots in MD5 collision research from 2004 — yet RADIUS over UDP with MD5-based Message-Authenticator handling persisted in industrial network gear for two decades. The flaw lets an attacker who can intercept RADIUS traffic forge an Access-Accept response, effectively bypassing authentication to network management. It's a striking example of how cryptographic debt accumulates silently in OT, where firmware lifecycles measure in decades.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term traces back to a 1922 paper by Nicolas Minorsky, who derived it while watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico. He noticed experienced helmsmen didn't just react to the current heading error — they also accounted for how long the ship had been off course and how fast it was drifting back, intuitively implementing proportional, integral, and derivative action. Minorsky's mathematical formalization predated the term 'PID' by decades and was originally rejected by the Navy as overcomplicated. Today essentially every process loop on Earth runs on a refinement of what one engineer learned by watching sailors.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on Sparkplug B over MQTT, which continues to gain traction as the de facto interoperability layer for IIoT and unified namespace architectures. Sparkplug adds structure to raw MQTT by defining birth/death certificates, state management, and a consistent topic namespace — solving the stateless, schema-less weaknesses that historically kept MQTT out of serious process applications. Combined with brokers like HiveMQ or EMQX, it gives engineers a publish/subscribe alternative to polling-based OPC UA for high-cardinality edge data. The practical implications are significant: a single broker can decouple hundreds of PLCs from dozens of consumers (historian, MES, analytics, cloud) without point-to-point integration. But the Yarbo advisory this week underlines the catch — broker security is everything. Hard-coded credentials, missing TLS, shared cloud tenancy, and unauthenticated topics turn a unified namespace into a unified attack surface. Adoption challenges remain around access control granularity (topic-level ACLs are coarse), schema governance (Sparkplug metric definitions drift without discipline), and the cultural shift required for control engineers used to deterministic, request-response paradigms to trust event-driven, eventually-consistent data flows.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-3 system security requirements continue to be the most frequently cited framework in vendor security advisories, and this week's Schneider EcoStruxure Panel Server advisory is a textbook case: the device is marketed on its 62443-4-2 component certification, yet a flaw in authentication handling still slipped through. This highlights a point worth remembering — 62443 certification attests to the security development lifecycle and capability set, not to the absence of vulnerabilities. Asset owners must still patch certified devices. On the standards horizon, ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) revision work continues, with the updated edition emphasizing risk assessment cadence and integration with safety lifecycle activities per IEC 61511. For engineers, the practical takeaway is to align your cyber risk assessments with your PHA/LOPA cycle rather than treating them as separate exercises. NIST's SP 800-82r3 also remains the most useful free reference for mapping OT-specific controls back to 800-53, and pairs well with 62443 when explaining requirements to IT counterparts.Read more →

Incident of the Week

The freshest case study is this week's claimed breach of California Water Service by the Iranian-linked Handala group. The 5GB leak reportedly includes customer PII and, more critically for OT, credentials for the RTKBase platform — an open-source GNSS reference station management system used for high-precision positioning. While RTKBase isn't directly a SCADA component, its presence in a water utility suggests use for asset surveying, leak detection vehicles, or precision agriculture tie-ins, and any leaked credentials likely overlap with broader IT/OT identity stores. The pattern matches prior Iranian-affiliated activity against U.S. water utilities (notably the 2023 Aliquippa Unitronics incident attributed to CyberAv3ngers): opportunistic targeting of small-to-mid water systems, exploitation of exposed or weakly-authenticated assets, and quick public leaks to maximize psychological impact. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, inventory every internet-reachable asset including auxiliary systems like GNSS, telemetry, and engineering tooling — not just HMIs and PLCs. Second, assume any credential leaked from an auxiliary platform will be tried against your historian, VPN, and AD. Third, water sector ISAC (WaterISAC) advisories should be in your weekly reading even if you're in another sector, because tradecraft transfers fast.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's CISA ICS advisories hit a broad swath of plant-floor and edge equipment. Schneider Electric issued two notable advisories: one on the Modicon Network Managed Switch family covering the RADIUS protocol forgery flaw (Blast-RADIUS class), and another on the EcoStruxure Panel Server where authentication weaknesses could allow unauthorized access to a gateway commonly used to bridge edge devices to cloud or supervisory layers. Siemens-distributed KACO Blueplanet inverters have multiple flaws letting attackers derive credentials from the device serial number — a recurring solar/DER weakness that has previously been exploited at scale. If you operate behind-the-meter generation or have Modicon-managed switching on your control LAN, these warrant immediate review. On the IIoT and physical security side, advisories landed for Brickcom IP cameras (unauthenticated access to feeds and admin takeover), the Naxclow IoT platform powering several consumer cameras and doorbells, and Yarbo's robotic lawn/snow equipment cloud — the latter is interesting because hard-coded credentials in the MQTT cloud infrastructure could allow remote operational commands to an entire robot fleet. The pattern is familiar to OT engineers: shared cloud brokers and embedded secrets continue to be the soft underbelly of any connected fleet, and the lessons translate directly to industrial MQTT brokers in Sparkplug B deployments. In broader news, SecurityWeek reports Iranian group Handala claims to have breached California Water Service, leaking 5GB including RTKBase (GNSS reference station) credentials — a reminder that water utilities remain a preferred target. Ivanti Sentry CVE-2026-10520 is now being hit by honeypots, and Microsoft's June Patch Tuesday addressed 204 vulnerabilities including 38 critical. SecurityWeek also notes ICS device exposure on the internet has remained flat year-on-year despite a widening attack surface, suggesting collective hardening efforts are at least keeping pace.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this week heavily favor enterprise and identity infrastructure that nonetheless touches OT environments. CVE-2026-50751 in Check Point Security Gateway allows unauthenticated VPN bypass via IKEv1 — directly relevant to any site using Check Point for remote engineering access to plants. CVE-2026-10520 (Ivanti Sentry) gives remote root and is now seeing active exploitation in honeypots; if Sentry fronts your mobile device management for field technicians, patch immediately. For plant engineers, the practical action items are: audit every perimeter VPN and remote access appliance against this week's KEV list, confirm patch status with IT, and ensure any compensating controls (MFA, jump hosts, conditional access) are functioning.
