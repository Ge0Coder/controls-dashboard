Today's Knowledge Bite

The humble 4-20 mA current loop, still the dominant analogue signalling standard in process plants, was designed in the 1950s partly because current (unlike voltage) is immune to wire resistance losses over long cable runs — meaning a transmitter 2 km away reads identically to one on the next panel. The 4 mA 'live zero' was a clever bonus: a broken wire reads 0 mA, instantly distinguishable from a legitimate zero-process value, giving you free fault detection decades before smart diagnostics existed.

⚛

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller, which runs more process loops than any other algorithm on earth, predates electronics entirely — Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship stabilisers in 1911 and Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 mathematical formalisation for US Navy steering gear were purely mechanical and hydraulic implementations. Minorsky derived his three-term control law by watching helmsmen and noticing they didn't just steer proportional to error, but also anticipated based on rate of change — the derivative term is literally a mathematical model of an experienced sailor's intuition. Over a century later, we're still tuning Kp, Ki and Kd on loops that would be perfectly comprehensible to a 1920s naval engineer.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on Ethernet-APL (Advanced Physical Layer), which is finally crossing from pilot into mainstream brownfield retrofit projects. APL extends Ethernet over the same two-wire, intrinsically-safe cabling that has carried 4-20 mA and HART for decades, delivering 10 Mbit/s full-duplex plus bus power to field instruments up to 1,000 metres away — all certified for Zone 0/Division 1 hazardous areas. For a process engineer, that means a Coriolis flowmeter in a hydrocarbon service can finally stream full diagnostic payloads, waveform data, and multi-variable measurements over a single pair, without a segment coupler zoo. The practical implication is a profound simplification of the lower layers of the Purdue model: field instruments speak PROFINET or EtherNet/IP natively to a field switch, which uplinks to the DCS. HART-IP and FDI device packages ride on top, so asset management workflows don't have to be reinvented. The main adoption friction right now is not the technology but the ecosystem — APL-capable transmitters from Endress+Hauser, Emerson, ABB and Siemens are shipping, but DCS integration patterns, cybersecurity zoning (every instrument is now an IP endpoint) and electrician skillsets are lagging. Expect the first wave of real deployments in greenfield LNG, pharma and specialty chemicals where the diagnostic ROI is clearest.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 remains the centre of gravity for OT cybersecurity standards work. The IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) parts continue to be the two most frequently cited in procurement specifications, and we're seeing more end users mandate SL-2 as a baseline for new PLC and RTU purchases, with SL-3 for safety-critical zones. If you're writing a technical bid specification this quarter, cite the specific Security Level per zone rather than a blanket 'must comply with 62443' — vendors interpret the latter very loosely. Worth noting is the growing cross-pollination between IEC 62443 and NIST's SP 800-82 Rev. 3, released in 2023, which explicitly maps its controls to 62443 zones and conduits. For US-regulated sectors (particularly water and wastewater following the EPA's renewed focus post-2024 incidents), 800-82r3 is becoming the de facto reference for site assessments. On the safety side, ISA TR84.00.09 continues to be the most practical guide for integrating cybersecurity into SIS lifecycle management — a topic that functional safety engineers often prefer to leave to 'the IT people', to everyone's eventual regret.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's retrospective is the 2024 American Water Works cyber incident, which remains highly instructive as the broader water sector grapples with modernisation. The largest regulated water and wastewater utility in the US detected unauthorised activity in its IT environment in early October 2024, took its customer portal and billing systems offline as a precaution, and notified regulators. Crucially, the company stated that water and wastewater treatment operations were not impacted — a direct result of segmentation between corporate IT and OT. What we know publicly suggests the attacker gained a foothold in the enterprise network, likely via phishing or an exposed remote access service, and American Water's incident response team isolated OT before any lateral movement could reach SCADA systems. The disruption was limited to customer-facing services and some back-office functions, not treatment or distribution. The lessons for controls engineers are concrete. First, segmentation works, but only if it's enforced in hardware with explicit allow-lists at the conduit, not just VLAN separation on a shared switch. Second, the ability to operate the plant 'manually' or in a degraded mode when the business network is unavailable needs to be rehearsed, not assumed — operators who have never run without the historian trending will struggle under pressure. Third, pre-plan which systems you will proactively isolate during an IT incident, and get that decision authority delegated to the shift supervisor in advance; waiting for a CISO call at 2am costs hours you don't have.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The week's news is dominated by enterprise and IT-adjacent stories, but several items warrant attention from plant floor engineers. Fortra patched critical authentication-bypass and remote code execution flaws in BoKS, a privileged access management product used in some regulated manufacturing and energy environments where it governs root access to Unix hosts underpinning historians and MES platforms. If BoKS is anywhere in your IT/OT boundary, patching is urgent. SANS ISC flagged continued abuse of ConnectWise ScreenConnect by threat actors — a reminder that remote support tools sitting on engineering workstations are a favoured living-off-the-land path into OT. Inventory which remote access agents are installed on HMIs, engineering laptops, and jump hosts, and ensure only an approved tool is whitelisted. The hijacking of Microsoft's official X account to shill crypto scams is a reminder that even high-trust corporate channels can be compromised; treat urgent 'security advisories' delivered via social media with scepticism and verify through vendor PSIRT portals. On the geopolitical front, the extradition of alleged Mabna Institute operator Amir Barati to the US is notable — Iranian state-aligned activity against energy, water and manufacturing targets has not slowed, and we should expect retaliatory probing of exposed OT assets. Finally, doxx.net's $38M raise for an 'agent defence network' underlines how fast agentic AI is moving into production workflows; controls engineers evaluating AI copilots for alarm rationalisation or procedure generation should be asking suppliers hard questions about identity, authorisation scope, and audit trails.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks are heavily weighted toward network edge devices that commonly sit at the IT/OT boundary. Fortinet FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286), Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-76504), Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway (CVE-2026-88771/88772), F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127), Check Point Security Gateway and management servers (CVE-2026-85102, CVE-2026-93616), Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952), MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-67279) and Zyxel GS1900 switches (CVE-2026-7273) are all now actively exploited. Several — notably NetScaler, F5 APM, Check Point VPN and the SD-WAN products — are frequently deployed as remote access concentrators for vendor support into substations, pumping stations and plants. Treat these as emergency patches, and if a maintenance window is weeks away, at minimum restrict management-plane access and review VPN session logs for anomalies. The Zyxel GS1900 flaw deserves specific mention: these inexpensive managed switches turn up in small sites, remote telemetry cabinets and OEM skids far more often than asset inventories admit. A LAN-based unauthenticated command execution on a switch is a textbook pivot point. Pull your switch inventory and look for GS1900-8, -16, -24 and -48 variants. Three Linux kernel entries (CVE-2025-39964, CVE-2026-53266, CVE-2025-39682) and a WordPress Core RFI matter less directly for PLCs but very much for the Linux-based historians, edge gateways, and corporate-facing plant information portals that quietly accumulate in brownfield sites.Read more →
