☀ Morning Briefing 2026-07-08 09:26 UTC

Your priority today is patch triage on two OT-adjacent CISA KEV additions: PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569, unauth RCE) and Lantronix EDS5000 serial device servers (CVE-2025-67038, root command injection). Both are being actively exploited and both live in places controls engineers own but rarely patch — PLM systems and the little grey boxes bolted next to legacy PLCs. Pull an asset inventory of Lantronix device servers by OUI and MAC scan today, and get Windchill either patched or firewalled off from anything but engineering workstations. If you self-host Gitea for PLC code or recipes, patch that too — CVE-2026-20896 is under mass exploitation.

Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix EDS and its cousins (MOXA NPort, Digi PortServer) collectively expose more legacy serial-only PLCs to Ethernet than any single PLC vendor has ever shipped. Because they translate RS-232/485 to TCP transparently, a compromised device server effectively gives an attacker native Modbus RTU or DF1 access to controllers that were designed in an era when "network security" meant a locked cabinet door.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely. Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilots in 1911 already implemented proportional, integral and derivative action mechanically, using flywheels and dashpots, to keep US Navy destroyers on heading. Nicholas Minorsky formalized the mathematics in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer — noticing that good ones didn't just react to current error, they also anticipated (derivative) and compensated for persistent bias (integral). Every loop you tune today is still, in essence, a mathematical portrait of a competent 1920s sailor.

Tech Spotlight

Ethernet-APL (Advanced Physical Layer) is finally hitting real project specifications this year and it's worth understanding. APL delivers 10 Mbit/s full-duplex Ethernet plus loop power over the same two-wire cabling process instruments have used for decades, and it is intrinsically-safe rated for Zone 0/Division 1 hazardous areas. In practice, this means a Coriolis meter or radar level transmitter can speak PROFINET or EtherNet/IP (and increasingly OPC UA FX) directly to your DCS without a HART multiplexer or fieldbus gateway in between. The practical implication for controls engineers is significant: instrument diagnostics, secondary variables and configuration data that used to be trapped behind HART-IP gateways become first-class citizens on the control network. That's great for asset management and predictive maintenance, but it also means every transmitter is now an IP endpoint with a MAC address, a firmware version and — potentially — a CVE. Adoption challenges are real: switch vendors are still catching up on APL field-switch portfolios, cable qualification for existing runs is not always straightforward, and most sites lack a segmentation model for treating instruments as network assets. If you're specifying a greenfield unit in 2026, ask your DCS vendor what their APL roadmap looks like before you commit to another decade of 4–20 mA.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often cited in new project specs, and increasingly EPCs are being asked to deliver a documented zone-and-conduit model as a contractual deliverable, not just an afterthought. If you haven't done one recently, the practical starting point is a data-flow diagram overlaid on your P&IDs — you'll typically discover two or three conduits nobody remembered existed, usually involving a vendor remote-support laptop or a historian pulling from a safety system. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the reference for OT security guidance and pairs well with the CISA Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs), which are becoming the de-facto baseline that insurers and auditors check against for water, energy and manufacturing operators. ISA's TR84.00.09 on cybersecurity for safety instrumented systems is also worth a re-read if your SIS shares any network infrastructure with the BPCS — the guidance on independence between security countermeasures and safety functions has aged well and is showing up in more FEED-stage HAZOP/LOPA discussions.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group (linked to Iranian IRGC) defaced a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers didn't exploit a zero-day — they scanned the internet for Unitronics PLCs exposed on TCP/20256 and logged in using the well-known default password "1111". Once in, they rewrote the HMI screen with a political message and forced the operator to switch to manual control. The attack succeeded because the PLC was directly internet-reachable, retained factory default credentials, and had no network-layer authentication on its programming protocol. Nobody was hurt and water quality wasn't affected, but the case became the reference incident that CISA now cites when pushing operators toward the CPGs. The three lessons that keep applying: (1) assume Shodan already has an inventory of your externally-reachable controllers — check yours before an adversary does; (2) default credentials on PLCs and HMIs are still the number-one initial access vector in water and small manufacturing, ahead of any CVE; (3) even a purely symbolic defacement forces manual operation, which for a small utility with thin staffing is itself a meaningful availability impact.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline items today are enterprise-IT vulnerabilities but several bleed directly into plant environments. Adobe ColdFusion CVE-2026-48282 (CVSS 10) is under active exploitation and remains embedded in many older MES, historian dashboards, and vendor engineering portals — a good day to inventory any ColdFusion instance still living in a DMZ. A critical Gitea authentication-bypass (CVE-2026-20896) is also being exploited in the wild; if your engineering or PLC-code repository sits on a self-hosted Gitea, patch immediately, as attackers are reading secrets and source with a single crafted HTTP header. On the geopolitical side, researchers report an Iran-linked actor using a modular C2 framework and, notably, pivoting through IT service providers to reach high-value Israeli targets. That supply-chain-through-MSP pattern is the same pathway that has previously hit water and manufacturing operators, and it warrants a quick review of what your integrators and remote-support vendors can reach inside your OT network. Expect that same technique to be used against your ICS vendors' code (by both defenders and adversaries) over the next 12 months. CISA Vulnerability Digest

Two additions this fortnight stand out for OT teams. CVE-2026-12569 in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM is an unauthenticated RCE reachable over the network — Windchill is the PLM backbone for a large slice of discrete manufacturing, holding CAD, BOM and change-management data. Patch per PTC's advisory and, at minimum, ensure Windchill is not reachable from untrusted networks or vendor VPN segments. CVE-2025-67038 in the Lantronix EDS5000 device server is arguably more painful: OS command injection via the username parameter, executed as root, on a serial-to-Ethernet gateway that is often bolted directly to legacy PLCs, RTUs, drives and meters. These devices rarely get patched, are often forgotten in asset inventories, and frequently have their web UIs exposed. Hunt them down by MAC OUI (00:80:A3) and by scanning for their default ports. The Ubiquiti UniFi OS trio (CVE-2026-34908/34909/34910) matters because UniFi has quietly become the wireless and switching layer in many small plants, remote pump stations and integrator-supplied cabinets. Command injection and path traversal on an in-network device puts an attacker one hop from your control LAN. Arista EOS CVE-2026-7473, an incorrect tunnel decapsulation bug, is less dramatic but worth checking if you use EOS as a distribution/core in Purdue Levels 3–3.5. Round out patch planning with the Cisco Unified CM SSRF (CVE-2026-20230), Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager path traversal (CVE-2026-20262) and Splunk Enterprise missing-auth (CVE-2026-20253) if you use Splunk for OT log aggregation — that one lets an unauthenticated caller truncate arbitrary files via a PostgreSQL sidecar endpoint.Read more →
