Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PID controller, patented by Elmer Sperry in 1911 for ship steering, was mechanical — using gyroscopes and hydraulic servos with no electronics whatsoever. Sperry empirically tuned the derivative term by watching how an experienced helmsman anticipated waves. Decades later, Ziegler and Nichols formalized the same intuition into the tuning rules controls engineers still use today.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

James Clerk Maxwell's 1868 paper 'On Governors' is generally regarded as the first mathematical treatment of feedback control — he wrote it to explain why Watt's centrifugal governors on steam engines sometimes hunted into violent oscillation instead of settling. Maxwell derived the stability condition by analyzing the roots of the characteristic equation, predating Routh and Hurwitz by a decade and effectively inventing control theory as a discipline. The same eigenvalue analysis you'd use today to tune a cascade loop traces directly back to a Victorian physicist trying to keep steam engines from shaking themselves apart.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to gain ground as the converged protocol stack meant to replace the alphabet soup of fieldbuses and industrial Ethernet variants. The promise is straightforward: deterministic, sub-millisecond Ethernet on standard switches, carrying both real-time control traffic and information-model-rich OPC UA messages on the same wire. For a controls engineer, that means motion-grade I/O, PLC-to-PLC peer messaging, and historian data flowing through one physical and logical infrastructure rather than separate Profinet, EtherCAT, and Ethernet/IP islands. The reality on plant floors is messier. TSN requires every switch and end device in the time-critical path to support IEEE 802.1AS time sync, 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic, and 802.1CB frame replication — and to interoperate across vendors. Most installed switching gear doesn't, so TSN today means a forklift upgrade or a parallel network. Vendor 'TSN-ready' marketing also obscures the fact that the OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) profile, which standardizes the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device data models, is still maturing. Early adopters are mostly in automotive and semiconductor where greenfield builds justify the cost; brownfield process plants will likely live with hybrid architectures for another decade.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) remain the most cited parts of the family in procurement specs, but worth watching is the increasing alignment between 62443 and the EU's Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), which begins to bite for products placed on the EU market from December 2027. CRA mandates secure-by-default configurations, vulnerability handling processes, and SBOM availability — requirements that map cleanly onto 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle). Vendors who have already certified to 62443-4-1 will have a much easier path to CRA conformity. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 (Guide to OT Security) remains the practical companion to the CSF 2.0 and is increasingly referenced in CISA advisories as the recommended baseline. For asset owners writing internal standards this year, the pragmatic move is to anchor your OT security program to CSF 2.0 functions, use 800-82r3 as the control narrative, and require 62443-4-1/4-2 evidence from vendors — that triad keeps you aligned with both regulators and the supply chain.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's case study is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers defaced the HMI screen with an anti-Israel message and forced the station offline; operators had to switch to manual control and continued service without interruption, but the PLC had to be replaced. CISA later confirmed multiple US water utilities were hit through the same vector. The root cause was almost embarrassingly simple: the Unitronics PLCs were exposed directly to the internet on TCP/20256 with the factory-default password '1111'. No exploit, no zero-day — just exposed devices with default credentials. The attackers used Shodan-style scanning to find them. Key lessons for controls engineers: first, no PLC or HMI should ever be directly internet-reachable; if remote access is required, it goes through an authenticated VPN with MFA into a jump host, full stop. Second, default credentials on field devices must be part of commissioning checklists and audited periodically — not assumed to be changed. Third, even when the operational impact was limited by manual fallback, the reputational and regulatory consequences were substantial; a 'we handled it' incident still triggers federal scrutiny. Asset inventory and external attack-surface monitoring (e.g., running your own Shodan queries against your public IP ranges) are cheap controls that would have caught this.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA issued a heavy slate of ICS advisories today, with the most concerning being the XCharge C6 EV charger vulnerabilities (CVSS 9.8) covering unsigned firmware updates, stack-based buffer overflows, and insecure resource initialization. For sites operating EV fleets or workplace charging infrastructure, these chargers sit on the same networks as building management and increasingly tie into energy management systems — they deserve segmentation and an urgent firmware check. Schneider Electric also disclosed a flaw in EcoStruxure Machine Expert HVAC, the programming software for Modicon M171/M172 controllers used widely in building HVAC, which could leak sensitive credentials from project files. On the maritime side, the MacGregor (Danelec) Voyage Data Recorder G4e advisory (CVSS 8.3) flags default credentials granting admin access. VDRs are the maritime equivalent of the cockpit voice recorder and are increasingly networked for remote diagnostics — a compromise can both falsify forensic evidence and pivot to bridge systems. ABB's Busch-Welcome 2-Wire door opener actuator advisory is a reminder that low-voltage building automation devices increasingly grant physical access, blurring the line between IT, OT and physical security. Several CCTV/NVR advisories (KMW, CP Plus) round out the day with credential and XSS issues typical of the SOHO-grade gear often deployed in industrial perimeters. On the broader cyber landscape, a supply-chain attack hit 32 Red Hat npm packages with a credential-stealing worm reminiscent of Shai-Hulud — relevant for any team using Node-based HMIs, dashboards, or historians (Grafana plugins, Node-RED flows). The SANS ISC is also reporting a fresh phishing wave delivering SVG files, a file type many secure email gateways still under-inspect. Finally, Dutch police dismantled a 17-million-device residential proxy botnet, a useful reminder that attacker infrastructure increasingly originates from 'clean' residential IPs, defeating geo-IP and reputation-based firewall rules that some OT remote-access setups still rely on.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

Two KEV additions stand out for OT-adjacent infrastructure. CVE-2026-0257 in Palo Alto PAN-OS is an authentication bypass allowing unauthorized VPN connections — directly relevant for any plant using GlobalProtect for vendor remote access into the DMZ. CVE-2026-20182 in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller and Manager is similar, allowing unauthenticated remote attackers to gain administrative privileges. Both should be patched on priority since SD-WAN and VPN concentrators commonly bridge corporate and plant networks. CVE-2024-21182 in Oracle WebLogic (T3/IIOP) was also added; while not OT-native, WebLogic underpins many MES, ERP and historian middleware stacks. Also worth noting are two npm/extension supply-chain entries (Nx Console CVE-2026-48027 and TanStack CVE-2026-45321), both tagged as known-ransomware vectors — engineering laptops running VS Code with these extensions can become the initial foothold into OT. Practical advice: prioritize the Palo Alto and Cisco SD-WAN patches this week, audit engineering workstations for the compromised npm/VS Code extensions, and verify that vendor remote-access paths still require MFA in front of (not just inside) the VPN tunnel.Read more →
