Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix product line traces its lineage back to 1989, when serial-to-Ethernet 'device servers' were invented largely to keep expensive AS/400 and DEC minicomputer peripherals reachable as Ethernet displaced coax and Token Ring. Nearly four decades later, those same device server architectures — often running lightly-modified embedded Linux — are still the glue holding legacy serial PLCs and analyzers onto modern plant networks. It is a good reminder that in OT, 'legacy' isn't a phase; it's a permanent tenant.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalized not in a university lab but on a US Navy ship: Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analyzing the automatic steering of the USS New Mexico, observing how a skilled helmsman corrected not just for current heading error, but for the rate of change and the accumulated drift. His paper 'Directional Stability of Automatically Steered Bodies' predates Ziegler-Nichols tuning by two decades. Every loop you tune today is, in a sense, still steering a battleship.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to move from trade show demo to real deployment, and it's worth understanding even if your current plant is firmly Profinet or EtherNet/IP. The premise is compelling: a single deterministic Ethernet fabric carrying both hard-real-time motion traffic and best-effort IT traffic, using standard IEEE 802.1 mechanisms (time synchronization via 802.1AS, traffic scheduling via 802.1Qbv, frame preemption via 802.1Qbu) rather than vendor-proprietary layer-2 tricks. On top, OPC UA PubSub provides a common, secure, semantically-rich data model. The practical implication is that a controls engineer could, in principle, replace three parallel networks — motion, general control, and IT — with one converged infrastructure, and gain built-in security (OPC UA has native signing and encryption) as a bonus. In practice, adoption is slowed by three things: TSN-capable switches and endpoints still carry a premium, engineering tools for configuring TSN schedules are immature and vendor-specific, and integrators are (rightly) cautious about mixing safety-adjacent motion traffic with corporate IT flows on shared hardware. Expect to see it first in greenfield semiconductor, automotive, and packaging lines where the ROI on cabling reduction is clearest.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) remain the two parts most likely to appear in procurement specifications this year. If you're writing an RFQ for a new skid or line, requiring the supplier to state their target Security Level Capability (SL-C) per 62443-4-2 for each component — and to provide a Security Requirements Specification aligned with 3-2 — puts you in a much stronger position than a generic 'must be cyber secure' clause. Also worth tracking: NIST is continuing work on updates to SP 800-82 (Guide to OT Security), which remains the most accessible reference for asset owners even if you're not US-based. The forthcoming updates emphasize zero-trust concepts adapted for OT, better guidance on cloud-connected OT services, and clearer expectations for secure remote access — an area where too many sites still rely on ad-hoc jump hosts and shared credentials. If your organization is drafting internal OT security standards, aligning them explicitly to 62443 zones/conduits and 800-82 controls avoids re-inventing terminology that auditors and insurers already recognize.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Frontier Software / Australian government payroll incident, which although not strictly ICS, illustrates a failure mode increasingly relevant to OT: a compromise of an engineering-adjacent SaaS platform that held configuration and identity data for downstream operational systems. Attackers gained access via a third-party integration, exfiltrated data, and used the resulting credentials to move laterally into customer environments — including some with connected operational technology. The parallel for controls engineers is direct: platforms like PLM (Windchill), asset management (Maximo, SAP PM), and historian/analytics clouds (AVEVA, Cognite, GE Proficy) are trusted by design to touch OT data and, increasingly, to push configuration back down. If any of these are compromised, the blast radius extends well beyond 'IT.' Three lessons: first, inventory every cloud or vendor system that has a write path into your OT environment, not just read. Second, insist on scoped, short-lived credentials for those integrations rather than long-lived service accounts. Third, ensure your OT change management process would actually catch an unauthorized configuration push originating from a legitimately-authenticated but compromised upstream system — most don't.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The most directly OT-relevant story this week is the addition of PTC Windchill and FlexPLM to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (CVE-2026-12569). Windchill sits at the heart of product lifecycle management for many manufacturers, holding CAD, BOM, and process data that feeds directly into shop floor execution systems. Unauthenticated remote code execution against a PLM platform is not just an IT problem — it's a supply chain integrity problem, because compromised engineering data can propagate to MES, ERP, and eventually to production recipes and control logic. SecurityWeek's report on agentic AI conducting a full ransomware attack via Langflow is a milestone worth taking seriously. The attack chained known exploits with LLM-driven decision making to autonomously escalate, pivot, and encrypt — compressing the human-in-the-loop timeline that many OT defenders quietly rely on. For plants that assume 'nobody would bother reverse-engineering our weird HMI setup,' the calculus is changing: an agent can now do that reconnaissance cheaply and at scale. Medtronic's disclosure that 3.8 million patients were affected by an April ShinyHunters intrusion is a reminder that medical device manufacturers face the same corporate IT exposure as any other OEM — and that patient data theft can precede or accompany campaigns against clinical device fleets. The extradition of an alleged Scattered Spider member and the FBI/Google takedown of the NetNut residential proxy network are net positives; NetNut in particular was a favored tool for obscuring the origin of scanning and initial access attempts against exposed OT assets. Rounding out the week, Lantronix EDS5000 serial-to-Ethernet gateways were added to KEV for a root-level OS command injection (CVE-2025-67038). These devices are ubiquitous in legacy plants for bridging RS-232/485 field equipment to modern networks, and they are frequently forgotten in asset inventories. If you have Lantronix gear anywhere in your DMZ or process network, this one needs urgent attention.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The Lantronix EDS5000 entry (CVE-2025-67038) is the standout for controls engineers. Command injection via the username field, executing as root on a device that typically sits between a serial-connected PLC, meter, or analyzer and the plant network, is about as bad as it gets. Segregate these gateways behind a firewall that blocks inbound management access, disable the web UI where possible, and check vendor advisories for firmware updates. If the device is EOL, plan replacement — do not rely on 'nobody knows it's there.' PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569) and Oracle PeopleSoft PeopleTools (CVE-2026-35273, with known ransomware exploitation) are enterprise systems that nonetheless touch OT via engineering data flows and identity. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager received two entries (CVE-2026-20262 and CVE-2026-20245), relevant for anyone using SD-WAN to backhaul remote sites or plants. Also notable: Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 authentication bypass (CVE-2026-50751, known ransomware use) — if you terminate site-to-site VPNs from vendors or remote pump stations on Check Point, patch now and audit VPN session logs. Splunk Enterprise (CVE-2026-20253) rounds out the list; unauthenticated file creation via a Postgres sidecar can be a foothold in monitoring infrastructure that often has broad network visibility.Read more →
