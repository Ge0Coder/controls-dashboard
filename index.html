Today's Knowledge Bite

The RADIUS protocol underlying the new Schneider Modicon switch advisory dates from 1991 and still relies on MD5 for response authentication — a hash function that was considered cryptographically broken by 2005. The reason it persists in industrial network gear is that RADIUS was baked into firmware and AAA workflows long before anyone imagined a managed switch sitting on a process network would need a modern KDF. The Blast-RADIUS class of attacks finally forced vendors to push Message-Authenticator enforcement, which is exactly what Schneider is now mandating.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 from watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico. He noticed that good helmsmen did not just react to the current heading error (proportional), they also weighted how long the ship had been off-course (integral) and anticipated where it was heading (derivative). The first commercial pneumatic PID controllers from Taylor Instrument and Foxboro in the 1930s were essentially mechanical analogues of a skilled human at the wheel, and the math has not had to change since.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) layered under OPC UA continues to mature as the convergence story for deterministic industrial Ethernet. The premise is simple: instead of running PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, and CC-Link IE on parallel cable plants, you get one standards-based Ethernet fabric that carries bounded-latency motion traffic, regular control I/O, and IT data side by side. The IEEE 802.1 TSN suite (Qbv scheduling, Qbu preemption, AS gPTP time sync) provides the determinism; OPC UA PubSub provides the publish/subscribe semantics with rich, semantically-modelled data. The practical implication is that a PLC, a servo drive, a vision system, and a historian can all share one switch port without a motion command ever being delayed by a firmware update download. For greenfield builds — particularly battery gigafactories and semiconductor fabs — this is already viable with Siemens, B&R, Beckhoff, and Rockwell silicon. The adoption brake is twofold: TSN-capable switches are still 2-3x the cost of plain managed switches, and engineering tooling for configuring schedules (CNC streams, gate control lists) is vendor-specific despite the underlying standards. Until configuration converges, most brownfield sites are better served by keeping fieldbus segments isolated and using OPC UA only at the cell-to-line boundary.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) remain the two parts most actively cited in procurement specifications this year. The ISA Global Cybersecurity Alliance has been pushing certification of components against 4-2, and you are increasingly likely to see suppliers like Schneider, Siemens, and Rockwell list specific PLC or switch firmware versions as 4-2 certified to a given Security Level. Today's Modicon Network Managed Switch advisory is a useful reminder that certification is a point-in-time statement: a 4-2 certified switch can still inherit a protocol-level flaw like the RADIUS Message-Authenticator weakness, which is why 62443-2-4 (service provider requirements) and 2-1 (asset owner program) emphasise ongoing vulnerability handling, not just initial design. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 is now the authoritative OT guidance and explicitly maps to the CSF 2.0 Govern function — relevant because regulators in water, energy, and chemicals are increasingly asking for evidence of governance, not just technical controls. The new White House NSPM-12 memo on National Security Systems does not directly bind civilian operators, but the CNSS reactivation tends to ripple into DoD contract language within a year, which catches defence-industrial-base integrators. For anyone writing a new control system URS this quarter: explicitly require IEC 62443-4-2 SL2 components, demand a vendor SBOM aligned with CISA's minimum elements, and include a clause requiring disclosure of any KEV-listed CVE affecting delivered firmware within 30 days.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's instructive case is the ongoing Cal Water situation, where Iranian-aligned actors have publicly claimed access to California Water Service systems. Cal Water's statement that there is no operational disruption is consistent with what we have seen repeatedly in the water sector since the 2023 Aliquippa PA incident — adversaries gaining footholds in internet-exposed HMI or remote-access systems, often via default credentials on Unitronics PLCs or weakly protected VPN appliances, and using the access for symbolic defacement rather than process manipulation. What tends to happen mechanically: a Shodan-discovered HMI or VPN with credentials like 1111 or vendor defaults is logged into directly, and the attacker either changes setpoints visibly or leaves a banner. The lesson for water and wastewater engineers is that you do not need an APT-grade adversary to suffer an embarrassing and potentially dangerous incident; you need only an internet-reachable controller with a factory password. Three practical takeaways: first, run an external attack-surface scan against your own public IP ranges this week and assume any TCP/20256, 502, 102, 44818, or 4840 finding is a finding worth a Sev-1 ticket. Second, if you operate Unitronics, Siemens LOGO!, or any small PLC with a built-in web server, disable the web interface entirely or put it behind a properly configured remote-access broker with MFA. Third, ensure your operators know what a defacement looks like versus a real process upset — tabletop the scenario so the first response is not to panic-trip the plant.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a heavy slate of ICS advisories today, with the most consequential for plant floor engineers being Schneider Electric's Modicon Network Managed Switches (RADIUS protocol Blast-RADIUS-class vulnerability), Schneider EcoStruxure Panel Server (authentication bypass risk on a widely deployed gateway), and Siemens KACO blueplanet inverters where credentials can be derived from device serial numbers — a textbook hard-coded credentials failure that affects renewable energy assets connected to the grid. Several IoT/camera/robotics vendors (Brickcom, Yarbo, Naxclow) also have advisories, mostly relevant where these products bleed into facility security or perimeter monitoring. On the geopolitical front, California Water Service is investigating claims by Iranian-aligned hackers of intrusion into its environment. Cal Water states no operational impact to water or wastewater systems has been observed, but the claim alone underscores the continued targeting of US water utilities — a sector CISA has repeatedly flagged for weak segmentation between IT and OT. Engineers running similar small-to-mid utility SCADA environments should re-verify that HMI and historian access from corporate or remote-access networks is fully brokered and MFA-enforced. In IT-adjacent but operationally relevant news, Cisco patched a second Catalyst SD-WAN Manager zero-day (CVE-2026-20262) being actively exploited. Many industrial sites use Catalyst SD-WAN to interconnect remote plants and substations; the arbitrary file write primitive is a viable foothold into the management plane. The Arch Linux AUR supply chain attack (1,500 malicious packages, account registration suspended) is a useful reminder that engineering workstations running Linux-based EDS tooling can be poisoned through casual package installs. The White House issued NSPM-12 reorganising National Security Systems cybersecurity governance and reestablishing CNSS — relevant for defence-sector controls engineers and integrators working under federal contracts. Separately, a tech coalition called Athena launched a coordinated pre-disclosure vulnerability triage platform for open-source software, which over time should improve the quality of patches reaching OT vendors that embed OSS components.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog added Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-20262 (path traversal, arbitrary file write) on 15 June, following CVE-2026-20245 added on 9 June (command injection as root via crafted file) — both actively exploited and both touching equipment commonly used to glue plants and remote sites together. If your SD-WAN Manager is reachable from IT or vendor networks, treat this as a stop-the-line patch and review logs for unexpected file writes under the vManage application directories. Also noteworthy for OT-adjacent infrastructure: Check Point Security Gateway CVE-2026-50751 (IKEv1 authentication bypass enabling unauthorised VPN, ransomware confirmed) and Palo Alto PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257 (VPN auth bypass). These are classic remote-access pivots into ICS environments. Ivanti Sentry CVE-2026-10520 (unauthenticated root RCE) and Oracle PeopleSoft PeopleTools CVE-2026-35273 (unauthenticated takeover, ransomware confirmed) round out the high-impact business-system entries that often sit one firewall hop from the historian DMZ. For controls engineers the practical sequence is: patch or compensating-control the SD-WAN Manager and VPN gateways first, audit RADIUS configurations on Modicon switches in light of today's Schneider advisory, and re-verify that any KACO inverter fleet has had default-derived credentials rotated and remote access disabled where possible.Read more →
