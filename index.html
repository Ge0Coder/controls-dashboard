Today's Knowledge Bite

The new HTTP QUERY verb defined in RFC 10008 exists specifically to solve a problem controls engineers have felt for years: GET requests can't safely carry large or structured request bodies, while POST is not idempotent or cacheable. QUERY is both — meaning future OPC UA over HTTP, historian query APIs, and asset-inventory endpoints could finally send complex filter payloads without abusing POST or stuffing everything into URL parameters.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilots in the 1910s implemented proportional-integral-derivative action mechanically using flyweights, dashpots, and springs, and Nicholas Minorsky formalised the mathematics in 1922 by observing how helmsmen actually steered the USS New Mexico. The 'derivative' term literally came from watching experienced sailors anticipate wave motion. Every modern PLC loop you tune is, in a sense, still a mathematical portrait of a good helmsman from a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on AI-gateway middleware — tools like LiteLLM, LangChain servers, and MCP (Model Context Protocol) endpoints — that are quietly appearing inside industrial environments. Vendors are shipping operator-assistant features, alarm-summarisation tools, and natural-language historian queries that route through these gateways. The gateway sits between your on-prem data (historian, batch records, alarm logs) and a hosted or local LLM, brokering context and authentication. For controls engineers, this is essentially a new class of middleware — think of it as an OPC server for language models. The practical implication is that these gateways are becoming high-value targets, as the CVE-2026-59822 LiteLLM auth bypass this month demonstrates. An attacker who compromises an AI gateway inherits whatever historian, MES, and document-repository credentials it holds — potentially a broader footprint than a compromised HMI. Adoption challenges are real: most plants have no policy on what process data an LLM may access, no logging equivalent to alarm journals for prompts, and no way to prove to an auditor that a generative response didn't leak proprietary recipe or setpoint data. If your organisation is piloting these tools, treat the AI gateway as a Level 3 asset with the same segmentation, patching cadence, and access review as your historian. Insist on on-prem or private-cloud model hosting for anything touching production data, and log every prompt and response for the same retention period as your batch records.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often misapplied in real projects. The recurring failure mode is treating the zone-and-conduit exercise as a documentation deliverable rather than an engineering activity that constrains network design. When teams place a Citrix NetScaler or Fortinet firewall as their sole conduit between Levels 3 and 3.5, and then don't reassess when that appliance racks up KEV entries like this month's, the risk assessment has effectively expired without anyone noticing. ISA's TR100.00.01 work on wireless in industrial environments is worth tracking as more sites deploy private 5G and Wi-Fi 6E for mobile HMIs and AGVs. The technical report addresses coexistence, but the security implications — particularly around SIM-based device identity and slicing isolation — are still catching up to deployments. Expect updates over the next year as operators feed back real-world experience. On the NIST side, SP 800-82 Rev 3 remains the most practical reference for OT security program design, and its guidance on remote access aligns closely with what this week's Cisco, Citrix, and ConnectWise KEV entries demand: assume the perimeter appliance will be compromised, and require a second independent authentication and monitoring layer before any session reaches a controller.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's case study is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, which remains one of the clearest teaching examples for controls engineers. Attackers affiliated with CyberAv3ngers compromised a Unitronics Vision series PLC used for booster-station pressure control, defacing the HMI screen with a political message and taking the unit offline. The attack vector was embarrassingly simple: the PLC was internet-exposed on TCP 20256 with the vendor default password of 1111. What made the incident notable was not sophistication but scale — the same actors hit dozens of Unitronics units across US water and wastewater utilities within days, because attackers had simply scanned Shodan for the port banner. Operators fell back to manual control and no customers lost service, but the incident triggered a CISA advisory and an EPA enforcement push. Three lessons stick with controls engineers. First, if a controller is on the internet, assume it will be found within hours — Shodan and Censys index the entire IPv4 space daily. Second, vendor default credentials on safety- or availability-critical devices are an unforgivable finding in 2026; make credential rotation a commissioning gate, not an operational aspiration. Third, the ability to fall back to local manual control saved this utility — preserve that capability deliberately in your designs rather than letting it atrophy as systems modernise.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's headlines are IT-heavy but carry direct implications for the plant floor. The Brevo supply-chain incident, where a compromised API key allowed attackers to deploy a malicious Cloudflare Worker that injected scripts into roughly 100,000 websites, is a reminder that third-party SaaS integrations increasingly reach into operational workflows — anything from vendor documentation portals to remote-monitoring dashboards can become a delivery vector. Similarly, the exploited Orkes Conductor RCE (CVE-2026-58138) demonstrates how workflow-orchestration platforms — the kind now creeping into MES and data-historian integration stacks — are being weaponised via unauthenticated inline workflow definitions. Microsoft's September patch cycle addressed 18 vulnerabilities across Azure and its AI product line, most involving privilege escalation. For sites moving historian, batch, or analytics workloads into Azure IoT or Fabric, the message is to review your cloud-side identity boundaries as carefully as your on-prem AD tiers. Meanwhile, the takedown of NightmareStresser removes one of the longest-running DDoS-for-hire services, but the ecosystem regenerates quickly — plants relying on cellular or SD-WAN backhaul for remote sites should not treat this as durable relief. On the technical curiosity side, SANS ISC flagged the IETF's publication of RFC 10008 defining a new HTTP QUERY method — the first new verb since PATCH in 2010. Expect it to slowly appear in modern REST APIs, including those exposed by newer SCADA and historian products; it's worth being aware of when writing firewall rules or WAF signatures that currently only whitelist GET/POST/PUT/PATCH.
