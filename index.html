Today's Knowledge Bite

The KNX protocol, now on CISA's KEV list, dates back to 1990 and was designed in an era when 'security' meant a physical key to the electrical cabinet. Its default group communication model still broadcasts unauthenticated telegrams on the bus, which is why so many hotel room lighting systems can be commandeered from the lobby Wi-Fi if the IP router is misconfigured.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalised not in a research lab but on a US Navy ship. Nicholas Minorsky's 1922 paper described a three-term controller for automatic ship steering on the USS New Mexico, and he derived the derivative term by watching how experienced helmsmen anticipated the ship's yaw rather than reacting to it. Every derivative gain you tune today is, in effect, a mathematical model of a human sailor's intuition from a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is quietly moving from marketing slideware to real deployments in 2026, particularly in automotive body shops and semiconductor fabs. The premise is straightforward: combine OPC UA's semantic, secure information model with IEEE 802.1 TSN's deterministic Ethernet timing (802.1AS for time sync, 802.1Qbv for scheduled traffic, 802.1CB for frame replication) so that a single converged network can carry motion control, safety, and IT traffic without the traditional Profinet IRT or EtherCAT physical segregation. For controls engineers, the practical implication is that the historical wall between fieldbus and enterprise networking is dissolving. A TSN-capable switch can prioritise a 31.25 µs motion cycle on the same copper that carries your MES traffic. That is genuinely useful, but it also means a misconfigured VLAN or a compromised switch now has blast radius into the servo loop. Vendors including Siemens, B&R, Rockwell, and Beckhoff all ship TSN-capable hardware, but interoperability across vendors is still patchy — the OPC Foundation's FLC (Field Level Communications) initiative is the specification you want to reference in RFQs. Adoption barriers remain real: TSN switch pricing is still 2–3x standard managed switches, tooling for diagnosing scheduled traffic conflicts is immature, and most sites lack anyone who understands both PTP time distribution and OPC UA PubSub. Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to see increased adoption as the practical entry point for OT security programs, especially where insurers are now demanding evidence of zone and conduit documentation before renewal. If you have not yet formalised your zone/conduit diagrams with associated Security Level Targets (SL-T), that is now the most defensible investment of engineering time you can make — it directly supports both 62443-3-3 system requirements and 62443-4-2 component requirements downstream. On the NIST side, the ongoing revision of SP 800-82 (Guide to OT Security) continues to align more closely with the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function, pushing OT-specific governance requirements including named asset owners and documented risk tolerance for safety-impacting systems. For sites in regulated verticals, this matters because auditors are increasingly citing 800-82r3 as the reference standard even where it is not legally mandated. The KNX Association KEV entry (CVE-2023-4346) is a useful reminder that building automation protocols — KNX, BACnet, LonWorks — are not covered by IEC 62443 by default because they historically sat outside 'industrial' scope. If your facility team owns the BMS but your OT security program does not, that gap is now explicit in CISA guidance.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's incident is the ongoing Iran-linked campaign against US water utilities, now confirmed at facilities in New Jersey and Alabama. The technique is consistent with the CyberAv3ngers activity first observed against Aliquippa, Pennsylvania in late 2023: reconnaissance for internet-exposed Unitronics Vision-series PLCs, exploitation via default port 20256 and factory password '1111', and defacement of the operator screen. In several cases the attackers modified pressure or chemical dosing setpoints before being detected. The root cause is not sophisticated malware; it is exposure. Small water utilities operate with minimal IT staff, often rely on integrators who deploy PLCs with vendor defaults, and connect HMIs to cellular modems for remote support. The attackers scan, connect, and act — no zero-days required. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, any PLC or HMI reachable from a public IP must be considered compromised until proven otherwise; put it behind a VPN or a proper remote access broker today. Second, change default credentials on every device at commissioning and document it in the FAT/SAT checklist — this is a one-hour job that would have prevented most of these intrusions. Third, alarm on setpoint changes outside of maintenance windows; if a chlorine dose or pressure setpoint moves at 3am on a Sunday, your historian should scream, not silently trend.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline development for plant floor engineers today is the expansion of Iran-linked cyberattacks against US water utilities. New Jersey and Alabama have joined the growing list of at least a dozen states where threat actors have targeted industrial control systems at water and wastewater facilities. The pattern remains consistent with prior campaigns: opportunistic exploitation of internet-exposed HMIs, default credentials on Unitronics-class PLCs, and weakly segmented engineering workstations. If you operate any small or mid-size utility, treat this as an active campaign, not a theoretical risk, and pull your Shodan/Censys exposure report today. On the AI-in-OT front, two stories are worth watching. OpenAI's forthcoming Astra model is being flagged internally as potentially reaching the maximum 'critical' cybersecurity capability threshold, meaning autonomous exploit generation and chained attacks against real infrastructure become materially more plausible. Separately, researchers described a 'Ghostjacking' technique in which AI agents ingest attacker-controlled text from logs or alerts and execute it as instruction. For sites deploying LLM copilots against historians, alarm databases, or SIEMs, this is a direct prompt-injection risk against your operational data. Cisco disclosed high-severity ClamAV vulnerabilities with public PoC that enable unauthenticated remote DoS. ClamAV is embedded in a surprising number of OT-adjacent products, including some jump host antivirus configurations and email gateway appliances that sit upstream of engineering networks. Patch or filter accordingly. On the housekeeping side, Mozilla rotated its Firefox GPG signing key after an accidental repository leak. This matters for anyone relying on signature verification for browsers deployed on Level 3.5 workstations or engineering laptops used to reach vendor portals. Update your trust anchors before your endpoint tooling starts flagging valid updates as untrusted.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the last three weeks are dominated by internet-edge and management-plane products that OT networks routinely depend on. Progress LoadMaster (CVE-2026-8037) has an unauthenticated command injection; JetBrains TeamCity (CVE-2026-63077) allows unauthenticated RCE via agent polling; and N-able N-central has two chained auth bypasses (CVE-2026-18556 and the incomplete-patch follow-up CVE-2026-18577) that directly threaten any MSP-managed OT environment. Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (CVE-2026-20316) has a hard-coded password enabling remote low-priv login — if your perimeter or IDMZ firewalls are FMC-managed, treat this as urgent. Fortinet FortiSandbox (CVE-2026-25089, CVE-2026-39808) and FortiOS (CVE-2025-68686) round out a bad month for the OT perimeter stack. Of direct interest to controls engineers is CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX protocol's Connection Authorization Option 1, added on 15 July. The overly restrictive lockout mechanism can be abused to purge devices and lock the BCU key, effectively bricking KNX installations in commercial buildings, HVAC systems, and lighting control. If you own building automation with KNX/IP couplers, verify that additional security options (KNX Data Secure or IP Secure) are enabled and that IP routers are not reachable from corporate networks. Practical priority order this week: patch or isolate any FMC, FortiOS/FortiSandbox, LoadMaster, N-central, and TeamCity instances touching your OT environment; audit KNX/BACnet exposure at facility level; and confirm your SharePoint (CVE-2026-50522, CVE-2026-58644) instances used for MOC documentation and P&IDs are current.Read more →
