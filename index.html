Today's Knowledge Bite

The MQTT protocol, now ubiquitous in IIoT, was originally designed in 1999 by Andy Stanford-Clark at IBM and Arlen Nipper at Arcom to monitor an oil pipeline over satellite links where bandwidth was billed by the byte. Its publish/subscribe model and tiny fixed 2-byte header were optimised for exactly the kind of intermittent, low-power connectivity we still see on remote pump stations today — and that design frugality is why it ended up dominating modern sensor networks.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively reverse-engineered from ship steering. Nicholas Minorsky published the first rigorous mathematical analysis of three-term control in 1922 after watching helmsmen on the USS New Mexico, noting that a good helmsman did not just steer toward the heading error — he also anticipated how fast the error was changing and remembered how long it had been off course. That intuitive human behaviour became the derivative and integral terms, meaning every PID loop in your plant traces its lineage back to the muscle memory of 1920s US Navy sailors.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to be one of the more consequential shifts in industrial networking, and 2026 has seen the first wave of brownfield deployments actually hit production. The core idea is straightforward: OPC UA's PubSub extension gives you a vendor-neutral application layer, and TSN (IEEE 802.1 Qbv, Qbu, AS) gives you deterministic, bounded-latency Ethernet underneath. Together, they promise what EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, and Sercos III each delivered within their own ecosystems — hard real-time motion and I/O — but on standard Ethernet infrastructure, mixed with best-effort IT traffic on the same wire. The practical reality for controls engineers is still messier than the marketing. TSN-capable switches are now reasonably available from Hirschmann, Cisco, Siemens, and Moxa, but the configuration model (CNC/CUC, centralised vs distributed) is genuinely complex, and most sites lack the Ethernet engineering skills to commission a Qbv gate schedule. Device support on the controller side is spotty — you can find PLCs with OPC UA PubSub, and you can find PLCs with TSN endpoints, but the overlap list is short. Expect the next two to three years to be dominated by hybrid deployments: TSN in motion-critical cells, OPC UA PubSub over standard Ethernet elsewhere, and PROFINET or EtherCAT islands untouched because they already work.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) remains the standard most plants are now being asked about during insurance renewals and customer audits, and the drumbeat of ransomware incidents has made 'zone and conduit' diagrams a near-mandatory deliverable. If you have not yet produced one for your facility, the minimum viable version is a PDF showing your Purdue levels as zones, every cross-zone data flow as a conduit, and a short risk statement per conduit — auditors are generally satisfied with evidence of structured thinking, not perfection. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 continues to be the reference for OT security programs, and its explicit alignment with the broader Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function is reshaping how OT risk gets reported upward. Expect boards and executives to start asking OT teams for metrics in the CSF categories (GV.RM, ID.AM, PR.AC, etc.), which means your asset inventory and change-management records are going to get more scrutiny than your firewall rules. Worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 certification activity for PLC and RTU vendors keeps expanding, and procurement teams at the larger end-users are beginning to require Security Level 2 as a baseline for new controller purchases. If you are speccing a greenfield project, add an SL-C (capability) requirement to your RFQ — it costs nothing and filters the field usefully.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC at the municipal water booster station. The attackers did not use a zero-day — they simply scanned for internet-exposed Unitronics devices still using the factory default password of 1111 on port 20256, logged in, and defaced the HMI with a political message. Operators noticed immediately because the PLC was taken offline, and manual operation maintained water pressure with no public health impact. What made this incident instructive was not the sophistication but the breadth: CISA subsequently found dozens of similar Unitronics PLCs exposed across US water utilities, all with the same default credential. The attack vector was essentially 'Shodan plus a web browser.' The Aliquippa system had been commissioned by an integrator who never changed defaults and exposed the PLC directly for remote support convenience. Three lessons remain fresh for controls engineers: first, inventory every PLC's reachability from outside your plant network and assume any internet-exposed device will be found within hours — not days. Second, default credentials on controllers are not a 'low severity' finding; they are the single most reliable initial access vector in OT. Third, your manual-operation runbook is your real resilience control — Aliquippa's operators kept water flowing precisely because they had practised running the station without the PLC.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's advisory load leans heavily toward Siemens, with two bulletins covering the 'Node2Shell' Brotli decoder flaw (CVE-2026-111497) in Node.js that is bundled into numerous Siemens Industrial Software and Industrial Automation products. The out-of-bounds write can enable remote memory corruption and arbitrary code execution, and Siemens has released updated versions. A separate advisory flags multiple vulnerabilities in the Mendix Studio Pro MCP Server, where network-adjacent attackers can abuse the model-context endpoint to trigger unauthorized tool execution or data access — a notable early example of how LLM tooling is now entering the ICS vendor advisory stream. On the controller side, Autonomy Logic's OpenPLC Runtime (<=4.2.4) has a race-condition flaw (CVE-2026-126088) that permits creation of multiple unauthenticated admin accounts — relevant to the many labs, universities, and small deployments running OpenPLC on shop floors. Elecbrakes trailer brake controllers (EB2, VM1, ELBC2000) received a particularly alarming advisory: chained vulnerabilities could allow an attacker to take over trailer brakes, disable brake lights, or push malicious firmware. While not plant-floor equipment, it is a clean reminder of how commodity BLE/wireless controllers end up in safety-critical roles. Finally, a libumqtt integer underflow (CVE-2026-125132, CVSS 8.6) affects an MQTT client library that ships inside many embedded IIoT gateways. Outside of CISA, SecurityWeek reports active exploitation of a Cisco Expressway vulnerability shortly after PoC release, with attackers staging lateral-movement tooling on a Russian victim's clustered deployment — engineering teams running Expressway for remote-support VPN bridges should patch now. Apache Roller and Apache Axis are also under active attack, with Axis being used as a pivot into Confluence Data Center. PHP patched seven critical RCEs, and SANS ISC continues to flag a run of 'shell'-class vulnerabilities (php2shell, Java4Shell, NGINX2Shell) — individually serious but collectively not yet warranting Infocon Yellow. For plant-floor engineers, the practical signal is this: patch cycles for Siemens TIA Portal, Mendix, and related engineering workstations are going to be heavy this month, and any engineering laptop with Node.js-based tooling should be inventoried for Brotli exposure. If you run OpenPLC in production or pilots, upgrade immediately and audit admin accounts.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalogue has been unusually busy, with twenty additions in the last two weeks. Of direct OT relevance, Cisco BroadWorks (CVE-2026-20320) has an XXE flaw exposing configuration data — a concern for sites using BroadWorks for unified communications tied into SCADA alarm callouts. Check Point's Gaia OS DynamicCLI permissions flaw (CVE-2026-123039) affects Security Gateways widely deployed at IT/OT boundaries; verify your firewall vendor patch status against this one specifically. Apache Axis (CVE-2026-90436) is being actively exploited and lurks inside many legacy historian and MES integrations as a SOAP endpoint. For engineering workstations and jump hosts, the OpenSSH on Linux authentication-bypass flaw (CVE-2026-123158) and the Puppet Enterprise deserialization bug (CVE-2026-115731) deserve priority — Puppet is commonly used to manage OT support infrastructure, and a client with any CA-signed agent cert can achieve RCE on the Puppet Server. SonicWall SSLVPN (CVE-2026-19868) has a SAML bypass that affects remote vendor-access paths many plants rely on. Synology DSM/BeeStation (CVE-2024-10441) is relevant where small NAS units are used as local historians or backup targets. Practically: prioritise patching anything internet- or DMZ-facing first (SonicWall, Check Point, Cisco BroadWorks/Expressway), then engineering support infrastructure (Puppet, OpenSSH, Synology), then audit any Java middleware for Axis. CISA remediation dates for most of these cluster in early December — do not wait.Read more →
