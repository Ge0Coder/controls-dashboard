Today's Knowledge Bite

The very first documented industrial 'cyber' incident predates the internet: in 1982, a CIA-doctored SCADA software package reportedly caused a Siberian natural gas pipeline to explode with a force visible from orbit — roughly 3 kilotons. While historians still debate the full details, the Farewell Dossier episode is often cited as proof that supply-chain tampering of control logic was understood as a nation-state weapon decades before Stuxnet.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term has a surprisingly nautical origin: Elmer Sperry and Nicolas Minorsky developed the three-term control law in the 1910s and 1920s specifically to automate ship steering, with Minorsky's 1922 paper on the USS New Mexico deriving the equations by watching helmsmen compensate for steady crosswinds — the integral term literally models what an experienced sailor does when they notice the ship consistently drifting to one side. Nearly every temperature loop, flow loop, and servo drive on your plant floor is running an algorithm first tuned against ocean swells a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

Today's spotlight is on GNSS resilience in OT — timely given the Carlson VASCO-B advisory. Modern plants increasingly depend on GNSS for precision time (PTP/IEEE 1588 grandmasters often discipline from GPS), for positioning in mobile equipment, and for RTK-corrected automation in mining, agriculture, and construction. What many engineers don't appreciate is that a compromised or spoofed GNSS receiver doesn't just affect location — it can drift substation time sync, break synchrophasor measurements, or nudge autonomous haul trucks off course. The practical implication is that GNSS receivers should be treated as first-class OT assets: firmware-managed, network-segmented, and monitored. Spoofing attacks using sub-$500 SDR hardware have been publicly demonstrated against commercial receivers since 2013, and the threat has only cheapened. For critical timing applications, consider multi-constellation (GPS+Galileo+GLONASS) receivers with anti-spoofing (OSNMA on Galileo is now operational), holdover-capable oscillators (OCXO or rubidium) that can ride through hours of jamming, and cross-checks against PTP or NTP reference sources. If your time discipline comes from a single rooftop antenna with no holdover, you have a single point of failure that a $200 jammer in a passing vehicle can trigger.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) remains the part most frequently misapplied in the field. Engineers often default to treating the whole plant as one zone, when the standard explicitly calls for decomposing the SuC into zones and conduits based on criticality, functional grouping, and required Security Level (SL-T). With GNSS receivers, network cameras, and SD-WAN appliances all landing in recent advisories, it's worth revisiting whether those assets are actually bounded by a conduit with documented dataflows and countermeasures, or whether they're quietly bridging your levels. On the NIST side, the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function continues to reshape how asset owners document OT risk ownership — particularly around third-party/vendor remote access, which ties directly to this week's Abnormal AI findings on trusted-relationship abuse. If you haven't updated your vendor access procedures to reflect CSF 2.0's GV.SC (Supply Chain Risk Management) category, that's a useful Q2 action item. Also keep an eye on ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 Edition 2, which continues to move through balloting and tightens requirements around patch management programs for asset owners — a gap most sites still struggle with for embedded devices like cameras and GNSS gear.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's retrospective is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment near-miss, which remains a foundational teaching case five years on. An attacker accessed the HMI of a small water utility via TeamViewer (shared password, Windows 7, direct internet exposure) and briefly raised the sodium hydroxide setpoint from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm. An alert operator watching the screen noticed the cursor moving and reverted the change within minutes; downstream pH alarms would have caught it regardless, but the human-in-the-loop was the effective control. Three lessons still apply directly today. First, remote access tools installed 'temporarily' during COVID never got uninstalled at thousands of sites — audit your engineering workstations for TeamViewer, AnyDesk, ScreenConnect, and similar. Second, setpoint changes on safety-relevant variables should have engineering limits enforced in the PLC itself, not just the HMI; an HMI limit is a suggestion, a PLC rung is a control. Third, operator vigilance is a genuine layer of defence but should never be the only one — independent alarm limits on downstream quality variables (pH, conductivity, turbidity) are what would have actually prevented harm if the operator had stepped away for coffee. Re-read the CISA advisory AA21-042A this week if you haven't lately.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA issued a notable batch of ICS advisories today, with the standout being a CVSS 9.8 file manipulation vulnerability (CVE-2026-6074) in the Intrado 911 Emergency Gateway affecting versions 5.x through 7.x. While not a traditional plant-floor device, any site relying on E911 routing for life-safety should coordinate with telecom/IT to patch immediately. Equally relevant to field operations is a CVSS 9.4 flaw in Carlson Software's VASCO-B GNSS Receiver (<1.4.0) that allows remote attackers to alter critical system functions — a concern for survey, construction, and any automation leveraging RTK GNSS for positioning or time sync. On the video and perimeter side, CISA disclosed multiple RCE and crash vulnerabilities in Milesight cameras (MS-Cxx63/64 series ≤51.7.0.77-r12) and an authentication bypass in Hangzhou Xiongmai XM530 IP cameras. IP cameras remain one of the most commonly overlooked OT-adjacent devices on plant networks, often dual-homed and rarely patched. Segment them off your control VLAN and confirm firmware levels this week. On the broader security scene, Palo Alto Networks researchers demonstrated 'Zealot,' a multi-agent AI capable of autonomous cloud reconnaissance, exploitation, and exfiltration with minimal human oversight — a preview of what defenders will face as agentic attackers mature. Abnormal AI's 'Behavioral Shift' report highlights how adversaries are abandoning technical exploits in favour of weaponising trusted internal workflows, which has direct implications for engineers who routinely grant vendor remote access. Apple also patched an actively exploited Notification Services flaw (CVE-2026-28950) in iOS 26.4.2 and 18.7.8 — worth pushing to any iPhones/iPads used for HMI apps or mobile operator rounds.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV catalogue added another Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager cluster this week (CVE-2026-20122, -20133, -20128), covering privileged API abuse, sensitive information exposure, and recoverable password storage. SD-WAN Manager sits in many industrial WAN architectures linking remote sites, substations, and pipelines back to central SCADA — if you run Catalyst SD-WAN, patch now and rotate DCA credentials, as the recoverable-format flaw means any prior filesystem access likely means compromised secrets. The most pressing KEV entry today is CVE-2026-39987, a pre-auth RCE in Marimo (the Python notebook platform), which is increasingly used for data science and process analytics work adjacent to plant historians. Also notable are CVE-2026-34197 (Apache ActiveMQ code injection) and CVE-2026-21643 (Fortinet FortiClient EMS SQLi) — both broker/endpoint-management products frequently found in OT DMZs. Microsoft added SharePoint (CVE-2026-32201) and Defender privilege escalation (CVE-2026-33825) entries, alongside a surprising resurrection of CVE-2009-0238 (Excel RCE) and CVE-2012-1854 (VBA), reminders that legacy Office files remain a live initial-access vector in engineering workstations. For controls teams, prioritise in this order: Cisco SD-WAN Manager patching and credential rotation, Fortinet FortiClient EMS (often used to manage jumphosts into OT), and any engineering workstation still running old Office macros. Confirm your historian/analytics environment isn't exposing Marimo or ActiveMQ to untrusted networks.Read more →
