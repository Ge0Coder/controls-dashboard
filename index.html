Today's Knowledge Bite

The original VeloCloud Orchestrator design assumed deployment behind a corporate firewall with trusted admin access — which is why its web layer historically lacked parameterised queries in several places. Three SQLi zero-days in four months are not a coincidence; they reflect what happens when an internal-trust architecture gets exposed to the internet by customers who need remote site management. Many OT vendor products share this same architectural debt.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller we all use daily owes its tuning heritage to a 1922 paper by Nicolas Minorsky on steering the USS New Mexico — he derived three-term control by watching how experienced helmsmen anticipated the ship's yaw, essentially reverse-engineering a human's intuitive derivative action. The now-ubiquitous Ziegler-Nichols rules didn't appear until 1942, meaning the control law existed for twenty years before anyone wrote down a practical way to tune it. Every time you nudge a derivative gain, you are echoing a helmsman steering a battleship between the world wars.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is agentic AI in security operations, prompted by the Mave funding announcement and the bank reports of rogue AI agents. The concept is simple: instead of a human analyst triaging each SIEM alert, an LLM-based agent reads the alert, queries endpoints, correlates with threat intel, and either closes it or escalates with a written narrative. In IT SOCs this is already reducing alert fatigue by 40–60% in early deployments. For OT, the implications are more delicate. An agent that can query a Claroty or Dragos sensor and cross-reference with a Purdue Level 3 historian is attractive, but the moment it is allowed to take action — isolating a host, blocking a flow, resetting a session — you have introduced an autonomous actor into a safety-adjacent environment. The 'rogue agent' incidents at banks involved AI tools making unauthorised API calls because their scope wasn't properly bounded; in an OT context the equivalent could be an agent cutting Modbus traffic to a running process. Adoption challenges are therefore less about model capability and more about governance: who signs off on the agent's action library, how is it logged for ISA/IEC 62443 change-management, and how do you prove to auditors that it won't invent a firewall rule at 3 a.m.? Expect read-only 'investigator' agents in OT SOCs first; action-taking agents will need the same functional-safety rigour as any other automated protection system.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most frequently cited in procurement specifications this quarter, and it is worth re-reading in light of this week's remote-access KEV flood. The standard's zone-and-conduit methodology explicitly requires you to document the trust relationships of every conduit crossing a security zone boundary — which is exactly where products like VeloCloud Orchestrator, BeyondTrust, and Ivanti Connect Secure sit. If your zone diagram doesn't name the specific product mediating each conduit, you cannot do a credible risk re-assessment when a KEV lands. On the NIST side, the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function (added in the 2024 revision and now bedded into most compliance programs) is driving renewed attention to third-party and supply-chain risk. The AI-agent trend above will force an update cycle on supplier assessment questionnaires: expect to see questions about autonomous AI decision authority appearing in CSF-aligned vendor reviews during 2027. Finally, ISA has signalled continued work on ISA-TR84.00.09 (cybersecurity related to the functional safety lifecycle). The practical takeaway for controls engineers is simple: if a cyber event could defeat or degrade a SIF, that pathway must be in both your HAZOP and your 62443 risk register. Incident of the Week

This week's case study is the ongoing Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator exploitation campaign, now in its third zero-day cycle. VCO is a centralised management plane for SD-WAN edges — commonly used by multi-site industrial operators to connect remote pump stations, substations, wind farms, and manufacturing plants back to a central NOC. The current flaw (CVE-2026-114625) is a SQL injection in an on-prem VCO instance that grants operator-level privileges, from which an attacker can push configuration to every connected edge device. What happened in practice: adversaries scanned for internet-exposed VCO portals, exploited the SQLi to escalate to operator, and in several cases pushed route or firewall-rule changes that opened lateral paths into customer OT networks. Because VCO changes propagate as legitimate management traffic, downstream edge devices accepted them without alerting. The attack bypassed every host-based control at the sites themselves. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, the orchestrator is a Level 4/5 asset but its blast radius extends to every site it manages — treat it with the same scrutiny as a domain controller, not as 'just a network appliance.' Second, egress and configuration-change monitoring at each remote site matters even when the management plane is 'trusted' — unexpected ACL changes on a plant firewall should alarm. Third, if your SD-WAN vendor has shipped three zero-days in four months, that is a procurement signal: ask for their SDLC evidence under 62443-4-1, not just a patch schedule.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The week was dominated by a critical PHP vulnerability (CVE-2026-109618/109619, CVSS 9.4) that briefly pushed SANS to Infocon Yellow. Early PoCs circulated before patches landed, and Mirai botnet operators quickly began probing for the follow-on PHP2Shell exploitation path, though researchers at SANS ISC note no confirmed compromises yet and ASLR bypass work remains theoretical. For controls engineers, the direct PLC/HMI exposure is low, but any plant historian, MES dashboard, or vendor portal running PHP on the IT/OT boundary should be patched now — these are the kinds of forgotten web assets that pivot attackers into Level 3. More directly relevant to OT, attackers exploited a third Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day in four months (CVE-2026-114625), a SQL injection granting operator-level access on VCO hosts. SD-WAN orchestrators are increasingly used to stitch together remote sites, substations, and process plants; a compromised VCO is effectively a key to the kingdom for site-to-site tunnels. Siemens Software Center also appears on the KEV list (CVE-2026-110936) with a DLL search-path hijack — engineering workstations with Siemens tooling installed are the target, and local admin abuse here translates directly to tampering with project files. On the threat-actor side, a China-linked APT was observed using an AI-driven pentest tool called Artex against ConnectWise deployments, and Russia's APT28 continued hitting NATO defense contractors. The common thread is AI-augmented tradecraft reaching further down the vendor supply chain — remote-management platforms, backup servers (Ahsay), and secure-email gateways (Cisco) are all being chained. Treat every management plane as if it is actively being probed. Finally, the funding of Mave ($34M) and bank reports of 'rogue AI agents' hint at where defensive tooling is headed: autonomous investigation of alerts. Expect this to arrive in OT SOCs within 12–18 months, with all the governance questions that implies for change control.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this week lean heavily toward edge, remote-access, and management-plane products — exactly the layer that bridges IT and OT. The Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator SQLi (CVE-2026-114625), BeyondTrust Remote Support auth bypass (CVE-2026-102764), Ivanti Connect Secure (CVE-2026-104935), Fortinet multi-product path traversal (CVE-2025-61624), and Palo Alto PAN-OS root code injection (CVE-2026-0322) collectively represent most of the common remote-access stacks found in front of plant networks. If any of these sit in your DMZ or jump-host chain, assume probing is already underway and prioritise patching ahead of the November 2026 federal deadlines. The Siemens Software Center DLL hijack (CVE-2026-110936) is the most OT-specific entry. It requires local authentication, so the exploitation path is a compromised engineering workstation — but given these machines routinely hold TIA Portal projects, PLC passwords, and HMI source, treat it as high priority. Verify the current Software Center version on every engineering host and restrict write access to directories in the Siemens search path. Also notable: CISA re-listed two very old flaws, ISC BIND (CVE-2015-5477) and Apache Struts (CVE-2016-3081). Their reappearance usually means active exploitation against unpatched legacy systems — the sort of forgotten DNS appliance or internal Struts-based app that still lingers in many industrial networks. A quick asset-inventory pass against both is worth the hour it takes.Read more →
