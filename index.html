Today's Knowledge Bite

The Squid proxy, just revealed to harbour the Squidbleed memory-disclosure flaw, was first released in 1996 and is still embedded in numerous industrial DMZ appliances and update-relay servers as a 'set and forget' component. Many sites running OT data diodes or vendor-update relays do not even know Squid is inside the box because it ships as a black-box service within a vendor appliance — a reminder that SBOM visibility matters as much for proxies as for PLC firmware.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller in your DCS owes its mathematical form to Nicholas Minorsky's 1922 analysis of automatic ship steering for the USS New Mexico, not to any chemical or process application. Minorsky derived the three-term control law by observing how a skilled helmsman corrected the ship's heading — using the current error, the accumulated past error, and the rate at which the error was changing. Every temperature loop you tune today is, in effect, an emulation of a 1920s naval helmsman's intuition, formalised into mathematics decades before digital computers existed to execute it.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is quietly maturing into the standard that could finally unify controller-to-controller communication across vendor boundaries. Released by the OPC Foundation with strong Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, and Beckhoff backing, OPC UA FX extends classic OPC UA pub/sub down to the field level with deterministic timing over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking), targeting the cycle times traditionally owned by PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, and EtherNet/IP CIP Motion. For controls engineers, the practical promise is meaningful: a single, secure, semantically rich protocol from sensor to cloud, with built-in certificate-based authentication and signing. That eliminates the protocol-translation gateways that today sit between field networks and MES historians — devices that are themselves common attack pivots. Pilot deployments in automotive body-shop lines are already running OPC UA FX over TSN-capable switches for cross-vendor robot coordination. Adoption challenges remain substantial. TSN switch interoperability across vendors is still rough, certificate lifecycle management at the I/O block level is unsolved at scale, and most existing brownfield I/O simply cannot speak UA FX without replacement. Expect UA FX to land first in greenfield discrete manufacturing and slowly creep into process industries over the next decade.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component requirements) continue to be the most-cited standards in procurement specifications, and the recent push from the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) is effectively forcing vendors of 'products with digital elements' to demonstrate 62443-4-2 conformance whether they want to or not. Plant engineers writing new control-system specifications should be explicit about which Security Level (SL-T) is required per zone — vendors will otherwise deliver SL-1 and call it done. NIST released the third public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 4 earlier this year, with expanded guidance on zero-trust architectures applied to OT and clearer mapping to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function. The draft is notable for explicitly acknowledging that classical zero-trust assumptions (continuous re-authentication, dynamic policy) break down at the deterministic control layer and offers compensating-control language engineers can cite when pushing back on overzealous IT mandates. With the post-quantum executive order signed yesterday, expect ISA and IEC working groups to accelerate guidance on crypto-agility in 62443 — particularly around OPC UA's security profile catalog and how to migrate field device certificates without an unplanned outage. Crypto-agility in firmware is going to be a procurement requirement, not a nice-to-have.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's reflection is on the 2024 American Water Works cyber incident, which has now been thoroughly post-mortemed in public disclosures. The largest regulated US water and wastewater utility detected unauthorized activity on internal systems in October 2024 and proactively disconnected its customer portal and billing systems. While the company maintained that water and wastewater operations were unaffected, the incident triggered SEC disclosure and forced a manual-billing fallback for weeks. The root cause aligned with a familiar pattern: initial access via compromised credentials on an internet-facing application, lateral movement through flat corporate identity infrastructure, and a defensive decision to isolate OT by pulling the cord on the IT/OT interconnect — which worked, but only because someone had drilled that disconnect procedure. Three lessons stand out. First, the ability to run plant operations in 'island mode' without corporate IT services (AD, DNS, historian replication, email-based work orders) is a survivability capability worth rehearsing quarterly. Second, billing and customer-facing systems are attractive ransomware targets even when OT is well-segmented; do not let executives assume 'we're fine, the plant is up' equates to 'we're fine'. Third, the decision authority to physically disconnect IT/OT must be pre-delegated to the on-shift operations supervisor — not escalated up a CISO chain at 3 AM.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The dominant story for plant-floor engineers is the FortiBleed campaign attributed to a Russian initial access broker. Using a custom sniffer, the actor has reportedly harvested over 110 million Fortinet credentials since February 2026. Fortinet appliances are ubiquitous at the IT/OT boundary, frequently terminating remote-engineering VPNs into Purdue Level 3 and 3.5 zones, so any site relying on FortiGate, FortiClient, or FortiManager for vendor or integrator access should assume credentials may be compromised and rotate accordingly. In the utility sector, London Hydro in Ontario disclosed a breach affecting customer PII. While the disclosure points to billing-side data rather than SCADA or AMI control systems, it is another reminder that distribution utilities remain a high-value target and that segmentation between enterprise CIS/billing platforms and the DMS/OMS must be verifiable, not assumed. On the policy front, President Trump signed an executive order accelerating post-quantum cryptography migration, requiring federal high-value and high-impact systems to transition by end of 2030 and 2031 respectively. Although aimed at federal IT, the downstream impact on industrial vendors — particularly those supplying TLS-based historians, OPC UA stacks, and remote access gateways into federal facilities and critical infrastructure — will be significant. Expect RFPs to start citing NIST PQC algorithms (ML-KEM, ML-DSA) within 12–18 months. SANS ISC highlighted CVE-2024-40766 in SonicWall devices, noting that even where the patch was applied, the underlying misconfiguration (local user passwords carried over from older firmware) was never remediated, leaving devices exploitable. This is a textbook OT lesson: patching alone does not close a vulnerability if configuration hygiene is ignored. Also notable: a Heartbleed-style flaw dubbed Squidbleed in the Squid proxy, which is sometimes used as a forward proxy from OT DMZs for vendor update fetching.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

Several of this month's KEV additions touch OT environments directly or sit on the IT/OT boundary. CVE-2026-50751 in Check Point Security Gateway allows an unauthenticated attacker to bypass IKEv1 authentication and establish a remote access VPN — a direct threat for any plant using Check Point at the corporate-to-DMZ edge. CVE-2026-0257 in Palo Alto PAN-OS similarly permits unauthorized VPN connections through an auth bypass. Both are confirmed exploited and Check Point's is associated with ransomware activity; treat as emergency change windows. Two Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager flaws (CVE-2026-20262 path traversal and CVE-2026-20245 command injection as root) are relevant for multi-site manufacturers using SD-WAN to backhaul plant traffic. CVE-2026-10520 in Ivanti Sentry gives unauthenticated root RCE — Sentry often fronts mobile device access to corporate systems but can be a pivot into shared identity infrastructure used by OT. CVE-2026-35273 in Oracle PeopleSoft (known ransomware use) and CVE-2024-21182 in WebLogic round out the enterprise-side exposures often integrated with MES and ERP. For controls engineers, the priority sequence this week: verify your perimeter VPN concentrators (Check Point, Palo Alto, Fortinet) are patched and credentials rotated; confirm any SD-WAN orchestrators reachable from corporate are isolated from process networks; and audit jump-host accounts used by integrators against the FortiBleed credential dump indicators once they become available.Read more →
