Today's Knowledge Bite

The Siemens Ruggedcom ROX advisories include CVEs originally assigned in 2019. Industrial routers often run customized BusyBox/Linux stacks where embedded library vulnerabilities take years to surface in vendor firmware because the underlying components are forked and frozen at design time. It's a reminder that an SBOM for your ICS gear is no longer optional — without it, a seven-year-old upstream CVE can quietly live on your DMZ router.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term was first formalized not in a chemical plant but on US Navy ships. In 1922, Nicolas Minorsky published a mathematical analysis of automatic ship steering after observing helmsmen on the USS New Mexico — he realized skilled helmsmen mentally integrated past heading error and anticipated future error, which he translated into the proportional-integral-derivative structure we still use today. So every PID loop tuning you do traces back to watching sailors steer a battleship a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is the extension of OPC UA down into controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication, designed to displace proprietary fieldbus protocols at the cell and field level. Where classic OPC UA was a north-south protocol between SCADA and PLCs, OPC UA FX standardizes east-west exchanges — PLC-to-PLC, drive-to-PLC, even sensor-to-controller — using the same information modeling and security primitives (certificates, signing, encryption) as the rest of the OPC UA stack. Combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) on standard Ethernet, it promises deterministic, vendor-neutral motion and safety-grade communication without a separate Profinet, EtherCAT, or Ethernet/IP infrastructure. Adoption is real but uneven. Siemens, Beckhoff, Rockwell, B&R, and Schneider have all demoed interoperable FX exchanges, and the first products with FX-capable firmware are shipping. The practical hurdles are familiar: TSN switches that actually implement the right IEEE 802.1 profiles cost more than commodity managed switches; certificate lifecycle management on field devices is still painful; and most engineering toolchains still favor their native fieldbus when it comes to diagnostics and motion tuning. For greenfield projects in the next 18-24 months, it's worth specifying FX-capable controllers even if you don't enable FX day one — the optionality is cheap insurance against vendor lock-in.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component-level technical requirements) continue to be the two parts most actively referenced in procurement specifications this year. A growing number of European asset owners are now requiring vendors to provide 62443-4-2 certification at a specific Security Level (typically SL 2) as a gating item for tender responses, particularly in water, energy, and pharma. If you're writing PLC or RTU specifications, citing the specific component requirements (CR 1.1 identification, CR 2.1 authorization enforcement, CR 3.4 software integrity, etc.) rather than just 'IEC 62443 compliant' produces far better vendor responses. On the framework side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the most pragmatic OT-specific overlay on the broader Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, and CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are increasingly used as a baseline checklist by regulators in critical infrastructure sectors. The CPGs are not a substitute for 62443 but they translate well into a board-level scorecard. Worth tracking: ISA's ongoing work on ISA/IEC 62443-2-2 (security program ratings) which aims to give asset owners a measurable maturity score rather than a binary certified/uncertified outcome.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's case study is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment intrusion — still one of the clearest cautionary tales for small utilities. An operator at the plant noticed his mouse cursor moving on its own through the HMI, watching an attacker push the sodium hydroxide setpoint from around 100 ppm to over 11,000 ppm before reverting it. The access vector was TeamViewer running on a Windows 7 HMI workstation, with a shared password reportedly known to multiple former staff, and the workstation was directly reachable from the internet. What saved the plant was an alert human-in-the-loop, not technical controls. Engineering process limits in the chemical dosing system would have caught the change downstream, but only after some dwell time. Three lessons remain sharply relevant: first, remote access tools installed for convenience during COVID-era operations frequently outlive their justification — inventory and remove them. Second, engineered safety limits in PLC logic (clamping setpoints to physically reasonable ranges) are a cheap, vendor-neutral defense that survives credential compromise. Third, shared passwords and end-of-life operating systems on HMIs are not 'legacy debt,' they are active liabilities; budgeting for HMI refresh cycles belongs in the capital plan, not the wish list.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's bulletin is dominated by a large batch of Siemens ICS advisories. Multiple Ruggedcom ROX issues stand out: an authenticated command injection in the Scheduler that yields root, an improper access control flaw allowing arbitrary root-level file reads, and a stack of third-party CVEs (some dating back to 2019) resolved in v2.17.1. SIMATIC HMI Unified Comfort Panels before V21.0 have a help-link flaw allowing an unauthenticated attacker to reach the underlying web browser via the Control Panel, which is a classic kiosk-escape risk on plant-floor HMIs. Siemens Teamcenter and a broad denial-of-service advisory across multiple Siemens industrial devices round out the set. If you run Ruggedcom or Unified Comfort Panels, plan a patch window now and verify Control Panel lockdown on every HMI. SecurityWeek reports a critical vulnerability (CVE-2026-8153) in Universal Robots PolyScope 5 that enables OS command injection across robot fleets. Anything reachable from engineering networks or shared with IT for fleet management is in scope; segmenting cobot teach pendants and disabling unnecessary network services is the immediate mitigation while UR pushes firmware. On the IT-adjacent side, the TeamPCP supply-chain campaign tracked by SANS ISC produced a confirmed Checkmarx Jenkins plugin compromise and a self-spreading 'Mini Shai-Hulud' worm propagating through npm and PyPI. Engineering teams that use Python/Node tooling for PLC code generation, historian ETL, or MES integrations should pin dependencies, verify package signatures, and audit any CI runners that touch OT artifacts. Also of note: a PoC for the DirtyDecrypt Linux kernel local-privilege-escalation bug (patched in April) is now public — relevant for Linux-based gateways, edge compute nodes, and historian hosts. Finally, INTERPOL's Operation Ramz produced 201 arrests across MENA, and several large US healthcare data breaches were disclosed. Healthcare OT (medical devices, building systems) often shares networks with breached IT, so expect downstream phishing and lateral-movement attempts targeting hospital engineering staff.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added two items this week that matter most to OT teams. CVE-2026-20182 is an authentication bypass in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Controller and Manager giving unauthenticated remote attackers admin privileges — and SD-WAN is increasingly the backbone for site-to-site OT connectivity and remote engineering access. Treat any internet-exposed vManage as compromised until patched, rotate credentials and certificates, and review configuration change logs for unauthorized template edits. CVE-2026-0300, an unauthenticated root-level RCE in PAN-OS User-ID Captive Portal on PA-Series and VM appliances, is the other one to action immediately if your DMZ or IT/OT firewall uses that feature; disable Captive Portal where not strictly required. Also relevant from the last 30 days: Ivanti EPMM authenticated RCE (CVE-2026-6973) for fleets that include ruggedized tablets and field devices; Linux kernel privilege escalation (CVE-2026-31431) affecting edge gateways and historians; and ConnectWise ScreenConnect plus SimpleHelp path-traversal flaws (both flagged as actively used in ransomware) — these remote-support tools are common backdoors into OT through vendor laptops and integrator support sessions. Inventory every remote-access tool that touches your plant network and confirm patch status today.Read more →
