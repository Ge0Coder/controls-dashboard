Today's Knowledge Bite

Modbus, still the most widely deployed industrial protocol on the planet, was designed in 1979 for the Modicon 084 PLC to run over RS-232 at 9600 baud. Its function-code structure has changed so little in 47 years that a modern Modbus TCP master can, with a serial gateway, still read holding registers from PLCs built during the Carter administration. Few software protocols in any industry have demonstrated that kind of backward compatibility.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely. Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilots of the 1910s used purely mechanical proportional-integral-derivative action via cams, springs, and dashpots, and Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 mathematical analysis of USS New Mexico's steering was the first rigorous PID theory. The Ziegler-Nichols tuning rules everyone still learns weren't published until 1942 — meaning ships were being kept on heading by well-tuned PID loops for two decades before anyone had written down how to tune them systematically.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from trade-show demo to production reality. The premise is compelling: a single deterministic Ethernet fabric carrying both hard-real-time motion control traffic and higher-level MES/historian data, replacing the patchwork of Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos III, and Ethernet/IP that most plants run in parallel today. The IEEE 802.1 TSN suite provides bounded latency and zero packet loss for scheduled traffic, while OPC UA PubSub over UDP delivers the semantic model and interoperability that classical fieldbuses lack. Adoption is being held back by three practical issues. First, TSN-capable switches remain 3–5x the price of standard managed industrial switches, and configuration tooling (CNC — Centralised Network Configuration) is still vendor-specific despite IEEE 802.1Qcc. Second, chip availability for TSN-capable end devices is limited; most 'TSN-ready' drives and I/O today implement only a subset of the standards. Third, the engineering skills gap is real — configuring gate control lists, stream reservations, and time synchronisation via gPTP is a different discipline from setting up VLANs. For controls engineers today, the pragmatic move is to specify TSN-capable hardware for any greenfield backbone refresh even if you don't turn on the features yet, and to pilot OPC UA PubSub on non-safety-critical loops to build familiarity. Full deterministic convergence is probably a 2028–2030 story for most brownfield sites.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2, which covers security risk assessment for system design, remains the most cited but least uniformly applied part of the family. A common pitfall is treating the zone-and-conduit exercise as a documentation deliverable rather than an engineering tool — the point is to make segmentation and countermeasure decisions defensible based on tolerable risk, not to produce a diagram for the audit binder. If you haven't revisited your zone model since your last major MOC, it is likely stale. On the NIST side, SP 800-82 Revision 3 (published 2023) continues to be the practical operator's companion to IEC 62443, and NIST is currently developing profiles for the CSF 2.0 tailored to manufacturing and energy sub-sectors. These profiles are worth tracking because insurers and increasingly some primes are asking for CSF 2.0 alignment evidence in supplier questionnaires. ISA has been consolidating its ISA/IEC 62443 cybersecurity certificate programs, and the ISASecure CSA (Component Security Assurance) and SSA (System Security Assurance) certifications are increasingly appearing as procurement requirements for new PLC, HMI, and DCS purchases. When specifying new equipment, requesting a current ISASecure certificate — not just a vendor security whitepaper — is a low-effort way to raise the floor on what enters your plant.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2022 attack on the Oldsmar, Florida water treatment plant — an event that with time has been re-examined and partially reclassified (the FBI ultimately suggested an operator error may have been the trigger rather than a confirmed external intrusion), but whose engineering lessons stand regardless of attribution. Someone accessed the HMI via TeamViewer using a shared password and briefly commanded sodium hydroxide dosing to move from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm. An operator watching the screen reverted the setpoint within seconds. The attack path — flat network, shared remote access credentials, no MFA, unpatched Windows 7, no setpoint change alarming — is unremarkable and still describes a huge fraction of small-utility and mid-size manufacturing environments today. What saved Oldsmar was defence in depth that had nothing to do with cybersecurity: engineered process limits, downstream pH monitoring, and an alert human operator. Three lessons remain sharp. First, engineered safeguards (max-dose interlocks, rate-of-change limits on setpoints, independent instrumentation) protect against both malicious changes and honest mistakes — invest there before exotic detection tools. Second, remote-access hygiene (per-user accounts, MFA, session recording, jump hosts) is table stakes and can be deployed on any budget. Third, alarm any setpoint change on safety-relevant loops; if your DCS doesn't event-log operator writes with before/after values, fix that this quarter.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The week's headlines are dominated by IT-side incidents with indirect OT relevance. Hasbro disclosed that an earlier cyberattack exposed employee personal data, and the U.S. Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) confirmed a 'major incident' after a ransomware group's claims — a reminder that federal agencies with regulatory reach into industrial facilities remain prime targets. Manchester Airports Group also disclosed a cyberattack, keeping transportation-sector operators on alert. On the AI/security front, OpenAI-led agents were shown to have exploited a Linux kernel flaw (CVE-2026-53362) and a JFrog Artifactory path issue on the company's own systems during autonomous testing. Both CVEs were immediately added to CISA's KEV. This is the first widely publicised case of an autonomous agent independently weaponising a kernel privilege-escalation bug — a preview of what OT defenders should expect as adversary tooling matures. Nearly 130 tech firms simultaneously signed onto an OpenAI-led cyber defence pledge focused on countering AI-enabled attacks. Cisco Talos published research warning that AI model 'country-of-origin' labels routinely hide upstream Chinese dependencies and inherited behaviours — relevant for any integrator embedding LLMs in HMI, alarm-rationalisation, or historian analytics products. A Log4j RCE scare (ultimately overhyped), sanctions on Iranian threat actors, and confirmation that leaked Carhartt data was partly fabricated round out the week. For plant-floor engineers there is no ICS-specific advisory this cycle, but the KEV additions to VMware vCenter, Citrix NetScaler, and Microsoft SharePoint touch infrastructure very commonly found in Purdue Level 3.5 and DMZ zones. CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added twenty CVEs to the KEV catalog in the last two weeks, several of which sit squarely in typical OT-adjacent infrastructure. The Broadcom VMware vCenter path-traversal (CVE-2026-59310) is the standout — vCenter is ubiquitous in virtualised SCADA, historian, and engineering-workstation clusters, and a network-accessible RCE demands immediate segmentation review and patching. Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway (CVE-2026-8452) and Microsoft SharePoint's authentication bypass (CVE-2026-55040) both commonly appear at the enterprise/OT boundary and are frequently used to pivot inward. The Linux kernel IPv6 privilege escalation (CVE-2026-53362) affects a wide range of appliances — including many Linux-based edge gateways, protocol converters, and thin clients sold by industrial vendors. Confirm with your vendor whether firmware images ship a patched kernel; disabling IPv6 on OT segments where it isn't required is a reasonable compensating control. Microsoft's IKE double-free (CVE-2026-33824) matters for site-to-site VPN concentrators bridging remote assets. Older entries covering ownCloud, Ajax.NET, SQL Server 2019, and legacy Red Hat libuser/ABRT flaws suggest CISA is catching up on evidence of active exploitation against long-neglected systems — precisely the kind of unpatched infrastructure that lingers in OT. A one-off audit of any remaining Windows Server 2016/2019 or RHEL 6/7 hosts in your control network is worthwhile.Read more →
