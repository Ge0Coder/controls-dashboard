Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, still the lingua franca of industrial devices in 2026, was published by Modicon in 1979 — the same year Sony released the Walkman. It was designed for serial links running at 9600 baud, yet its register model is now routinely tunneled over TCP, TLS, and even MQTT-Sparkplug bridges. Almost 50 years on, its function code table has barely changed.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronic computing by decades — Elmer Sperry's ship-steering autopilots in the 1910s already used proportional and derivative action, and Nicholas Minorsky formalized the math in a 1922 paper analyzing the steering of USS New Mexico. The 'integral' term was originally called 'reset' because pneumatic controllers physically reset a bellows to eliminate offset. That's why old-timers still say 'reset' when they mean 'I' gain, and why tuning parameters on some legacy DCS platforms are labeled P, I/R, and D to this day.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to inch from demo booth to production floor. The premise is simple: replace proprietary deterministic fieldbuses like PROFINET IRT and EtherCAT with a single, standards-based Ethernet layer that carries both deterministic control traffic and best-effort IT traffic on the same wire. TSN provides the sub-microsecond timing guarantees via IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic and 802.1AS time sync, while OPC UA Pub/Sub sits on top to carry the actual process data with a rich, semantic information model. For controls engineers, the practical implication is convergence: one cable plant, one managed switch infrastructure, one address scheme for both PLC-to-PLC motion synchronization and MES data collection. That sounds great until you have to actually configure it. The current adoption bottleneck is tooling — CNC-configuration workflows for TSN schedules are still vendor-specific, and getting a Beckhoff, Siemens, and Rockwell device to share a TSN domain interoperably remains a lab exercise more often than a factory reality. Most greenfield deployments in 2026 are hybrid: TSN for the backbone, legacy fieldbus surviving on individual machines. Watch the automotive and semiconductor sectors — they are pushing hardest because their line rebalancing cycles reward the flexibility. If your next capex includes a machine refresh, ask the OEM specifically which TSN profile (IEC/IEEE 60802) they support, and whether they can demonstrate a scheduled cyclic exchange with a device from a competing vendor.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) continue to be the two parts most engineers actually encounter in tenders and factory acceptance tests. The recent trend from major EPCs is to demand 62443-4-2 SL-2 certification on any new PLC, RTU, or protocol gateway procurement, which is squeezing smaller vendors who historically relied on network segmentation as their entire security story. If you specify equipment, ask for the ISASecure or TÜV certificate number, not just a marketing claim of compliance. On the NIST side, the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 profile work for manufacturing is maturing, and the newer NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the most practical single reference document for OT security controls — particularly its mappings between 800-53 controls and typical ICS constraints like patching windows and legacy protocol support. If you are building a site security program from scratch, 800-82 plus 62443-2-1 (the asset owner requirements) is a defensible baseline. Worth watching: the EU Cyber Resilience Act's transition period is now approaching enforcement, and it will require CE-marked products with digital elements — including virtually every modern PLC, HMI, and drive — to demonstrate secure-by-default configuration, vulnerability handling, and SBOM availability. Vendors are quietly scrambling; asset owners should start asking for SBOMs in RFQs now.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful recent case to reflect on is the 2024 Arkansas City, Kansas water treatment cyberattack, which forced the plant into manual operation for several days. The utility detected anomalous activity on its network, isolated the SCADA system as a precaution, and ran the plant with operators making all control decisions locally — chlorine dosing, pump sequencing, and tank levels — until the environment could be cleaned and validated. Water quality was reportedly never compromised, which is the good news. What made this incident instructive was not the attack sophistication but the response. The utility had documented manual operating procedures, staff who had actually practiced them, and a clean-network rebuild plan that did not depend on restoring from potentially tainted backups. Many sites talk about 'islanding' or 'manual fallback' as a resilience strategy; far fewer actually maintain the runbooks, the trained operators, or the physical local controls to make it real. Three lessons stand out for controls engineers. First, when did your team last physically operate the plant without SCADA — not in theory, but with a stopwatch? Second, are your local HMI panels and hardwired controls still functional, or have they been quietly decommissioned during upgrades? Third, does your incident response plan distinguish between 'restore SCADA' and 'safely run the process' — because in the first hours of an incident, those are very different objectives.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's news cycle is light on direct ICS product advisories but heavy on infrastructure-adjacent risk. CISA has added the Gitea remote code execution flaw (CVE-2026-60004) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after active exploitation in the wild. Gitea is commonly used by automation teams to host PLC logic, HMI projects, and engineering workstation configurations under version control, so a compromise of a self-hosted Gitea instance can hand attackers direct access to the crown jewels of a controls environment. If your site runs an internal Gitea for engineering artifacts, patch to 1.27.1 or later immediately and audit repository write-access accounts. On the healthcare front, Nutex Health disclosed unauthorized access and data exfiltration to the SEC. While not an OT event, healthcare providers continue to be prime targets, and hospital biomedical and BMS teams should treat these disclosures as reminders to re-verify segmentation between clinical IT and building automation networks. Separately, WordPress sites running the MiniOrange SAML SSO plugin are under attack via two authentication bypass flaws — worth checking if any of your site's public-facing engineering portals rely on that plugin. On the broader technology front, the Linux Foundation has taken governance of TRACE, a new open standard for AI runtime attestation contributed by AMD, Intel, Microsoft, OPAQUE, and TII. This is an early but important development for anyone considering AI-assisted analytics or anomaly detection in OT, since attestation of model integrity at runtime is going to become a compliance expectation. WhatsApp added multi-passkey support and stronger two-step verification — mundane on its face, but relevant because so many field service technicians still use WhatsApp for photos of nameplates, wiring, and setpoints. SANS ISC continues to run useful operational content this week, including a piece on obfuscating IP addresses as hostnames in SSRF attacks (relevant for anyone exposing historian or MES APIs) and a Powershell/Graph script for hunting down accounts missed in MFA rollouts. If you have a mixed IT/OT Entra tenant, that script is worth borrowing.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The standout KEV addition this week is Gitea CVE-2026-60004, exploited to plant malicious Git hooks that execute as the Gitea service account. Controls teams using Gitea as an engineering-artifact repository should assume adversaries can weaponize any write-access token or compromised developer credential to gain code execution on the server — which typically has broad network reach into engineering VLANs. Patch to 1.27.1+, rotate service account credentials, and review hook directories for unexpected scripts. Several other recent KEV entries have direct OT relevance. Broadcom VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-59310) path traversal is critical because most sites still run their OT virtualization stack on vSphere; a compromised vCenter is game-over for historian, HMI, and engineering VMs. Cisco ASA/FTD (CVE-2026-20349) can be triggered unauthenticated to reload the device — a denial-of-service against your IT/OT firewall boundary. Microsoft IKE (CVE-2026-33824) and SharePoint (CVE-2026-55040) round out the Windows-side risks; SharePoint in particular is often the unofficial document store for P&IDs and SOPs. Progress LoadMaster (CVE-2026-8037) and JetBrains TeamCity (CVE-2026-63077) are also worth flagging if your build pipeline touches OT firmware or configuration artifacts.Read more →
