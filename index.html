Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Purdue Enterprise Reference Architecture that underpins nearly every OT network diagram wasn't designed for security at all — Theodore Williams and the Purdue Consortium published it in 1990 as a computer-integrated manufacturing (CIM) reference to organize data flow between business and manufacturing systems. The security-zone interpretation we all use today was retrofitted by ISA-99 (now IEC 62443) more than a decade later. That's why the model struggles with modern realities like cloud historians and edge analytics: it was never a security model to begin with.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely — the mathematical structure was worked out by Nicolas Minorsky in 1922 while he was designing automatic steering systems for US Navy battleships, by observing how experienced helmsmen actually behaved at the wheel. He noticed they didn't just react to current heading error (P), but also to how long the ship had been off course (I) and how fast it was swinging back (D). The three-term controller you tune every week was reverse-engineered from human intuition, not derived from first principles, and it took the process industry another twenty years to realize the same math worked for temperature and flow.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet using OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow crawl from spec sheets to real deployments. The promise is compelling: a single converged Ethernet fabric carrying motion-control-grade cyclic traffic (sub-millisecond, bounded jitter) alongside historian data, video, and MQTT — all with standardized publisher/subscriber semantics via OPC UA PubSub. In practice this means one cable, one switch family, and one protocol stack replacing the current mix of EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, Ethernet/IP CIP Motion and standard TCP traffic on separate VLANs. Adoption in 2026 is still concentrated in greenfield semiconductor and automotive body-shop projects. The barriers are less technical than ecosystem: TSN requires synchronized 802.1AS clocks across every switch, careful stream reservation via 802.1Qcc, and PLC firmware that actually supports OPC UA PubSub with the FLC (Field Level Communication) profiles. Most brownfield sites will only see TSN arrive incrementally, typically as a machine-level fabric behind a gateway. For controls engineers the practical implication is worth watching: when your next big drive or robot vendor quotes a system, ask specifically for the TSN and OPC UA FLC roadmap. If the answer is vague, you're buying a fieldbus island that will need a gateway for the next twenty years. If it's concrete, you may be looking at genuinely vendor-neutral motion control for the first time in the industry's history.Read more →

Standards Watch

The big standards event this week is NIST SP 800-82 Revision 4 entering public comment through 30 November. Rev 3 (2023) already broadened scope from ICS specifically to OT generally and pulled in IoT and IIoT considerations; Rev 4 is expected to deepen alignment with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (with its new Govern function) and to reflect three years of ransomware lessons in critical manufacturing and water. If your compliance program cites 800-82 anywhere, plan a review cycle now — auditors will expect Rev 4 awareness within a year of publication. The parallel CISA/FBI advisory on ICS integrators is effectively a governance nudge toward IEC 62443-2-4 (security program requirements for service providers) and 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle for suppliers). These are the two parts of 62443 that most asset owners still don't contractually require, despite the fact that they are the most useful for holding integrators accountable. If you are writing an RFQ this quarter, cite them explicitly and require evidence of certification or documented conformance. Worth noting in passing: ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) remains the most under-utilized part of the family. Zone-and-conduit models built purely from network diagrams miss consequence-based scoping. If you haven't done a proper 3-2 style assessment on your process, you don't really know what your target security level should be.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the CyberAv3ngers group (linked to Iran's IRGC) compromised a Unitronics Vision series PLC controlling a booster station on the municipal water system. The attack was trivial: the PLC was internet-exposed on TCP/20256 with the factory default password '1111'. The attackers defaced the HMI screen with an anti-Israel message and forced the station offline; operators reverted to manual control and no water quality impact occurred. What made this incident matter was scale. CISA and the FBI subsequently found dozens of similarly exposed Unitronics devices across US water and wastewater utilities — and later industrial sectors globally. The vector wasn't a sophisticated exploit; it was that small utilities and OEMs had put PLCs directly on the public internet for remote access convenience, using vendor defaults. Three lessons remain live for controls engineers today. First, Shodan yourself: know exactly what of yours is reachable from the public internet, on which ports. Second, changing default credentials is not optional and never has been — bake it into commissioning checklists and integrator acceptance testing. Third, small sites are the target: remote lift stations, unmanned booster pumps, and skid-mounted units are where poor practices hide. The KEV entries this month for MikroTik and ConnectWise ScreenConnect are directly relevant to exactly this profile of asset.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline item for plant-floor engineers today is the release of NIST SP 800-82 Revision 4 for public comment, alongside a joint CISA/FBI advisory specifically addressing ICS integrators. The integrator advisory is overdue recognition that third-party engineering firms — the folks who commission your PLCs, write your HMI screens, and dial in remotely to troubleshoot drives — are a persistent soft underbelly of OT security. Comments on the draft are open until 30 November, and this is the moment to push back on anything that doesn't reflect real plant reality. On the IT-adjacent side, SolarWinds patched two unauthenticated RCE bugs (CVE-2026-28324/28325) in Observability Self-Hosted. Many sites use SolarWinds for network monitoring bridging the enterprise and Purdue Level 3.5 boundary, so audit any deployment touching your DMZ or historian zone. Enterprise browser vendor Island's $400M raise is a reminder that the market is consolidating around browser-isolation strategies for remote engineering access — a control worth evaluating for vendor and integrator access. SANS ISC continues to flag social-engineering-heavy campaigns (Macfinger ClickFix, LausivLoader malspam impersonating suppliers). These matter because procurement and engineering inboxes remain the initial-access path of choice for adversaries pivoting to OT. Reinforce with your team that unsolicited RFQs and quote requests are now a primary lure, and any 'ClickFix' style prompt asking a user to paste commands into Run or PowerShell should be treated as an incident. Finally, the Ryuk sentencing (Vardanyan, $1.2M restitution) closes a chapter on a ransomware family that hit numerous manufacturers and utilities in 2019-2020. Symbolic, but the affiliate model that replaced it is what continues to hurt operators today.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this month are dominated by perimeter and remote-access infrastructure — exactly the gear that sits between your corporate network and OT. Citrix NetScaler (CVE-2026-19490) has an authentication-bypass affecting AAA/Gateway configurations, and Fortinet FortiOS/FortiSwitchManager/FortiSASE (CVE-2025-25249) has a pre-auth heap overflow. Both are commonly used as the VPN concentrator for third-party integrator access into OT environments. Patch immediately, and audit which accounts still have persistent VPN access into Level 3 or below. Check Point added two entries the same week: an unauthenticated path traversal with arbitrary script upload on management servers (CVE-2026-93616), and an improper certificate validation flaw on Security Gateway/Spark firewalls using S2S or Remote Access VPN (CVE-2026-85102) enabling RCE on the gateway itself. F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127) rounds out the VPN trifecta with a pre-auth heap overflow when OAuth is configured. If your enterprise VPN is the sole barrier to your OT DMZ, that is now a demonstrated single point of failure. On the network-device side, MikroTik RouterOS gets two entries (CVE-2026-86060 privilege escalation and CVE-2026-67277 kernel memory disclosure) — worth checking, as MikroTik shows up in smaller sites, remote pump stations and skid-mounted equipment more often than asset inventories admit. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2026-84869) allowing unauthorized file transfer/execution in active sessions is a direct threat to any site using it for vendor support; require MFA, session approval, and recording, and consider retiring it from OT paths.Read more →
