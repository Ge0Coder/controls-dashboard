Today's Knowledge Bite

The HTTP QUERY method defined in RFC 10008 finally solves a decade-old awkwardness: how to send a request body with a GET-like semantic. Until now, developers of REST-based historian and SCADA APIs have abused POST for read operations, which breaks caching and idempotency assumptions. Expect modern OPC UA REST bindings and time-series databases to adopt QUERY within the next couple of firmware cycles.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalized not in a university lab but on U.S. Navy warships in the 1920s. Nicolas Minorsky, observing how helmsmen steered the USS New Mexico, noticed they applied corrections proportional to heading error, integral to accumulated drift, and derivative to how fast the ship was swinging — and mathematically formalized it in 1922. Every temperature loop, flow controller, and motion axis you tune today traces its lineage back to a human being at a ship's wheel.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) as the transport layer for OPC UA FX (Field eXchange). OPC UA has long been the darling of the IT/OT convergence crowd, but its traditional TCP-based transport could not compete with EtherCAT or PROFINET IRT for hard-real-time control loops. OPC UA FX over TSN changes that by combining OPC UA's rich information model with IEEE 802.1 TSN's deterministic Ethernet scheduling — giving you sub-microsecond synchronization and guaranteed frame delivery on a converged network. For a controls engineer, the practical implication is that within a few years you may be able to run a servo drive, a vision system, and a plant-wide historian feed on the same switched Ethernet fabric, without dedicated fieldbuses. That reduces cable count, spares inventory, and integration effort. It also means the network switch becomes safety- and control-critical infrastructure, requiring the same change-management rigor you apply to a PLC. Adoption challenges remain significant. TSN requires end-to-end support — every switch, every NIC, every device — and today's TSN-capable managed switches are expensive and configuration-heavy. Multi-vendor interoperability testing (the OPC Foundation's FX plugfests) is progressing, but greenfield deployments still dominate. Most brownfield sites should watch, pilot on a non-critical cell, and wait for the third generation of TSN silicon before committing to large-scale rollouts.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues its steady march through the ratification pipeline. The most operationally relevant recent development is the ongoing work on IEC 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners), which is aligning more closely with the SL-based approach in 62443-3-3. For asset owners, this means your site's security management system documentation should map cleanly against maturity levels, not just checklist compliance — a shift that has been reflected in updated ISASecure certification schemes. On the NIST side, the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function is now feeding into revised OT-specific guidance in NIST SP 800-82 Rev.3. The practical takeaway is that governance and supply-chain risk management are being treated as first-class citizens alongside the classic Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond/Recover cycle. If your organization has not yet mapped OT vendor risk (spare parts, firmware update channels, remote support) into your enterprise TPRM program, expect auditors to start asking about it. Worth keeping an eye on: ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 component requirements are increasingly cited in procurement specifications for new PLCs, RTUs, and gateways. If you are speccing equipment this quarter, requiring documented SL-C 2 or higher capability certification is now a reasonable minimum for most process industries, and vendors like Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, and ABB all have certified product families to point to.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's retrospective is the 2024 Frontier Communications ransomware incident, which remains instructive because of what it revealed about IT-to-OT blast radius in critical infrastructure. Attackers (later attributed to the RansomHub affiliate ecosystem) gained initial access through a compromised employee identity and moved laterally through the corporate Active Directory forest. While Frontier's core telecom switching plane was not directly encrypted, provisioning systems, customer support portals, and network management tooling were — degrading the ability to operate and maintain the network for weeks. How it happened: shared identity infrastructure between corporate IT and network operations, insufficient segmentation between the two, and remote-access tooling that had been onboarded without security review. It mirrors patterns we saw in Colonial Pipeline (2021) and MKS Instruments (2023) — the ransomware never touches the control system, but the operational impact is nonetheless severe because business systems needed to run the plant are unavailable. Key lessons for controls engineers: first, map every dependency your control systems have on IT services (DNS, AD, NTP, licensing servers, historian replication) and design for graceful degradation when those services vanish. Second, insist on separate identity stores for OT — federated with IT for convenience is fine, but the OT domain must be able to authenticate operators when the corporate AD is offline. Third, tabletop-exercise a scenario where you can run the plant but cannot ship product because SAP/MES is down — the operational decisions in that window are rarely documented anywhere.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's news cycle was dominated by IT-side stories with clear OT ripple effects. SecurityWeek flagged a critical SAP flaw in its 'In Other News' roundup — relevant to plant engineers because SAP frequently bridges the enterprise/OT boundary via MES, batch reporting, and historian integrations. Mandiant's 2026 AI risk report and the 'Plugin4Shell' AI attack technique also landed, underscoring that AI-assisted exploit development is now producing working proofs-of-concept against enterprise SaaS, as Hacktron demonstrated by chaining an AI-built exploit with an OpenAI sign-in flaw to reach internal source code. On the supply-chain front, attackers used a compromised Brevo API key to inject malicious Cloudflare workers into roughly 100,000 websites — a reminder that third-party SaaS keys in your vendor portals, remote-support tools, or engineering software update channels remain a soft underbelly. The Gyazo breach (23 million records) and Microsoft's patching of 18 Azure/AI vulnerabilities reinforce that identity providers and cloud tenants used by OT-adjacent applications need the same rigor as the PLC network. SANS ISC highlighted a new IETF standard, RFC 10008, defining the HTTP QUERY method — the first new HTTP verb since PATCH in 2010. Expect this to appear over the next couple of years in modern SCADA web HMIs, historian REST APIs, and OPC UA gateways; firewall and WAF rulesets that only allow GET/POST/PUT will need updating. ISC also documented LausivLoader malspam and scans targeting hospitality applications — low-noise indicators worth checking against any internet-facing OT vendor portals. No dedicated ICS-CERT advisories dominated this cycle, but the volume of network-edge KEV additions (Cisco ISE, NetScaler, Fortinet, MikroTik, Cisco FMC) means the perimeter and DMZ equipment protecting most plants deserves priority patching over the weekend.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added a heavy batch of network-edge and infrastructure vulnerabilities to the KEV catalog over the past week, several of which are directly relevant to OT environments. CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler authentication bypass), CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet FortiOS heap overflow), CVE-2026-20079 (Cisco Firewall Management Center auth bypass), and CVE-2026-76460 (Cisco ISE unauthenticated access) all affect devices commonly deployed at the IT/OT boundary or as remote-access gateways for engineering staff. If any of these front your Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ or terminate vendor VPNs, treat them as emergency patches. MikroTik RouterOS also picked up two KEV entries (CVE-2026-86060, CVE-2026-67277) — worth checking because MikroTik gear frequently shows up in small water, wastewater, and remote telemetry sites. Three Linux kernel bugs (CVE-2025-39964, CVE-2026-53266, CVE-2025-39682) were added on 18 September, affecting AF_ALG sockets, ebtables SNAT, and the TLS receive path. These matter for the growing population of Linux-based edge gateways, protocol converters, and virtualized HMIs. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2026-84869) is also on the list — a common remote-support tool used by system integrators, and one worth auditing for unauthorized file transfer activity on any engineering workstation where it's installed. Practical remediation: prioritize the network-edge devices first (NetScaler, Fortinet, Cisco), then remote-access tooling (ScreenConnect), then Linux-based OT appliances during your next maintenance window. For KEV items you cannot patch immediately, document compensating controls — ACL restrictions, segmentation, and enhanced logging — as CISA BOD 22-01 requires.Read more →
