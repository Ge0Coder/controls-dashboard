Today's Knowledge Bite

KNX, the building-automation protocol that just landed in CISA's KEV, traces its lineage back to the European Installation Bus (EIB) from 1990 — meaning much of the security model in KNX predates the public internet. That's why 'KNX Secure,' with proper authentication and encryption, was only formalized in 2013 and is still not the default in most installations you'll encounter in the field.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics: Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while working on automatic steering for US Navy battleships, watching helmsmen and noticing they didn't just correct for present error — they anticipated it and remembered how the ship had been drifting. His paper 'Directional Stability of Automatically Steered Bodies' effectively described integral and derivative action from human behavior before any pneumatic or electronic controller implemented it. Every loop you tune today is, in a real sense, an engineered imitation of a good helmsman's intuition.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to move from spec into shipping products, and 2026 has been the year several major vendors finally have controllers, drives, and switches with interoperable TSN profiles. The promise is straightforward: a single Ethernet fabric carrying motion-grade cyclic traffic, standard OPC UA information models, and regular IT traffic, with guaranteed latency and bounded jitter. For a controls engineer that means one cable plant instead of separate Profinet, EtherCAT, and enterprise networks — and pub/sub semantics that map naturally onto MQTT/Sparkplug-style architectures upstream. The practical reality is messier. TSN requires end-to-end support: every switch in the path must speak the same TSN features (IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduling, 802.1AS time sync, 802.1CB frame replication), and mixing vendors still exposes rough edges around configuration models and network engineering tools. Configuration is not point-and-click; you need to model traffic classes, compute schedules, and think like a network engineer, not just a controls engineer. Cybersecurity also shifts — a compromised switch in a TSN fabric can starve safety-relevant traffic just as effectively as a malicious controller could. The pragmatic path most sites are taking is to pilot TSN on greenfield cells — a robot workcell, a servo-heavy packaging line — where the vendor stack is homogeneous, and use the deployment to build in-house skills before extending it plant-wide. Expect three to five more years before this is genuinely commodity.Read more →

Standards Watch

The refreshed multinational SBOM guidance published this week is the standards item with the most direct impact on procurement and IEC 62443 compliance programs. IEC 62443-4-1 already expects product suppliers to manage third-party components across the lifecycle, and asset owners under 62443-2-1 need visibility into what's inside the boxes on their DIN rails. The updated guidance tightens definitions around component identifiers, dependency relationships, and vulnerability linkage, which will make SBOMs more consumable by asset management and vulnerability tools. If your CMMS or OT asset inventory platform accepts SBOMs, this refresh is the moment to update your ingestion mapping. On the IEC 62443 front itself, work continues on aligning 62443-3-2 (risk assessment for system design) with the newer 62443-2-1 (SPE — Security Program Elements for asset owners), which was substantially rewritten in 2024. The alignment matters because many operators have been running risk assessments to the old 2-1 language while designers reference the new 3-2 flow — inconsistencies show up when zone and conduit diagrams don't tie cleanly to your organizational security policies. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the practical reference on the US side, and it continues to be the standard most useful for translating IT security controls into terms plant engineers can actually implement — particularly its explicit treatment of safety-security interactions, which the ISA/IEC standards still handle more abstractly.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 attack on a US water utility where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised Unitronics Vision-series PLCs by exploiting internet-exposed devices still running the vendor default password '1111.' At the Aliquippa, Pennsylvania municipal water authority, attackers defaced the PLC HMI and forced operators to switch to manual control of a booster station. No water quality impact occurred, but the incident triggered CISA advisories and legislative attention that continue to shape OT policy today. The mechanism was mundane: TCP/20256 (Unitronics PCOM) exposed to the internet, default credentials, and no VPN or jump host. What made it consequential was the geopolitical framing — a nation-state-aligned group targeting small, under-resourced water utilities specifically because they were soft targets. Similar exposures were subsequently found across dozens of US utilities in Shodan sweeps. Three lessons remain relevant. First, never trust that 'nobody knows this device is here' — Shodan and its imitators index everything, including obscure protocols. Second, default credentials on PLCs are not a theoretical risk; changing them is a fifteen-minute job that has prevented documented compromises. Third, when your response plan assumes 'we'll switch to manual,' actually test that fallback: operators at Aliquippa did switch to manual successfully, and that preparation is why the story ended without harm.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline story for anyone with a perimeter firewall in front of their plant network is CVE-2026-20316, a hard-coded password vulnerability in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (formerly Firepower Management Center) that is being actively exploited. An unauthenticated remote attacker can log in with low privileges, and given how many IT/OT boundaries rely on FMC-managed Firepower appliances, this is a direct threat to the segmentation many controls engineers depend on. CISA added it to KEV on 29 July, so federal timelines apply and most mature OT programs will treat that as a hard deadline. Claroty published research showing that roughly 20% of cyber-physical assets in large data centers — UPS, CRAC/CRAH units, BMS controllers, PDUs — are one or two hops from an attacker who has landed on the corporate network. The findings mirror what many manufacturing sites look like: BACnet, Modbus, and proprietary building-automation protocols exposed with minimal segmentation. For plant engineers, the parallel is worth internalizing, because the same design habits that leave data-center facilities gear exposed also leave process HVAC, chillers, and utility skids exposed. On the policy side, CISA and international partners released an updated Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) guidance document, refreshing terminology and adding new data fields. Vendors of PLCs, HMIs, gateways, and historians will increasingly be asked to deliver SBOMs, and asset owners should be pushing that into procurement language now. The US also announced a ban on foreign-made humanoid robots, citing national security; while narrow today, it foreshadows tighter controls on Chinese-origin automation hardware that will filter into robot cells and material-handling projects. Rounding out the week, Chrome 151 shipped fixes for 370 vulnerabilities (roughly 80 critical/high) — relevant for engineering workstations and HMIs that use embedded Chromium — and Apple pushed a full OS update wave. Neither is an OT emergency, but both belong on the patch tracker for engineering laptops and any thin clients used to reach control systems.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The most operationally urgent KEV addition is CVE-2026-20316 in Cisco Secure FMC. Because FMC is the management plane, compromise gives attackers the ability to modify rules on the very firewalls enforcing IT/OT separation. Patch immediately, restrict management-interface access to a dedicated jump network, and audit recent policy changes and admin logins for anomalies. If you cannot patch within your maintenance window, at minimum block external reachability to the FMC and require MFA-gated jump hosts. For OT specifically, the addition of CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX Protocol Connection Authorization is notable. KNX is widespread in building automation and increasingly bridges into process facilities via HVAC, lighting, and access control. The flaw lets an attacker exhaust device authorization attempts and effectively lock out or reconfigure BCU keys. If you operate KNX segments, confirm KNX Secure is enabled, isolate KNX from routable IP networks, and inventory any IP-to-KNX gateways. Also worth attention: two more Fortinet items (FortiOS symlink-persistence bypass and two FortiSandbox command-injection flaws), SonicWall SMA1000 SSRF and code injection, and continuing SharePoint deserialization exposure — all typical of the remote-access and document-sharing pathways that lead from corporate into OT DMZs. Prioritize edge appliances and any SharePoint instance hosting engineering documentation or drawings.Read more →
