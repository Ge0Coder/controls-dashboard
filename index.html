Today's Knowledge Bite

The KNX BCU (Bus Coupling Unit) key that just landed in CISA's KEV is only 4 bytes long — 32 bits — and in many field installations it has never been changed from the factory default of 0xFFFFFFFF. That means the primary line of defence on a building automation bus controlling doors, HVAC, and lighting is often equivalent in strength to a four-character password with no lockout. Worth checking on any site you support.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's derivative term has a surprising origin: it was reverse-engineered from watching helmsmen steer ships. In 1922, Nicolas Minorsky was analysing why experienced helmsmen kept the USS New Mexico on course better than any automatic device of the era, and he noticed they didn't just react to heading error — they anticipated based on how fast the error was changing. He formalised that anticipation as the derivative term, giving us the modern three-term controller. Every temperature loop you tune today owes its D-term to a sailor's intuition about the sea.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is Ethernet-APL (Advanced Physical Layer), which is quietly moving from pilot to mainstream deployment in process plants. Ethernet-APL delivers 10 Mbit/s full-duplex Ethernet plus power over a single twisted pair, over distances up to 1,000 m, and — crucially — is intrinsically safe for Zone 0/1 hazardous areas. It effectively replaces 4–20 mA and HART at the field-device level with a proper IP-addressable network, letting you run PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, or OPC UA all the way down to a pressure transmitter. The practical implication is significant: parameterisation, diagnostics, and firmware updates that today require a HART handheld or asset management gateway become native IP traffic. That's brilliant for maintenance efficiency but painful for security. Every transmitter is now a network endpoint with a MAC address, a stack, and — inevitably — CVEs. If your OT segmentation model stops at the I/O rack, Ethernet-APL pushes the attack surface right into the field junction box. Adoption challenges remain: switch and field-device costs are still well above traditional 4–20 mA equivalents, and few sites have engineers comfortable troubleshooting both intrinsic-safety barriers and Ethernet at the same time. Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often misapplied in the field. The 2020 edition remains current, but the IEC working groups have been consulting on a revised second edition throughout 2025–2026 aimed at tightening the definitions of zones and conduits and improving alignment with 62443-3-3 security levels. If you're specifying a new control system this year, insist the vendor's risk assessment identifies zone boundaries at the P&ID level, not just the network diagram — that's where the current standard is most commonly gamed. On the US side, NIST is progressing SP 800-82 Rev. 3 supplementary guidance targeted at safety instrumented systems and manufacturing, following industry feedback that Rev. 3 (published 2023) was still too IT-oriented for SIS designers. Expect draft material later this year. Meanwhile, ISA's TR84.00.09 on cybersecurity for SIS remains the most concrete practical reference if you're wrestling with how to satisfy both IEC 61511 and 62443 for a single logic solver. For those in the EU, the Cyber Resilience Act's substantive obligations for manufacturers of products with digital elements come into force in December 2027 — 18 months away. If your OEMs haven't started talking to you about SBOMs, vulnerability handling processes, and secure-by-default configurations, that's a red flag worth raising in your next factory acceptance test.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's incident is the Coca-Cola Fairlife ransomware attack that forced the suspension of US production. Public details remain limited, but the pattern is now familiar: an IT-side ransomware detonation triggers a precautionary shutdown of manufacturing because the MES, warehouse management, labelling, or quality systems that OT depends on are unavailable or untrusted. Fairlife's Canadian operations continued, suggesting network segmentation between geographies limited the blast radius — a small but real win. What controls engineers should take from this: first, know exactly which IT services your production lines cannot run without. If your PLCs can hold recipe and continue for four hours but your labeller stops in fifteen minutes because it can't reach a print server, that's your real MTTR. Second, test degraded-mode operation. Many sites discover during a real incident that their 'run standalone' procedures haven't been validated since commissioning and no longer match current recipes or SKUs. Third, geographic and business-unit segmentation matters as much as Purdue-level segmentation. Flat Active Directory forests and shared MES instances across sites are what turn a single ransomware event into a multi-plant outage. This is the second major F&B production halt in a fortnight (Nichirei being the other) — the sector is clearly being actively targeted, and boards are paying attention.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The dominant story for plant floor engineers this week is a pair of food & beverage manufacturing disruptions. Japanese frozen food giant Nichirei disconnected systems on 13 July after a cyberattack and is now beginning a staged restore — a reminder that isolation remains the first-line containment tactic, even when it halts production. Simultaneously, Coca-Cola has suspended US Fairlife production following a ransomware incident; Canadian production and product safety are reportedly unaffected, but the outage underscores how ransomware continues to punch through into OT-adjacent business systems and force manual production halts. On the vulnerability side, a fresh Microsoft SharePoint deserialization flaw (CVE-2026-58644) is being exploited in the wild within days of disclosure. While SharePoint is not a control system, many sites still use it for engineering documentation, MOC workflows, drawings, and shift handover — an attractive pivot for adversaries mapping a plant. Combine that with July's Patch Tuesday delivering 622 Microsoft CVEs (62 critical, two already exploited) and IT patch cycles are going to spill into OT change windows. SecurityWeek's feature on legacy OT vulnerability disclosure is worth reading in full: it captures the recurring tension between coordinated disclosure and the reality that many DCS, PLC, and safety systems cannot be patched on vendor timelines, if at all. Meanwhile, UK courts sentenced two Scattered Spider members over the 2024 Transport for London attack — the group remains one of the more capable social-engineering threat actors targeting help desks, a common weak point for OT support contracts. Finally, Risk Ledger's $32M Series B highlights growing investor appetite for supply chain risk platforms — relevant as EU NIS2 and similar regimes increasingly hold asset owners accountable for third-party integrator and OEM security posture.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The standout OT-relevant KEV addition is CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX protocol's Connection Authorization Option 1, added 15 July. The flaw's weak account lockout mechanism allows an attacker to brute-force the BCU key, purge devices, and effectively take over KNX installations that rely on the default protection. KNX is widespread in building automation — HVAC, lighting, access — which sits adjacent to many industrial sites and is often on the same converged network as process controls. If you own KNX segments, verify that IP Secure or Data Secure is enabled, that BCU keys have been changed from defaults, and that KNX/IP routers are not reachable from corporate or guest networks. The other cluster worth attention is enterprise-edge appliances feeding into OT: two Fortinet FortiSandbox flaws (CVE-2026-25089, CVE-2026-39808) allow unauthenticated command injection, and two SonicWall SMA1000 issues (CVE-2026-15409/15410) enable SSRF and authenticated RCE. These are the same remote-access and inspection appliances many sites use as their IT/OT boundary or vendor tunnel termination. Patch on priority, and audit any lingering vendor VPN accounts. Also notable: PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569) — a pre-auth RCE in a PLM system that likely holds your CAD, BoMs, and control system configurations. Treat Windchill exposure with the same seriousness as an engineering workstation.Read more →
