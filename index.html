Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, still ubiquitous on plant floors nearly 50 years after its 1979 introduction by Modicon, was originally designed to run over RS-232 at 9600 baud with no authentication because it was assumed the physical serial cable was the security boundary. When it was wrapped in TCP in 1999 the assumption was never revisited, which is why every Modbus/TCP device today still trusts any packet that reaches port 502.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronic computing by decades — Elmer Sperry's ship autopilots in the 1910s implemented proportional-integral-derivative control mechanically using gyroscopes, springs, and dashpots, and Nicholas Minorsky formalized the mathematics in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico. What Minorsky noticed was that skilled helmsmen didn't just react to the current heading error; they also considered how long the ship had been off course and how quickly it was swinging back. That behavioural observation, translated into three tunable terms, still runs roughly 90% of the control loops in the world today.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) continues its slow migration from demo booths to real deployments, and it's worth understanding what it actually delivers. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 extensions that add deterministic scheduling, time synchronization (via 802.1AS, derived from PTP), and traffic shaping to standard Ethernet, so cyclic control traffic can share the same wire as engineering downloads and video without jitter. OPC UA PubSub sits on top, providing a vendor-neutral publish/subscribe model that replaces the point-to-point client/server pattern with something closer in behavior to PROFINET IRT or EtherCAT — but on commodity switches from any vendor that supports the TSN profiles. The practical promise is a single converged network from sensor to cloud with sub-millisecond determinism, eliminating the traditional split between fieldbus and enterprise Ethernet. The practical reality in 2026 is that TSN-capable switches from Cisco, Siemens, Hirschmann, and Moxa are shipping, but interoperability testing between vendors is still maturing, PLC-side support beyond Siemens and B&R is thin, and engineering tools for configuring TSN streams remain clunky. Most greenfield projects still pick a proven fieldbus. Expect meaningful adoption first in automotive and semiconductor fabs where the motion-control-plus-vision use case justifies the complexity.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2, the standard covering security risk assessment for system design, remains the most practically important document for controls engineers this year as more asset owners embed it into capital project specifications. The 2020 edition drives the zone-and-conduit methodology that increasingly appears in FEED-stage documentation for oil and gas and pharma projects, and the ISA99 working groups have been refining the accompanying technical reports on risk scoring consistency. On the IEC 62443-4-2 (component requirements) front, the shift from paper-based vendor claims to third-party certification through ISASecure and TÜV is accelerating. Several major PLC vendors now ship product lines with SL 2 certification out of the box, and increasingly procurement teams are requiring this in bid documents. If you're specifying new equipment, ask for the certification report rather than a marketing datasheet — the report tells you which requirements were met with compensating controls versus native features, and that distinction matters when you're designing your zone architecture. On the NIST side, the CSF 2.0 profile for manufacturing continues to see refinement, and NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3 remains the go-to reference for OT-specific implementation guidance. Worth revisiting if your last read was Rev 2 — the treatment of cloud-connected OT and remote engineering access is substantially expanded.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment intrusion, given its parallels to the ScreenConnect KEV entry above. An attacker gained access to a plant operator's workstation via TeamViewer, which was installed for remote support and shared among staff with a common password. Once inside, the intruder briefly raised the sodium hydroxide setpoint from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm before an alert operator noticed the cursor moving and reversed the change. The incident was caught by human observation, not by any technical control. The attack succeeded because remote access was flat, shared, and unmonitored — a configuration that persists in countless small utilities today. Subsequent investigation suggested the initial access may have come via a compromised contractor site rather than direct exposure, illustrating the supply chain dimension. Three lessons remain relevant: first, remote access tools intended for IT support should never share credentials across users or sites, and should log every session with video capture. Second, safety-critical setpoints deserve rate-of-change limits and independent high-high alarms enforced in the safety system or a separate PLC, not just the HMI. Third, human vigilance caught this one — but relying on an operator to spot a moving cursor is not a control strategy. Automated setpoint change alerts to a SIEM or operations supervisor close that gap.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's news cycle is dominated by IT-side vulnerabilities and cybercrime enforcement rather than direct ICS incidents, but several items have downstream implications for OT environments. The guilty plea from the Kosovar operator of the Rydox marketplace is a reminder that stolen credentials and PII feeding initial-access brokers remain a primary supply chain for intrusions that eventually reach industrial networks through IT-to-OT pivots. Research published this week showing that Windows, Linux, and Android file-change notification systems leak keystroke timing and application activity is worth noting for engineering workstations. Any HMI, historian, or engineering laptop that dual-purposes as a general-use machine could inadvertently leak sensitive process activity through these side channels. While not an immediate patch item, it reinforces the case for dedicated, hardened engineering workstations isolated from browsing and messaging tools. The SalesBleed disclosures in Salesforce Agentforce and the emergence of Kontext Security's AI agent runtime controls both point to the accelerating adoption of AI agents in enterprise workflows and the accompanying trust boundary problems. As vendors push AI copilots into SCADA, historian analytics, and asset management platforms, controls engineers should be asking how those agents authenticate, what data they see, and whether they can trigger writes back into control systems. SANS ISC continues tracking the Macfinger ClickFix campaign, a social-engineering vector where users are tricked into pasting attacker-supplied commands into Run dialogs or terminals. This technique bypasses many endpoint controls and is highly effective against engineers and operators who routinely execute scripts during troubleshooting. Consider a brief awareness note to your maintenance and integration teams.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks are heavy on perimeter and remote-access infrastructure that commonly sits between IT and OT. F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127, unauthenticated RCE via a heap overflow in OAuth-configured virtual servers) and two Check Point issues — a path traversal in Security Management servers (CVE-2026-93616) and an improper certificate validation flaw in Site-to-Site and Remote Access VPN gateways (CVE-2026-85102) — are all high-impact for any plant with remote engineering access or vendor VPN tunnels. Patch these immediately on any device fronting OT, and audit VPN certificate chains. MikroTik RouterOS gains two entries (CVE-2026-86060 privilege escalation and CVE-2026-67277 unauthenticated kernel memory disclosure), and Zyxel GS1900 switches carry an unauthenticated LAN-side buffer overflow (CVE-2026-7273). These low-cost devices frequently appear in small remote sites, pump stations, and contractor-installed segments where inventory visibility is poor — worth a network sweep. Cisco ISE (CVE-2026-76460) and Cisco Secure Email Gateway (CVE-2026-76461) both allow unauthenticated remote code execution or unauthorized access and should be prioritized on the IT side since ISE often governs OT network access policy. Finally, ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2026-84869) allows unauthorized file transfer and execution during an active remote session — directly relevant to any integrator or OEM using ScreenConnect for remote support of PLCs, drives, or HMIs. Confirm your remote support tooling is patched and consider requiring host confirmation prompts for all file transfers.Read more →
