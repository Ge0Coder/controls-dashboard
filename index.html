Two OT-relevant CVEs added to CISA KEV this week deserve your attention before anything else: PTC Windchill/FlexPLM unauthenticated RCE (CVE-2026-12569) and Lantronix EDS5000 root command injection (CVE-2025-67038). Both are actively exploited and both sit in places most sites under-monitor — PLM adjacent to engineering, and serial-to-Ethernet gateways bridging legacy field devices. Inventory both today, verify exposure, and either patch or firewall-isolate before end of shift. Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix serial device server product line traces back to the mid-1990s, and the underlying architecture of exposing a TTY over TCP hasn't fundamentally changed since. That means a 2026 RCE in an EDS5000 is exploiting the same conceptual attack surface — a network-exposed command channel to a serial gateway — that security researchers warned about at industry conferences over 20 years ago. Legacy protocol bridges are the geological strata of OT security.

⚛

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronic computing by nearly a century. Nicolas Minorsky formalized proportional-integral-derivative control mathematically in 1922 while analyzing the automatic steering of the USS New Mexico, observing how a skilled helmsman anticipates the ship's response — the derivative term encoded human intuition. The pneumatic three-term controllers that dominated process industries from the 1930s through the 1960s implemented the same math using nothing but air pressure, bellows, and nozzles. Every modern DCS loop is, at its core, a digital reimplementation of a helmsman's instinct made mathematical over a hundred years ago.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the extension of OPC UA down to the field/controller-to-controller layer, which is now moving from spec into shipping products from Siemens, Beckhoff, Rockwell, Phoenix Contact, and others. FX adds a standardized way for controllers to exchange data peer-to-peer with defined semantics, offline engineering, and — when combined with TSN — deterministic timing suitable for motion and safety-adjacent applications. In practice, it aims to do for controller-to-controller communication what OPC UA already did for supervisory data: replace a patchwork of vendor protocols with a single information model. For controls engineers, the practical implication is that heterogeneous cells (say, a Beckhoff line controller talking to a Siemens robot cell and a Rockwell packaging skid) could in principle exchange tag-level data without gateways or protocol converters. That's the promise. The reality in 2026 is that TSN switch interoperability remains uneven, engineering-tool support for offline FX configuration varies widely between vendors, and the certified device population is still small. Most greenfield projects using FX today are pilot lines, not production rollouts. If you're specifying new equipment, it is worth requiring OPC UA FX capability as a future-proofing clause, but don't yet plan a project around it being turnkey.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the most-cited standard in OT procurement language this year, and increasingly it is being referenced in EU NIS2 and CRA-related conformity discussions. If you haven't yet formalized zone and conduit diagrams for your facility, that's rapidly becoming the baseline evidence auditors and insurers ask for — not just a nice-to-have architectural artifact. On the product side, IEC 62443-4-2 certification (component-level security requirements) is now a genuine differentiator when evaluating PLCs, RTUs, and network gear. Both Siemens and Rockwell have been publishing certified-configuration guidance for specific controller families, and reading those documents closely often reveals that the certified state requires disabling features many integrators leave on by default — legacy web servers, unauthenticated PROFINET discovery, or default engineering ports. Treat the certified configuration as a target hardening baseline even if you never plan to claim certification yourself. Also worth tracking: NIST SP 800-82r3 remains the most practical free reference for OT security program design, and ISA is progressing work on integrating safety (IEC 61511) and security (62443) lifecycles — an area that will matter enormously as more sites face regulatory pressure to demonstrate cybersecurity does not undermine SIL claims.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's incident is the ongoing exploitation of the SimpleHelp OIDC authentication bypass (CVE-2026-48558) against managed service providers and their downstream industrial customers. Multiple integrators used SimpleHelp as their standard remote-support tool for reaching customer HMIs, historians, and engineering workstations. Attackers discovered that when OIDC was configured, the SimpleHelp server accepted identity tokens without verifying their signatures — allowing them to forge tokens for any user, including administrators, and drop into customer environments through the integrator's legitimate remote access channel. What makes this a plant-floor story rather than an IT story is the trust model. Sites had allowed SimpleHelp connections from their integrator through the firewall specifically because that integrator was trusted. When the integrator's server was compromised, attackers inherited that trust into environments they should never have reached. The lessons are ones controls engineers have heard before but are worth repeating. First, vendor remote access is a supply-chain attack surface — inventory every tool every integrator uses to touch your plant, and require them to attest to patch status. Second, time-box and log all remote sessions; standing access is legacy thinking. Third, network segmentation between the remote-access jump host and actual control devices should assume the jump host will eventually be compromised, and constrain what it can reach and do.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The most consequential news for plant floor engineers this week is not in the headlines about Apple, Chrome, or Azure — it is buried in CISA's KEV additions. PTC Windchill and FlexPLM, widely used PLM systems that sit adjacent to engineering workstations and manufacturing execution layers, now have a confirmed exploited unauthenticated RCE (CVE-2026-12569). Any organization exposing Windchill to broader corporate networks should treat this as urgent, because compromise of PLM often yields authoritative product and process data attackers can leverage downstream. Alongside Windchill, Lantronix EDS5000 device servers — commonly used as serial-to-Ethernet gateways for legacy PLCs, meters, and analyzers — have a code injection flaw allowing arbitrary OS commands as root via the username parameter (CVE-2025-67038). These devices are frequently deployed with minimal segmentation and are often forgotten after commissioning. If you have any Lantronix serial servers bridging Modbus RTU, DNP3, or vendor-proprietary serial protocols into IP networks, inventory them today and confirm they are not reachable from IT or the internet. On the broader security front, the BlueHammer Microsoft Defender zero-day (CVE-2026-33825) is being used in ransomware campaigns. Because Defender is present on most Windows-based HMIs, engineering workstations, and historians, this matters directly for OT. Confirm your Windows-based OT hosts have received the patch and that Defender signature/platform updates are actually reaching air-gapped or restricted networks through your update relay. Secondary items include Google's massive 382-CVE Chrome release (relevant wherever browsers run on OT-adjacent workstations for vendor portals or SCADA thin clients), Apple's cross-platform patches, and an 81-million-attempt Azure CLI password spray campaign — a reminder that cloud-side identity for hybrid OT solutions (remote access brokers, cloud historians, IIoT platforms) is under continuous, industrialized attack.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks are unusually OT-relevant. PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569, unauthenticated RCE) and Lantronix EDS5000 (CVE-2025-67038, root command injection) are both direct hits on the manufacturing stack. Patch Windchill immediately per PTC's advisory, and if you cannot patch EDS5000 devices quickly, isolate them behind a firewall permitting only the specific control-system endpoints they serve. Do not expose either to VPN concentrators terminating vendor remote access without additional restrictions. Several network infrastructure entries also warrant attention: three Ubiquiti UniFi OS flaws (CVE-2026-34908/34909/34910) covering command injection, path traversal, and improper access control — relevant because UniFi is increasingly used in facility and small-plant networks; Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-20262, 20245) and Cisco Unified CM SSRF (CVE-2026-20230); Arista EOS tunnel decapsulation (CVE-2026-7473); and Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 auth bypass (CVE-2026-50751, known ransomware use). Any of these sitting between IT and OT deserve prompt patching and a review of firewall rules that assume the device itself is trustworthy. Finally, SimpleHelp (CVE-2026-48558) — a remote support tool often used by integrators and OEMs to reach customer plants — has an OIDC signature-verification bypass. If your vendors use SimpleHelp to reach your site, ask them for confirmation of remediation before their next connection.Read more →
