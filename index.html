Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix EDS-class serial device servers on this week's KEV list trace their lineage back to the late-1990s XPort and MSS100 modules — some of the earliest devices to put a TCP/IP stack behind a DB9 connector. Many are still bridging 1980s-vintage PLCs and analytical instruments today, meaning a single command-injection flaw can quietly compromise equipment that predates the vulnerability's discovery by three decades.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was reverse-engineered from a helmsman, not derived from theory. In the 1920s, Nicolas Minorsky watched USS New Mexico's helmsmen steer and realised they weren't just correcting for present heading error — they anticipated the ship's rate of turn (derivative) and remembered persistent drift from wind and current (integral). His 1922 paper formalised what good sailors had done intuitively for centuries, and every temperature loop in your plant today is, in essence, a mathematical model of an experienced helmsman's hands on a wheel.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is finally moving from trade-show demos into real deployments in 2026, particularly in automotive body shops and semiconductor fabs. The promise is deterministic, sub-millisecond communication on standard Ethernet — a single converged network carrying safety, motion control, and business data with guaranteed latency, replacing the parallel Profinet/EtherCAT/Sercos silos that plants have juggled for two decades. The OPC Foundation's Field Level Communications (FLC) initiative defines the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device profiles that make this possible. For controls engineers, the practical implication is that your next greenfield line may see a mix of PLCs from different vendors talking peer-to-peer over one TSN backbone, with published information models rather than proprietary tag databases. Motion synchronisation across vendors — historically nearly impossible — becomes tractable. Adoption challenges remain significant. TSN requires end-to-end support: every switch, NIC, and endpoint must implement the correct IEEE 802.1 profiles (Qbv, Qbu, AS), and troubleshooting tools are still maturing. Most sites will hit interoperability quirks between vendors' TSN implementations, and the security story — authenticated Pub/Sub, certificate management at scale — adds operational overhead that few plant IT/OT teams are staffed for today. Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component security requirements) continue to be the two parts most frequently invoked in OEM tenders this year, and we're seeing more asset owners require documented Security Level (SL-T) targets per zone as part of FEED-stage deliverables rather than as a bolt-on after commissioning. If you're specifying a new line, ask your integrator for the zone and conduit diagram alongside the P&ID — it should be a routine deliverable now, not a specialty artefact. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev.3 (Guide to OT Security) remains the go-to reference for aligning ICS controls with the broader 800-53 catalog, and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function is quietly reshaping how boards ask questions about OT — expect more requests for evidence of documented OT risk ownership, not just technical controls. ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) is the standard that maps most cleanly to those governance questions and is worth revisiting if your last read was the 2010 edition. With Microsoft's ADFS and SharePoint zero-days this week, it's also worth noting that IEC 62443-3-3 SR 1.1 (human user identification and authentication) explicitly calls out the risk of shared identity infrastructure between IT and OT. If your OT domain trusts corporate ADFS for engineering-workstation SSO, that trust relationship is exactly the kind of shared conduit the standard expects you to have assessed and documented.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers didn't exploit a sophisticated vulnerability — the PLC was internet-exposed on TCP/20256 with the default password '1111', and they defaced the HMI with a political message. Operators noticed immediately and switched to manual control; there was no impact to water quality or supply. What made it noteworthy wasn't the sophistication but the scale of the underlying exposure: subsequent Shodan sweeps found hundreds of similar Unitronics devices reachable from the public internet across US water utilities, and CISA issued an urgent advisory the same week. The attackers were opportunistic, scanning for a specific vendor banner and trying default credentials — no zero-day required. Three lessons stand carrying forward into 2026: first, 'small utility' is not a threat model that protects you — nation-state-aligned groups will target you specifically because you're small and under-resourced. Second, default credentials on any internet-reachable controller are an existential risk; assume every vendor-default is already in a scanner's dictionary. Third, the fact that manual fallback worked in Aliquippa is exactly why keeping trained operators and manual-mode procedures alive matters — automation resilience is ultimately a human capability, not just a technical one.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Microsoft's July 2026 Patch Tuesday is one for the record books: 622 vulnerabilities addressed, 62 rated critical, with two already exploited in the wild — an Active Directory Federation Services privilege escalation (CVE-2026-56155) and a SharePoint Server missing-authentication flaw (CVE-2026-56164). Both landed on CISA's KEV list the same day. For plant environments that lean on domain-joined engineering workstations, historian servers, and SharePoint-based document control, this is not a patch cycle to defer to next quarter's window. Expect ADFS-fronted SSO and any on-prem SharePoint to be probed aggressively over the coming weeks. SonicWall issued an urgent advisory for two SMA1000 zero-days (CVE-2026-15409 SSRF and CVE-2026-15410 authenticated code injection) already being chained for RCE. Many industrial sites still use SMA appliances as the remote-access gateway for OEM vendors and roving engineers, so this one directly hits the OT perimeter. If you cannot patch immediately, restrict management interfaces to known jump hosts and audit administrator accounts for anomalous sessions. On the software supply side, Adobe patched critical ColdFusion vulnerabilities (following CVE-2026-48282 hitting KEV last week), VMware fixed seven serious Avi Load Balancer flaws including auth bypass and RCE, and Chrome 150 / Firefox 152 shipped critical browser fixes — relevant because HMI thin clients and engineering laptops frequently lag on browser updates. Separately, the D1R extortion group claimed to have breached Synopsys and Bosch; Synopsys says it has found no evidence of a breach, but the noise around a major automation-adjacent vendor is worth monitoring. Finally, SANS ISC is reporting active scanning for exposed MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers and AI assistant credentials. As more engineering teams experiment with AI copilots that touch documentation, code repos, and even PLC project files, unauthenticated MCP endpoints are becoming a new class of shadow-IT exposure worth inventorying.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

Four Microsoft/SonicWall entries added on 14 July dominate this week's KEV additions and are directly relevant to OT: the two exploited Microsoft zero-days (ADFS CVE-2026-56155 and SharePoint CVE-2026-56164) plus both SonicWall SMA1000 flaws (CVE-2026-15409, CVE-2026-15410). SonicWall SMA appliances are common vendor-remote-access gateways into plant DMZs; treat these as emergency patches and, at minimum, restrict administrative access and review VPN session logs for anomalous connections. Of broader OT interest from the last 30 days: PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569) has an unauthenticated RCE — Windchill is widely used for PLM and engineering document control in manufacturing, so exposure of these servers, even internally, warrants urgent patching. Lantronix EDS5000 (CVE-2025-67038) is a serial-to-Ethernet device gadget frequently found bridging legacy RS-232/485 equipment; root-level command injection there is essentially a free ride into the process network. Ubiquiti UniFi OS received three KEV entries (34908/34909/34910), which affects the many small sites using UniFi for OT/IT convergence networking. Splunk Enterprise (CVE-2026-20253) also warrants attention if you use it as your OT SIEM.
