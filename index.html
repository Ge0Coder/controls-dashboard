Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, still ubiquitous on plant floors 47 years after Modicon introduced it in 1979, was originally designed for the Modicon 084 PLC to communicate over a proprietary serial bus at 19.2 kbit/s — and the protocol specification remained a de facto standard for over two decades before Schneider Electric transferred it to the independent Modbus Organization in 2004. Its longevity is largely because it does exactly one thing well: move register values with minimal overhead and no authentication, which is also why it remains a top ICS attack surface today.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely: Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilots in 1911 used mechanical proportional-integral-derivative action via a system of levers, springs and dashpots, and by 1922 Nicolas Minorsky had formally analysed the mathematics after watching helmsmen intuitively apply what we now call derivative action to anticipate ship yaw. The 'three-term controller' therefore emerged from empirical observation of human operators, not from theory — meaning your DCS tuning today still encodes how a good helmsman steered a battleship a century ago. Even more curiously, Minorsky's paper sat largely ignored for over a decade before pneumatic controllers made it practical in process industries in the 1930s.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is finally moving from trade-show demos to production pilots, and it's worth understanding because it fundamentally changes how you think about deterministic control networks. Traditional industrial Ethernet variants — PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, EtherNet/IP with CIP Sync — each solved determinism by defining their own layer-2 behaviour, forcing you to pick a vendor ecosystem. TSN pushes determinism into standard IEEE 802.1 Ethernet using scheduled traffic (802.1Qbv), frame preemption (802.1Qbu) and precise time sync (802.1AS), meaning one converged network can carry deterministic control traffic alongside video, engineering and IT flows. OPC UA sits on top as the application-layer information model, with the OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) profile extending it down to controller-to-controller and eventually controller-to-device communication. The practical promise: vendor-neutral, semantically rich data exchange from sensor to cloud on one wire. The adoption challenges are real, though. TSN configuration is genuinely complex — you need a Central Network Configuration entity, careful schedule planning, and switches that actually implement the full stack correctly. Interoperability between vendor implementations is still maturing, and most brownfield sites can't justify ripping out working PROFINET or EtherNet/IP. Expect to see TSN first in greenfield automotive, semiconductor and packaging lines where the deterministic-plus-converged story pays back quickly, with broader process-industry adoption several years out.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security Risk Assessment for System Design) continues to be the standard most controls engineers actually need to internalise, because it defines how you break a system into Zones and Conduits and assign a target Security Level (SL-T) to each. The recent trend in asset-owner RFQs is to require SL-T 2 as a baseline for control zones and SL-T 3 for safety-related or highly critical zones, which pushes real requirements onto integrators: role-based access, secure remote access, network segmentation with documented conduit controls, and event logging that survives a controller reboot. On the certification side, IEC 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) are increasingly showing up as procurement gates — several major end users now refuse to specify PLCs, drives or HMIs without at least a 4-2 SL 2 certification. If you're evaluating vendors this quarter, ask specifically which product family and firmware version was certified, because certifications are often narrower than marketing suggests. Worth noting: NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3, released in 2023, remains the best free English-language complement to 62443 and is being widely referenced in US federal CNI guidance. With the UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill amendments this week echoing similar supply-chain themes, expect regulators on both sides of the Atlantic to lean harder on 62443-based conformance in the next 12 months.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week, given the SonicWall SMA1000 KEV additions, is the 2023 Aliquippa water authority incident in Pennsylvania, where attackers linked to the CyberAv3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station and defaced the HMI with an anti-Israel message. The intrusion did not disrupt water supply, but operators had to switch to manual control while the device was replaced. What happened technically was almost embarrassingly simple: the Unitronics PLC was directly reachable from the internet on TCP/20256 with the factory-default password (1111). No zero-day, no sophisticated tradecraft — just an exposed controller with default credentials. The same campaign hit multiple water utilities across several US states within days. The lessons for controls engineers remain painfully relevant. First, no controller should ever be directly internet-reachable; if remote access is required, it belongs behind an authenticated, logged jump host or VPN concentrator — which is precisely why the SonicWall SMA1000 KEVs this week matter. Second, default credentials on field devices are not a paperwork issue but an active-exploitation issue, and commissioning checklists must enforce changes with evidence. Third, know what you have exposed: a Shodan or Censys sweep of your own public IP ranges takes an afternoon and routinely finds surprises.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline item for plant floor engineers today is Rockwell Automation's release of advisories covering more than a dozen vulnerabilities across RSLinx Classic, ArmorStart, ControlFLASH, FactoryTalk and other products. Because RSLinx Classic and FactoryTalk sit at the heart of most Rockwell-based control networks — mediating between engineering workstations, historians, HMIs and the PLC layer — these patches need to be assessed against your MOC process this week. Expect a mix of memory corruption, authentication weakness and denial-of-service issues; treat any engineering-workstation-resident software as high priority given its trust relationships with controllers. On the network infrastructure side, Cisco has patched critical vulnerabilities in IOS XR and Nexus switches enabling remote code execution and authentication bypass, and has warned of unpatched flaws in Secure Email that could expose S/MIME-encrypted content. Nexus switches are widely deployed in industrial data centres and Purdue Level 3/3.5 aggregation roles, so plan an out-of-cycle review of firmware versions on any Nexus gear bridging IT and OT. Geopolitically, the UK is moving late amendments through the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill that would give ministers powers to bar high-risk technology suppliers from critical national infrastructure. For asset owners this signals more supply-chain provenance obligations coming down the line — worth flagging to procurement now so vendor questionnaires and SBOM requirements are aligned. Meanwhile, the 153 million driver licence images offered on the dark web (allegedly from IDScan.net) is a reminder that identity-proofing vendors are increasingly attractive targets, with knock-on impacts for any OT site using government-ID-based visitor or contractor onboarding. Finally, on the emerging-tech front, OpenLeash launched a tool that intercepts risky AI agent actions and requests human approval — a pattern worth watching as agentic AI creeps into engineering workflows and copilots begin touching real configuration data.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added a substantial batch of KEV entries this week, but the most directly OT-relevant are the two SonicWall SMA1000 flaws (CVE-2026-83548 SSRF and CVE-2026-83549 authenticated OS command injection). SMA1000 appliances are commonly used to broker vendor and engineer remote access into OT environments, so any exploitation gives adversaries a foothold precisely where segmentation is weakest. If you have SMA1000 in your remote-access path, patch immediately and audit admin accounts and session logs for the past 60 days. Also notable for industrial environments: JFrog Artifactory picked up two KEV entries (CVE-2026-82329 default-config auth bypass to admin, and CVE-2026-66384 path traversal), which matters if you use Artifactory to store firmware, container images or engineering artefacts. PaperCut NG/MF (CVE-2026-82078 and CVE-2026-81578, chainable to unauthenticated RCE) is worth checking on any shared print infrastructure straddling office and plant networks. The Linux kernel IPv6 privilege escalation (CVE-2026-53362) will hit a lot of embedded gateways and edge appliances — inventory your Linux-based OT devices and pressure vendors for patch timelines. Older KEV additions (Microsoft SQL Server 2019, Red Hat libuser, AjaxPro deserialization) suggest active exploitation of long-lived legacy systems, exactly the kind of stack still running historians and MES.Read more →
