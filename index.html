Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Modbus protocol, published by Modicon in 1979, was designed to run over RS-232 at 9600 baud and had no concept of authentication because the physical serial cable was the security boundary. Nearly fifty years later, Modbus/TCP still carries that same trust model into Ethernet networks, which is why a single misconfigured firewall rule can expose a PLC's coil registers to the internet. The protocol's longevity is a testament to its simplicity — and a cautionary tale about assumptions that outlive their physical context.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalised not in a university lab but aboard US Navy ships in the 1920s, when Elmer Sperry and Nicolas Minorsky were trying to automate ship steering. Minorsky's 1922 analysis of the USS New Mexico's steering system is the first rigorous mathematical treatment of three-term control, and he derived it by watching helmsmen compensate for wave action — effectively reverse-engineering the human brain's control strategy into equations. The 'derivative' term was literally the helmsman's anticipation of how fast the ship was swinging. Every loop you tune today traces back to a sailor fighting a wheel in heavy seas.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) layered under OPC-UA is finally moving from trade-show demos into production deployments in 2026, particularly in automotive body shops and semiconductor fabs. The combination gives you deterministic, sub-millisecond Ethernet with the semantic richness of OPC-UA's information models — effectively replacing EtherCAT or PROFINET IRT for motion control while keeping a single IT-friendly protocol stack from sensor to cloud. Beckhoff, Siemens, and B&R are all shipping TSN-capable I/O, and Cisco's IE9300 series now supports the full 802.1Qbv scheduling profile. The practical implication for controls engineers is a convergence story that actually delivers: one cable plant, one addressing scheme, and the ability to mix hard-real-time motion traffic with best-effort SCADA polling on the same switch without jitter. The challenge is that TSN requires careful network engineering — stream reservations, gate control lists, and precise clock synchronisation via 802.1AS — that most plant electricians have never configured. Expect a steep learning curve and plan on vendor-led commissioning for your first deployment. Adoption is still gated by switch cost (TSN-capable managed switches run 2-3x conventional industrial Ethernet) and by the maturity of configuration tooling. The IEC/IEEE 60802 joint profile, expected to finalise in 2026, should simplify multi-vendor interoperability significantly.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the most-cited standard in RFP language this year, with asset owners increasingly requiring integrators to deliver a zone-and-conduit diagram and target security level (SL-T) assignment as a project deliverable. If you're specifying a new skid or control system, build this into your purchase order now — retrofitting it later is painful and expensive. ISA has also been pushing adoption of 62443-2-4 (security requirements for service providers), which gives you contractual leverage over integrators and OEMs who touch your network. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3, published in late 2023, remains the pragmatic reference for OT security in regulated US sectors and is now being echoed in sector-specific guidance from TSA (pipelines), EPA (water), and FERC/NERC (bulk electric). The Rev. 3 update's expanded coverage of safety-security interactions and its explicit treatment of IIoT and cloud-connected OT make it more useful than the 2015 edition for anything commissioned in the last five years. Worth watching: the ongoing ISA/IEC work on 62443-4-2 Edition 2, which tightens component-level technical requirements and is expected to influence the next round of PLC and HMI certification programs from TÜV and exida.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's standout is the BAE Systems contractor incident that took the US Bankruptcy Noticing Center offline for a full week. While not a plant-floor incident, it is a textbook example of how a cyber event at a trusted third party cascades into operational unavailability — exactly the pattern we've seen with Colonial Pipeline (Mandiant/third-party IT), Clorox (Cognizant), and more recently several European water utilities hit through managed service providers. The initial access vector has not been fully disclosed, but the week-long outage suggests either ransomware or deep operational disruption requiring full environment rebuild. The mechanism matters: BAE provided a specific IT service, that service had privileged connectivity into a government operational system, and compromise of the contractor equalled compromise of the service. For controls engineers, substitute 'systems integrator with VPN into your DCS' or 'drive OEM with permanent remote-support tunnel' and the risk model is identical. Three takeaways. First, inventory every persistent remote-access path into your OT environment and ask what happens operationally if that vendor is breached tomorrow. Second, require time-boxed, request-based access rather than always-on tunnels — this week's BeyondTrust zero-day is a case study in why always-on vendor appliances are a liability. Third, rehearse operating your process without vendor remote support for at least 72 hours; if you can't, that's your real risk exposure.Read more →

Finally, the BAE Systems incident taking down the US Bankruptcy Noticing Center for a week is a reminder that contractor compromise cascades into operational outages far from the original target. CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this week are heavy on remote-access and perimeter gear that routinely sits between corporate IT and plant OT: BeyondTrust Remote Support (CVE-2026-102764, auth bypass by spoofing), Ivanti Connect Secure (CVE-2026-104935, unauthenticated RCE), Palo Alto PAN-OS (CVE-2026-0322, authenticated root RCE), Fortinet FortiOS/FortiProxy/FortiPAM (CVE-2025-61624, path traversal), FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286), and Cisco Secure Email Gateway (CVE-2026-76519). If any of these front your Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ or vendor remote-access path, prioritise patching this weekend and audit session logs for the last 30 days. For OT environments specifically, the BeyondTrust and Ivanti bugs are the ones to lose sleep over — both allow unauthenticated or spoofed access and both are commonly used to reach jump hosts that connect to engineering workstations. Pull vendor remote support offline until patched, rotate any credentials cached on those appliances, and verify your historian and HMI logs for lateral movement indicators. The Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager flaw (CVE-2026-76504) is also worth noting for multi-site operators using SD-WAN to connect remote plants. The presence of decade-old vulnerabilities (ISC BIND 2015, ProFTPD 2015, Apache Struts 2016) in this batch is a reminder that CISA is still finding these actively exploited — likely against unmanaged OT-adjacent assets like legacy historians, FTP file-transfer gateways, and plant file servers that nobody has touched since commissioning.Read more →
