Today's Knowledge Bite

The GPS/GNSS week-number rollover isn't just a Y2K-style curiosity — it bit real infrastructure in 2019 when legacy receivers jumped back to 1999, knocking out timing at some utilities and transit systems. Modern receivers like the Carlson VASCO-B now handle rollover in firmware, which is precisely why firmware-level vulnerabilities in GNSS gear (as in today's advisory) can cascade into time-sync failures across SCADA, PMUs, and sequence-of-events recorders. One compromised receiver can silently skew timestamps across an entire substation fleet.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalized not in a lab but on a warship — Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analyzing the automatic steering of the USS New Mexico, observing how a skilled helmsman anticipated the ship's motion rather than just reacting to heading error. His 1922 paper "Directional Stability of Automatically Steered Bodies" is essentially the mathematical description of a human helmsman's intuition. Every time you tune a loop on a reactor or a flow valve, you're applying a control law that was born from watching a sailor turn a wheel.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) combined with OPC UA PubSub is quietly reshaping what's possible on the plant floor. Traditionally, if you needed sub-millisecond, jitter-free communication between a drive, a controller, and a safety module, you reached for a fieldbus like EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, or Sercos III — each with its own silicon, cabling conventions, and engineering tools. TSN standardizes the determinism at the IEEE 802.1 layer, meaning the same switch and the same cable can carry motion control traffic, video from a vision system, and routine MES telemetry without one starving the other. For controls engineers the practical implication is convergence: one network infrastructure, one set of diagnostic tools, and vendor-neutral interoperability between controllers from different manufacturers. OPC UA over TSN lets a Siemens PLC talk directly to a Rockwell drive with guaranteed latency, without a gateway translating in between. B&R, Beckhoff, Siemens, and Rockwell all have shipping TSN products, and the CC-Link TSN ecosystem is mature in Asia. Adoption challenges are real, though. TSN configuration is genuinely complex — stream reservation, gate control lists, and time synchronization via PTP all need to be engineered, not just plugged in. Most sites lack the networking skill set, and brownfield integration with non-TSN switches requires careful boundary design. Expect TSN to land first in new greenfield automotive, semiconductor, and packaging lines before it reaches the average process plant.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security Risk Assessment for System Design) continues to be the standard most often cited in procurement specs but least well understood on the floor. It defines the zone-and-conduit methodology that underpins segmentation decisions, and crucially it ties target security levels (SL-T) to a documented risk assessment rather than to a gut feel. If your site has zones drawn on a Visio diagram but no corresponding SL-T justification, you're doing 62443 theatre, not 62443. The companion part 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for IACS components) is increasingly showing up in RFQs — OEMs are being asked to certify PLCs, HMIs, and drives to SL 1 or SL 2. ISASecure's CSA certification program has been expanding its certified-product list steadily, and several major vendors now advertise 62443-4-2 SL 2 out of the box. For engineers specifying new equipment, asking for the certification report (not just the marketing claim) is a cheap way to filter vendors. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 (finalized in 2023) remains the reference to pair with 62443 for federal or federally-adjacent work. It explicitly maps to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, which added the "Govern" function — worth reviewing if your site's OT security program still reports into IT without a clear governance line to plant leadership.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania municipal water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision series PLC at a booster station. The attackers defaced the HMI with an anti-Israel message and forced the station into manual operation. No water contamination occurred, but the compromise was trivial: the PLC was reachable from the internet on its default port 20256, and the default password "1111" had never been changed. What makes this incident enduringly relevant is how unsophisticated it was. No zero-day, no custom malware — just Shodan, a default credential, and an exposed control port. CISA's follow-up advisory confirmed dozens of similar Unitronics devices exposed across US water utilities. The attack chain would have been stopped by any one of three basic controls: removing the device from direct internet exposure, changing default credentials, or enforcing VPN-only remote access. Key lessons for controls engineers: first, inventory is foundational — you cannot protect what you don't know is online, and Shodan/Censys searches of your own public IP space take minutes. Second, vendor defaults are attacker defaults; treat every commissioning checklist as a security checklist. Third, the smallest, most forgotten PLCs — booster pumps, lift stations, remote telemetry units — are exactly the ones adversaries look for because they're rarely monitored and rarely patched.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a batch of ICS advisories today with several items that warrant attention. Top of the list is a CVSS 9.8 vulnerability (CVE-2026-6074) in the Intrado 911 Emergency Gateway across versions 5.x through 7.x, allowing attackers to read, modify, or delete files — a direct concern for any facility relying on this platform for emergency communications integration. Carlson Software's VASCO-B GNSS Receiver carries a 9.4 CVSS flaw (CVE-2026-3893) that could let a remote attacker alter critical system functions; GNSS receivers increasingly underpin precise time sync and positioning in substations, pipelines, and survey work, making this more than a niche concern. Milesight cameras and Xiongmai XM530 IP cameras round out the camera-side exposure, with RCE and authentication bypass flaws that are classic footholds into OT video networks. On the supply-chain front, SecurityWeek reports a fresh Bitwarden NPM package compromise tied to the resurgent Shai-Hulud worm family, claimed by a group calling itself TeamPCP. While Bitwarden itself isn't OT, the pattern matters: npm-based tooling increasingly shows up in engineering workstations, HMI development environments, and vendor build pipelines. Any controls team using Node-based SCADA dashboards, Ignition modules with JS tooling, or web-based HMI frameworks should audit lockfiles and pin dependencies. SANS ISC flagged an Apple out-of-band patch (iOS 26.4.2 / 18.7.8) fixing an actively exploited Notification Services flaw, CVE-2026-28950. Plant personnel carrying iPhones with access to remote-support apps, Teams, or vendor portals should be pushed to update promptly. ISC also published a guest diary on Telegram tdata as a credential-harvesting vector — a reminder that unsanctioned chat clients on engineering laptops remain a quiet but persistent exfiltration path. Finally, a Chinese firm (360 Digital Security Group) is claiming 1,000 AI-discovered vulnerabilities including finds from the Tianfu Cup. Treat the marketing skeptically, but the underlying trend — LLM-assisted vulnerability hunting against embedded and industrial stacks — is real and accelerating. Expect more PLC, RTU, and gateway CVEs from this direction over the coming year.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past week include several items with direct OT relevance. Fortinet FortiClient EMS (CVE-2026-21643), an unauthenticated SQL injection, is now known-exploited — many OT sites use FortiClient EMS to manage endpoint VPN clients for remote engineering access, so patching is urgent and exposed management interfaces should be pulled off any internet-reachable path. Three Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager flaws (CVE-2026-20122, 20133, 20128) cover malicious file upload, information disclosure, and recoverable password storage — relevant anywhere SD-WAN is stitching together remote plants, substations, or branch sites back to a central SOC/NOC. Apache ActiveMQ (CVE-2026-34197) picked up a code-injection entry; ActiveMQ continues to show up as a message broker in MES, historian, and custom integration layers, and the 2023 ActiveMQ flaw was heavily abused by ransomware, so assume the same trajectory here. Older but newly-KEV'd Microsoft Office/VBA issues (CVE-2009-0238, CVE-2012-1854) are a reminder that engineering workstations running legacy Office for macro-heavy reports remain a soft target. Practical remediation priorities for controls teams this week: patch or isolate FortiClient EMS; verify SD-WAN Manager versions and rotate DCA credentials; inventory any ActiveMQ brokers sitting between IT and OT and apply vendor fixes; and confirm engineering workstations are on supported Office builds with macro policies enforced.Read more →
