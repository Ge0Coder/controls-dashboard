Today's Knowledge Bite

The first documented use of a 'firewall' in industrial context predates computer networking: in 19th-century locomotive design, a steel firewall was placed between the boiler and the crew cab to protect operators from flame and heat. The network term was borrowed directly from this safety concept in the late 1980s, which is why OT engineers often grasp defense-in-depth intuitively — it's the same layered-barrier logic as pressure relief, rupture discs, and SIS trip points.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term exists because of a 1920s problem with ship steering: Nicholas Minorsky, watching helmsmen on the USS New Mexico, noticed that good helmsmen didn't just correct for current heading error (proportional) and rate of swing (derivative) — they also remembered persistent bias from wind and current, and applied steady counter-rudder to cancel it. He mathematized this human intuition in 1922, and every modern PID loop in your plant is essentially an emulation of an attentive sailor's wrist. The three-term controller was a human behavior before it was an algorithm.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is finally moving from demo booths into real deployments in 2026, particularly in automotive and semiconductor fabs. The promise is deterministic, sub-millisecond Ethernet communication that can replace both EtherCAT/PROFINET-IRT at the field level and OPC UA client-server at the supervisory level — one protocol stack from sensor to cloud. TSN provides the bounded latency via IEEE 802.1Qbv time-aware scheduling, while OPC UA PubSub provides the semantics and security. The practical implication for controls engineers is a potential collapse of the Purdue model's lower levels into a single converged network, which is both appealing (simpler cabling, unified diagnostics) and terrifying (one broken switch config takes down safety-adjacent traffic). Adoption is being slowed by three realities: TSN-capable switches from different vendors still don't fully interoperate despite the standard, PLC vendors are hedging by keeping their proprietary protocols alive in parallel, and the engineering tooling to configure TSN schedules remains immature compared to configuring a PROFINET network. If you're greenfielding a line in 2027, ask vendors for a TSN roadmap but don't bet production on it yet.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most frequently cited in RFPs this year, driven by the EU's NIS2 enforcement ramp and the US TSA pipeline security directives referencing 62443 concepts. The ISA/IEC 62443 committee is actively working on revisions to Part 4-2 (component requirements) to better accommodate virtualized and container-based control components, which traditional CR/SR language doesn't map to cleanly. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the practical companion document for US-based engineers and now explicitly aligns its control catalog to SP 800-53 Rev. 5, making it easier to inherit controls from IT compliance programs. Worth noting: NIST is quietly developing guidance on AI/ML in OT environments as a successor theme to the AI RMF, with early drafts expected in 2027. If your organization is piloting LLM-based alarm summarization or anomaly detection on process data, start documenting data lineage and model-change-control now — it will almost certainly be required later. On the safety side, IEC 61511 Edition 3 work continues with increasing attention to cybersecurity's impact on SIL claims. The core principle is unchanged: if a cyber event can defeat your safety function, your PFDavg calculation is a fiction. Expect auditors to start asking for evidence that your SIS is actually independent from the BPCS at the network and engineering-tool level, not just the logic solver.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week, given the ConnectWise/Artex story, is the 2024 compromise of a US water utility via an exposed ScreenConnect instance used by its SCADA integrator. The integrator maintained a persistent remote-support tunnel into the utility's engineering workstation; attackers obtained the integrator's credentials via infostealer malware on a technician's laptop, logged into ScreenConnect, and reached the HMI. Operators noticed anomalous setpoint changes within hours and reverted to manual control; no public health impact resulted, but it was close. The mechanics are mundane and that's the lesson. There was no zero-day, no clever PLC exploit — just a legitimate remote-support tool used exactly as designed, by someone holding stolen credentials. The integrator had no MFA on ScreenConnect, the utility had no session approval workflow, and the engineering workstation had direct network reach to the HMI and PLC subnet. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, inventory every remote-access path into your OT environment, including those owned by vendors and integrators, and require brokered access with per-session approval. Second, assume integrator laptops are compromised and architect accordingly — jump hosts, MFA, time-boxed access, and session recording. Third, operators spotting the anomaly and reverting to manual was the actual save; invest in operator training and clearly documented manual-fallback procedures for every critical loop.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline story this week is a China-linked APT weaponizing an AI-driven pentesting framework called Artex to automate exploitation of ConnectWise ScreenConnect instances, with hundreds of organizations hit and privileged footholds established in several. ConnectWise is widely deployed by MSPs and integrators who provide remote support into plant networks, so controls engineers should assume any ScreenConnect server reachable from the IT side is a candidate pivot into OT. Audit which vendors and integrators have persistent remote-support tunnels into your DMZ and confirm those sessions are brokered, logged, and time-bound. VMware VeloCloud SD-WAN Orchestrator has been hit by its third zero-day in four months, this time a critical SQL injection granting operator-level privileges. SD-WAN appliances increasingly sit at the edge of distributed plant and remote-site networks, carrying both IT and OT traffic across cellular and broadband underlays. If your organization uses VeloCloud to backhaul SCADA polls or historian replication from remote sites, treat the orchestrator as a crown-jewel asset and verify segmentation between SD-WAN management planes and process VLANs. On the supply-chain front, LiteLLM was compromised via stolen GitHub CLI OAuth tokens, with malicious packages pushed to PyPI and Docker Hub. This matters because LiteLLM is becoming a common proxy for engineering teams experimenting with LLMs against alarm logs, work orders, and P&ID data. Any Python environment or container built in the last week that pulls LiteLLM should be rebuilt from a pinned, verified version. Separately, PHP has pushed an emergency fix for CVE-2026-109618/109619 (CVSS 9.4) with PoCs circulating pre-patch — relevant to any web-based HMI, historian dashboards, or vendor portals built on PHP. Atlassian shipped 75 fixes across Data Center and Server products (176 total entries, 14 critical) — all in third-party components — a reminder that engineering document repositories, Jira-based change management, and Confluence runbook libraries carry the same transitive-dependency risk as any modern app. Finally, Capcom and Quest Diagnostics breaches round out the week, underscoring that credential reuse from personal services continues to seed enterprise and OT compromises.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The most OT-relevant KEV addition this cycle is CVE-2026-110936, an uncontrolled search path (DLL hijack) in Siemens Software Center. Software Center is the delivery mechanism for TIA Portal, PCS 7, SIMATIC, and most other Siemens engineering tools, so an authenticated local attacker who can drop a crafted DLL into a search path gains code execution in the engineering context. Engineering workstations are typically the softest high-value target in any Siemens shop — patch per Siemens advisory, lock down write permissions on install and temp directories, and consider application allowlisting on EWS hosts. Also notable for OT-adjacent infrastructure: CVE-2026-102764 (BeyondTrust Remote Support auth bypass), CVE-2026-104935 (Ivanti Connect Secure auth bypass to RCE), CVE-2026-0322 (Palo Alto PAN-OS authenticated root RCE), and CVE-2026-76519 (Cisco Secure Email Gateway path traversal). Any of these sitting between the enterprise and the Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ is a direct pathway into OT. The BeyondTrust and Ivanti entries are especially painful because they are the exact products often used to grant third-party integrator access. CISA also back-added several very old vulnerabilities (BIND 2015, Struts 2016, ProFTPD 2015, ONLYOFFICE 2021) which suggests ongoing exploitation against unpatched legacy infrastructure — exactly the kind of forgotten Linux boxes running MES adapters, OPC bridges, or data historians in plant basements. Worth a sweep of your asset inventory for anything running these services.Read more →
