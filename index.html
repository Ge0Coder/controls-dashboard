Today's Knowledge Bite

The VNC protocol Apple built Screen Sharing on top of dates to 1998 and was developed at Olivetti Research Lab in Cambridge — the same lab that produced the first webcam (pointed at a coffee pot). VNC was originally designed so researchers could access their thin-client desktops from anywhere on the LAN, with security as an afterthought. Nearly three decades later, that same unencrypted TCP/5900 design DNA is still causing CVEs, a useful reminder that protocol choices outlive their designers' assumptions.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's ship-steering gyrocompass autopilot from 1911 already implemented proportional-plus-derivative action mechanically, and Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 paper analysing the USS New Mexico's steering formalised the three-term controller mathematically. Even more striking, James Clerk Maxwell's 1868 paper "On Governors" — written to explain the hunting behaviour of Watt's steam-engine flyball governor — is arguably the founding document of control theory, predating the electric motor's practical use. Every time you tune a loop, you're using math worked out to keep 19th-century steam engines from tearing themselves apart.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from standard to shipping product. The promise is compelling for controls engineers: a single deterministic Ethernet fabric carrying everything from motion-control cyclic data at sub-millisecond intervals up to enterprise MES traffic, all using one information model. In principle, this collapses the historical layering of Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, and standard Ethernet into one converged network with guaranteed latency bounds. In practice, adoption remains gated by ecosystem maturity. TSN requires end-to-end support — switches, NICs, PLCs, drives, and configuration tooling all need to speak the same subset of IEEE 802.1 standards (Qbv scheduling, Qbu preemption, ASrp reservation). Vendors like Siemens, B&R, Beckhoff, and Rockwell have shipping products, but interoperability across brands is still patchy, and few brownfield sites can justify a rip-and-replace. The realistic near-term deployment pattern is greenfield packaging lines and semiconductor fabs where determinism directly drives throughput. For the plant engineer, the practical implication today is to specify TSN-capable switching in any new backbone refresh even if you're not using TSN features yet — the price delta is shrinking and it preserves optionality. Also watch the OPC Foundation's FLC (Field Level Communications) initiative, which is defining the profiles that will make cross-vendor TSN actually work.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 remains the reference framework everyone is aligning to, and the 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) parts are seeing the most vendor uptake right now. If you are specifying new PLCs or RTUs, ask vendors explicitly for their 62443-4-2 Security Level certification and the associated security capability documentation — not just a marketing claim of compliance. The gap between "designed to" and "certified against" is where most procurement pain hides. NIST CSF 2.0's Govern function continues to reshape how utilities and manufacturers structure OT security programs, particularly the requirement to define cybersecurity roles, responsibilities, and authorities distinctly from IT. For controls engineers this is quietly good news: it formalises the argument that OT needs its own risk register, its own change-control cadence, and its own incident response playbook rather than being absorbed into corporate IT processes. Watch also for continued alignment work between IEC 62443 and the EU's NIS2 and Cyber Resilience Act (CRA). CRA in particular will drive component vendors to bake security-by-design into every product with digital elements sold into the EU from late 2027 — meaning the PLC firmware update process, SBOMs, and vulnerability disclosure practices you interact with are all about to become contractually enforceable rather than best-effort.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa water authority incident in Pennsylvania, where attackers affiliated with the CyberAv3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster pump station. The intrusion was trivial: the PLC was internet-exposed on TCP/20256 with the factory-default password (1111) still in place. The attackers defaced the HMI with a political message but did not manipulate the process — a near-miss rather than a physical incident. What made it consequential was the pattern. CISA and the FBI subsequently identified dozens of similarly exposed Unitronics devices across US water utilities, plus food and beverage plants. The root causes were the same everywhere: direct internet exposure to enable vendor remote support, default credentials never changed at commissioning, and no network monitoring to detect the intrusion — the utility only noticed because the HMI screen changed. The lessons for controls engineers are unglamorous but enduring. First, no PLC should be directly reachable from the internet, full stop — vendor remote access belongs behind a jump host with MFA. Second, commissioning checklists must include a mandatory credential change and a documented handover of the new credentials to the asset owner. Third, if you cannot see your Level 1 devices on the network, you cannot defend them; even passive monitoring at the OT/IT boundary would have caught the initial connection attempts.Read more →

Rapid7's analysis of the AI-driven vulnerability disclosure surge argues that traditional patch cycles have collapsed under the volume — a warning that lands hard for plant-floor teams who already struggle with quarterly outage windows. CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added a large batch to the KEV catalog with direct OT relevance. The Broadcom VMware vCenter path traversal (CVE-2026-59310) is the standout — many OT environments run virtualized historians, engineering workstations, and SCADA servers on vSphere, and vCenter compromise typically means full hypervisor-tier control. Pair that with the Cisco ASA/FTD heap inspection flaw (CVE-2026-20349) causing unauthenticated reloads and the Cisco FMC hard-coded password (CVE-2026-20316), and any OT DMZ built on Cisco firewalls needs urgent review of exposure and management-plane segmentation. Microsoft's IKE Service Extensions double-free (CVE-2026-33824) enables remote code execution against Windows IPsec endpoints — relevant for any site-to-site VPN terminating on Windows servers between corporate and plant networks. SharePoint sees two entries (weak-auth bypass CVE-2026-55040 and deserialization CVE-2026-50522); SharePoint frequently hosts operating procedures, P&IDs, and shift logs, so treat it as sensitive OT-adjacent infrastructure. Progress LoadMaster (CVE-2026-8037) and Fortinet FortiOS (CVE-2025-68686) round out the perimeter picture. Practical priorities: patch or isolate vCenter and Cisco ASA/FMC first, verify no management interfaces are exposed beyond jump hosts, confirm your Windows IPsec tunnels are patched or replaced with appliance-based VPN, and audit any Mac used for engineering screen-sharing. Where patching in an OT window is impossible before the next outage, document the compensating controls — ACL restrictions, disabled services, and monitoring — as an interim risk acceptance.Read more →
