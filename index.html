Today's Knowledge Bite

The MacGregor VDR advisory is a useful reminder that Voyage Data Recorders — the maritime equivalent of an aircraft black box — are networked Linux devices, not sealed hardware. Modern VDRs record bridge audio, radar, AIS, engine telemetry and rudder position to ruggedised capsules, but they expose admin interfaces over Ethernet and have been found with default credentials more than once. A compromised VDR can be used to falsify or erase incident evidence before insurers or investigators arrive.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term has its mathematical origin not in process control but in 18th-century ship steering. Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term controller in 1922 while analysing how helmsmen on the USS New Mexico kept course — he noticed they instinctively applied rudder proportional to heading error, plus extra correction for sustained drift (integral), plus anticipation of swing (derivative). Every loop you tune today is, in effect, a mathematical model of a sailor's hands on a wheel a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on Module Type Package (MTP), the VDI/NAMUR/ZVEI standard (IEC 63280 series) for modular process automation. MTP defines a vendor-neutral package describing a process module's services, HMI, alarms, and communication interfaces — essentially a 'driver' that a Process Orchestration Layer (POL) can ingest to integrate a skid in hours rather than months. Think of it as plug-and-produce for chemical, pharma and food modules: a skid vendor ships their unit with an MTP file, you drop it into your orchestration system, and the recipes, faceplates and OPC UA tags appear ready to run. For controls engineers, the practical implication is a shift away from custom integration code each time a skid arrives. Recipe management, alarm aggregation and operator faceplates become declarative rather than hand-built. Adoption is accelerating in pharma (where modular bioprocessing is booming) and specialty chemicals, with Siemens, ABB, Emerson and Yokogawa all shipping MTP-capable POLs. The current challenges are practical: MTP currently focuses on process orchestration rather than tight safety-system integration, tooling maturity varies between vendors, and many existing brownfield skids lack MTP files, requiring retrofitting. Still, if you procure modular equipment, specifying MTP-conformant delivery in your URS is becoming a sensible default.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the most frequently misapplied part of the family. The standard mandates a zone-and-conduit model with documented target security levels (SL-T) per zone, but many sites stop at drawing zones on a Visio diagram without performing the cybersecurity risk assessment (CSRA) that justifies the SL-T. The advisories this week — particularly the EV charger, building actuator and VDR — are textbook examples of devices that often end up in 'miscellaneous' zones with no defined SL-T, leaving them in the same conduit as critical control assets. NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the most accessible companion to 62443 for North American sites, and its guidance on segmenting IIoT and cloud-connected assets pairs well with 62443-4-2 component requirements. Worth noting: ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) was substantially revised in 2024 and now aligns more closely with ISO 27001's management-system structure, making integrated IT/OT security programs easier to certify. If your organisation is being asked by customers or insurers to demonstrate OT cyber maturity, the new 62443-2-1 is the document to map your program against.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's case study is the 2024 Nidec Corporation ransomware incident, which the Japanese motor giant continued to disclose details about into 2025. Attackers — later attributed to the 8Base group — gained initial access through a compromised VPN credential at a Vietnamese subsidiary, then moved laterally over flat WAN links into corporate file shares containing engineering drawings, supplier contracts, and internal OT network diagrams. Although manufacturing systems were not directly encrypted, the exfiltration of detailed plant network topologies created a long-tail risk: adversaries now hold a roadmap to Nidec's OT environment. The attack succeeded because of three common failures: a subsidiary VPN without MFA, trust relationships that let subsidiary credentials reach group file servers, and OT documentation stored alongside general corporate data without additional controls. There was no Stuxnet-style payload — just a credential, a flat network, and poor data classification. Key lessons for controls engineers: first, OT network diagrams, IP schedules and PLC program backups are crown-jewel data and belong in a separately controlled repository, not the corporate SharePoint. Second, treat every subsidiary and contractor VPN as a potential ingress to your OT; mandate MFA and conditional access. Third, assume your network topology will eventually leak — design defence-in-depth that does not rely on the attacker being ignorant of your architecture.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA released a substantial batch of ICS advisories this week, several of which are directly relevant to industrial and facility environments. Most critical is the XCharge C6 EV charger (CVSS 9.8), which suffers from download of code without integrity check, a stack-based buffer overflow, and insecure resource initialization — collectively allowing remote code execution or admin takeover. For sites operating fleet charging or integrating EV infrastructure into building/site OT networks, this is a near-term patching priority and a reminder that EV chargers are increasingly part of the OT attack surface. Schneider Electric disclosed a vulnerability in EcoStruxure Machine Expert HVAC, the programming software for Modicon M171/M172 controllers used widely in building HVAC systems. The flaw can reveal sensitive information from engineering workstations — a recurring theme where the engineering tool, not the controller itself, becomes the weak link. Engineers running M171/M172 fleets should review Schneider's advisory and tighten access to engineering laptops. ABB's Busch-Welcome 2 Wire Door Opener Actuator advisory similarly affects building automation, with exploitation enabling unauthorized physical building access. On the marine and surveillance side, the Danelec MacGregor Voyage Data Recorder G4e (CVSS 8.3) uses default credentials that grant administrator access — a serious concern for any operator whose vessel VDRs may be remotely accessible. KMW CCTV cameras (CVSS 9.1, unverified password change) and CP Plus 8-channel NVRs (XSS) round out a week reminding us that low-cost video gear remains a soft underbelly of many industrial sites. On the broader IT side, Chrome 148 patched 151 vulnerabilities including critical RCE bugs — relevant because engineering HMIs and historian dashboards increasingly run in browsers on the plant floor. A Gogs zero-day (CVSS 9.4) affects self-hosted Git, which some automation teams use for PLC code versioning. The Akira ransomware kill-chain analysis from SANS is worth reading for anyone defending flat networks with VPN ingress, as Akira continues to pivot from IT into OT.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The headline KEV addition is CVE-2026-0257, an authentication bypass in Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS that allows attackers to establish unauthorized VPN connections. Combined with the earlier CVE-2026-0300 (PAN-OS captive portal RCE as root, added 6 May), this is the second actively exploited PAN-OS flaw in a month. For OT environments using PAN firewalls as the IT/OT boundary or for remote vendor access, patch immediately and audit GlobalProtect logs for unexpected sessions. An unauthorized VPN tunnel into your DMZ is a direct path to engineering networks. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN (CVE-2026-20182) carries a similar authentication-bypass-to-admin pattern, relevant to multi-site operators using SD-WAN to stitch remote facilities together. Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) and Trend Micro Apex One (CVE-2026-34926) also landed in KEV — both are common in OT-adjacent IT estates managing engineering laptops and mobile devices. Finally, the supply-chain compromises of Nx Console and TanStack npm packages (both flagged as ransomware-linked) underscore that developer tooling used by automation integrators is now a credential-harvesting vector; if your SI partners build HMI or edge code with Node tooling, ask them about their package hygiene.Read more →
