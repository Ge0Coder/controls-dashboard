Today's Knowledge Bite

The Danfoss AK-SM controller family began life in the 1990s as a supermarket refrigeration monitoring platform, and many units in service today still run embedded Linux stacks built around libraries that pre-date modern memory protections. That's why a single malformed HTTP request can still produce a clean stack smash in 2026 — the attack surface hasn't moved much, but the exposure has, because retailers connected them to the internet for remote energy monitoring.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively reverse-engineered from helmsmen. In the 1920s, Nicholas Minorsky watched how experienced sailors steered the USS New Mexico and noticed they corrected not just for current heading error (proportional) but also for how long the ship had been off-course (integral) and how fast the error was changing (derivative). His 1922 paper formalising this became the mathematical backbone of nearly every process loop running in your plant today — meaning the algorithm in your DCS was, in a very real sense, trained on the intuition of early-20th-century naval helmsmen.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from whitepaper to production. The promise is compelling: a single deterministic Ethernet fabric carrying motion control traffic, process I/O, HMI updates and even video — replacing the current stack of Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, EtherNet/IP and parallel office networks. TSN provides the time synchronisation (IEEE 802.1AS), traffic scheduling (802.1Qbv) and frame preemption (802.1Qbu) that give OPC UA PubSub the determinism it historically lacked. In practice, adoption is gated by three things. First, silicon: TSN-capable switch ASICs and endpoint NICs are available but still carry a premium, and mixed-vendor interoperability testing is immature. Second, engineering tooling — configuring a TSN schedule across a plant is not plug-and-play; it requires a centralised network configuration (CNC) model that few integrators have built muscle memory around. Third, brownfield reality: most plants cannot rip out their existing fieldbus islands, so TSN tends to appear first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor fabs where the ROI from converged networking is clearest. For controls engineers, the near-term implication is that your next motion application may well arrive as a TSN project, and understanding gPTP time distribution is becoming as fundamental as understanding RPI used to be.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-4-2, the component-level security requirements standard, is seeing increased uptake in procurement language this year as European NIS2 enforcement bites. Expect tender documents to start naming specific Security Levels (SL-C) that controllers and HMIs must meet, rather than the vague "shall be secure" phrasing of five years ago. The Danfoss advisories this week illustrate exactly why: a stack buffer overflow in a web interface would fail SL-C 1 requirements for malformed input handling under FR3 (System Integrity). ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) deserves a re-read in light of the Elecbrakes advisory. The standard's zone and conduit methodology was designed for plant networks but applies equally to safety-critical IoT: the brake controller, trailer CAN bus and cloud telemetry should each be separate zones with explicit conduits, and the risk assessment should explicitly consider safety consequence — something the Elecbrakes architecture evidently did not. NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3 remains the practical US reference and continues to track 62443 structurally. Worth noting: the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 "Govern" function is increasingly being audited against in US critical infrastructure, and it demands documented ownership of OT risk — a gap in many plants where IT, engineering and operations each assume someone else owns the controller patch cycle.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's spotlight is the recent wave of maritime tanker cyberattacks referenced in the news feed, which follow a pattern worth dissecting. Multiple tanker operators have reported disruption to navigation, cargo management and ballast control systems over the past months, with initial access frequently traced to the vessel's satellite-connected IT network bridging into OT systems that control cargo pumps and valve actuators. In at least one reported case, attackers reached the cargo management system via a flat network shared with the crew Wi-Fi. The mechanics are depressingly familiar to anyone who's worked a plant incident: an unsegmented network, a shared Windows jump host used by both IT and engineering, and remote support VPNs that bypass the DMZ entirely. The marine environment adds a wrinkle — satellite links make patching painful, so vessels routinely run OT systems years behind land-based equivalents. Three takeaways for shore-based controls engineers. First, verify your "air gap" with a packet capture, not a network diagram; nine times out of ten there's a forgotten conduit. Second, treat vendor remote-support tools as first-class assets requiring MFA, logging and session recording — not as invisible plumbing. Third, map your safety-critical control loops and ask what happens if the HMI lies to the operator; if the answer is "the operator trusts it," that's your next engineering control to design.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a heavy slate of ICS advisories this week with direct plant-floor relevance. Most urgent is Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA series refrigeration system controllers, where a stack-based buffer overflow (CVE-2026-129713, CVSS 9.8) can give an attacker arbitrary code execution with webserver privileges. CISA simultaneously added three older Danfoss AK-SM 800A flaws (CVE-2023-25914/25915 and CVE-2025-41450) to the KEV catalogue, confirming active exploitation in the wild against the predecessor platform. Supermarket, cold-storage and food-processing operators running these controllers should treat this as a now-now patching exercise and segregate the management web interfaces immediately. Schneider Electric EcoStruxure Power Build Rapsody has a memory corruption flaw triggered by opening malicious project files — the same client-side pattern seen in the AutomationDirect C-More EA9 Programming Software advisory (CVE-2026-127280). Both are reminders that engineering workstations remain the softest target in OT: phishing a panel-builder or integrator with a crafted .pbr or .eap file is often easier than attacking the device itself. Autonomy Logic OpenPLC Runtime (CVE-2026-126088) allows creation of unauthenticated admin accounts via a race condition — relevant to the growing hobbyist and education footprint, but also to small pilot deployments. On the IT-adjacent front, HPE/Juniper Session Smart Conductor and Session Smart Router are under active attack via CVE-2026-103406, an unauthenticated OS command injection as root in the salt-minion ID handling. These SD-WAN appliances increasingly sit at remote site boundaries connecting OT networks back to corporate; a compromised Conductor is effectively a pivot into every spoke. Broadcom VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890) auth bypass is also now KEV-listed, a concern for the many OT virtualisation stacks that still rely on vCenter for HMI and historian hosting. In broader news, SAP confirmed a GitHub token theft with alleged source code exfiltration, and the EU adopted a Quantum Act framework. Elecbrakes trailer brake controllers received a chilling advisory — attackers can disable brake lights or modify braking behaviour remotely, a sobering example of safety-critical IoT without adequate authentication.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalogue grew substantially this week with four Danfoss AK-SM 800A entries dating back to 2023, now flagged as actively exploited. This is a classic OT pattern: vulnerabilities disclosed years ago in building/refrigeration management systems that remained internet-exposed and unpatched are finally being weaponised at scale. If you operate retail cold chains, pharmaceutical storage, or food production using Danfoss controllers, inventory now — the AK-SM product line is often forgotten under facilities rather than controls engineering. Mitel MiCollab added six KEV entries in one go (CVE-2026-133303/133311/133323/133324/133275/133469) covering XML injection, OS command injection, file upload and auth bypass in the AWV conferencing component. While not strictly OT, MiCollab commonly resides on the same enterprise segments that house jump hosts into plant networks. The Juniper Session Smart (CVE-2026-103406) and VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890) additions are the two most consequential for OT architectures: both sit at the IT/OT boundary in many sites. Practically, controls engineers should pull their asset inventory against this week's KEV additions, prioritise anything touching the industrial DMZ or Purdue Level 3/3.5, and verify that engineering project files from suppliers are opened only on hardened, network-segmented workstations — the C-More and Schneider Rapsody advisories make that control non-optional.Read more →
