Today's Knowledge Bite

The KACO inverter advisory is a textbook example of a deeper pattern: many industrial devices derive default or recovery credentials from serial numbers using deterministic algorithms. Because serial numbers are often printed on outward-facing labels, scraped from procurement databases, or even broadcast in network discovery responses, this effectively turns the asset tag into a master key. Several PLC and gateway product lines have had near-identical findings over the past decade.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electricity in industrial use: Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilot, demonstrated on the USS New Mexico in 1922, implemented proportional, integral, and derivative action mechanically using gears, weights, and dashpots. Nicholas Minorsky's theoretical analysis the same year — based on watching helmsmen anticipate the ship's swing — gave us the mathematical framework we still teach today. The 'derivative' term wasn't invented by an engineer staring at equations; it was reverse-engineered from how experienced sailors actually steered. Every time you tune a loop, you're standing on the shoulders of a helmsman.

Tech Spotlight

Today's spotlight is on Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) carrying OPC UA PubSub — increasingly visible in greenfield motion and process designs. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 extensions that turn standard Ethernet into a deterministic fabric capable of bounded latency and zero packet loss for scheduled traffic, while still allowing best-effort IT traffic on the same wire. Paired with OPC UA PubSub, it lets controllers, drives, and I/O exchange data at sub-millisecond cycle times using an open, vendor-neutral protocol instead of proprietary fieldbuses like PROFINET IRT or EtherCAT. The practical implication is significant: one cable plant, one protocol stack, from sensor to MES. You can co-mingle a servo update loop and a video stream on the same switch without the motion loop missing its deadline. Several major automation vendors (Siemens, Rockwell, B&R, Beckhoff) now ship TSN-capable controllers and switches, and the OPC Foundation's Field Level Communications initiative is the umbrella effort driving interoperability profiles. Adoption challenges remain real. TSN configuration is complex — you need a centralized network configurator (CNC) that understands every talker, listener, and stream schedule, and tooling maturity varies wildly between vendors. Mixed-vendor TSN networks still hit interop edge cases, particularly around the gPTP time sync and stream reservation protocols. For most plants, the pragmatic path is to pilot TSN on a single machine or skid where determinism matters and OPC UA semantics simplify integration, rather than ripping out a working PROFINET backbone.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical requirements for components) continue to be the centre of gravity for procurement language. A useful reminder triggered by this week's KACO inverter advisory: 62443-4-2 explicitly requires that authenticators not be derivable from publicly available device information — the kind of serial-number-derived credential scheme being patched in those inverters is a direct CR 1.5 (Authenticator management) failure. When you write tender documents, citing the specific component requirement gives vendors a clear, testable bar rather than vague 'must be secure' language. On the NIST side, CSF 2.0's Govern function continues to reshape OT security programmes by elevating policy, roles, and supply-chain risk to a peer of Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond/Recover. The TeamPCP supply-chain story this week is exactly the scenario the Govern function was designed to surface — who owns risk acceptance when an engineering workstation pulls a compromised vendor extension? If your OT security programme still maps only to CSF 1.1, it's worth a refresh. Finally, ISA/IEC 62443-2-4 (service provider requirements) remains underused. With remote-access VPN appliances headlining KEV again this week, contractual obligations on integrators and OEMs around patching cadence, MFA, and incident notification are arguably more impactful than any single control on your own network.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's case study revisits the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, which remains highly instructive. Attackers attributed to the CyberAv3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station, defaced its HMI with a political message, and forced the station offline. Operators reverted to manual control and there was no impact to water quality, but the optics — a foreign-affiliated actor reaching into US municipal water — drove a wave of federal advisories. The root cause was banal: the PLC was reachable from the internet on its default port (TCP 20256) with the default password '1111'. No zero-day, no sophisticated malware — just an exposed device with vendor defaults intact. The attacker simply scanned, connected, and wrote to the HMI. Lessons for controls engineers: first, treat any PLC, RTU, or HMI with a public IP as compromised until proven otherwise — Shodan and Censys make this trivial to audit yourself. Second, vendor default credentials must be changed during commissioning and verified in FAT/SAT checklists; this is a 62443-4-2 CR 1.5 issue you can fix today. Third, ensure your operations team has rehearsed manual fallback — Aliquippa's quick reversion to manual control is the only reason this was a defacement story and not a service-disruption story.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's CISA advisory bundle is heavy on industrial gear that controls engineers actually touch. Schneider Electric has issued two notable advisories: a RADIUS protocol forgery weakness affecting Modicon Network Managed Switches (the Blast-RADIUS family of issues continues to ripple through OT switching), and an authentication flaw in the EcoStruxure Panel Server gateway that could permit unauthorized access to the edge/cloud connection path. Siemens, on behalf of KACO new energy, has disclosed multiple vulnerabilities in KACO blueplanet solar inverters where device credentials can be derived directly from the serial number — a particularly nasty design flaw for fleet-deployed renewables assets. On the IoT and physical-security edge, CISA published advisories on Brickcom IP cameras (unauthenticated remote takeover and live feed access), the Naxclow IoT platform covering smart doorbells and V720/ix cams (credential harvesting, device impersonation, MQTT abuse), and the Yarbo robot fleet (hard-coded credentials in mobile apps and cloud MQTT allowing operational commands to physical robots). While these are largely consumer/light-commercial, controls engineers should remember that exactly these classes of devices routinely show up on corporate Wi-Fi adjacent to OT networks, or are pulled into facilities/security integrations. In the wider IT-with-OT-impact column, Ivanti Sentry is under active exploitation (CVE-2026-10520, root RCE via OS command injection) — Sentry frequently sits at the boundary between corporate mobility and operational systems, so this one warrants priority patching. Microsoft's June Patch Tuesday landed 204 CVEs (38 critical, three previously disclosed), and Google confirmed in-the-wild exploitation of an Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day (CVE-2026-35273) by ShinyHunters — relevant where PeopleSoft is bridged to MES, EAM, or workforce-management systems on the plant side. Finally, the SANS ISC continues tracking the TeamPCP supply-chain campaign, now formally acknowledged by US government bodies. Supply-chain compromise of security scanners and developer tools is a growing risk vector for engineering workstations that pull libraries, vendor add-ons, or PLC tooling extensions from public registries.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog added several entries this week that matter to OT. CVE-2026-10520 (Ivanti Sentry) is the standout: unauthenticated root RCE, now being hit on honeypots — patch or isolate immediately if Sentry is in your environment, and review any trust relationships it has into OT or mobility-managed HMIs. CVE-2026-50751 (Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 auth bypass) is flagged as ransomware-linked and allows VPN establishment without credentials — a direct route into remote-access concentrators that often front engineering vendors. CVE-2026-0257 (Palo Alto PAN-OS) is a similar VPN auth-bypass theme; together these reinforce that perimeter VPN appliances remain the soft underbelly of OT remote access. For infrastructure-class issues, CVE-2026-7473 in Arista EOS (improper tunneled-packet decapsulation) is worth attention if Arista underpins your plant or DMZ networking, and CVE-2026-20245 in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager allows local-authenticated root command execution. Two older entries also landed: CVE-2022-0492 (Linux cgroups v1 privilege escalation) is a reminder to audit container hosts running historian or analytics workloads, and CVE-2008-4250 (MS08-067) re-listed seventeen years on — a quiet indictment of how much unpatched legacy Windows still sits on plant floors. Practical priorities: patch Ivanti Sentry today, validate IKEv1 is disabled or patched on Check Point and any other IPsec terminators, confirm PAN-OS GlobalProtect is current, and run a sweep for any internet-exposed Windows boxes still vulnerable to MS08-067 — they exist, and KEV's re-listing suggests active opportunistic abuse.Read more →
