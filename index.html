Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Modbus protocol, introduced by Modicon in 1979, was designed assuming the serial line itself was the only security boundary — there is no authentication, no encryption, and no concept of a session. When Modbus/TCP was published in 1999, the spec explicitly preserved this trust model for backwards compatibility, which is why almost every modern Modbus device on an Ethernet network is one routable packet away from being commanded by anyone who can reach it. Modbus is now older than most engineers using it, and its security model is older than the public internet.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics: Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico, noticing that good helmsmen instinctively applied corrections proportional to error, integral of past error, and rate of change — exactly the algorithm we still tune today. The first hardware implementations were pneumatic, using bellows and orifices to perform integration and differentiation with compressed air. Modern digital PID loops in your DCS are doing the same maths an experienced sailor's nervous system was doing a century ago, just at 100 ms scan rates instead of human reaction time.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is moving from pilot demos into real deployments, and it deserves a fresh look. The combination promises something controls engineers have wanted for decades: a single, deterministic Ethernet network that carries hard-real-time motion data, soft-real-time process I/O, and best-effort enterprise traffic without separate physical networks or proprietary fieldbuses. TSN provides the bounded-latency scheduling at the switch level; OPC UA PubSub provides the semantic, secure, vendor-neutral data model on top. For a controls engineer, the practical implication is that you may eventually replace EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, and Sercos with a converged stack — but 'eventually' is doing heavy lifting. Adoption challenges are real: TSN requires switches that support IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduling and proper configuration of gate control lists, which is a level of network engineering most automation vendors aren't yet shipping turnkey. Tool support for designing schedules is immature, multi-vendor interoperability still surprises in field trials, and the security story around PubSub key management at scale is not solved. Expect TSN to land first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines where the motion-plus-vision-plus-MES convergence justifies the engineering effort.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often misapplied in practice. The standard requires you to partition the system under consideration into zones and conduits, perform an initial cyber risk assessment, and then assign a target security level (SL-T) to each zone — but many organisations stop at drawing a network diagram and calling it segmentation. The standard explicitly expects the SL-T to be driven by consequence analysis, not by what your firewall happens to support. Worth noting that ISA/IEC 62443-2-1, the operational security programme requirements standard, was substantially revised in 2024 to align with the rest of the family and now maps far more cleanly onto NIST CSF 2.0 functions. If your site is being asked to demonstrate compliance with both an NIST-flavoured corporate framework and an IEC 62443 contractual requirement (increasingly common in OEM specifications), the new 2-1 makes that crosswalk much less painful. The companion IEC 62443-3-3 system security requirements remain stable and continue to be the document auditors actually read line by line — keep a copy on your desk.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment intrusion, because new analysis published over the past year has reframed what we thought happened. The original story — a remote attacker raised sodium hydroxide setpoints from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm via TeamViewer before an operator noticed and reverted — became the canonical example of remote-access risk in water utilities. Subsequent investigation suggests the 'intrusion' may have been a misattributed operator action or a contractor session, but the technical exposure was unquestionably real: a shared TeamViewer password, no MFA, flat network, and the HMI directly reachable from the internet-facing remote support PC. The lessons hold regardless of attribution. First, remote access tools deployed during COVID-era 'just get it working' periods are still in production at thousands of sites and rarely get the same scrutiny as engineered solutions — audit yours. Second, dose-rate and setpoint changes should have engineered limits enforced at the controller, not just at the HMI; a PLC that refuses a physically implausible setpoint is the last line of defence regardless of who is on the keyboard. Third, operator vigilance saved the day — keep your HMI trends visible, your alarms meaningful, and your operators empowered to question a value that 'looks wrong'.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA released a notable batch of ICS advisories today touching several product categories controls engineers will care about. Schneider Electric's EcoStruxure Machine Expert HVAC — the programming environment for Modicon M171/M172 logic controllers — has a vulnerability that could expose sensitive information including credentials. If you have building HVAC controllers programmed with this toolchain, prioritise patching engineering workstations and rotating any credentials that may have been cached. Separately, ABB's Busch-Welcome 2-Wire Door Opener Actuator advisory is a reminder that physical access control devices are increasingly part of the OT attack surface, with successful exploitation granting unauthorised building entry. Two high-severity advisories stand out at the edge of OT/IoT. XCharge C6 EV chargers carry a CVSS 9.8 chain (unsigned firmware download, stack buffer overflow, weak initialisation) allowing admin takeover or code execution — relevant to anyone integrating EV charging into industrial campuses or fleet depots. The MacGregor Voyage Data Recorder G4e (Danelec) below v5.250 ships with default credentials granting administrator access, a serious issue for maritime operators and any shore-side integration. KMW IP cameras and CP Plus NVRs round out a strong week of video-surveillance vulnerabilities — common but often overlooked components on plant networks. On the broader IT side, SecurityWeek is highlighting an HTTP/2 'compression bomb' attack that can knock out web servers in seconds using default configurations — worth checking on any HMI or historian web frontends exposed beyond the cell zone. SANS ISC continues to track a phishing surge using SVG file attachments to bypass email gateways; brief your engineering staff, since SVGs look harmless but can carry embedded scripts and links. Microsoft also walked back legal threats against zero-day researchers after community backlash, a useful signal that responsible disclosure norms are holding.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The standout KEV addition is CVE-2022-0492, a Linux kernel cgroups v1 improper-authentication flaw now confirmed exploited in the wild. This matters in OT because countless industrial gateways, edge compute nodes, historian appliances, and containerised SCADA components run on Linux kernels where cgroups v1 is still default. The bug enables container escape and privilege escalation — meaning a compromised application container on an edge device could pivot to the host. Inventory your Linux-based field devices, check kernel versions, and prioritise patching anywhere container workloads run near process control. Also significant for OT: CVE-2026-0257 in Palo Alto PAN-OS allows authentication bypass to establish unauthorised VPN sessions — directly relevant if you use Palo Alto for IT/OT segmentation or remote engineering access. CVE-2026-20182 in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN provides unauthenticated admin takeover, a concern for multi-site manufacturers using SD-WAN to interconnect plants. The Oracle WebLogic T3/IIOP flaw (CVE-2024-21182) and Drupal SQLi (CVE-2026-9082) round out a week heavy on infrastructure perimeter risks.
