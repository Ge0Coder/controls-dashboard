Today's Knowledge Bite

The IEC 61850 MMS protocol referenced in today's ABB advisory is actually built on top of ISO 9506, a manufacturing messaging standard from the late 1980s originally developed for General Motors' MAP (Manufacturing Automation Protocol) initiative. MAP largely failed commercially, but its messaging layer survived — meaning every modern digital substation in the world is still speaking a dialect of a 1980s GM factory protocol.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term has a strange historical origin: it was first implemented mechanically in 1922 by Nicolas Minorsky, who was trying to stabilise the steering of the USS New Mexico. He derived the three-term control law not from mathematics first, but by carefully observing how experienced helmsmen corrected the ship's heading — they applied immediate rudder for current error, anticipated based on rate of swing, and added persistent bias when the ship stubbornly refused to come about. Every PID loop you tune today is, in essence, a formalised model of an early-20th-century sailor's intuition.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet is finally moving from trade-show demos into real plant deployments, and OPC UA over TSN is the combination most likely to land on your network in the next two years. The promise is concrete: deterministic, sub-millisecond delivery of control traffic on the same physical Ethernet that carries your historian, video, and engineering traffic — eliminating the need for parallel Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, or Sercos networks for new lines. The practical mechanism is IEEE 802.1Qbv time-aware shaping, which carves the Ethernet frame schedule into protected windows for cyclic control traffic, with PTP (IEEE 1588) keeping every switch and endpoint synchronised to within nanoseconds. OPC UA Pub/Sub then rides those guaranteed slots, giving you a single semantic model from sensor to MES. Adoption challenges are real. Most installed managed switches don't support Qbv, so it's a forklift upgrade. Engineering tools for configuring TSN schedules are still vendor-specific despite the standards, and troubleshooting a misconfigured schedule is genuinely harder than diagnosing a Profinet update rate. For greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines it makes sense today; for brownfield process plants, plan it as a 5–10 year migration aligned with switch refresh cycles.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) continue to be the standards most directly cited in vendor advisories like today's ABB batch — the SL-C ratings ABB references in their security notifications come straight out of 4-2. If you haven't already, it's worth pulling the latest 4-2 (2024 edition incorporating amendments) to align your component procurement specs; several large EPCs are now requiring SL 2 minimum on new tenders for safety-adjacent equipment. On the US side, CISA's new zero-trust guidance for OT, referenced in this week's news roundup, is notable because it explicitly acknowledges that classical ZT models built around per-request authentication don't fit deterministic control traffic. Instead it leans on segmentation, asset identity, and continuous monitoring — much closer to 62443's zone-and-conduit model than to a pure NIST SP 800-207 implementation. Expect this to shape how auditors interpret 'zero trust' requirements in the next round of NERC CIP and TSA pipeline directives. Also worth tracking: ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) is in active revision, with the draft expanding expectations around SBOM consumption and vulnerability handling. If your organisation is building out a vulnerability management process for OT, designing it against the draft now will save rework later.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, which remains one of the cleanest public examples of a low-sophistication attacker causing real OT impact. The CyberAv3ngers group, linked to Iran's IRGC, compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC at a booster station by simply connecting to it over the internet on TCP/20256 using the default password '1111'. They defaced the HMI screen with a political message and forced the station offline; operators reverted to manual control to maintain pressure. The how is depressingly familiar: the PLC was directly exposed to the internet by the integrator to enable remote support, the default password was never changed, and there was no network monitoring that would have flagged the inbound connection. The same campaign hit dozens of similar facilities globally over the following weeks because Unitronics PLCs were trivially Shodan-discoverable. Three lessons stand out. First, no PLC should ever have a public IP — full stop; if remote support is needed, put it behind a VPN with MFA and jump host. Second, default credentials on field devices are still the single highest-yield attack vector; commission processes must include a credential rotation step with evidence. Third, basic egress and ingress logging at the site router would have detected this in minutes — even cheap monitoring beats none.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's biggest signal for plant floor engineers is a coordinated wave of CISA advisories covering the ABB portfolio. ABB Ability OPTIMAX 6.1–6.3 carries an authentication bypass affecting Azure AD SSO integrations, meaning federated logins can be spoofed before the operator session ever lands. Separately, ABB AWIN GW100 gateways allow unauthenticated reboot and configuration disclosure — a direct availability risk for any building or process network using these as data concentrators. Edgenius Management Portal 3.2.x and PCM600 (1.5 through 2.13) both have remote code execution paths via crafted messages to the system node, with PCM600 in particular widely deployed for protection relay configuration in substations. The Symphony Plus Engineering advisory is essentially a downstream PostgreSQL 13.11-and-earlier issue: an attacker on the S+ Client/Server network could execute arbitrary code via the embedded database. The 800xA / Symphony Plus IEC 61850 advisory targets the MMS client stack used by automation controllers — GOOSE is not affected, but MMS is the workhorse for client-server SCADA-to-IED communication, so this matters for substation operators and any utility running 61850-integrated DCS. Patches and mitigations are available; the practical issue is many of these systems sit on isolated networks with long change windows. On the wider security front, two US security professionals (Goldberg and Martin) were sentenced to four years for moonlighting with a ransomware crew — a reminder that insider risk in IR firms is real. CISA also released zero-trust guidance specifically for OT this week, which is worth pulling into your reference architecture discussions. Sophisticated Python-based 'Deep#Door' espionage backdoor and continued abuse of Hugging Face for malware distribution round out the IT-side threats most likely to land on engineering laptops via supply chain or developer pivots. For controls engineers, the action item is simple: if you run ABB gear, pull the six advisories, map versions, and queue patches before the next outage. For everyone else, treat the OPTIMAX SSO bypass as a template — Azure AD/Entra integrations into HMI and historian platforms are now a recurring weak point worth re-auditing.Read more →

The KEV catalogue added 20 entries in the last two weeks, and while none are ICS-specific, several are highly relevant to OT pivots. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) and SimpleHelp (CVE-2024-57726/57728) are both remote support tools commonly whitelisted into engineering and vendor jump hosts — path traversal and privilege escalation in either gives attackers a direct route from IT support infrastructure into the DMZ. If your integrators or OEM remote-support contracts rely on these, verify patch status this week and consider rotating any API keys generated before the fix. The Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager trio (CVE-2026-20122/20128/20133) matters anywhere SD-WAN is used to back-haul site traffic from remote facilities — an increasingly common pattern for water, oil and gas, and distributed manufacturing.
