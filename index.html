Rockwell is your priority today: six CISA advisories dropped covering CompactLogix and Logix 5370/5570 (CIP-borne DoS that triggers an MNRF), FLEX I/O EtherNet/IP adapters, RSLinx Classic and FactoryTalk Analytics PavilionX. The Logix CIP DoS (CVE-2026-11317) is the one to act on first — it is exploitable from anywhere on the controller's Layer 2 segment and recovery from a Major Non-Recoverable Fault is a physical-presence, controller-download event. Today's Knowledge Bite

The 'Major Non-Recoverable Fault' on a Logix controller isn't just a soft reset — it clears the controller's program memory and forces an OS-level reload from non-volatile memory or a download. On a CompactLogix without an SD card configured for autoload, a single malformed CIP packet exploiting CVE-2026-11317 can mean a multi-hour recovery requiring physical access and the original .ACD file. Many sites discover during the incident that their last verified controller backup is older than they thought.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass autopilots for US Navy ships in 1911 already implemented proportional, integral and derivative action mechanically, using flywheels, dashpots and bellows. Nicholas Minorsky's 1922 paper formalising the mathematics came from analysing why Sperry's autopilots worked so well, not from designing them in the first place. In other words, the theory we still teach as the foundation of process control was reverse-engineered from a working product — a reminder that in controls, practice has occasionally led theory by more than a decade.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to inch from demo booths into real deployments. The promise is straightforward: a single deterministic Ethernet fabric carrying both hard-real-time motion traffic (formerly the domain of EtherCAT, Profinet IRT or Sercos) and higher-level OPC UA PubSub data, all sharing the same switches and cabling. IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic, 802.1AS time synchronization and 802.1CB frame replication form the core, with OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) defining the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device profiles on top. For controls engineers the practical implications are significant. You get a converged network where a vision system, a servo drive and a SCADA historian coexist without VLAN gymnastics, and where cycle times under 100 microseconds are achievable end-to-end. But the engineering effort shifts: you now need to plan the TSN schedule (gate control lists) at design time, and any topology change means recomputing it. Network configuration becomes part of the control system design, not an IT afterthought. Adoption is still gated by switch availability — managed TSN switches from Cisco, Hirschmann, Moxa and a few others exist but remain expensive — and by the fact that most field devices still ship with classical Profinet or EtherNet/IP only. The realistic horizon for greenfield brownfield-replacement projects is 2027-2028 for serious uptake; for now, expect TSN islands inside larger conventional networks.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-2-1:2024 (Security program requirements for IACS asset owners) is now the version auditors and insurers are increasingly citing, replacing the 2010 edition. The headline change is the shift from a prescriptive control list to a risk-based 'security program element' (SPE) structure aligned with the 62443-3-3 system requirements and 62443-4-2 component requirements. For asset owners this means your security program documentation needs to map activities to SPEs rather than the old foundational requirements, and evidence of continuous improvement is now explicit rather than implied. For controls engineers the practical effect is that procurement specifications can — and increasingly should — reference 62443-4-2 SL-C (component security level capability) for new PLCs, drives and HMIs. Several major vendors (Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, Phoenix Contact) now publish certified SL-C ratings for specific product lines, which makes apples-to-apples comparison possible for the first time. If you're writing a specification this quarter, asking for 'SL-C 2 minimum, SL-C 3 preferred for safety-related functions' is now a defensible, vendor-neutral requirement. NIST is also worth watching: SP 800-82 Rev 3 has been the OT reference for a couple of years now, and CISA's cross-sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) continue to be updated as the de facto baseline for US critical infrastructure. The CPGs are deliberately less prescriptive than 62443 but are what regulators will point to first.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful one to revisit this week is the 2024 Schneider Electric Modicon M340 incident at a European water utility, which became public in detail only in mid-2025 after the post-incident report was shared with the WaterISAC. An attacker reached the controller network through a contractor's VPN that had been left active after commissioning, then used a legitimate engineering workstation with Unity Pro installed to push a modified program to two M340s controlling chemical dosing. The modification was subtle: it changed a single setpoint scaling factor in a derived function block, so chlorine dosing readings displayed correctly on the HMI but actual dosing ran at roughly 60% of intended. The problem was caught not by the SCADA system but by a routine grab-sample lab test 11 days later. Forensics showed the attacker had also disabled the controller's audit log buffer before making changes. Three lessons stand out. First, contractor VPN accounts need automatic expiry tied to project milestones, not indefinite life. Second, controller program integrity needs an out-of-band check — a periodic CRC or hash comparison against a known-good baseline catches subtle logic tampering that HMI displays cannot. Third, process measurements should be cross-validated against independent indicators (lab samples, flow-versus-tank-level mass balance) at a cadence matched to safety consequence, not convenience.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today is dominated by Rockwell Automation. CISA published six advisories covering CompactLogix 5370 L1/L2/L3, Logix 5370 and 5570 controllers (CVE-2026-11317, a CIP-borne denial-of-service that can trigger a Major Non-Recoverable Fault), FLEX I/O 1794-AENTR/AENTRXT EtherNet/IP adapters (CVE-2026-0646/0647, unauthorized access and account takeover), RSLinx Classic (a re-emerged 2020 third-party DoS), and FactoryTalk Analytics PavilionX (CVE-2025-14272, privileged operation execution). The common thread is availability impact on the controller layer — exactly the kind of bug that turns a curious scan into a line-down event. SecurityWeek's wrap-up confirms the breadth of the Rockwell patch cycle across Logix, CompactLogix, Flex, RSLinx and FactoryTalk. Outside Rockwell, CISA also flagged Yarbo's mobile app and cloud MQTT infrastructure for hard-coded credentials and command-injection paths into a robot fleet — a useful reminder that consumer/prosumer robotics increasingly share the same MQTT brokers and cloud patterns as light industrial telemetry. On the enterprise side that touches OT engineering workstations: Microsoft is rushing a patch for the 'RoguePlanet' Defender race-condition zero-day with public PoC code, Chrome and Firefox both shipped critical memory-safety fixes, and Oracle's June CPU dropped 245 patches across Communications, EBS and Enterprise Manager. DragonForce ransomware has been observed abusing Microsoft Teams relay servers for C2 using a new Go backdoor — relevant if your historian or MES hosts share IT identity boundaries with Teams. Practical takeaway for the plant floor: prioritise the Logix CIP DoS (CVE-2026-11317) and the FLEX I/O adapter bugs in your next maintenance window. Both are reachable from any compromised HMI or engineering workstation on the same Layer 2 segment, and an MNRF on a CompactLogix is not a soft failure — it requires a physical visit and a download to recover.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog over the past two weeks leans heavily web/IT, but several entries have direct OT relevance. CVE-2026-50751 (Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 auth bypass, known ransomware use) and CVE-2026-0257 (Palo Alto PAN-OS auth bypass enabling unauthorized VPN connections) both undermine the perimeter most plants rely on for remote vendor access. If either appliance fronts your IDMZ or vendor jump path, treat these as emergency change-control items. CVE-2026-20262 and CVE-2026-20245 add Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (vManage) path traversal and command injection — relevant for multi-site manufacturers using SD-WAN to backhaul plant traffic. CVE-2026-35273 (PeopleSoft PeopleTools missing-auth takeover, confirmed ransomware) and CVE-2024-21182 (Oracle WebLogic via T3/IIOP) matter where ERP integrates with MES via direct database or web service calls. CVE-2026-10520 (Ivanti Sentry unauth RCE as root) is a quiet but serious one for sites using MobileIron-managed engineer tablets. Remediation priority for OT teams: patch or virtually-patch the Check Point and PAN-OS bugs first, audit any SD-WAN Manager exposure, and verify your engineering laptops aren't running vulnerable Ivanti Sentry-managed profiles.
