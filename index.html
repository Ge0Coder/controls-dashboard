Today's Knowledge Bite

The MQTT protocol, now ubiquitous in industrial IoT and used in libraries like the newly-vulnerable libumqtt, was originally designed in 1999 by IBM's Andy Stanford-Clark and Arlen Nipper to monitor an oil pipeline over satellite links where bandwidth was billed by the byte. Its tiny 2-byte fixed header was shaped by that constraint. Twenty-plus years later, that same frugality is why MQTT dominates edge gateways and sensor fleets — and why a single library flaw can ripple across millions of devices.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller that still runs most of your loops traces its mathematical form to Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 analysis of US Navy ship steering, not to any chemical or process plant. Minorsky derived proportional-integral-derivative action by watching how a skilled helmsman anticipated a ship's yaw — the derivative term was literally a mathematical description of a human looking ahead. Ziegler and Nichols didn't publish their famous tuning rules until 1942, meaning engineers used PID successfully for two decades before anyone had a systematic way to tune it. The next time a loop oscillates, remember you're debugging a century-old model of a sailor's intuition.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers appearing inside engineering tools like Siemens Mendix Studio Pro. MCP is the emerging standard — originally pushed by Anthropic — for letting AI assistants interact with external tools, files, and services through a defined server interface. Vendors are rapidly embedding MCP servers into IDEs, PLC programming suites, and historians so that copilots can read tags, propose logic changes, or query alarms in natural language. The practical implication for controls engineers is that your engineering workstation is quietly growing a new network-listening service that speaks to AI agents and, by extension, to whatever browsers or cloud services those agents touch. Today's Mendix advisory is the canary: the MCP Server there has flaws letting attackers interact remotely or trick a browser into unauthorized requests. Expect similar advisories across Rockwell, Siemens TIA, and AVEVA tooling as MCP integrations roll out. Adoption challenges are real. There is no mature authentication or authorization model for MCP in OT contexts, no equivalent of CIP Security for agent-to-tool traffic, and little guidance on segmenting these servers from the control network. Pragmatic advice: treat any MCP endpoint on an engineering workstation like an exposed API — firewall it to localhost where possible, disable when unused, and demand from vendors a clear statement on auth, logging, and attack surface.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-4-2, which specifies component-level security requirements for embedded devices, host devices, network devices, and software applications, is quietly becoming the yardstick by which the OpenPLC-, libumqtt-, and Mendix-class advisories will be judged. The standard's CR 1.1 (human user identification) and CR 1.2 (software process and device identification) requirements map almost directly onto the OpenPLC Runtime flaw that permits unauthenticated admin account creation — a Security Level 1 failure, not a sophisticated attack. If your procurement specs cite 62443-4-2 SL-2 or above, these advisories are evidence to push vendors on independent certification rather than self-attestation. On the US side, NIST's Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (released in early 2024 and now widely adopted in critical-infrastructure risk assessments) continues to drive the inclusion of the new Govern function in OT programs. Expect auditors to ask not just whether you patched Node2Shell, but whether your governance process identified Node.js as a dependency inside Siemens software in the first place — an SBOM question that most asset owners still can't answer. Also worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) is seeing renewed interest as sites formalise zone and conduit models to satisfy NIS2 in the EU and the upcoming CIRCIA reporting rules in the US. If you haven't revisited your zone diagrams since adding MQTT brokers, cloud historians, or MCP-enabled engineering tools, that's an obvious gap.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's instructive case is the recently confirmed cyberattack on a Norwegian energy firm, part of a cluster of incidents SecurityWeek reported targeting Norwegian energy operators. While full technical details remain embargoed, initial reporting indicates the attackers gained a foothold through exposed remote-access infrastructure and moved toward operational systems before being contained. It echoes the pattern seen in prior Nordic energy intrusions: IT-origin access, OT-adjacent impact, and detection via anomalous authentication rather than process-level alarms. What makes it worth a controls engineer's attention is the increasingly blurred line between corporate VPN compromise and OT risk. In several recent Nordic cases, the initial access vector was a remote-access appliance (SSLVPN or similar) — precisely the asset class dominating today's KEV list with SonicWall CVE-2026-19868 and Check Point CVE-2026-123039. Attackers don't need to touch a PLC to disrupt operations; shutting down the engineering workstation fleet or the historian is often enough to trigger safe-state responses that halt production. Three takeaways: first, inventory every remote-access path into your plant, including vendor jump hosts you didn't commission. Second, assume SAML and MFA bypass flaws will keep appearing, so layer conditional access with source-IP allowlists at the firewall. Third, rehearse the "IT is down, can we still run?" scenario — many sites discover during real incidents that their historians, batch systems, and even recipe management are more coupled to IT services than their network diagrams suggest.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a cluster of ICS advisories today with direct relevance to the plant floor. Two Siemens bulletins cover widespread exposure to the Node.js Brotli decoder flaw dubbed Node2Shell (CVE-2026-111497), an out-of-bounds write in bundled Node.js that can lead to memory corruption and RCE. Because Node.js is embedded in a growing swath of Siemens industrial and automation software, expect a long tail of patches across engineering workstations, HMI servers, and cloud connectors. A separate Siemens advisory addresses multiple flaws in Mendix Studio Pro's MCP Server, where network-accessible endpoints and browser-triggered requests could lead to unauthorized data access or code execution on developer systems — a reminder that low-code tooling now carries classic AppSec risk. On the smaller-vendor side, Autonomy Logic's OpenPLC Runtime (<=4.2.4) has a race-condition flaw (CVE-2026-126088, CVSS 8.1) that allows creation of unauthenticated admin accounts. OpenPLC shows up in a lot of pilot labs, universities, and even some production edge deployments, so inventory it and patch. The libumqtt advisory covers an integer underflow (CVE-2026-125132, CVSS 8.6) that can cause DoS, memory corruption, or info disclosure on MQTT clients built on that library — potentially affecting a wide range of IoT and edge gateway firmware. The most eye-catching advisory is Elecbrakes EB2, VM1, and ELBC2000 trailer brake controllers. Exploitation could let an attacker take over trailer brakes, modify firmware, disable brake lights, or disable safety features. This is a vehicle-safety ICS issue that straddles the line into transportation OT and underlines how Bluetooth/app-controlled industrial gear keeps appearing in unexpected places. On the IT-adjacent front, SecurityWeek is reporting Norwegian energy firm cyberattacks and a confirmed AI-assisted attack that forced the European Commission to take services offline. N-able attributed a Mail Assure / SpamExperts outage to a cyberattack with possible credential exposure — relevant if your corporate mail-filtering chain touches OT alerting. SANS ISC notes they considered raising Infocon to Yellow amid a run of "2Shell"-class bugs (php2shell, Java4Shell, NGINX2Shell, now Ruby2Shell CVE-2026-132825 and Node2Shell) but held off; the pattern itself is worth noting for any OT asset inventory containing modern runtimes.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the last two weeks lean IT-heavy but include several items that will touch OT environments. CVE-2026-123039 in Check Point Gaia (Firewall, Security Gateway, Management Server) is a critical-resource permissions flaw — if Check Point sits at your IT/OT boundary, prioritize it. CVE-2026-123158 (OpenSSH on Linux auth bypass) and CVE-2026-19868 (SonicWall SonicOS SSLVPN SAML bypass) are both directly relevant to remote-access paths many sites use for vendor support into Level 3 and the DMZ. The Barracuda cluster (CVE-2026-111560 OS command injection, CVE-2026-116606 ESG auth bypass) hits perimeter mail security that often feeds OT alerting and ticketing. For engineering-workstation and shared-services risk, CVE-2026-124194 (Outlook spoofing with no required interaction) and CVE-2026-125350 (Chromium XML UAF affecting any Chromium-based browser or embedded webview, including many HMI thin clients) are both worth pushing through change control quickly. CVE-2026-115731 in Puppet Enterprise is notable if you use Puppet for config management of Linux-based historians or SCADA servers — any CA-signed agent can achieve RCE on the Puppet Server. Practical remediation posture: inventory your DMZ remote-access stack (SonicWall, Check Point, OpenSSH jump hosts), confirm Chromium-embedded HMI clients are on the current patch line, and treat Puppet/Ansible controllers as crown-jewel assets with tight network segmentation. None of the KEV entries have confirmed ransomware linkage yet, but the SSLVPN and Barracuda bugs historically precede ransomware campaigns by weeks.Read more →
