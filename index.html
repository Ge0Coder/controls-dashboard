Today's Knowledge Bite

GRASSMARLIN, the passive ICS network mapper just flagged with an XXE bug, was originally released by the NSA in 2015 as open-source software — one of the very few times the agency published a defensive tool publicly. It identifies devices purely from passively captured traffic, so it never sends a packet to the PLC, which is why many asset owners still trust it on live process networks where active scanning is forbidden.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively reverse-engineered from the human helmsman. In the 1920s, Nicolas Minorsky watched US Navy quartermasters steer the USS New Mexico and noticed they did not just react to current heading error — they also accounted for how fast the ship was swinging and how long it had been off course. He formalised that intuition mathematically in 1922, and the three-term controller that now runs millions of loops worldwide is essentially a description of what a skilled human did with a wheel a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet continues to mature as the bearer for OPC UA PubSub, and 2026 is the year it is finally showing up in mainstream PLC product lines from Siemens, Rockwell, B&R, and Beckhoff. The promise is a single converged network that carries deterministic motion-control traffic, IO updates, and IT data on the same cable, replacing the patchwork of Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos, and Ethernet/IP CIP Sync that engineers have lived with for two decades. The practical reality is messier. TSN requires every switch in the path to support the relevant IEEE 802.1 sub-standards (Qbv scheduled traffic, Qci ingress policing, AS gPTP time sync), and mixing vendors still exposes interoperability gaps despite Avnu Alliance certification. Many controls engineers are finding that the easiest adoption path is greenfield machine cells with single-vendor TSN switches, while brownfield plants stay on their existing fieldbus. From a security perspective, TSN's reliance on precise time synchronisation introduces gPTP as a new attack surface — spoofed time can break a deterministic schedule as effectively as a denial-of-service flood, and there is no widely deployed authenticated PTP profile yet.Read more →

Standards Watch

ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) remains the standard most often cited in 2026 procurement specs, but the quieter news is the ongoing work on 62443-2-2, which defines the IACS Security Protection Scheme and gives asset owners a structured way to demonstrate operational maturity rather than just design-time compliance. If you have ever struggled to translate a 62443 SL-T target into something an operations team can actually run day to day, 2-2 is the document to track. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 continues to be the reference most US-regulated operators map against, and its alignment with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function has pushed more plants to formalise OT-specific governance separate from corporate IT policy. Expect auditors over the next year to start asking how your OT risk register is reviewed, by whom, and at what cadence — a question that used to be answered with a shrug. Worth flagging: the EU Cyber Resilience Act enforcement window is now closing in, with mandatory vulnerability handling and SBOM provision for products with digital elements taking effect in stages through late 2027. PLC, HMI, and gateway vendors are quietly scrambling, and customers should be asking for SBOMs in RFQs now if they are not already.Read more →

Incident of the Week

In late 2024 a US water utility in Arkansas City, Kansas was forced to switch its treatment plant to fully manual operation after a cyber incident affected its supervisory systems. The utility detected anomalous activity on a Sunday morning, isolated the SCADA environment, and ran the plant on local controls and manual valve operation for several days while incident response proceeded. Water quality was never affected and customers saw no service disruption — a textbook example of why manual fallback procedures still matter. What made it work was not a sophisticated security stack; it was that operators still knew how to run the plant without the HMI. Investigators later attributed initial access to exposed remote-access infrastructure and weak authentication on a vendor management interface, the same theme running through the current ScreenConnect and SimpleHelp KEV additions. Three lessons hold up two years on: first, every critical control loop should have a documented and rehearsed manual mode, and operators should drill it at least annually. Second, vendor remote-access paths are now the most likely intrusion vector into small and mid-sized utilities — inventory them, MFA them, log them, and disable them when not in use. Third, the value of fast detection is measured in how quickly you can disconnect, not in how cleverly you can analyse; a good kill switch beats a great SIEM when the water keeps flowing.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA released a heavy slate of ICS advisories overnight. The most consequential for plant operators is the Carlson Software VASCO-B GNSS Receiver flaw (CVE-2026-3893, CVSS 9.4), where misconfigured access controls let a remote attacker alter critical system functions or disrupt the device — relevant anywhere GNSS time or position is used for survey, autonomous equipment, or precision agriculture. The Intrado 911 Emergency Gateway issue (CVE-2026-6074, CVSS 9.8) affects all supported branches and allows arbitrary file read, modify, or delete; sites with on-prem 911 routing should patch immediately. Multiple Milesight camera models can be crashed or remotely code-executed across five CVEs, a reminder that perimeter video infrastructure is still a soft target. Also noteworthy is an advisory against the NSA's own GRASSMARLIN passive ICS network mapping tool — an XXE flaw that could leak sensitive data from parsed PCAPs or topology files. If your team still runs GRASSMARLIN for asset discovery, treat its output handling as untrusted. The Yadea T5 e-bike weak-authentication advisory is a curiosity but underlines how IoT bolt-ons keep finding their way into industrial fleets and campuses. On the IT side bleeding into OT, SecurityWeek reports a critical RCE in GitHub (CVE-2026-3854) impacting both GitHub.com and Enterprise Server — relevant for anyone storing PLC code, HMI projects, or CI/CD pipelines for OT software in self-hosted GHES. SANS continues to track the TeamPCP supply-chain campaign, with three new concurrent compromises affecting Checkmarx KICS, Bitwarden CLI, and the xinference PyPI package; supply-chain risk to engineering workstations remains the dominant indirect threat to OT this quarter.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this past week skew toward remote management and IT infrastructure that is commonly bridged into OT environments. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) path traversal and two SimpleHelp vulnerabilities (CVE-2024-57728, CVE-2024-57726) were added on 24–28 April; both products are widely used by integrators and OEMs to remotely support customer plants, making them a high-leverage pivot point. If your vendors connect via either platform, demand patch confirmation and review session logs. Three Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager flaws (CVE-2026-20122, 20128, 20133) joined the KEV on 20 April covering file-upload abuse, credential recovery, and information disclosure — directly relevant to multi-site manufacturers using SD-WAN to stitch plants together. Also added: Samsung MagicINFO 9 (digital signage frequently sitting on plant networks), D-Link DIR-823X command injection, and the older but still-exploited PaperCut NG/MF auth bypass with known ransomware association. Practical action: prioritise patching ScreenConnect, SimpleHelp, and Cisco SD-WAN Manager this week; audit any IT/OT boundary device that exposes these to engineering VLANs; and confirm MagicINFO and PaperCut servers are not silently routable from the process network.Read more →
