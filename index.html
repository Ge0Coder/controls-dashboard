Today's Knowledge Bite

The EPICS (Experimental Physics and Industrial Control System) framework underpinning today's advisory runs some of the largest control systems on Earth — including CERN's accelerator complex, ITER, and most major synchrotrons — yet it was originally developed in the late 1980s at Los Alamos and Argonne as a toolkit for a single accelerator project. It uses a publish/subscribe 'Channel Access' protocol that predates OPC and still outperforms many modern alternatives for sub-millisecond determinism across tens of millions of process variables.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The modern PID controller owes its tuning rules to a 1942 paper by John Ziegler and Nathaniel Nichols at Taylor Instrument Companies — but what's less well known is that their work was driven by WWII-era anti-aircraft gun directors, where closing the loop fast enough on a maneuvering bomber was literally a matter of life and death. The same quarter-amplitude-decay heuristic you still see baked into DCS auto-tuners traces its lineage to the servo-mechanisms that aimed Bofors guns over the English Channel. Control theory and ballistics have been quietly entangled ever since Norbert Wiener formalised 'cybernetics' from the same wartime problem.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) continues to inch toward production reality in process plants, with the IEEE 802.1 TSN profile for industrial automation (IEC/IEEE 60802) now near final publication. In plain terms, TSN lets you carry hard-real-time cyclic traffic — think EtherNet/IP CIP Motion or PROFINET IRT — alongside ordinary IT traffic on the same switched Ethernet, by using synchronised clocks (gPTP) and scheduled gates on every switch port. The promise is converged networks: no more parallel control, safety and video LANs. For controls engineers, the practical implication is twofold. First, TSN-capable switches and PLC CPUs are becoming the default on new product lines from Rockwell, Siemens, B&R and Schneider, so greenfield designs should at least specify TSN-ready hardware even if you don't enable scheduling day one. Second, troubleshooting changes: when a flow misses its cycle, the fault may not be a cable or a storm but a misconfigured schedule or a drifted PTP grandmaster. Expect to add gPTP health, CNC (Centralised Network Configuration) tooling, and schedule-aware packet captures to your diagnostic toolkit. Adoption is still being slowed by the shortage of multi-vendor interop testing and by brownfield switches that lack the required hardware queues.Read more →

Standards Watch

ISA/IEC 62443 continues its gradual expansion with the ongoing work on 62443-2-2 (Security Protection Scheme ratings) and refinements to 62443-3-2 on risk assessment and zone/conduit definition. If you haven't revisited your zone-and-conduit model recently, it's worth noting that regulators and insurers are increasingly asking for the artefacts 3-2 prescribes — a documented SuC definition, initial risk assessment, zone diagram, and target security level per zone — not just a general 'we follow 62443' statement. In parallel, NIST SP 800-82r3 (Guide to OT Security) remains the de facto reference in North America and now explicitly maps its controls back to the CSF 2.0 Govern function, which was added last year. For engineers, the most useful change is the new emphasis on 'engineering-informed cyber' — treating safety instrumented systems, process hazard analyses and cyber risk as a single integrated discipline. Also worth watching: the EU Cyber Resilience Act transition period, with product manufacturers facing conformity obligations from late 2027, meaning the PLCs and HMIs you specify in 2026 projects should already have a documented SBOM and vulnerability-handling process.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week is the 2024 Nova Scotia Power / Emera breach disclosed in mid-2025, where attackers maintained access to the utility's IT environment for roughly six weeks before being discovered during the Easter weekend. Although the utility publicly stressed that generation, transmission and distribution OT systems were not directly affected, customer billing, meter data, and sensitive personal information were exfiltrated, and some business operations — including meter reading and bill generation — were disrupted for weeks. The root cause pattern was familiar: IT-side intrusion through corporate access paths, lateral movement, and prolonged dwell time before detection. What makes it instructive for OT is the dependency chain revealed when AMI and billing systems went offline: field crews reverted to manual reads, outage management workflows were strained, and customer trust took a measurable hit even without a single relay misoperation. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, inventory the IT systems your OT operations silently depend on — historian replication targets, AD domain controllers, certificate authorities, ticketing — because an 'IT-only' incident often has OT consequences through these links. Second, rehearse degraded-mode operations for AMI, EMS and DMS without their IT dependencies; tabletop exercises uncover gaps that architecture diagrams hide. Third, assume dwell times measured in weeks, not days, and ensure your OT logging retention and historian baselines are long enough to support retrospective hunting.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's CISA ICS advisory batch is unusually dense and touches a wide swath of plant-floor and substation equipment. Hitachi Energy published two advisories: the CoreTec product line is affected by OS command injection and 'Dirty Frag' flaws impacting confidentiality, integrity and availability, while the TRO600 series wireless gateways — widely deployed in distribution automation and remote telemetry — carry multiple RCE and information-disclosure vulnerabilities. Operators of substation automation and SCADA radio networks should prioritise firmware reviews, especially for TRO600 devices exposed to any form of routable network. Siemens received two advisories as well. The Mendix SAML module has an authentication bypass allowing an unauthenticated attacker to replay a valid IdP-initiated SAML assertion — a serious risk for anyone using Mendix low-code apps as HMI or MES front-ends. Separately, several SIMATIC IPC models shipped with a test signing key still trusted by UEFI, enabling Secure Boot bypass. The IPC issue is particularly relevant because these industrial PCs often host WinCC, PCS 7 engineering stations, or edge gateways where boot integrity is the last line of defence against persistent malware. Mitsubishi Electric and Iconics Digital Solutions issued a sweeping advisory covering GENESIS, GENESIS64, ICONICS Suite, Hyper Historian, AnalytiX, MobileHMI, IoTWorX, MC Works64, GENESIS32 and BizViz. The flaws include arbitrary code execution via the libwebp CVE-2023-4863, two authentication bypasses, and an arbitrary-operation issue — in combination, enough to compromise historians and HMI servers that are typically trusted deep inside the process network. Finally, EPICS Base ≤7.0.10 — the backbone of large physics and accelerator control systems — has four new CVEs ranging from DoS to potential RCE. On the broader IT side, a max-severity GitLab integer overflow (CVE-2026-97229) is being actively exploited against Alpine Linux build infrastructure, and a TeamViewer flaw (CVE-2026-111585) can let a malicious host take over the support technician's machine — directly relevant to vendors who dial into plants for remote support. Treat inbound TeamViewer sessions from unknown endpoints with the same scrutiny as outbound ones.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added two particularly consequential entries to the KEV catalog this week. CVE-2026-76418 affects Cisco Secure Firewall ASA and FTD software, allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to trigger a NULL pointer dereference and force a reload — a reliable DoS primitive against perimeter firewalls that frequently segment IT from OT. If your DMZ or Purdue Level 3.5 relies on ASA/FTD, patch now and verify failover behaviour, because an attacker can crash-loop the device to create a maintenance window of their choosing. CVE-2026-97229 in GitLab CE/EE is an unauthenticated RCE being used in the wild. Many OT shops now run GitLab for PLC code version control, IaC for virtualised SCADA, and CI/CD of HMI configurations — a compromised instance is effectively a supply-chain foothold into every project it hosts. Also noteworthy for OT: Siemens Software Center DLL hijack (CVE-2026-110936), BeyondTrust Remote Support authentication bypass (CVE-2026-102764), and Fortinet FortiOS/FortiProxy path traversal (CVE-2025-61624) — all common components in industrial remote access stacks. Prioritise patching anything that provides vendor or integrator access into the plant.Read more →
