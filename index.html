Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix EDS-family serial servers vulnerable this week trace their lineage to devices first shipped in the late 1990s specifically to keep RS-232 instruments alive as Ethernet took over the plant floor. A quarter century later, many are still running the same firmware they were commissioned with — meaning some field devices in current production have longer uninterrupted service records than the engineers maintaining them.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalized not in a university lab but on US Navy ships in the 1920s, when Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law by watching how helmsmen steered the USS New Mexico. He noticed that good helmsmen didn't just correct for current heading error (P), they also anticipated based on how fast the error was changing (D) and accounted for persistent bias like wind and current (I). Nearly every loop running in your plant this morning is, in essence, a mathematical impression of a sailor at a ship's wheel a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is finally moving from vendor demos into real installed base, particularly in automotive and semiconductor fabs. The idea is straightforward: standard 802.1 Ethernet augmented with time synchronization (802.1AS), scheduled traffic (802.1Qbv), and frame preemption (802.1Qbu) so that safety, motion, and best-effort IT traffic can share the same physical wire with bounded latency in the microsecond range. Combined with OPC UA PubSub, this is the technical foundation the ODVA, PI, and OPC Foundation have converged on for what they call 'Ethernet-APL upstream' — a single converged network from sensor to cloud. For a controls engineer today, the practical implications are mixed. On one hand, TSN promises to end the era of parallel EtherNet/IP, PROFINET IRT, and Sercos III islands, and to make motion control, vision, and safety coexist without dedicated backplanes. On the other hand, switch pricing is still 2-4x conventional managed industrial switches, tooling for configuring the 802.1Qbv gate schedules is vendor-specific, and interoperability across vendors remains fragile outside of tightly controlled certification pairs. Expect greenfield builds — especially battery gigafactories and EV lines — to adopt TSN first, while brownfield sites will keep their protocol islands for years to come.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often cited in RFQs from asset owners this year, largely because it forces a formal zones-and-conduits exercise before procurement rather than after commissioning. If you are on the integrator side, expect end users to start requiring the zone/conduit diagram and target security level (SL-T) assignments as contract deliverables, not just design artifacts. The companion 62443-4-2 component requirements are increasingly showing up as pass/fail line items in PLC and drive specifications, especially for utilities and pharma. On the NIST side, CSF 2.0's 'Govern' function is influencing how boards ask questions about OT — specifically around third-party risk, which is exactly the vector highlighted by this week's SimpleHelp KEV entry. If your MSP or system integrator reaches into your network via a remote support tool, CSF 2.0 essentially requires you to have documented, tested controls over that access. NIST SP 800-82r3 remains the most practical reference document for translating these governance expectations into concrete OT controls, and it pairs well with 62443 rather than duplicating it.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week, given the PTC Windchill KEV entry, is the 2023 compromise of a mid-sized North American aerospace supplier via its PLM system. Attackers gained initial access through a vulnerable internet-exposed Windchill instance used for supplier collaboration, then pivoted through shared service accounts into the engineering workstation VLAN. From there they accessed CAD files and, more damagingly, the release-controlled NC programs pushed to shop-floor CNCs. Production was not halted, but several batches of parts had to be re-inspected after investigators could not rule out tampering with toolpaths. The root cause was not a single flaw but a chain: an unpatched PLM appliance, a service account shared between PLM and MES, and no integrity verification on the files consumed by the DNC distribution system. Recovery took weeks primarily because the company could not prove which artifacts were trustworthy. Three lessons carry directly to today's Windchill CVE. First, PLM is production-critical even though it sits in IT — treat it accordingly for patching and exposure. Second, any file consumed by a controller (recipes, part programs, PLC logic, HMI projects) needs a hash or signature check at the point of use, not just at release. Third, shared service accounts between IT platforms and OT systems remove your ability to contain an incident; separate identities are cheap insurance.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Medtronic states products and manufacturing were unaffected, but the incident is another reminder that even mature OT-adjacent enterprises can be blindsided through IT-side compromise, and that segmentation between corporate ERP/PLM systems and plant operations is what keeps a data-loss event from becoming a production event. CISA Vulnerability Digest

Two additions this fortnight land squarely in OT territory. CVE-2026-12569 in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM is an unauthenticated remote code execution via malformed request — Windchill sits at the heart of many manufacturers' PLM, CAD vaulting, and BOM release workflows, so a compromise can poison engineering artifacts flowing to the plant. Patch immediately, and confirm Windchill instances are not directly reachable from the internet or from Level 3 without a broker. CVE-2025-67038 in the Lantronix EDS5000 device server is a root-level OS command injection via the username field; these serial-to-Ethernet gateways commonly front legacy RTUs, weigh scales, and analyzers, and they are frequently forgotten during patch cycles. Inventory them and restrict management interfaces to an engineering VLAN. Also notable for OT-adjacent infrastructure: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (two CVEs), Arista EOS tunnel decapsulation (CVE-2026-7473), Ubiquiti UniFi OS (three CVEs covering command injection, path traversal, and access control), and Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 authentication bypass (CVE-2026-50751, known ransomware use). Any of these sitting between your enterprise and plant networks warrants urgent review. Splunk Enterprise's unauthenticated file write via a PostgreSQL sidecar (CVE-2026-20253) affects sites using Splunk for OT log aggregation. On the IT side but likely to touch engineering workstations: SharePoint deserialization RCE (CVE-2026-45659), SimpleHelp OIDC signature-bypass (CVE-2026-48558) used by many MSPs providing remote support to plants, and SolarWinds Serv-U DoS. Prioritize any SimpleHelp instance that a third-party integrator uses to reach your DMZ.Read more →
