Today's Knowledge Bite

The ABB Terra AC Wallbox advisory is a reminder that EV chargers are now full-fledged industrial endpoints — most modern AC units run embedded Linux, speak OCPP over WebSockets, and contain a PLC-class controller managing contactor logic. A compromised charger can act as a pivot into building electrical systems, and in some installations shares a VLAN with HVAC controls. Treat charger firmware updates with the same rigor as a VFD or RTU.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term has a peculiar origin: it was first implemented mechanically by Elmer Sperry's team in the 1910s for ship autopilots, using a flywheel and friction-clutch arrangement that physically accumulated heading error over time. The 'reset' action we now type as a Ki coefficient was literally a brass disc winding up against a spring, and the term 'reset windup' — the bane of every commissioning engineer — described an actual mechanical phenomenon where the flywheel could spin past its useful range and take seconds to unwind. Nearly every digital PID block in your DCS today is a direct mathematical descendant of a piece of naval hardware that predates the vacuum tube.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is the extension of OPC UA designed to push the protocol all the way down to controller-to-controller and controller-to-field-device communication, replacing the patchwork of EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, and Modbus links between PLCs. Combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) at layer 2, OPC UA FX promises deterministic, sub-millisecond cycle times over standard Ethernet — meaning a single converged network can carry motion control, safety, and supervisory traffic simultaneously. For controls engineers, the practical implication is significant: instead of bridging PROFINET segments to a SCADA OPC server via a gateway, your Siemens, Rockwell, Beckhoff, and B&R controllers could exchange tagged, semantically rich data natively. Vendor demos at Hannover Messe 2025 showed mixed-vendor TSN cells achieving 100 µs cycle times. The challenge is adoption: TSN switches remain expensive, vendor TSN profiles are still settling, and most installed PLCs lack hardware TSN support. Expect a 5–10 year transition where OPC UA FX runs alongside legacy fieldbuses rather than replacing them outright. From a security perspective, OPC UA FX inherits OPC UA's certificate-based authentication and signing — a meaningful upgrade over the wide-open broadcast traffic typical of PROFINET RT or EtherNet/IP implicit messaging. But it also means PKI becomes a plant-floor concern, with certificate lifecycle management at the device level. Start thinking about who owns the issuing CA before your first FX-capable device shows up on site.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) remain the two parts most often invoked in 2026 procurement specifications. Increasing numbers of European utilities are now requiring 62443-4-2 SL-2 certification as a baseline for new PLCs and RTUs, and the ABB B&R advisories highlighted today illustrate why component-level certification isn't a guarantee — third-party libraries embedded in certified products still drag in CVEs. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3, released in late 2023, is now the de facto reference being cited in U.S. critical-infrastructure audits, especially for water and wastewater following the sector's regulatory tightening. Rev. 3 expanded coverage of cloud-connected OT and explicitly addresses IIoT gateways and remote-access patterns — worth re-reading if you're designing remote support architectures. On the horizon, ISA's TR100.00.02 work on wireless coexistence and the ongoing IEC 62443-2-4 revision (requirements for service providers) are both worth tracking. The 2-4 update is expected to tighten obligations on system integrators around vulnerability disclosure timelines and patch delivery SLAs — meaningful if you contract out commissioning or long-term support.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment intrusion, which has had several details clarified in follow-up reporting and a 2023 academic post-mortem. An operator noticed his mouse cursor moving on a HMI workstation; the intruder briefly raised the sodium hydroxide setpoint from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm before the operator reverted it. The access vector was a TeamViewer instance left installed on a shared operator workstation, with a password reused across multiple plant PCs, reachable from the internet because the firewall permitted outbound persistent connections. The lessons remain directly applicable. First, remote access tools deployed for convenience during COVID-era operations frequently outlive their justification — inventory and remove them. Second, alarm and rate-of-change limits in the control logic (not just the HMI) would have prevented the setpoint excursion regardless of operator vigilance; lye dosing has well-understood safe bounds that should be enforced in the PLC. Third, the incident was caught by human observation, not by any security tool — invest in logging HMI session activity and alerting on unexpected remote sessions to operator stations. The broader takeaway is that small utilities and plants with lean staff are the soft underbelly of critical infrastructure, and the defenses that work there are the cheap ones: MFA on remote access, unique credentials per site, engineering setpoint clamps in ladder logic, and a culture where operators report anomalies immediately.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a notable cluster of six ICS advisories today, five of which affect ABB products: B&R Automation Runtime, B&R Automation Studio, B&R industrial PCs, and the Terra AC Wallbox EV charger. The B&R Automation Runtime and Automation Studio issues center on browser session takeover and outdated third-party components, while the B&R PCs advisory flags network-exploitable RCE, DoS, and DNS cache poisoning paths — suggesting an embedded networking stack with known CVEs. The Terra AC Wallbox issue is particularly noteworthy: heap memory pollution can enable firmware modification, a serious concern for fleet operators and any site running EV charging tied into building or process networks. Hitachi Energy issued an advisory for the GMS600 generator monitoring system covering the long-known OpenSSL timing oracle (CVE-2022-4304), and Kieback & Peter's DDC4000-series building controllers received an advisory for a browser-takeover flaw (CVE-2026-4293) in versions up to 1.12.14. Facilities and BMS engineers should prioritize patching any internet-exposed DDC web interfaces, and verify that Hitachi GMS600 units aren't reachable from corporate networks. On the broader IT side, several stories matter to OT teams because they touch the supply chain. The 'Megalodon' campaign poisoned 5,500+ GitHub repositories with malicious Actions workflows that exfiltrate CI secrets, and Laravel-Lang packages were trojaned within a 15-minute window. If your engineering team pulls libraries, scripts, or HMI assets via npm, Composer, or GitHub Actions, audit recent dependency updates and rotate any CI tokens. Anthropic's 'Mythos' tool also flagged 23,000 potential vulnerabilities across 1,000 OSS projects — expect a surge of new CVEs in libraries embedded deep in OT vendor firmware over the coming months. Finally, Drupal CVE-2026-9082 (SQL injection enabling RCE) is being actively exploited within days of disclosure. Plant intranets, vendor portals, and SCADA documentation sites commonly run Drupal — patch immediately or take affected sites offline. Wireshark 4.6.6 was also released with one security fix and 11 bug fixes; update your analyzer workstations.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog added Drupal CVE-2026-9082 on 22 May, with confirmed in-the-wild exploitation against thousands of sites. Any Drupal-based site supporting OT operations — engineering wikis, vendor extranets, asset-management front-ends — should be patched or isolated today. Also added: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN auth bypass (CVE-2026-20182) and Palo Alto PAN-OS User-ID portal RCE (CVE-2026-0300, unauth root). Both sit at the IT/OT border in many facilities and should be top of the patch queue if your perimeter relies on them. For OT-specific platforms, watch Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) if you manage rugged tablets or field devices through it, and Trend Micro Apex One (CVE-2026-34926) if it's deployed on engineering workstations. The reappearance of legacy KEV entries from 2008–2010 (MS08-067, DirectShow, Acrobat, IE use-after-frees) is a reminder that CISA continues to validate exploitation against unpatched legacy systems — exactly the kind of long-lived Windows XP/7 HMIs and engineering laptops still common on plant floors. If you have islanded legacy assets, treat network segmentation and removable-media hygiene as compensating controls. Practical guidance: review your perimeter for Cisco SD-WAN and PAN-OS, force-patch Drupal across all subdomains, and audit Trend Micro Apex One deployments on OT-adjacent endpoints for the directory traversal fix.Read more →
