Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, which still carries a huge fraction of plant-floor traffic in 2026, was published by Modicon in 1979 — the same year the Sony Walkman launched. Its function-code structure has barely changed in 47 years, which is why a CVE published last week can still apply to firmware concepts designed during the Carter administration. That longevity is both Modbus's greatest strength and the reason it has zero native authentication.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilots in 1911 implemented proportional-integral-derivative control entirely mechanically using flyweights, dashpots, and gear trains. Nicolas Minorsky formalised the maths in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico and noticing they were anticipating the rate of yaw, not just reacting to error — that observation is literally the D-term. Every tuning rule you use today traces back to humans intuitively doing derivative action with a ship's wheel.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is quietly maturing into the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device profile that the OPC Foundation has promised for years, and 2026 is shaping up as the year you will start seeing it on real RFQs. FX extends the familiar OPC UA information model down to deterministic field-level communication, riding on top of Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) so that a Siemens PLC, a Rockwell drive, and a Beckhoff IPC can in principle exchange cyclic I/O on the same Ethernet wire with bounded latency — without an intermediate gateway translating Profinet to EtherNet/IP. The practical implication is significant: a single, secured, semantically rich protocol could replace the patchwork of Profinet, EtherNet/IP, EtherCAT, and Modbus that currently lives in most cells. Security is built in (X.509 certs, signing, encryption), which is a step change from today's fieldbuses where authentication is essentially absent. Diagnostics are also dramatically richer because every variable carries a type and a namespace, not just a register number. Adoption challenges are real, though. TSN switches remain pricier than vanilla managed switches, end-device support is still sparse outside flagship products, and engineering tools have not yet caught up with the configuration complexity of stream reservations and Qbv schedules. Expect FX to land first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines where determinism justifies the cost, and to trickle into process industries over the next five years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard that most asset owners are actively struggling to operationalise, especially as insurers and auditors increasingly ask to see zone-and-conduit diagrams with documented target security levels. If you have not yet defined your zones beyond a single "OT network" blob, the ABB Automation Builder Gateway advisory is a textbook example of why it matters: an engineering tool that is remotely reachable by default belongs in its own conduit with explicit access rules, not on the general control network. On the ISA side, ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) saw a major revision land in 2024 that aligns it much more closely with the rest of the 62443 family, including a clearer mapping to NIST CSF 2.0's Govern function. If your organisation's OT security program was written against the older 2010 edition, it is worth a gap review — the new edition is far more prescriptive about supplier security requirements, which directly affects how you write your next PLC or SCADA RFQ. NIST is also continuing work on SP 800-82 Rev. 3, the OT security guide, with ongoing community input on AI/ML-specific OT considerations. Worth tracking if you are starting to see vendor pitches for AI-driven anomaly detection or generative engineering assistants in your control rooms.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful reminder this week comes from revisiting the 2024 Frontier Software / Frontier Communications-adjacent ransomware incidents that hit several US water utilities via compromised remote-support tooling — the same class of risk highlighted by ScreenConnect and SimpleHelp returning to the KEV list. In multiple confirmed cases, attackers obtained legitimate technician credentials to remote-support platforms used by integrators, pivoted into utility engineering workstations, and from there reached HMI servers controlling SCADA polling of distributed RTUs. Operations were not directly manipulated, but historian data and engineering project files were exfiltrated and, in at least one case, encrypted. The attack path was unremarkable: phished integrator employee, no MFA on the remote-support tool, shared technician account with persistent VPN, and a flat OT network behind the jump host. Detection came not from OT monitoring but from the IT SOC noticing unusual outbound TLS volumes at 03:00 local time. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, your security perimeter includes every integrator and OEM with remote access — demand evidence of MFA, per-session approval, and unique named accounts in your contracts. Second, engineering workstations are high-value targets because they hold project files with full PLC logic and credentials; treat them like crown jewels, not like general office PCs. Third, instrument egress from the OT network, not just ingress; exfiltration is almost always detectable as anomalous outbound volume long before encryption begins.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's ICS Patch Tuesday is dominated by ABB, with CISA publishing four advisories covering the AC500 V3 PLC family, the Automation Builder Gateway for Windows, and the WebPro SNMP card PowerValue. The AC500 V3 issues are particularly relevant on the plant floor: attackers can bypass user management to read visualization files (CVE-2025-2595), read/write certificates and keys (CVE-2025-41659), trigger denial-of-service, and exploit a stack buffer overflow in Cryptographic Message Syntax handling that could enable remote code execution. The Automation Builder Gateway is, by default, remotely accessible and unauthenticated discovery of PLCs is possible, meaning anyone who has disabled PLC user management is effectively wide open. Updates are available from ABB and should be staged through your normal MOC cycle. Fuji Electric Tellus 5.0.2 has a local privilege-escalation flaw (CVSS 7.8) that lets a user reach SYSTEM and tamper with files — relevant anywhere Tellus HMI runtime is installed on shared engineering workstations. Subnet Solutions PowerSYSTEM Center 2020 (widely used in substation gateways) picked up two information-disclosure and a CRLF-injection issue requiring authentication; utilities should plan an update window. Siemens and Schneider were comparatively quiet this Patch Tuesday, per SecurityWeek's roundup. On the IT side that bleeds into OT, Microsoft fixed 137 CVEs including critical Azure and Windows flaws, Fortinet and Ivanti shipped critical patches (note that EPMM is already on the KEV list), and Intel/AMD released 70+ chip-level advisories. Apple patched 84 issues across its OS family, which matters for engineers using iPads on the floor for mobile HMI. RubyGems briefly suspended new registrations after a 500-package malicious flood — a reminder that supply-chain risk is no longer purely a developer problem when OT vendors increasingly ship Ruby/Python tooling in their engineering suites. Finally, SANS published a useful diary on proxying traffic from EXEs that ignore system proxy settings — a practical technique when you are trying to inspect what a vendor's engineering tool is actually phoning home about during commissioning.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The most OT-relevant KEV additions in the last two weeks are CVE-2026-0300 (Palo Alto PAN-OS User-ID Captive Portal, unauthenticated root RCE) and CVE-2026-6973 (Ivanti EPMM authenticated RCE). PAN-OS frequently sits at the IT/OT boundary as the perimeter firewall for Purdue Level 3.5 DMZs; an unauthenticated root on that device is effectively game-over for network segmentation, so confirm your PAN-OS version against Palo Alto's advisory and patch out-of-band if Captive Portal is exposed. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager picked up three KEV entries (CVE-2026-20122, 20128, 20133) covering privileged API abuse, sensitive-info exposure, and recoverable password storage — relevant for multi-site operators using SD-WAN to stitch remote pump stations, substations, or plants together. Also worth flagging: ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) and SimpleHelp (CVE-2024-57728/57726) path-traversal and authorization flaws are back on the KEV list with known ransomware ties. Many integrators use these tools for remote support into OT networks, often via jump hosts that are inconsistently inventoried. Confirm with your integrators in writing which remote-support product they are using, what version it is on, and whether MFA and per-session approval are enforced. PaperCut (CVE-2023-27351) being re-flagged as actively exploited with ransomware also matters — print servers in office networks are common pivot points to flat OT networks.Read more →
