☀ Morning Briefing 2026-10-28 13:52 UTC

The headline today is a sweeping Mitsubishi Electric ICONICS/GENESIS advisory covering authentication bypass and RCE across almost the entire HMI and historian portfolio — if you run ICONICS, GENESIS64, Hyper Historian, or MC Works64, start inventory and patching now. Siemens Software Center (CVE-2026-110936) is now on CISA KEV, so engineering workstations are an active target, and the Industrial Edge App Publisher cert-validation chain lets attackers RCE developer hosts via a spoofed management server. Also patch any perimeter PAN-OS and Ivanti Connect Secure appliances brokering OT remote access — both were added to KEV in the last two weeks. Practical action: block outbound internet from engineering workstations, restrict Software Center installs to a controlled share, and verify ICONICS web gateways are not reachable from the enterprise network.

Today's Knowledge Bite

The libwebp heap overflow CVE-2023-4863 keeps resurfacing in ICS advisories three years after its original Chrome disclosure, including in today's Mitsubishi bulletin. It's a reminder that industrial software bundles browser engines (CEF, Electron, embedded WebKit) for HMI rendering, and those components inherit every browser-world vulnerability — but on a patch cadence measured in years, not days.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalised not in a university lab but on a US Navy destroyer. Nicholas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analysing the automatic steering of USS New Mexico, observing that an experienced helmsman effectively applied proportional, integral, and derivative action by watching the ship's heading, its accumulated drift, and its rate of swing. His paper 'Directional Stability of Automatically Steered Bodies' predated Ziegler and Nichols' famous tuning rules by two decades, and the law he wrote down for a 32,000-ton battleship is the same one running in the loop block of your DCS this morning.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is quietly maturing from spec into shipping product. FX extends OPC UA from the traditional north-south supervisory role down to controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communications at the field level, standardising how PLCs exchange process data, safety signals, and motion data without proprietary profiles. When run over Ethernet TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking), it promises deterministic latency in the single-digit microseconds, enough for synchronised motion applications that today require EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, or Sercos. The practical implication for plant floor engineers is a potential end to multi-protocol gateways between vendors. A Siemens drive, Rockwell PLC, and Beckhoff motion controller could, in theory, exchange data natively over the same TSN fabric using FX connections described by companion specifications. Early demonstrators from the Shaper Group — including Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, ABB, Phoenix Contact, and others — have shown interoperable setups at Hannover Messe for several years running. Adoption challenges remain real. TSN requires switches, NICs, and controllers that all support the IEEE 802.1 TSN sub-standards consistently, and tooling for TSN stream configuration is still vendor-specific despite CC-Link IE TSN and PROFINET-over-TSN both existing. Expect FX to appear first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor projects where cross-vendor motion coordination justifies the engineering effort; brownfield retrofits over existing PROFINET or EtherNet/IP infrastructure will lag by years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2, the risk assessment standard for system design, is seeing growing attention as asset owners move from pilot zone-and-conduit exercises to full-site rollouts. The standard requires a documented risk assessment per zone, with target security levels (SL-T) justified against tolerable risk — and increasingly, regulators and insurers are asking to see the artefacts, not just the policy statement. If you've been treating 62443-3-2 as a paper exercise, 2026 is the year to start producing defensible zone diagrams with assigned SL-Ts tied to specific threat scenarios. On the product side, IEC 62443-4-1 (secure development lifecycle) and 4-2 (component security requirements) certifications are becoming de facto procurement requirements in Europe under the NIS2 and Cyber Resilience Act regimes. Siemens, Schneider, Rockwell, ABB, and Honeywell all now publish 62443-4-2 certification status per product family — worth checking before specifying a new PLC or RTU, because an uncertified component forces you to compensate at the zone level. NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the most practical companion document for US-based operators, particularly for mapping 62443 controls to NIST CSF 2.0 functions. The CSF 2.0 'Govern' function, added last cycle, is especially relevant for OT because it formalises the long-standing gap in who actually owns ICS cybersecurity risk — operations, IT, or a joint function. Expect audit frameworks to lean on Govern-function evidence heavily over the next 12 months.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Arkansas City, Kansas water treatment incident, in which the utility was forced to switch to full manual operations after detecting anomalous activity on its SCADA network. No water quality impact occurred, but operators ran chemical dosing, pumps, and clarifier controls by hand for several days while the network was rebuilt. Public reporting pointed to a compromise reached through the business network, with lateral movement into the control environment via shared credentials and insufficient segmentation at the IT/OT boundary. The mechanics matter: operators had a well-rehearsed manual mode and physical access to local controls, which turned a potential safety event into an operational inconvenience. That is a direct consequence of design decisions — retaining hardwired local HOA switches, maintaining paper SOPs for manual dosing, and training operators on degraded-mode operations. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, assume your HMI will one day be untrustworthy and design for graceful degradation to local control, not just failover to a redundant server. Second, audit every service account and vendor remote-access path that crosses the IT/OT boundary; shared AD credentials remain the single most common pivot vector in water and wastewater incidents. Third, exercise manual operations annually — not as a tabletop, but as a live drill with the SCADA deliberately isolated. Muscle memory is a security control.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a dense set of ICS advisories today, led by a wide-ranging Mitsubishi Electric bulletin covering GENESIS, GENESIS64, ICONICS Suite, Hyper Historian, AnalytiX, MobileHMI, IoTWorX, MC Works64, GENESIS32 and BizViz. The vulnerabilities span arbitrary code execution via the long-running libwebp bug CVE-2023-4863, two authentication bypasses (CVE-2026-16436/16437), and an arbitrary operations flaw (CVE-2026-16438). Any site running ICONICS-family HMIs or historians should treat this as a priority — these products often sit directly on process networks and brokered OPC UA/DA traffic, so an auth bypass is a quick path to tag manipulation or recipe tampering. Siemens disclosed three separate issues: a DLL search-path hijack in Siemens Software Center (CVE-2026-110936, already on CISA KEV), a certificate validation and template injection chain in Industrial Edge App Publisher that permits RCE on engineering workstations via a spoofed management server, and an out-of-bounds read in Capital Embedded AR Classic R20-11 affecting AUTOSAR Classic ECU code. The App Publisher chain is particularly worrying for sites rolling out Industrial Edge, because the attack targets the developer host rather than the Edge device itself. Two items affect research and embedded infrastructure. EPICS Base ≤7.0.10 has four newly disclosed flaws (CVE-2026-114595 through 114598) that an autonomous fuzzer uncovered — these can crash IOCs or potentially execute code at particle accelerators, fusion labs, and large-scale experimental facilities. Separately, FoxIO found three TLS bugs in the GoAhead embedded web server, which ships in hundreds of millions of devices including many IP cameras, routers, and lower-end industrial gateways; revoked certificates may be accepted and some sessions decrypted. Expect a long tail of unpatched OT edge devices. On the broader threat side, China-linked actors are chaining PHP2Shell and Fragnesia exploits to root firewalls at a US government-affiliated organisation, reinforcing that perimeter appliances remain the preferred foothold into segmented networks. Chrome 156 and Firefox 159 patched a combined 219 bugs — relevant for the browser-based HMIs and vendor web consoles that now dominate engineering workstations.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added 20 vulnerabilities to the KEV catalog over the last two weeks, several with direct OT relevance. CVE-2026-110936 (Siemens Software Center DLL hijack) is the standout for engineering workstations — any station that runs Software Center for TIA Portal, PCS 7, or SIMATIC updates should be patched and users should avoid running installers from writable shared folders. CVE-2026-0322 in Palo Alto PAN-OS and CVE-2026-104935 in Ivanti Connect Secure are both authenticated/auth-bypass RCEs on perimeter gear commonly used to broker remote access into OT networks; if you rely on either for vendor VPN or jump-host access, assume active exploitation and patch now. Several upstream library bugs are quietly consequential for OT. CVE-2026-109618 and 109619 in PHP, and CVE-2026-111497 in Google's Brotli compression library, affect countless embedded web consoles on HMIs, protocol gateways, drives, and historians. Vendors will take months to roll these into firmware, so compensating controls — restricting management-plane exposure, blocking direct engineer-to-device web access except via a hardened jump host — are the realistic near-term play. Fortinet (CVE-2025-61624), Cisco Secure Email Gateway, BeyondTrust Remote Support, and Atlassian Data Center products round out the list. BeyondTrust and Atlassian are frequently the pivot point from IT into OT via shared identity, so verify that your DMZ and Purdue Level 3.5 brokers are patched and that OT-side service accounts are not reused on these systems.Read more →
