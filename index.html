Today's Knowledge Bite

The MS08-067 vulnerability (CVE-2008-4250) being re-listed in CISA's KEV this month is the same flaw Conficker used in 2008 and that Stuxnet weaponized in 2010 to spread between Windows machines en route to Natanz's centrifuges. Eighteen years later it's still being exploited in the wild — almost certainly against industrial environments where Windows XP and Server 2003 HMIs remain in service. It is the longest-lived practical ICS attack primitive in history.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship-steering autopilot from 1911 implemented proportional-integral-derivative control entirely through mechanical linkages, dashpots, and flyweights, and the U.S. Navy was using it operationally before World War I. Nicholas Minorsky formalised the mathematics in 1922 by observing how a helmsman actually steered, noting that good helmsmen anticipated the ship's rate of turn (derivative) and corrected for persistent offsets (integral). Every loop you tune today is still, in essence, a mathematical imitation of an experienced sailor's hands on a wheel a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is finally moving from demo booths to pilot deployments in 2026. The premise is simple: combine OPC UA's vendor-neutral semantic model with IEEE 802.1 TSN's deterministic Ethernet scheduling, so you get both rich information modelling and the sub-millisecond, bounded-jitter performance previously reserved for fieldbuses like PROFINET IRT or EtherCAT. The OPC Foundation's Field Level Communications (FLC) initiative defines the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device profiles that make this work end-to-end. For controls engineers, the practical implication is a path to converged networks where motion control, safety, standard I/O, and MES/historian traffic share the same physical infrastructure with guaranteed quality of service per stream. That means fewer parallel networks to maintain, simpler cable plants, and the ability to pull rich device metadata (units, ranges, diagnostics) directly into asset management without custom drivers. Adoption challenges remain significant: TSN-capable switches and NICs are still pricier than standard managed switches; configuration of TSN streams (using either fully-distributed or centrally-managed CNC models) requires engineering tools the industry is still maturing; and very few field devices ship with native OPC UA FX support today. Most plants will see this first in greenfield automotive, semiconductor, and battery production lines before it filters down to typical process and discrete manufacturing in the late 2020s.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues to be the centre of gravity for OT cybersecurity standardisation. The 62443-2-1 revision published in 2024 reframed the OT security program requirements around a more practical risk-based approach aligned with ISO/IEC 27001 structure, and asset owners are now well into adoption cycles. If you haven't yet mapped your existing policies to the new SPE (Security Program Element) structure, that exercise typically reveals gaps in patch management governance and supply-chain assurance — both areas highlighted by this week's Nx and TanStack npm compromises. Worth watching: IEC 62443-4-2 certification activity from product vendors is accelerating, and procurement specifications increasingly cite specific Security Levels (SL-C) for components. When you write a spec for a new PLC, RTU, or HMI, requiring SL2 or SL3 component certification gives you contractual leverage that generic 'must be cyber-secure' language never did. The certifying labs (ISASecure, TÜV, exida) publish public lists you can reference. On the U.S. side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 (Guide to OT Security, finalised in 2023) remains the most pragmatic free reference and is closely aligned with 62443 concepts. For European operators, NIS2 enforcement actions are now being seen across member states, and the demonstrable use of IEC 62443 controls is increasingly the evidence regulators expect to see when assessing 'state of the art' due diligence.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A worthwhile case to revisit this week is the 2024 attack on a U.S. water utility in which an Iranian-linked group, CyberAv3ngers, compromised Unitronics Vision-series PLCs at multiple water authorities — most publicly the Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa, Pennsylvania. The attackers defaced the PLC HMI screen with anti-Israel messaging and forced the booster station into manual operation. No water quality impact occurred because operators detected the anomaly quickly and fell back to manual control, but the intrusion bypassed essentially nothing — the PLCs were directly internet-exposed on TCP/20256 with the factory-default password '1111'. The root causes were depressingly mundane: internet-facing controllers, default credentials, no VPN or jump host, and no network segmentation between the PLC and the public internet. CISA's follow-up advisory found dozens of similar Unitronics devices exposed across U.S. water utilities. The attackers used no novel exploit; Shodan and a default password were sufficient. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, inventory every device with a routable IP and assume internet exposure until proven otherwise — your firewall rules and your ISP's NAT are not the same as segmentation. Second, default passwords on field devices are not a vendor problem you can defer; commission acceptance should fail if any device still has them. Third, ensure your operators are trained and authorised to fall back to manual or local control quickly; in this incident, that capability prevented a cyber event from becoming a public-health event.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's CISA ICS advisory batch hit a broad range of edge and embedded systems that controls engineers frequently overlook. Most notable is the XCharge C6 EV charger (CVSS 9.8) with code-without-integrity-check and stack buffer overflow flaws — relevant for facilities adding EV infrastructure to their utility/parking networks. KMW CCTV cameras (CVSS 9.1) carry an unverified password change flaw permitting full unauthorized access, and CP Plus NVRs are vulnerable to authenticated XSS. The MacGregor Voyage Data Recorder G4e (CVSS 8.3) ships with default credentials, a familiar pattern in marine and transportation OT. On the process side, Schneider Electric disclosed an information-disclosure issue in EcoStruxure Machine Expert HVAC, the programming software for Modicon M171/M172 controllers — review your engineering workstations and project file handling. ABB's Busch-Welcome 2-Wire Door Opener Actuator advisory is a reminder that building-access systems sitting on shared networks can be turned into physical-intrusion enablers. Geopolitically, Western officials warned that Russian intelligence services are intensifying efforts to acquire Western technology and infrastructure data through front companies and cyber operations as sanctions bite. This raises the baseline targeting risk for energy, water, and manufacturing operators — particularly those with European exposure. Pair this with SecurityWeek's report on the Charter Communications breach (42M records leaked by ShinyHunters) which, while IT-side, often produces credential reuse that bleeds into OT remote-access portals. Finally, the SANS ISC piece reconstructing an Akira ransomware kill chain from perimeter and endpoint logs is worth reading: it underscores that the actionable forensic signals always sit days before encryption, in firewall and EDR telemetry that rarely gets correlated. For OT, where you may not have EDR on PLCs or HMIs, joining firewall logs with Windows event logs from engineering stations is often your only early-warning channel.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The standout KEV entry this fortnight is CVE-2026-0257, a Palo Alto PAN-OS authentication bypass allowing unauthorized VPN connections — directly relevant to any site using PAN-OS as the IT/OT boundary firewall or for vendor remote access. Combined with CVE-2026-0300 (PAN-OS GlobalProtect captive portal out-of-bounds write giving unauthenticated root RCE) added earlier this month, Palo Alto perimeter devices need urgent patching and audit of recent VPN session logs. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-20182) auth bypass rounds out the perimeter trio. Supply-chain poisoning continues as a theme: malicious versions of Nx Console (CVE-2026-48027) and TanStack (CVE-2026-45321) were published to npm with credential-harvesting payloads, both flagged as ransomware-associated. If your engineering or HMI development teams use Node-based tooling, audit lockfiles and rotate any credentials that may have been on developer machines. Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) and Trend Micro Apex One (CVE-2026-34926) round out enterprise-side risks that frequently touch OT through shared management infrastructure. Curious additions are several 2008–2010 Microsoft vulnerabilities (MS08-067, DirectShow, IE use-after-frees) being re-added to KEV — likely reflecting fresh exploitation against unpatched legacy Windows systems still alive on plant floors. If you have Windows XP or Server 2003 islands behind air gaps, assume these are exploitable on any pivot and prioritize network segmentation enforcement.Read more →
