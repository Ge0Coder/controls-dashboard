Today's Knowledge Bite

The Ruggedcom ROX platform — now appearing in three of today's advisories — was originally developed by RuggedCom Inc. in Concord, Ontario, primarily for electrical substation hardening, and its operating system was based on a heavily customised Debian. When Siemens acquired RuggedCom in 2012, ROX became one of the few openly Linux-derived products in the Siemens automation portfolio, which is why ROX advisories so often include long lists of upstream CVEs (curl, OpenSSL, sudo) that look more like server patches than PLC firmware.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term, which feels like a modern digital invention, was first implemented mechanically in 1922 by Nicolas Minorsky to automatically steer the USS New Mexico. Minorsky derived it not from control theory — which didn't really exist yet — but by watching how helmsmen actually steered: they didn't just react to current heading error, they remembered how long the ship had been off course and anticipated where it was going. Every PLC's PID block is, in effect, a digital tribute to a human helmsman's intuition codified a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet is steadily moving from trade-show demo to real plant deployment, particularly as OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) profiles mature. The promise is genuinely significant for controls engineers: deterministic, bounded-latency communication on the same physical network that already carries MES, video, and engineering traffic — eliminating the historical need for parallel fieldbus cabling like Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, or Sercos for synchronised motion. The practical implications are larger than they first appear. TSN allows a single converged network to handle 1 ms cycle-time motion control and bulk historian uploads simultaneously, with traffic shaping enforced by the switches themselves (IEEE 802.1Qbv gate scheduling). That changes how you design network segmentation: VLANs alone are no longer sufficient; you need TSN-aware switches and a centralised configuration model (the CUC/CNC architecture in 802.1Qcc). Adoption challenges are real. Switch vendors implement different subsets of the TSN standards, interoperability between Siemens, Rockwell, B&R, and Beckhoff TSN stacks is improving but not seamless, and most existing brownfield Profinet/EtherIP infrastructure won't be ripped out tomorrow. Expect TSN to land first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines, then propagate as switch refresh cycles allow.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (Component requirements) continue to be the practical reference points being pulled into procurement language across Europe, particularly as NIS2 enforcement bites. A growing number of EU operators are now requiring suppliers to demonstrate 62443-4-1 secure development lifecycle certification as a contractual gate, not just a marketing claim. If you spec equipment, asking vendors for their IECEE CB scheme certificate (not just a self-declared whitepaper) is becoming standard practice. On the NIST side, SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the canonical OT security guide and is increasingly cross-referenced from the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function — useful when convincing a board that OT cybersecurity is a governance issue, not just an IT spend line. ISA/IEC TR 62443-3-1 on security technologies is also worth re-reading if you're scoping intrusion-detection or anomaly-detection deployments; it remains pragmatic about where passive monitoring beats active scanning on a live process network. Worth watching: the ongoing harmonisation between IEC 62443 and the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), which from late 2027 will mandate vulnerability handling and SBOM requirements on essentially every 'product with digital elements' sold in the EU — including PLCs, drives, and HMIs.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week, given today's SD-WAN zero-day, is the 2024 attack on a US water utility in which threat actors pivoted from a compromised remote-access appliance into the operational network and altered chemical dosing setpoints on a SCADA HMI before an operator noticed anomalous tank levels. The initial access was not a clever exploit — it was a remote-management tool installed by an integrator years earlier, with default credentials, reachable from the public internet, and forgotten by the asset owner. What made the near-miss recoverable was an alert plant operator who recognised that the chlorine dose did not match the requested setpoint and switched to manual control while calling the integrator. The attackers had administrative access to the HMI but not detailed process knowledge, so their changes were detectable. Three lessons translate directly to today's news. First, inventory every remote-support tool — ScreenConnect, SimpleHelp, AnyDesk, vendor-specific VPNs — on every OT asset, because today's KEV list shows these are actively weaponised. Second, ensure your SD-WAN and firewall management planes are isolated from the OT network they protect; a compromised Catalyst SD-WAN Manager should not have a routable path to a PLC. Third, train operators to trust the process, not the screen: if the indicated value and the field behaviour diverge, the screen is wrong until proven otherwise.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a bumper batch of Siemens advisories today covering SIMATIC HMI Unified Comfort Panels, Teamcenter, a broad 'Industrial Devices' DoS bundle, and three separate Ruggedcom ROX issues (third-party CVEs, an authenticated root command injection in the Scheduler, and an arbitrary file read via improper access control). The Ruggedcom command-injection in particular deserves attention: ROX is widely deployed as a substation and utility router, and although the flaws require authentication, weak segmentation between engineering VLANs and management networks often makes that bar low. Plant-floor teams running ROX MX5000-class devices should plan for the 2.17.1 upgrade window now. The SIMATIC HMI Unified Comfort issue (unauthenticated browser access via a help link in the Control Panel) is a classic kiosk-escape pattern — anyone with physical access to a panel can pivot into the underlying OS unless the screen-saver/Control Panel password protections are explicitly enabled. Outside the Siemens stream, Cisco patched its sixth actively exploited SD-WAN zero-day of 2026 (CVE-2026-20182, authentication bypass to admin on Catalyst SD-WAN Controller/Manager), exploited by an actor tracked as UAT-8616. Many manufacturers rely on Catalyst SD-WAN to interconnect plants, remote pump stations, and cloud historians, so this is a direct concern for OT WAN edges. CISA added the CVE to KEV the same day. On the malware side, SecurityWeek reports TeamPCP has released the source code of the Shai-Hulud npm supply-chain worm, lowering the barrier for copycat attacks. Controls engineers don't typically ship npm packages, but every modern SCADA HMI build pipeline, Ignition module, and engineering workstation tooling chain increasingly pulls Node-based dependencies. A new Linux kernel privilege-escalation (Fragnesia, CVE-2026-46300) joins the Dirty Frag/Copy Fail family and is relevant to anyone running Linux-based edge gateways, virtualised historians, or Rockwell/Schneider appliances built on hardened Linux. Finally, Chrome 148 patches multiple critical use-after-free bugs — a reminder that engineering workstations browsing vendor portals are a soft target, and HMI-embedded browsers (like the one exposed in the SIMATIC advisory above) often lag desktop Chrome by months or years.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog this week is dominated by network-edge and remote-access products that frequently terminate at the OT boundary. CVE-2026-20182 (Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN, added 14 May) joins three earlier April Catalyst SD-WAN entries (CVE-2026-20122, 20133, 20128) — meaning any organisation using Cisco SD-WAN to stitch together plants should treat the Manager/Controller as a compromised-until-patched asset and verify whether the appliances ever held credentials for OT jump hosts. Palo Alto PAN-OS Captive Portal (CVE-2026-0300) and Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) are similarly perimeter-adjacent. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) and SimpleHelp (CVE-2024-57726/57728) appear with known ransomware association — these remote-support tools are frequently installed on engineering laptops and OEM-managed PLCs without the asset owner's knowledge, so a hunt for unauthorised instances is worthwhile. For plant-floor relevance, the Linux kernel entry (CVE-2026-31431) matters because many OT edge devices, historians, and virtualisation hosts run long-lived kernels that rarely get same-day patches. Combine that with the new Fragnesia disclosure and you have a credible local-privilege-escalation chain on any Linux-based industrial appliance an attacker has already gained a shell on. Practical remediation priorities: patch internet-exposed SD-WAN and Ivanti first, inventory remote-support agents on OT assets, and confirm your Linux-based edge gear has a vendor-supported patch path rather than a frozen kernel.Read more →
