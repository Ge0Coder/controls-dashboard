Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PID controller predates electronics: Elmer Sperry's 1911 gyrocompass autopilot for the US Navy used mechanical linkages to implement proportional, integral, and derivative action on ship steering long before Ziegler and Nichols formalised tuning in 1942. The 'three-term controller' name comes from those three brass mechanical stages, not from any equation. Every time you tune a loop today, you're using math that was originally cut from gears.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The Nyquist stability criterion, which every controls engineer learns as a frequency-domain tool for feedback systems, was developed by Harry Nyquist in 1932 not for control loops but to explain why long-distance telephone amplifiers oscillated. Bell Labs had a business problem — cross-country calls needed dozens of vacuum-tube repeaters in series, and they kept howling — and Nyquist's contour analysis was the fix. The entire discipline of classical control theory essentially fell out of a telephone company trying to keep New York-to-San Francisco calls intelligible.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet is quietly reaching the point where it's showing up in real projects, not just vendor demos. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 standards (notably 802.1AS for time sync, 802.1Qbv for scheduled traffic, and 802.1CB for frame replication) that let deterministic control traffic share the same wire as best-effort IT traffic without jitter. In practice, this means a motion-control update, a video stream from a vision system, and a historian query can all traverse the same switch, with the motion frames guaranteed a slot every cycle. The practical implication for controls engineers is convergence: instead of parallel PROFINET, EtherCAT, and Ethernet/IP networks with separate switches and cabling, you can specify one converged fabric. Siemens, Rockwell, B&R, and CC-Link have all released TSN-capable products, and OPC UA over TSN is the shared aspiration for a vendor-neutral field layer. Adoption challenges remain real though — TSN configuration is genuinely complex, engineering tools for schedule computation are immature, and mixed-vendor interoperability is still hit-or-miss. Most greenfield projects that specify TSN today are doing so for future-proofing rather than exploiting its full determinism, but the tooling gap is closing quickly.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often cited in OT procurement specifications this year, and it's worth revisiting if you're scoping a project. The standard formalises the concept of zones and conduits, then walks you through assigning a target security level (SL-T) to each based on threat and consequence. In practice, most sites still default every zone to SL 2, which is a missed opportunity — a safety-instrumented system zone probably warrants SL 3 or 4, while a standalone offline test cell might genuinely be SL 1. On the NIST side, the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function has now filtered into most utility and pipeline regulator expectations, meaning boards are being asked about OT cyber risk in ways they weren't two years ago. If you haven't already, mapping your existing IEC 62443 controls to NIST CSF 2.0 categories is an afternoon's work that pays off the next time an auditor or executive asks how your OT program 'aligns.' ISA is also making steady progress on ISA/IEC 62443-2-2 (security program ratings), which will give operators a quantitative way to score their program maturity rather than the more subjective assessments used today.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group (an IRGC-linked persona) compromised a Unitronics Vision PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers didn't exploit any zero-day — the PLC was internet-exposed on TCP/20256 with the factory-default password '1111', and they simply logged in, defaced the HMI screen with an anti-Israel message, and stopped the pump. Operators fell back to manual control and no customers lost water, but the incident triggered a joint CISA/FBI/NSA/EPA advisory and became the template case for the 2024 congressional water sector hearings. What made it instructive isn't the sophistication — it's the ordinariness. Unitronics ships PLCs with a well-known default credential, and Shodan showed hundreds of similar units exposed across US water utilities. The threat actor was opportunistic, scanning by vendor banner rather than targeting Aliquippa specifically. Three lessons stand for controls engineers: never expose a PLC directly to the internet, no matter how small the site or how convenient the vendor's remote-access story; change default credentials as part of factory acceptance testing, not commissioning (because commissioning always slips); and know your fallback to manual — Aliquippa's saving grace was that operators had practiced running the station without the PLC and could do so immediately.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The security news cycle this week is dominated by IT and cloud-focused items, but several have direct implications for OT teams. Researchers disclosed a 'Cryptographic Context Injection' technique that hides malicious prompts from Grok and Gemini safety guardrails until they're decrypted inside a trusted execution environment. As controls engineers begin experimenting with LLMs for code review, alarm rationalisation, and P&ID analysis, this is a reminder that AI safety controls are still immature and any AI touching engineering documents should be treated as an untrusted intermediary. A new phishing toolkit, iAuthFlow V2, can register an attacker-controlled passkey during a phishing session, giving persistent access even after passwords are reset and sessions revoked. For OT organisations rolling out passkeys and FIDO2 to replace passwords on remote-access jumphosts and engineering portals, response playbooks now need to include an audit of registered authenticators — not just credential rotation — following any suspected compromise. On the supply chain front, North Korean actors were linked to a poisoned version of the Rust 'arrayref' crate that pulled a malicious payload from a remote server. This continues the steady drumbeat of language-ecosystem attacks (npm, PyPI, Rust) that increasingly affect OT-adjacent tools: HMI build pipelines, historian connectors, and Python-based data science stacks pulling from public registries. Pin versions, mirror internally, and treat crate/package updates with the same change-control rigour as PLC firmware. SANS ISC ran a useful week-long series on using Microsoft Graph and PowerShell to hunt for stale accounts, unlicensed users missed in MFA rollouts, and risky sign-ins. For controls engineers, the takeaway is that identity is now a plant-floor problem: many OT vendor cloud portals (AVEVA Connect, Rockwell FactoryTalk Hub, Siemens Xcelerator) federate to your corporate Entra tenant, so a missed MFA enrolment on a contractor account is a path to your engineering data.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added a heavy batch of KEV entries in the past two weeks, and while none are ICS-branded, several sit squarely in the OT attack path. Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD (CVE-2026-20349, DoS via heap inspection) and Cisco Secure FMC (CVE-2026-20316, hard-coded credentials) are widely used as the IT/OT boundary firewall and its management plane; a hard-coded credential bug on the management console is exactly the sort of finding that lets an intruder rewrite ACLs between Purdue levels. Broadcom VMware vCenter path traversal (CVE-2026-59310) is likewise critical for anyone running virtualised historians, MES, or engineering workstations on VMware, and Fortinet FortiOS (CVE-2025-68686) continues the year's theme of Fortinet appliances being exploited at the perimeter. Also notable are the two N-able N-central authentication bypasses (CVE-2026-18556 and the incomplete-patch follow-up CVE-2026-18577). N-central is a common RMM tool used by OT integrators and MSPs managing plant IT assets — a bypass here can hand an attacker administrative reach into every managed endpoint, including engineering laptops. Prioritise verifying that any integrator remote-monitoring tooling touching your environment is patched to the latest build, not just the first fix. The Microsoft IKE double-free (CVE-2026-33824) is also worth attention on any Windows-based IPsec VPN termination used for site-to-site OT tunnels.Read more →
