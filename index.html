Today's Knowledge Bite

The first documented use of a programmable logic controller in production was at a General Motors Hydramatic plant in 1969, where Modicon's 084 replaced a wall of relay logic. The reason GM pushed for it was not reliability or speed — it was the sheer cost and downtime of rewiring the relay panels every model year. The modern practice of "recipe management" in batch control traces its lineage directly to that annual-changeover problem.

⚛

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term exists because of a 1920s problem with ship steering. Nicolas Minorsky, observing helmsmen on the USS New Mexico, noticed they instinctively applied rudder based not just on the current heading error but on how long the error had persisted and how fast it was changing — and he formalised that observation mathematically in 1922. Every temperature loop you tune today is, in a very direct sense, imitating a 1920s quartermaster correcting for a wandering bow.

Tech Spotlight

APL (Advanced Physical Layer) Ethernet is quietly moving from pilot to real deployment in process plants. Ethernet-APL extends 10BASE-T1L over a single twisted pair up to 1,000 metres with intrinsic-safety-compatible power, meaning a Coriolis flow meter or radar level transmitter in a Zone 1 hazardous area can now speak native Ethernet (and HART-IP, PROFINET, or EtherNet/IP) all the way back to the DCS without a fieldbus gateway in between. The practical implication is enormous: engineers get the full device description, diagnostics, and firmware management of the smart instrument without the HART multiplexer, Foundation Fieldbus H1 segment, or Profibus PA coupler acting as a translation bottleneck. It also collapses the demarcation between instrumentation and network engineering — your field device is now an IP endpoint with a MAC address, which means asset inventory tools can finally see it directly. Adoption challenges are real, though. Trained I&C technicians are not yet comfortable with switch-based topologies in the field, APL field switches cost significantly more than a terminal block, and the cybersecurity model for thousands of newly-IP-addressable sensors is still being written. Expect brownfield projects to be hybrid for a decade; greenfield FEEDs starting now should at least evaluate APL for new unit construction.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most frequently cited in EPC specifications this year, and the ISA Global Cybersecurity Alliance has been pushing hard on the companion 62443-2-1 revision covering the asset owner's security program. The 2024 edition of 2-1 reframed the standard around a set of security program elements rather than a prescriptive checklist, which some integrators are still digesting. If your organisation is building or refreshing a CSMS (cyber security management system), 2-1 is the one to map your policies against. Worth watching: NIST is progressing updates to SP 800-82 Rev. 3's companion guidance, and the IEC 62443-4-2 component security requirements continue to show up in procurement language from major end users like Shell, Dow, and Saudi Aramco. If you are a controls engineer writing a PLC or HMI specification, requiring the vendor to demonstrate 4-2 Security Level 2 capability (as a minimum) is becoming table stakes — and increasingly, vendors like Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, and Emerson have the certifications to back the claim. Ask for the certificate, not the marketing slide.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water-treatment intrusion, which the FBI's final reporting later downgraded from "sophisticated cyberattack" to "likely operator error or misconfigured remote access." Regardless of root cause, the facts are instructive: a TeamViewer session on an HMI was observed moving the sodium hydroxide setpoint from roughly 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm, and an alert operator reverted the change within minutes. Multiple engineering workstations shared a single TeamViewer password, Windows 7 was still in use, and the system was reachable from the internet. The lessons map directly onto what we keep seeing in KEV entries like this week's ScreenConnect abuse report. First, remote access to safety-relevant setpoints must be mediated by a jump host with MFA and session recording — never a flat remote-desktop tool with a shared password. Second, independent protection layers matter: a hard-wired high-pH alarm or an interlock on the chemical dosing pump would have caught the setpoint change regardless of how it arrived. Third, operator vigilance is still a control; the HMI mouse moving on its own is exactly the kind of anomaly worth training panel operators to report immediately.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The quieter weekend news cycle is dominated by IT security stories, but several carry direct implications for plant-floor engineers. Fortra patched critical authentication-bypass and shell-execution flaws in its BoKS access-management product, which many large process industries use to centralise privileged access to Unix hosts, historians, and jump servers bridging Levels 2 and 3. If BoKS underpins your OT remote access, patch validation should move up the queue this week. SANS ISC flagged attackers abusing ScreenConnect clients as a living-off-the-land technique. Remote support tools of this class (ScreenConnect, AnyDesk, TeamViewer, VNC variants) are notoriously common on engineering workstations and vendor laptops, often installed outside IT governance. Inventory your OT endpoints for any unsanctioned remote-support agents and ensure your allow-list on the DMZ firewall does not silently permit their call-home traffic. On the geopolitical front, an alleged Mabna Institute operative was extradited to the US — a reminder that Iranian-nexus actors continue to target universities, research, and industrial IP. Separately, a fake Zoom installer dropping the CloudSyncD backdoor on macOS is relevant because engineering and management staff increasingly use Macs for office work that touches OT documentation and VPN access. The Microsoft X account takeover and crypto-scammer stories are peripheral but illustrate how trusted channels keep being weaponised for social engineering — worth a reminder in your next toolbox talk.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks are heavy on the perimeter devices that sit directly in front of, or inside, OT environments. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-76504), Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway (CVE-2026-88771/88772), F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127), Fortinet FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286), Check Point Security Gateway with site-to-site/remote-access VPN (CVE-2026-85102 and 93616), and Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952) are all under active exploitation. Many sites use exactly these products to terminate vendor VPNs, backhaul remote sites, or front engineering portals. All should be treated as emergency change candidates: patch, rotate credentials and certificates, and audit configuration for signs of post-exploitation persistence. Also notable for OT-adjacent infrastructure: MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-67279) is widely deployed in substations, pump stations, and small remote assets as a cheap industrial router; the unauthenticated session/exec flaw there deserves urgent attention. Zyxel GS1900 switches (CVE-2026-7273) are common in SMB plant networks. Three Linux kernel CVEs round out the list — relevant to any Linux-based HMI, historian, or edge gateway where vendors are often slow to ship kernel updates. If your vendor does not publish a patched image promptly, ask them in writing when they will.Read more →
