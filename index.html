☀ Morning Briefing 2026-07-23 09:26 UTC

CISA and partner agencies have re-issued their warning that Iranian threat actors are actively targeting Siemens, Schneider, and Rockwell PLCs using well-documented techniques — exposed engineering ports, default credentials, and abuse of native protocols. If you operate any of these controllers, spend an hour today confirming none are internet-reachable and that default passwords are gone, particularly on remote pump stations, RTUs, and skid-mounted equipment where commissioning shortcuts often persist. Separately, if Check Point firewalls sit between your IT and OT networks, patch CVE-2026-16232 immediately — it is being exploited and grants full admin over your segmentation policy.

Today's Knowledge Bite

KNX, the building automation protocol just added to CISA's KEV list, dates back to 1990 and was originally designed to run over its own dedicated twisted-pair bus at 9600 baud. Security was never part of the original specification because the assumption was that physical access to the bus wire equalled authorisation. KNX Secure, which adds AES-128 authentication and encryption, was only ratified in 2017, and adoption remains under 20% of installed base by most industry estimates.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely: the first mathematical formalisation is credited to Nicolas Minorsky in 1922, who derived it by watching how helmsmen steered US Navy ships. He noticed that experienced helmsmen didn't just correct for current heading error (proportional), but also for how long the ship had been off course (integral) and how fast the heading was changing (derivative). The three-term controller you tune every week was, in essence, reverse-engineered from human intuition nearly a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet using Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) combined with OPC UA over the pub/sub model is finally moving from trade-show demos to real brownfield deployments in 2026. The value proposition is a single converged network that carries motion-control-class deterministic traffic (sub-millisecond, bounded jitter) alongside best-effort IT traffic on the same wire, replacing the current patchwork of EtherCAT, Profinet IRT, Sercos, and Ethernet/IP CIP Motion. For controls engineers, the practical implication is that a future line rebuild might use one type of managed switch, one cable plant, and one engineering tool to configure both the drives and the MES connectivity. Rockwell, Siemens, Beckhoff, and B&R are all shipping TSN-capable I/O and drives now, and the OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) specification released by the OPC Foundation defines controller-to-controller and controller-to-device semantics that were previously vendor-specific. The adoption challenges are real. TSN requires end-to-end support: any non-TSN switch in the path breaks determinism. Configuration of the gPTP time sync, CBS/TAS traffic shapers, and stream reservations is materially more complex than setting a Profinet update rate, and most integrators do not yet have staff fluent in it. Expect greenfield high-value applications (semiconductor, EV battery, high-speed packaging) to lead, with process industries watching from the sidelines for another 3-5 years.Read more →

Standards Watch

ISA/IEC 62443-3-2, the standard covering security risk assessment and system design for IACS, continues to see growing regulatory uptake. The EU's NIS2 directive and the forthcoming Cyber Resilience Act both reference 62443 as an acceptable conformity route for industrial products and operators, and several EU member state regulators have now published mappings showing how 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle) and 62443-4-2 (component security requirements) satisfy CRA essential requirements. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the reference for federal OT environments, and CISA's cross-sector Cyber Performance Goals (CPGs) continue to be the practical checklist most water, energy, and manufacturing sites are being measured against. Notably, the recurring Iranian ICS advisories map cleanly to CPG items 2.A (changing default passwords), 2.B (minimum password strength), and 2.E (separating user and privileged accounts) — items that would have blunted the CyberAv3ngers Unitronics campaign entirely. For controls engineers writing specifications this quarter, referencing IEC 62443-4-2 Security Level 2 as a minimum component requirement in tender documents is now a low-friction way to force vendors to disclose their security posture. Most tier-one PLC and drive vendors have certified product lines; asking for the certificate number in the bid response separates the serious from the aspirational.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's reflection is on the 2023-2024 CyberAv3ngers campaign against Unitronics Vision-series PLCs at US water utilities, which the new CISA advisory directly extends. In November 2023, the Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa, Pennsylvania had a booster station PLC defaced with an anti-Israel message and taken offline, forcing manual operation of a pressure regulation station. Similar hits landed at dozens of small water utilities across multiple states over the following months. The attack was almost embarrassingly simple: the Unitronics PLCs were exposed directly to the internet on TCP/20256 (the PCOM protocol port), and many still used the factory default password of 1111. No zero-day, no supply-chain compromise, no sophisticated tradecraft — just Shodan and a default credential. The attackers uploaded a modified project file that displayed their message on the operator panel and halted the ladder logic. The lessons remain painfully relevant given today's advisory. First, no PLC should ever be directly reachable from the internet; if remote access is required, it goes through an authenticated VPN or purpose-built remote-access broker with MFA. Second, changing default passwords on every field device must be part of commissioning sign-off, not a follow-up action item. Third, having a tested manual-operation procedure for critical control loops meant Aliquippa kept water flowing; sites without that runbook would have faced a genuine service outage.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline item for plant floor engineers today is a joint federal advisory warning that Iranian state-aligned threat actors are actively targeting Siemens, Schneider Electric, and Rockwell Automation programmable logic controllers. The updated advisory reiterates and expands techniques previously seen in the CyberAv3ngers campaigns against water and wastewater utilities, including exploitation of internet-exposed controllers, use of default and weak credentials, abuse of engineering protocols such as CIP and S7comm, and manipulation of HMI screens for psychological effect. Facilities running Unitronics, Siemens S7-1200/1500, Schneider Modicon, or Rockwell ControlLogix families with any degree of remote access exposure should treat this as a high-priority review. Separately, Check Point disclosed CVE-2026-16232, a SmartConsole authentication bypass being exploited in the wild. Because SmartConsole is often used to manage the perimeter firewalls that sit between IT and OT networks, a compromise here could give an attacker administrative control over the very policy enforcement points isolating the process network. Any site using Check Point at the IT/OT boundary should verify configurations against the vendor advisory today rather than waiting for a maintenance window. On the broader front, SANS ISC is tracking active exploitation of a WordPress core SQL injection chain (CVE-2026-63030 combined with CVE-2026-60137) achieving unauthenticated RCE. While WordPress is not an OT system, many plant sites, distributor portals, and vendor documentation servers run on it and often sit inside corporate networks trusted by OT support staff. A compromised WordPress instance is a proven pivot point into engineering workstations via watering-hole techniques. Palo Alto Networks announcing the acquisition of observability firm Embrace is a longer-horizon signal that the major security vendors are absorbing telemetry and APM capabilities. Expect this trend to bleed into OT visibility platforms over the next 12-24 months as vendors push unified IT/OT observability offerings.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The most OT-relevant KEV addition this cycle is CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX Protocol Connection Authorization Option 1, added on 15 July. The flaw allows an attacker to abuse an overly restrictive lockout mechanism to purge devices and lock the BCU key, effectively bricking KNX installations without the additional security options enabled. This affects building automation across HVAC, lighting, and access control in commercial and industrial facilities. Remediation requires enabling KNX Secure or, at minimum, ensuring KNX IP routers are not reachable from untrusted networks. Given KNX often shares infrastructure with process facilities' building services, controls engineers should coordinate with facilities teams to inventory KNX IP gateways. The Check Point SmartConsole flaw (CVE-2026-16232) and the two SonicWall SMA1000 vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-15409/15410) directly affect perimeter and remote-access appliances commonly used to broker vendor and integrator access into OT networks. Patch or mitigate per vendor guidance, and audit recent administrative sessions for anomalies. Two Fortinet FortiSandbox command injection flaws (CVE-2026-25089, CVE-2026-39808) round out the network-appliance additions. Microsoft SharePoint sees three new KEV entries (CVE-2026-50522, CVE-2026-58644, CVE-2026-56164). SharePoint is heavily used for engineering document control, P&IDs, and MOC workflows; a compromised on-prem SharePoint farm has repeatedly served as a launchpad into engineering environments. If you cannot patch immediately, restrict SharePoint access to authenticated corporate users only and disable anonymous document links.Read more →
