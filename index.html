☀ Morning Briefing 2026-05-08 08:23 UTC

Today's priority is patching at the IT/OT boundary: an actively exploited Palo Alto PAN-OS zero-day (CVE-2026-0300) gives unauthenticated root RCE on the Captive Portal, and Ivanti EPMM has another exploited zero-day (CVE-2026-6973) — both are common at the Level 3.5 DMZ. Push your IT team on the perimeter patches today, and start scoping a maintenance window for the B&R and PCM600 updates before the end of the month.

Today's Knowledge Bite

OPC UA's security model uses certificate-based mutual authentication, but in practice many sites run with self-signed certs that engineers manually 'trust' on first connection — the same TOFU model as SSH. The ABB B&R Automation Studio advisory today is essentially a reminder that if the client doesn't strictly validate the server certificate chain, an attacker on the same VLAN can impersonate a controller. Strict trust list management, not just enabling 'Sign & Encrypt,' is what actually delivers the security OPC UA promises.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass autopilot for ships in 1911 already implemented proportional, integral and derivative action mechanically using gears and feedback linkages. Nicolas Minorsky formalised the math in 1922 by carefully watching how an experienced helmsman steered the USS New Mexico, essentially reverse-engineering a human into a transfer function. Every loop you tune today traces its lineage to a sailor's hands on a wheel.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) carrying OPC UA PubSub is finally moving from trade-show demos into real deployments, and it deserves attention. The idea is straightforward: take deterministic, scheduled Ethernet (IEEE 802.1Qbv gating, 802.1AS time sync, 802.1CB frame replication) and run OPC UA's publish-subscribe model on top, so a single converged network can carry motion-control traffic, PLC-to-PLC data, and SCADA polling without the historical zoo of EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, Sercos and CIP Motion. For controls engineers the practical implication is that the line between fieldbus and IT network keeps blurring. Switches must be TSN-capable end-to-end, the engineering tool needs to compute and push schedules (the CNC — Centralized Network Configuration), and a single misconfigured 'best-effort' device can break determinism. Adoption challenges are real: multi-vendor interop is still rough, the configuration tooling is immature compared to PROFINET GSDML workflows, and most plants have brownfield switches that are nowhere near TSN-ready. Expect new builds in semiconductor, automotive and packaging to lead, with process industries lagging by years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues to be the touchstone, and the part most relevant to today's advisories is 62443-4-2 (component requirements) — specifically CR 3.1 (communication integrity) and CR 1.5 (authenticator management), which are exactly what the ABB B&R Automation Studio TLS/OPC-UA impersonation flaw and the Hitachi Energy PCM600 integrity issue map to. If your asset owner contracts already cite 62443-4-1/4-2 certification, these advisories are useful evidence to push vendors on their SDL maturity and patch SLAs. Worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (risk assessment for system design) is increasingly being referenced in regulatory regimes — NIS2 in the EU and the TSA pipeline directives in the US lean on its zone-and-conduit thinking even when not naming it. NIST SP 800-82r3 remains the most accessible companion document for engineers who want concrete guidance without the certification overhead, and it pairs well with the CISA Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) when justifying budget for things as basic as removing shared accounts on engineering workstations.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 attack on the Aliquippa, Pennsylvania municipal water authority, attributed to the Iran-linked CyberAv3ngers group. The attackers compromised an internet-exposed Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station, defaced the HMI, and forced operators to switch to manual control. No water quality impact occurred, but service was disrupted and the incident triggered a joint CISA/FBI/NSA advisory. The root cause was almost embarrassingly simple: the PLC was reachable from the public internet on TCP 20256 with the factory-default password '1111'. There was no VPN, no firewall rule restricting source IPs, and no monitoring on the device. The attackers were not sophisticated — they were opportunistic, scanning Shodan for the Unitronics signature. Three lessons travel well to any plant. First, assume any device with an IP address will be found; Shodan and Censys index PLCs daily. Second, default credentials remain the single highest-leverage finding in OT pen tests — change them, and where the device doesn't support stronger auth, put it behind something that does. Third, segmentation is cheap insurance: a small water authority with two staff doesn't need an SOC, but it does need its PLCs unreachable from the internet, which is a one-line firewall rule.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a notable cluster of ICS advisories today touching products commonly found on plant floors and in substations. ABB B&R Automation Studio carries a flaw in ANSL-over-TLS and OPC-UA connection handling that lets an attacker masquerade as a trusted server, while ABB B&R Automation Runtime (versions <6.5 and the R4.93 branch) has a denial-of-service condition that can stop the runtime — directly relevant for anyone running B&R PLCs in production. A separate PVI advisory addresses log-data information disclosure (mitigated by the fact that logging is off by default). Hitachi Energy PCM600, the configuration tool for Relion protection relays, has an integrity-affecting vulnerability that substation engineers should patch promptly given how widely PCM600 is used in IEC 61850 environments. On the building-automation and access-control side, Johnson Controls CEM AC2000 (versions 10.6, 11.0 and 12.0) has a CVSS 8.7 local privilege escalation that lets a standard user become administrator on the host — a particular concern wherever AC2000 servers are shared with other functions. MAXHUB's Pivot client also got an advisory covering email metadata disclosure and DoS, primarily an IT-adjacent concern but worth noting in conference rooms tied to OT networks. In the wider security world, Palo Alto Networks confirmed an actively exploited PAN-OS zero-day (CVE-2026-0300) in the User-ID Captive Portal showing hallmarks of Chinese state activity, and Ivanti patched another EPMM zero-day (CVE-2026-6973) used in targeted attacks. Both products commonly sit at the IT/OT boundary or manage engineering laptops, so the blast radius reaches into OT. SANS also flagged a second Linux kernel LPE in two weeks, dubbed "Dirty Frag," hot on the heels of "Copy Fail" (CVE-2026-31431), reinforcing that engineering workstations and Linux-based HMIs need a disciplined kernel patch cadence. Finally, RansomHouse claims to have breached Trellix and is leaking screenshots of internal services — a reminder that even security vendors are targets, and OT teams relying on Trellix EDR/XDR should watch for follow-on advisories about telemetry or signing-key exposure.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this week are dominated by perimeter and management-plane bugs that matter to OT because they sit at the IT/OT seam. CVE-2026-0300 in Palo Alto PAN-OS is an unauthenticated out-of-bounds write in the Captive Portal giving root-level RCE on PA-Series and VM-Series — if your DMZ firewalls between corporate and Purdue Level 3.5 are PAN-OS, this is a drop-everything patch. CVE-2026-6973 in Ivanti EPMM requires admin auth but enables RCE on the MDM server that very likely manages your engineers' phones and tablets used for plant rounds. CVE-2026-31431 (the Linux "Copy Fail" kernel LPE) was added on 1 May and is now joined operationally by the newly disclosed "Dirty Frag" — any Linux-based HMI, historian, edge gateway, or engineering VM should be on the patch list. Older entries newly weaponised include ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708), SimpleHelp (CVE-2024-57728/57726) and PaperCut (CVE-2023-27351) — all remote-support and print tools that commonly bridge into OT environments and have ransomware operator interest. Practical action: inventory remote-support tooling allowed through your OT firewalls, confirm versions, and pull anything end-of-life or unpatched off the network.Read more →
