FortiBleed is today's headline: roughly half of internet-facing Fortinet firewalls and VPNs have leaked credentials, and many of those terminate vendor and integrator access into plants. Treat any account that touched an exposed FortiGate as compromised — rotate it, including service accounts used by SCADA vendors and EPCs. Today's Knowledge Bite

The PLC scan cycle as we know it — read inputs, solve logic, write outputs — was not invented for safety or determinism but for memory economy. In the late 1960s Dick Morley's original Modicon 084 had so little RAM that snapshotting I/O once per cycle was the only way to keep logic-solving consistent without expensive interlocks. The architectural quirk became the defining feature of an entire industry.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics. Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analysing how helmsmen steered the USS New Mexico, observing that good helmsmen instinctively applied corrections proportional to error, anticipated future error from its rate of change, and compensated for steady drift by integrating past error. The mathematics that runs in every modern controller was essentially reverse-engineered from human intuition aboard a battleship — and Minorsky's paper still reads as a remarkably clear description of what your loops are doing right now.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is quietly moving from specification to silicon. Released as a set of companion specifications by the OPC Foundation, FX extends OPC UA below the controller layer into field devices, with the goal of replacing the patchwork of fieldbuses between PLCs, drives, and I/O blocks with a single information model that runs over standard Ethernet — and, when deterministic timing is required, over Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN). The practical implication is that a drive, a safety controller, and a vision system from three different vendors could expose semantically rich, discoverable data using the same model used by your SCADA northbound. For controls engineers that means less time writing custom AOIs to scrape Modbus or proprietary tags, and clearer paths for diagnostics and asset management. It also means PLC-to-PLC communication across brands becomes a configuration exercise rather than a gateway-and-prayer project. Adoption is the catch. TSN-capable switches and endpoints are still pricier than standard managed switches, vendor implementations of the FX profiles vary in maturity, and the IEC 62443 security overlay for UA FX must be configured carefully — certificates and trust lists at field level are a new operational burden. Expect to see FX first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor plants, then trickle into process industries over the next three to five years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2, the standard governing security risk assessment for system design, continues to be the most-cited but least-consistently-applied part of the family. The IEC working groups have been refining guidance on zone and conduit definition to better handle modern realities like cloud-connected historians and remote engineering workstations, where the classical Purdue boundaries no longer fit cleanly. If you are scoping a new project, the current best practice is to define zones by required security level (SL-T) rather than by physical network segment, and to document conduits with explicit data-flow direction and protocol — auditors increasingly ask to see this artifact rather than a network diagram alone. In parallel, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 has now been in the field long enough that its emphasis on OT-specific overlays of the SP 800-53 controls is showing up in customer security questionnaires from asset owners. If your organisation builds or integrates systems for US critical infrastructure, expect to be asked which 800-82r3 overlays you implement, particularly around remote access, supply chain, and recovery. Finally, ISA's TR84.00.09 on cybersecurity for safety instrumented systems remains the practical reference for the SIS-versus-BPCS-network question. The short version: a SIS should reside in its own zone with conduits that are read-only outward where possible, and any engineering access path should require multi-factor authentication and session recording. Recent VPN bypass CVEs make that last point newly urgent.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, in which the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised an internet-exposed Unitronics Vision-series PLC at a booster station. The attackers did not exploit a sophisticated vulnerability — the device was reachable on its default port 20256, used the default password '1111', and was directly addressable from the public internet. They defaced the HMI screen and the operator took the station offline; manual control kept water flowing, but the disruption was real and the political message was unmistakable. What made this incident significant was its sheer ordinariness. There was no zero-day, no insider, no nation-state malware — just a small utility that had connected a PLC to the internet for vendor convenience and never changed the credentials. CISA's follow-up advisories found dozens of similarly exposed Unitronics devices across US water systems within days. The lessons for controls engineers are unglamorous but enduring. First, never expose a PLC or HMI directly to the internet, even behind a non-standard port; use a properly authenticated remote-access broker or jump host. Second, change default credentials as part of commissioning sign-off, and have a written record that this was done. Third, design for graceful degradation: Aliquippa kept water moving because operators could run the station manually, which is a control-system design choice as much as a procedural one. Resilience at the process layer buys you the time to recover at the cyber layer.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The week's dominant story for anyone running an industrial DMZ is FortiBleed: roughly 86,000 internet-accessible Fortinet firewalls and VPN concentrators — about half the exposed population — have had credentials harvested in a large-scale campaign. For plants that rely on FortiGate appliances to terminate vendor remote-access tunnels or to segment IT and OT, this is a credential rotation event, not just a patching event. Assume any account that has authenticated through an exposed FortiGate since the campaign began is compromised, including service accounts used by integrators and SCADA vendors. The Klue supply-chain breach is a quieter but instructive story. Attackers pivoted through Klue's Salesforce integration to exfiltrate data from downstream customers including Huntress and Recorded Future. The lesson for OT teams is that third-party SaaS connected to your CRM, ticketing, or asset-management systems is now a credible path to information about your plants — site lists, network diagrams attached to support tickets, and integrator contacts are all attractive reconnaissance targets. On the malware front, CryptoBandits has been observed combining infostealer behaviour with a SOCKS5-over-Tor backdoor, giving operators interactive remote access while masquerading as commodity crimeware. That blurring of categories matters: an alert tagged as a low-priority stealer on an engineering workstation may actually be a foothold for hands-on-keyboard intrusion. Meanwhile SANS ISC flagged phishing using IPv4-mapped IPv6 addresses to evade URL filters, a reminder to verify that OT proxy and DNS controls actually inspect IPv6 traffic rather than blindly passing it. On the geopolitical and corporate side, Macron's call for democratic coordination on AI regulation and Cisco's acquisition of WideField to bolster Splunk's agentic SOC both point at the same underlying trend — AI-driven detection and response is becoming the default assumption in IT SOCs, which will eventually push OT teams to articulate what 'agentic' monitoring should and should not do near live process control.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV additions over the past two weeks are heavily weighted toward perimeter and identity infrastructure that sits directly in front of OT environments. CVE-2026-50751 in Check Point Security Gateway allows unauthenticated VPN access by bypassing IKEv1 authentication and is already linked to ransomware activity. CVE-2026-0257 in Palo Alto PAN-OS similarly permits unauthorised VPN establishment. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager picked up two entries — CVE-2026-20262 (path traversal) and CVE-2026-20245 (root command execution via crafted file) — both relevant where SD-WAN underlays multi-site plant connectivity. If your remote-access path to a site relies on any of these, treat patching as urgent and audit VPN session logs for anomalies. Several application-layer flaws also warrant attention. CVE-2026-20253 in Splunk Enterprise allows unauthenticated file creation or truncation via a PostgreSQL sidecar — relevant if you've extended Splunk into OT for historian or syslog aggregation. CVE-2026-35273 in Oracle PeopleSoft is being used by ransomware crews, and CVE-2024-21182 in Oracle WebLogic was added as well; both commonly appear in plant business systems and MES adjuncies. CVE-2022-0492, a Linux kernel cgroups v1 privilege-escalation, is an older bug now formally flagged, worth checking on any long-lived Linux-based HMIs, edge gateways, or virtualised historians. Practically, prioritise: patch or mitigate Check Point and PAN-OS VPNs this weekend, rotate Fortinet credentials, audit SD-WAN Manager access, and add the older Linux and WebLogic CVEs to your next maintenance window rather than emergency change.Read more →
