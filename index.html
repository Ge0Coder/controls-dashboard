Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix-style serial device server has been quietly carrying production-critical Modbus RTU and DF1 traffic in many plants for over two decades — often the single longest-lived networked device on the floor. Many installed units still run firmware from before TLS 1.2 was standardised, yet they sit on flat networks routable from corporate IT. The original 'tunnel serial over TCP' design philosophy assumed a physically isolated network, an assumption that virtually no modern plant actually satisfies.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively reverse-engineered from the human helmsman. Nicholas Minorsky's 1922 paper on automatic ship steering for the USS New Mexico explicitly modelled how experienced helmsmen anticipated the ship's drift (derivative action) and corrected for persistent crosswinds (integral action), not just current heading error (proportional). Every loop you tune today still encodes the cognitive habits of a 1920s sailor at a wheel.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) is moving from spec to silicon this year, and it's worth understanding because it directly targets the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device layer that has historically belonged to EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, and EtherCAT. OPC UA FX extends classic OPC UA with deterministic communication over Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN), standardised offline engineering descriptors, and a peer-to-peer publish-subscribe model that doesn't require a central server. The pitch: one protocol from sensor to cloud, with security and information modelling baked in rather than bolted on. Practically, what this means for a controls engineer is that a Beckhoff PLC, a Siemens drive, and a Rockwell I/O block could — in theory — exchange cyclic process data on the same TSN-enabled switch fabric without protocol gateways. Early demonstrators at Hannover and SPS shows have proven the concept, and Siemens, B&R, Phoenix Contact, and Beckhoff all have shipping or near-shipping FX-capable hardware. The adoption challenges are real, though. TSN configuration is still painful — CNC (Centralised Network Configuration) tools are vendor-specific and immature, and most plant switches aren't TSN-capable. Certificate management for OPC UA at scale (thousands of field devices) remains an unsolved operational problem in most shops. Expect FX to land first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines where the integrator owns the entire stack, and to take five-plus years to displace fieldbus in brownfield process plants.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often cited in OT procurement contracts this year, particularly as the EU NIS2 directive's national transpositions mature and operators of essential services need defensible documentation of their zone-and-conduit models. If you haven't formally mapped your plant into zones and conduits with documented security level targets (SL-T), expect that question from auditors and from your cyber-insurance underwriter within the next renewal cycle. On the product side, IEC 62443-4-2 certification (component security requirements) is becoming a de-facto purchasing filter for new PLCs, HMIs, and network gear. Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, ABB, and Honeywell all now have certified product lines at SL 2 or higher. The practical takeaway: when specifying replacement controllers, ask for the 4-2 certificate and the security capability matrix, not just a marketing claim of 'IEC 62443 compliant' — the two are very different things. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the most readable free reference for OT security architecture and is well-aligned with 62443's concepts. For engineers building internal standards, pairing 800-82 Rev. 3 with the ISA-62443-2-1 program requirements gives you a defensible programme structure without paying for the full IEC document set up front.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful one to revisit this week given the SimpleHelp KEV entry is the 2024 CDK Global ransomware incident, which crippled roughly 15,000 North American car dealerships for nearly three weeks. While CDK is an IT/SaaS provider rather than a controls platform, the initial access vector — abuse of remote management tooling combined with weak segmentation between the SaaS multi-tenant environment and customer endpoints — mirrors exactly the SimpleHelp/ScreenConnect pattern now being actively exploited against industrial integrators. What happened: attackers established persistence via remote support tooling, escalated through shared service accounts, and detonated ransomware across the hosted environment. Customers had no local fallback because the systems were SaaS-only, and recovery took weeks. The parallel for manufacturing is the integrator who supports twenty plants from one engineering workstation via a single remote-support tool. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, treat your integrator's remote-access tool as part of your attack surface and require evidence of patching cadence and MFA — contractually. Second, ensure offline engineering backups (PLC programs, HMI projects, drive parameters) exist on physically separated media so you can rebuild without vendor cloud access. Third, run a tabletop exercise where your primary integrator is unavailable for three weeks — most plants discover uncomfortable single points of knowledge in the first hour.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The most directly relevant story for plant-floor engineers this week is CISA's advisory on three vulnerabilities in Daktronics highway sign and billboard controllers. While these aren't process-critical assets, they're a textbook example of internet-exposed field controllers running outdated firmware with weak authentication — the same architecture pattern that plagues water, transport, and energy SCADA edge devices. Expect copycat scanning across similar embedded controller fleets, and treat it as a prompt to audit any vendor-managed signage, lighting, or display systems on your sites. The SimpleHelp remote support tool is again being actively exploited, this time via an OIDC authentication bypass (CVE-2026-48558) that allows attackers to skip token signature verification. Many OEMs and integrators use SimpleHelp or similar tools for remote PLC/HMI support. If your site allows a system integrator to dial in via SimpleHelp, BeyondTrust, ScreenConnect, or comparable tools, this is the week to verify patch status and confirm jump-host isolation from the control network. The Oracle PeopleSoft campaign — now confirmed to have hit Nissan employee data — leverages CVE-2026-35273, a missing-authentication flaw already on CISA's KEV with known ransomware association. PeopleSoft itself is enterprise IT, but the broader lesson is the IT/OT identity bridge: HR and ERP compromises frequently lead to lateral movement into manufacturing execution systems and historians that share Active Directory. On the broader theme, SecurityWeek's piece on agentic-AI token costs in SecOps is worth a glance for engineers considering AI-augmented anomaly detection in OT. The economics of running LLM-based detection against high-volume PLC telemetry are non-trivial, and most current vendor pitches gloss over per-event inference costs. Finally, SANS ISC continues to highlight reconnaissance automation — favicon hashing for host discovery being the latest. This is the same technique Shodan and Censys use to fingerprint exposed HMIs and engineering workstations, so assume any web-facing OT asset is already indexed.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The June KEV additions skew heavily toward remote access and identity infrastructure that sits adjacent to OT networks. SimpleHelp (CVE-2026-48558), Ivanti Sentry (CVE-2026-10520, unauth root RCE), Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 bypass (CVE-2026-50751, ransomware-linked), and Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-20262, CVE-2026-20245) collectively define the perimeter most plants rely on for vendor access and site-to-site connectivity. Any of these in your DMZ or remote-access path warrants emergency patching this week. For industrial-specific entries, PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569) is the one to flag — unauth RCE in PLM software used heavily in discrete manufacturing for engineering BoMs, CAD, and product data. Lantronix EDS5000 (CVE-2025-67038) is more pointed: that's a serial-to-Ethernet device gateway commonly found bridging legacy RS-232/485 instrumentation to TCP/IP, and a root-level command injection here gives an attacker a foothold directly on the control network edge. Inventory your Lantronix and similar terminal servers and confirm they are not exposed beyond a tightly controlled management VLAN. The Ubiquiti UniFi OS trio (CVE-2026-34908/09/10) deserves attention because UniFi has crept into many smaller OT shops and remote pumping/lift stations as a cheap managed-switch option. Treat these as control-network assets and patch accordingly; assume they are not hardened by default.Read more →
