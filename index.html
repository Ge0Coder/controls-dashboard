Today's Knowledge Bite

The KNX protocol, ubiquitous in European building automation, still ships in many installations with a factory BCU key of 0xFFFFFFFF — effectively no key at all. Because changing the key requires physical or authenticated access to every device on the line, integrators frequently leave it default to avoid a truck roll. This is why a protocol designed in the early 1990s continues to generate CVEs three decades later.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronic computers by nearly a century — Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 by carefully observing how helmsmen steered the USS New Mexico, noticing they applied rudder proportional not just to heading error but also to how fast the error was changing and how long it had persisted. His paper described human cognition as an analog control algorithm. Nearly every process loop running in your plant today is, in a very real sense, a mathematical model of an early 20th-century US Navy sailor's intuition.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) combined with OPC UA PubSub is quietly moving from vendor demos into real greenfield projects in 2026. The premise is that a single converged network can carry motion control traffic with sub-microsecond jitter alongside conventional SCADA polling and even video, eliminating the parallel EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, and Sercos networks that today force engineers to specify and stock separate switches, cables, and NICs. In practice, adoption is throttled by two things. First, the TSN standard is really a toolbox of eight or so sub-standards (IEEE 802.1AS, Qbv, Qbu, CB, and friends), and vendors implement different subsets — a Beckhoff TSN switch and a Siemens TSN drive may not interoperate cleanly without careful profile matching. Second, network engineering skills required to design a schedule-based network (with gate control lists and cycle times) are closer to those of a controls engineer than a traditional IT netadmin, creating an awkward org-chart gap. The practical implication is that if you are specifying a new line in the next 18 months, ask vendors specifically which IEC/IEEE 60802 industrial TSN profile they support, not just "TSN capable." Otherwise you risk paying premium hardware prices for a network that still needs VLAN gymnastics to actually deliver determinism.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component requirements) continue to dominate procurement language, and increasingly asset owners are requiring vendors to demonstrate 4-2 SL 2 capability certification before a PLC or RTU can even enter the bid list. If you are on the specifying side, the most useful lever right now is requiring the vendor's ISASecure CSA certificate number in the technical bid — not just a marketing claim of "62443 compliant." On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the operative guide for OT security, and CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are being folded into water-sector regulatory expectations in the wake of the multi-state attacks reported this week. EPA's memorandum requiring cybersecurity assessments as part of sanitary surveys was reinstated earlier this year, and the current incident wave will almost certainly accelerate enforcement. Worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 was updated in 2024 to align the security program requirements with the risk assessment model in 3-2, and many organizations that certified against the older 2010 edition are now discovering their programs need meaningful rework — not just documentation refresh — to conform.Read more →

Incident of the Week

The Clayton County, Georgia water utility pump station disruption, disclosed this week as part of the broader 12-state water sector attack pattern, is this week's incident of note. Public details remain sparse, but the disruption reportedly involved unauthorized manipulation of pump control logic, consistent with the pattern seen at Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa in 2023 and Muleshoe, Texas in 2024, where internet-exposed Unitronics and similar PLCs with default or weak credentials were reached directly over TCP 20256 and manipulated. The common failure mode across all three incidents is depressingly consistent: HMIs or PLCs reachable from the public internet, either directly or via a poorly secured cellular modem, with either default credentials or no authentication on the programming port. In several cases the operator was unaware the device was internet-exposed because a contractor had configured remote access years earlier and moved on. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, run an external scan of your own public IP ranges quarterly — Shodan and Censys will find your exposed devices before an attacker does, and often faster than your IT team. Second, treat the PLC programming port as equivalent to a root shell: it must never be reachable from anything but an engineering workstation on a controlled VLAN. Third, if you inherit a site, assume undocumented remote access exists until you have physically traced every cellular modem, 4G router, and vendor VPN tunnel on the property.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline event for OT operators is the reported wave of cyberattacks against US water utilities spanning at least 12 states, with Clayton County, Georgia confirming a pump station disruption. This is a significant escalation from the isolated water sector intrusions of prior years and suggests coordinated targeting of small and mid-sized water systems that typically lack dedicated OT security staff. Plant floor engineers at water and wastewater facilities should assume active reconnaissance and verify that HMI and PLC interfaces are not directly reachable from the internet, that VPN access is MFA-protected, and that setpoint changes are logged and alerted on. On the supply chain front, the ChainDrop attack has infected over 400 npm packages with credential-stealing malware that self-propagates via compromised npm and GitHub tokens. While npm is not typically a direct dependency of PLC firmware, it is deeply embedded in modern SCADA web front-ends, historian dashboards, engineering workstation tooling, and vendor cloud portals. Any Node-based custom HMI or reporting stack should have its lockfiles audited and CI/CD tokens rotated this week. Black Hat USA 2026 is generating the usual flood of vendor announcements. Notably, Oligo secured $60M for runtime application security, reflecting continued investor appetite for eBPF-based monitoring that has begun filtering into OT-adjacent Linux workloads such as edge gateways and virtualised historians. SANS ISC also flagged a botnet actively scanning for vulnerabilities in diagnostic tool URLs — worth checking your perimeter logs for probes against vendor web-based diagnostic endpoints commonly exposed by drives, meters, and building controllers. Finally, phishing campaigns are increasingly impersonating AI service providers such as ChatGPT and Anthropic, targeting engineers who have integrated LLMs into daily workflows. If your team uses AI-assisted PLC code review or documentation tools, treat any credential prompt with the same suspicion you would a bank login.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added two N-able N-central authentication bypass vulnerabilities to KEV this week (CVE-2026-18556 and the follow-up CVE-2026-18577, which patches an incomplete fix for the first). N-central is widely deployed by MSPs that manage remote IT and increasingly OT infrastructure at small utilities and manufacturers — an authentication bypass here means an attacker who reaches the RMM console can push arbitrary scripts and software to every managed endpoint, which historically has included jump hosts and engineering workstations. If your site is managed by an MSP using N-central, demand written confirmation of patching this week. Directly relevant to building and facility controls, CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX protocol's Connection Authorization Option 1 was added on 15 July. The flaw's overly restrictive lockout mechanism lets an attacker purge devices and set the BCU key to lock out legitimate maintenance — effectively a denial-of-service against building automation with painful recovery. Any KNX installation should have Secure mode enabled and the IP interface segmented off the general corporate network. Also worth noting: the Cisco Secure FMC hard-coded password (CVE-2026-20316), Fortinet FortiSandbox command injections, and repeated SharePoint deserialization flaws all sit on the IT/OT boundary. Firewall management planes and document servers holding P&IDs, control narratives, and PLC backups are high-value pivots into the process network.Read more →
