Today's Knowledge Bite

KNX, the protocol featured in this week's KEV addition, traces its origins to the 1990 European Installation Bus and is standardised under ISO/IEC 14543-3. What surprises many engineers is that a single KNX twisted pair can carry both 29V DC bus power and telegrams to over 250 devices per line, with up to 61,455 devices in a fully expanded installation — all without a central controller, using pure peer-to-peer CSMA/CA arbitration.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades. Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilot from 1911 already used proportional and derivative action to counter rudder overshoot, and Nicolas Minorsky formalised the three-term equation in 1922 while analysing the USS New Mexico's steering — observing that a helmsman intuitively applied not just current error but also its rate of change and accumulated history. The tuning rules we still use daily, Ziegler-Nichols, came 20 years later in 1942 from pneumatic controller work at Taylor Instrument. So the next time you tune a loop, you're using a technique older than the transistor, born from watching a sailor steer a battleship.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the field-level extension of OPC UA formally released by the OPC Foundation and now steadily appearing in controller firmware from Siemens, Rockwell, Beckhoff, and B&R. OPC UA FX extends the familiar client-server and pub-sub models down to controller-to-controller and eventually controller-to-device communication, with deterministic behaviour when combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN). The promise is a single, vendor-neutral information model spanning field devices, PLCs, edge, and cloud — replacing the current patchwork of PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, EtherCAT, and Modbus at least at the coordination layer. For controls engineers the practical implication is significant: standardised motion, safety, and diagnostic companion specs mean a servo drive from vendor A can, in principle, be swapped with vendor B's without rewriting the application layer. Combined with TSN's bounded latency (sub-millisecond cycle times are achievable), OPC UA FX becomes viable for hard real-time control, not just supervisory data. Adoption challenges remain real. TSN requires managed switches with IEEE 802.1Qbv/Qbu support throughout the path, which most brownfield sites don't have. Certificate management at the field level is operationally painful — nobody wants to renew PKI on 500 I/O blocks. And the companion specifications for many device classes are still maturing. Expect real production deployments to remain limited to greenfield high-end machinery through 2027, with broader uptake following as switch prices drop.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-4-2, which specifies technical security requirements for IEC components (embedded devices, network devices, host devices, software applications), continues to be the standard OEMs are racing to certify against. Recent months have seen a noticeable uptick in Siemens, Schneider, and Rockwell product families achieving 4-2 SL2 certifications, and asset owners increasingly cite the standard in tender documents. If you're specifying a new PLC or RTU this quarter, ask the vendor for the certification report — not just the marketing claim — and check which security level the component achieves and under what configuration. On the process side, ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) is where most site engineering teams still struggle. The standard requires zone and conduit definition based on tolerable risk, but many organisations conflate this with network segmentation exercises. The distinction matters: a zone is a grouping of assets with a common security requirement, not merely a VLAN. Recent ISA training materials have leaned harder on this point. NIST is progressing SP 800-82 Rev 3 uptake, which explicitly aligns with the NIST CSF 2.0 Govern function added in 2024. For US-regulated sectors and federal contractors, expect audit questions about how OT-specific risk feeds into enterprise governance — a gap that has historically been papered over. The CMMC Phase 2 pause announced this week doesn't change the underlying DFARS 252.204-7012 obligation to protect CUI, so defence supply chain manufacturers should keep their OT enclave work moving.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's incident is the ongoing Nichirei cyberattack, disclosed 13 July 2026. Nichirei, one of Japan's largest frozen food producers, disconnected corporate and manufacturing systems in response to what appears to be a ransomware event, halting production and order processing across multiple plants. As of the weekend the company is 'gradually restoring operations' — meaning at least six days of disruption in a cold-chain business where temperature-sensitive inventory has hard economic clocks. While Nichirei hasn't disclosed the intrusion vector, the pattern matches recent food sector incidents (JBS 2021, Dole 2023, Americold's affiliates): initial access through IT (phishing, VPN, or exposed remote access), lateral movement to shared authentication infrastructure, then blast radius extending into MES, warehouse management, and freezer telemetry historians. Even when PLCs and DCS remain untouched, loss of MES and shipping systems is enough to halt production because operators can't confirm lot codes, allergens, or dispatch schedules. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, offline production capability matters: can your line run on local HMIs and paper batch records for 72 hours if the MES is gone? Test it. Second, cold-chain telemetry (freezer temps, ammonia refrigeration alarms) should have an independent monitoring path that survives an IT outage — a standalone SCADA with SMS alerting is not old-fashioned, it's resilient. Third, restore priority ordering should be an engineering decision, not just an IT one; the historian and the recipe management server often need to come back before ERP.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The week's most operationally significant story for plant floor engineers is the cyberattack on Japanese frozen food giant Nichirei, which forced the company to disconnect systems on 13 July and is only now gradually restoring operations. Food and beverage manufacturing continues to be a favoured ransomware target because perishable inventory and just-in-time logistics create enormous pressure to pay quickly. Expect knock-on effects in cold chain visibility and possible order fulfilment delays for downstream customers over the coming weeks. In defence manufacturing, ransomware has hit German naval shipbuilder TKMS (ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems), a reminder that shipyard OT environments — welding automation, CNC, PLM systems like Windchill — remain soft targets despite classified-adjacent work. Separately, SecurityWeek reports Iran-linked activity tracking US military phones, which while primarily a mobility issue, has implications for engineers who carry work phones onto sensitive sites and use them for HMI photos, vendor calls, or Teams-based remote support. Microsoft's July Patch Tuesday was extraordinary in scale — 622 Microsoft CVEs plus 427 in Chromium, 62 rated critical, with two already exploited in the wild. For OT sites this means engineering workstations, historian servers, and jump hosts running Windows Server will need staged patching over the next maintenance windows. Two of the exploited items relate to SharePoint (see KEV section), which is heavily used for engineering document control and MOC workflows in many process plants. On the tooling side, Beacon Security and Risk Ledger both closed significant funding rounds ($13M and $32M), reflecting continued investor appetite for asset visibility and supply chain risk platforms. Risk Ledger in particular is relevant to procurement and engineering leaders trying to get a handle on third-party integrator and OEM exposure — an area regulators are increasingly scrutinising under NIS2 and equivalent regimes.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The standout OT-relevant addition this week is CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX Protocol Connection Authorization Option 1, added to KEV on 15 July. KNX is widely deployed in building automation — HVAC, lighting, access control — and this flaw lets an attacker abuse an overly restrictive lockout mechanism to purge all devices and set the BCU key to a locked state, effectively bricking a KNX installation. Facilities engineers should verify that KNX IP interfaces are not exposed to enterprise networks, enable KNX Secure where supported, and rotate BCU keys where feasible. This is a rare KEV entry that directly names an industrial protocol. On the IT side but highly relevant to OT boundary systems: three SharePoint CVEs (2026-58644, 2026-56164, 2026-45659) covering unauthenticated deserialization RCE and authentication bypass — patch engineering document repositories immediately. Two Fortinet FortiSandbox command injection flaws (CVE-2026-25089, 2026-39808) affect a product commonly deployed in front of DMZ email and file transfer paths into OT. SonicWall SMA1000 (CVE-2026-15409/15410) is frequently used as the remote access VPN for third-party integrators — verify appliance versions this week. Also noteworthy: PTC Windchill/FlexPLM (CVE-2026-12569) unauthenticated RCE hits directly at the engineering PLM layer used for CAD, BOM, and manufacturing execution integration. SimpleHelp (CVE-2026-48558) OIDC signature bypass affects a remote support tool many OEMs use to reach into customer sites — audit any inbound SimpleHelp deployments.Read more →
