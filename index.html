Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Modbus protocol, published by Modicon in 1979, was designed to run over RS-232 at 9600 baud with no authentication because the physical wire was the security boundary — anyone connected to it was, by definition, trusted. Nearly half a century later, the majority of Modbus TCP deployments still inherit that same trust model, which is why a single misconfigured firewall rule can expose thousands of coils and registers to the open internet. Shodan routinely indexes over 20,000 Modbus endpoints on TCP/502 worldwide.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely — the first mathematically-analysed three-term controller was described by Nicolas Minorsky in 1922 for automatic ship steering on the USS New Mexico, using purely mechanical and hydraulic elements. Minorsky derived the derivative term by watching how experienced helmsmen anticipated the ship's swing rather than reacting to its current heading, essentially reverse-engineering human intuition into an equation. Every temperature loop you tune today is a direct descendant of a naval officer trying to keep a battleship pointed straight in heavy seas.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is Ethernet-APL (Advanced Physical Layer), which is quietly transforming field instrumentation in process industries. Ethernet-APL extends standard 10BASE-T1L two-wire Ethernet with intrinsic safety and bus power, allowing a single twisted pair to carry both power and 10 Mbit/s Ethernet up to 1,000 metres — even into Zone 0 hazardous areas. In practice, this means a Coriolis flowmeter or pH probe in a hazardous chemical plant can speak native TCP/IP (typically PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, or OPC UA) instead of being throttled through a HART multiplexer or 4-20 mA loop. The practical implication is enormous bandwidth uplift for diagnostics and asset management: instead of polling a single process variable every few seconds, you can stream full device telemetry, valve signatures, and secondary variables continuously to a historian or edge analytics platform. Adoption challenges remain, however. APL requires new field switches, new instrument electronics, and — critically — a rethink of cybersecurity, because every field device now has an IP address. Segmentation, certificate management, and secure onboarding (using standards like OPC UA GDS or FDI) become field-level concerns rather than control-room ones. Expect Ethernet-APL to appear in more greenfield process projects through 2027, particularly in pharma and specialty chemicals where data richness justifies the capex.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to gain traction as the de facto reference cited in procurement contracts, particularly in Europe following NIS2 enforcement. The interesting recent development is how asset owners are being pulled into the standard: historically 62443-4-1 and 4-2 were vendor concerns (secure development and component security), but 3-2's zone-and-conduit methodology squarely lands on the end user. If you haven't produced a formal zone/conduit diagram with target security levels (SL-T) for your plant, that's now a common gap flagged in audits. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the practical reference for OT security programs, and CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are increasingly being mapped against it for critical infrastructure sectors. ISA is also progressing work on ISA/IEC 62443-2-2 (security program ratings), which will provide a scoring methodology for how mature an operator's cybersecurity management system actually is — expect insurers and regulators to lean on this once it stabilises. For engineers, the pragmatic action is to ensure your change-management, backup, and remote-access procedures are documented in a form that maps cleanly onto both 62443-2-1 and NIST 800-82 controls; auditors are increasingly asking for evidence, not policy statements.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster pump station. The attackers didn't exploit a sophisticated zero-day — the PLC was exposed to the internet with the default password '1111' on TCP/20256, and the group defaced the HMI screen with a political message. Operators noticed only because the PLC went into a fault state and an alarm fired; manual operation kept water service uninterrupted. The root causes were mundane and repeat across the sector: a controller placed directly on the public internet by an integrator, a default credential never changed at commissioning, and no network-level monitoring to detect the connection. Three lessons stand out for controls engineers. First, never accept an integrator handover without verifying that no controller, HMI, or gateway is directly internet-reachable — use Shodan or Censys against your own public IP ranges as a sanity check. Second, treat vendor default credentials as an incident-in-waiting; enforce a commissioning checklist that requires evidence of credential rotation. Third, ensure your safety and manual-operation procedures are practised, because they were the reason Aliquippa was a headline rather than a public health event.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline concern for OT-adjacent teams this week is GigaWiper, a multi-stage destructive toolkit that bundles a standalone wiper, ransomware-style encryption, and a multi-pass overwrite command into a single backdoor. While no confirmed ICS-targeted campaign has been attributed yet, the design pattern echoes prior industrial disruptors like Industroyer2 and CaddyWiper — sabotage first, extortion as cover. Any Windows-based engineering workstation, historian, or HMI is a plausible landing point, so segmentation and offline configuration backups deserve a hard look today. Two AI-flavoured stories are also worth noting. 'HalluSquatting' abuses the fact that LLM coding assistants routinely hallucinate non-existent package names; attackers register those names on public repositories with malicious payloads, turning AI-suggested imports into a supply-chain botnet vector. In parallel, researchers uncovered a network of roughly 200 GitHub repositories chaining Go modules and PowerShell loaders to fetch payloads from dead-drop resolvers. Both stories reinforce that anyone pulling code, libraries, or scripts into engineering laptops — including sample PLC drivers or Python OPC UA clients — needs a curated, pinned dependency policy. On the vendor front, Palo Alto Networks issued fixes for 13 PAN-OS vulnerabilities spanning buffer overflow, command injection, SSRF, and authentication bypass. Since PAN firewalls frequently sit at the IT/OT boundary in Purdue level 3.5 DMZs, this patch cycle should be prioritised in your next change window. The UK government's rollout of an 'agentic AI defense plan' also flags the direction regulators are heading: expect procurement questionnaires to soon ask how you validate AI-assisted engineering tools before they touch production.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks include several entries that directly affect OT and adjacent infrastructure. CVE-2025-67038 in the Lantronix EDS5000 serial device server is particularly notable — it allows OS command injection via the username field, executed as root. EDS devices are commonly deployed as serial-to-Ethernet gateways for legacy PLCs, meters, and RTUs, and are frequently forgotten during patch cycles. Inventory them, restrict management interfaces to a jump host, and apply Lantronix's advisory immediately. Similarly, CVE-2026-12569 in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM enables unauthenticated remote code execution; these PLM systems often hold the master CAD, BoM, and change-control data for manufacturing operations. Three Ubiquiti UniFi OS flaws (CVE-2026-34908/34909/34910) cover command injection, path traversal, and improper access control from the local network. UniFi gear increasingly shows up in smaller plants and remote pump stations as 'good enough' networking; if any of it is reachable from process VLANs, treat it as urgent. Additional entries — Cisco Unified CM SSRF, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager path traversal, Splunk Enterprise missing auth, Adobe ColdFusion path traversal, and SimpleHelp OIDC signature bypass — matter because these platforms often live in the IT/OT DMZ or support remote-access workflows. Prioritise anything with unauthenticated RCE and network-facing exposure first, then move to authenticated vectors.Read more →
