Today's Knowledge Bite

The RADIUS protocol vulnerability behind today's Schneider Modicon advisory (Blast-RADIUS) exploits an MD5 chosen-prefix collision in a protocol designed in 1991 — and RADIUS is still the default authentication backend for most managed industrial switches, firewalls, and remote-access gateways. The fix isn't a code patch alone; it requires enabling Message-Authenticator attributes on both client and server, which many older PLCs and field devices simply don't support. That asymmetry is why so many OT networks will remain exposed long after IT has moved on.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) is finally moving from trade-show demos to real plant deployments, particularly in automotive body shops and semiconductor fabs. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 extensions to standard Ethernet that adds deterministic scheduling, bounded latency, and clock synchronization (via 802.1AS, a profile of PTP) so that motion control and safety traffic can share a wire with video, file transfers, and OPC UA pub/sub. The promise is the end of parallel fieldbus runs: one converged Ethernet fabric carrying everything from a 31.25 µs servo update to a corporate backup. The practical reality is messier. TSN requires every switch and end-device in the deterministic path to support the same set of standards — and vendors have picked different subsets. Configuration is centralized through a Centralized Network Configuration (CNC) entity, but interoperability between, say, a Siemens Scalance switch and a B&R controller still typically needs vendor-blessed reference architectures. OPC UA over TSN, championed by the OPC Foundation's Field Level Communications initiative, is the most likely path to broad adoption, but expect a multi-year transition where TSN islands sit inside conventional networks before becoming the default. If you're specifying a new line in 2026, ask vendors which IEEE 802.1 subset they implement and whether their CNC can manage mixed-vendor topologies.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) continue to be the standards most often cited in tenders this year, particularly from European utilities and large EPCs. A subtle but important point: 62443-4-2's component-level Security Levels (SL-C) are not the same as 62443-3-3's system-level Target Security Levels (SL-T). A device certified to SL-C 2 doesn't automatically deliver SL-T 2 in your zone — the integration, the conduits, and compensating controls all factor in. Auditors are getting sharper about this distinction, so make sure your zone-and-conduit drawings actually map component SLs to the target SL of the zone they sit in. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82r3 remains the operative guidance for OT security, and CISA continues to push the Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) as a pragmatic minimum baseline — useful for smaller operators who find 62443 overwhelming. Worth keeping an eye on is ISA's ongoing work to align the ISA/IEC 62443 lifecycle with safety lifecycle standards (IEC 61511 in process industries), which addresses the long-standing awkwardness of running two parallel risk assessments for the same SIS. Expect more formal guidance over the next 12-18 months on how cyber-induced hazards should be treated within HAZOP and LOPA studies.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers didn't exploit a sophisticated vulnerability — they reached the PLC because it was directly exposed to the internet on TCP/20256 with the vendor's default password '1111'. They defaced the HMI with a political message and forced operators to switch to manual control, but no water quality impact occurred. The same campaign hit dozens of small utilities worldwide using identical tradecraft. The technical lesson is unglamorous: shodan-discoverable PLCs with default credentials remain the dominant attack vector against small operators, not zero-days. The organizational lesson is harder — small water and wastewater utilities often lack the staff to maintain a hardened remote-access architecture, and the integrator who installed the equipment may have insisted on direct internet exposure for their own support convenience. ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a heavy slate of ICS advisories today with direct plant-floor relevance. Schneider Electric disclosed two issues: a RADIUS protocol weakness (the Blast-RADIUS class flaw) in Modicon Network Managed Switches that could enable authentication forgery against the management plane, and an authentication bypass in the EcoStruxure Panel Server gateway used to bridge edge devices to control and cloud applications. Siemens published an advisory for KACO blueplanet inverters where credentials can be derived from device serial numbers, an exposure pattern that has bitten the solar and DER sector repeatedly. Engineers running Modicon-based networks or EcoStruxure architectures should pull the CSAFs today and identify which firmware baselines are deployed. On the IIoT and edge side, advisories landed for Brickcom IP cameras (unauthenticated access to live feeds and admin takeover), Yarbo's robotic groundskeeping fleet (hard-coded credentials in mobile apps and MQTT cloud), and the Naxclow IoT camera and doorbell platform (device impersonation and credential harvesting at scale). While these are largely consumer-grade, the same MQTT and mobile-app design patterns show up in commercial building automation and field robotics — useful reading for anyone evaluating MQTT broker hygiene or outsourced cloud telemetry in their own deployments. In the broader security ecosystem, the FBI and Google announced the takedown of the Outsider Enterprise phishing-as-a-service platform tied to roughly $1.9B in losses, a Ukrainian developer pleaded guilty to building loaders for the Conti ransomware crew, and pharmaceutical giant Novo Nordisk confirmed an IT breach. France's sovereign Tchap messaging platform was compromised affecting around 73,000 government accounts, and ShinyHunters is extorting the Council of Europe over an alleged 297 GB data theft. None of these are OT incidents, but the continued pressure on pharma and government supply chains underscores the third-party risk that quietly creeps into manufacturing networks via shared IT services.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV catalog added several entries this month directly relevant to OT perimeters and jump hosts. CVE-2026-50751 in Check Point Security Gateway allows an unauthenticated IKEv1 authentication bypass to establish a remote-access VPN — known ransomware association. CVE-2026-0257 is a similar PAN-OS authentication bypass enabling unauthorized VPN sessions. Both directly threaten the IT/OT boundary; if you rely on Check Point or Palo Alto firewalls for plant remote access, patch and audit VPN session logs this week. CVE-2026-10520 in Ivanti Sentry yields unauthenticated root RCE, and CVE-2026-7473 in Arista EOS involves incorrect decapsulation and forwarding of tunneled packets — a concern wherever Arista switches sit between IT and OT VRFs or carry overlay traffic. On the enterprise side, Oracle PeopleSoft (CVE-2026-35273) and WebLogic (CVE-2024-21182), Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-20245), SolarWinds Serv-U (CVE-2026-28318), and Drupal core (CVE-2026-9082) all picked up KEV status. These typically live in corporate environments but frequently host the historian replication, MES integration, and engineering file shares that touch OT.
