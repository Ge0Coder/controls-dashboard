Today's Knowledge Bite

The ANSL (Automation Network Service Layer) protocol called out in today's ABB B&R Automation Studio advisory is a proprietary Ethernet-based protocol B&R has used since the late 1990s, predating widespread OPC-UA adoption. Many sites still run ANSL on engineering networks without realising it is a distinct protocol from POWERLINK or OPC-UA. If you've inherited a B&R system, an Wireshark capture on the engineering VLAN will often reveal ANSL traffic you never explicitly configured.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively invented to solve a steering problem, not a process problem. Nicolas Minorsky published the theoretical basis in 1922 after observing helmsmen on the USS New Mexico — he noticed they didn't just steer proportionally to heading error, but also anticipated based on the rate of change (derivative) and corrected for persistent drift (integral). The same three-term structure that keeps your reactor temperature stable was first derived from watching sailors hold a course in heavy seas.

Tech Spotlight

OPC-UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from spec to plant floor, and 2026 is shaping up to be the year it leaves pilot status at several major automotive and semiconductor sites. The promise is straightforward: deterministic, microsecond-scale Ethernet that carries both real-time motion control traffic and standard IT traffic on the same wire, replacing the need for separate Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, or Sercos networks. The IEEE 802.1 TSN profile combined with the OPC Foundation's FLC (Field Level Communications) initiative aims to deliver vendor-neutral controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication. The practical implications for controls engineers are significant but the migration path is bumpy. TSN requires switches that support 802.1Qbv (time-aware shaping) and 802.1AS (precision time sync) — most existing managed industrial switches do not. Configuration is also markedly more complex than VLAN-and-QoS work most of us are used to; you must engineer the schedule of transmission windows, and a single misconfigured device can disturb the whole domain. Tooling is improving but still vendor-specific. Adoption challenges aside, the security implications are worth flagging now: TSN's centralised configuration model (via NETCONF/YANG or proprietary tools) creates new high-value targets. If an attacker compromises the TSN configuration controller, they can disrupt determinism without taking any device offline — a subtle, hard-to-diagnose failure mode. Treat the TSN configurator with the same rigor as your engineering workstation.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-4-2, which defines technical security requirements for ICS components, continues to gain traction as procurement teams increasingly demand certified products. The B&R Automation Studio TLS masquerading vulnerability disclosed today is a textbook example of the kind of finding 62443-4-2 CR 3.1 (communication integrity) and CR 4.1 (information confidentiality) are intended to prevent. Expect to see more end users explicitly require SL2 or SL3 component certifications in upcoming RFQs, especially in pharmaceuticals, power, and water sectors. On the framework side, NIST SP 800-82r3 (Guide to OT Security) remains the de facto reference for US-regulated industries and increasingly globally. The revision 3 made significant strides aligning with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0's new "Govern" function, which is now driving how OT cybersecurity programs are governed at board level. If you're being asked to map your site's OT controls to a framework for the first time, 800-82r3 plus the CSF 2.0 mapping appendix is the lowest-friction starting point. Worth watching: ISA/IEC is progressing work on 62443-2-2 (security program ratings) which will give asset owners a concrete way to express the maturity of their security program — a useful tool when negotiating cyber insurance or demonstrating due diligence to regulators.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's instructive incident is the 2024 American Water Works cyberattack, which has had its full post-mortem details emerge over the past year. American Water, the largest regulated water and wastewater utility in the US serving 14 million people, detected unauthorised activity in its IT environment in October 2024 and proactively shut down its customer billing portal and call center. Critically, the attackers reached IT systems but did not pivot to OT — operations at treatment plants continued normally, which the company attributed to enforced segmentation. What happened: initial access was via a compromised remote-access account belonging to a contractor, which then enabled lateral movement through under-segmented IT corporate systems. The attackers reportedly attempted to reach historian and SCADA networks but were blocked by a firewall ruleset that explicitly denied corporate-to-OT traffic except through a documented jump-host pathway requiring MFA. Recovery took roughly two weeks for the customer-facing systems. Key lessons: First, your contractors' credentials are your credentials — apply the same MFA, conditional access, and session monitoring requirements you would for direct employees. Second, deny-by-default IT-to-OT firewalling really does work; American Water's segmentation is the only reason this wasn't a treatment-plant story. Third, have a tested manual-operations playbook for billing, SCADA HMIs, and customer comms — operators who can run the plant from local panels for two weeks while IT is rebuilt are an under-appreciated form of resilience.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a substantial batch of ICS advisories today, with the heaviest hitter being a privilege escalation in Johnson Controls CEM AC2000 access control systems (CVSS v3 8.7, CVE-2026-21661) affecting versions 10.6 through 12.0. Standard users on the host machine can escalate privileges, which is particularly concerning given AC2000 deployments in critical facilities, airports, and utilities. Plant and facility engineers running this platform should plan for an out-of-cycle patch window. ABB B&R features prominently with three advisories covering Automation Studio, Automation Runtime, and PVI. The Automation Studio flaw allows an attacker to masquerade as a trusted party during ANSL-over-TLS or OPC-UA connection establishment — a meaningful issue for anyone relying on TLS-secured engineering workflows. Automation Runtime versions below 6.5 (and specifically R4.93) suffer a denial-of-service condition (CVE-2025-11044), while the PVI client logs sensitive information when logging is enabled (off by default). Hitachi Energy's PCM600 substation engineering tool also received an integrity-impact advisory — relevant to protection and control engineers in transmission and distribution. On the broader cyber front, Google's Threat Intelligence Group reported what it describes as the first AI-generated zero-day exploit observed in the wild, developed by a known cybercrime group to bypass 2FA. While not OT-specific, it's a sign that adversary tooling is accelerating, and IT-side phishing/account takeover risks against engineers with VPN or remote-access privileges remain a leading vector into OT. SANS also flagged a second Linux local privilege escalation in two weeks ("Dirty Frag"), following Copy Fail (CVE-2026-31431) which is already on the KEV list — worth checking your Linux-based HMIs, historians, and edge gateways. Finally, Apple released a sweeping update patching 84 vulnerabilities across its ecosystem. For sites where engineers use iPads as mobile HMIs or for remote operator rounds, push these updates promptly.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog grew significantly in the last 30 days with several entries that have direct OT relevance even though none are ICS-labelled. The Palo Alto PAN-OS User-ID Captive Portal vulnerability (CVE-2026-0300) allows unauthenticated remote code execution as root on PA-Series and VM firewalls — these often sit at the IT/OT boundary, so patching is urgent. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager had three entries (CVE-2026-20122, -20128, -20133) covering file upload abuse, recoverable password storage, and information disclosure; many industrial WAN backbones connecting remote sites run on this platform. Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) and ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) are both heavily used for remote support of field assets — ScreenConnect in particular has been repeatedly weaponised against managed service providers serving industrial customers. SimpleHelp's two entries (CVE-2024-57726 and -57728) are in the same category. The Linux kernel "Copy Fail" LPE (CVE-2026-31431) should prompt review of any Linux-based engineering workstations, historians, and gateway appliances. Practical action: prioritise the perimeter and remote-access stack first (PAN-OS, SD-WAN Manager, EPMM, ScreenConnect, SimpleHelp), then sweep Linux endpoints for kernel patch level. None of the new KEV entries have confirmed ransomware association except cPanel and PaperCut, but the OT-adjacent attack surface here is broad enough to warrant treating them all as time-sensitive.Read more →
