Today's Knowledge Bite

The Danfoss AK-SM family quietly runs refrigeration and HVAC for a huge share of the world's supermarkets and cold-chain warehouses — a single compromised site manager can orchestrate hundreds of compressors, evaporators, and defrost cycles. A disgruntled attacker doesn't need to destroy anything dramatic; subtly skewing setpoints by two degrees for a week is enough to spoil millions of dollars of inventory without tripping a single alarm. It's a reminder that integrity attacks on process variables can be far more damaging than outright denial of service.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively born on a ship, not in a factory. Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 paper described three-term control after watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico — he noticed experienced helmsmen didn't just react to heading error (proportional), they anticipated the ship's swing (derivative) and corrected for persistent weather helm (integral). Nearly every loop you tuned this morning descends from a sailor's intuition about a 32,000-ton battleship fighting the Atlantic.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet continues to move from marketing slideware toward real deployments, and 2026 has been the year several big vendors shipped PLC backplanes and remote I/O that genuinely speak IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic. The promise is simple: deterministic sub-millisecond cycle times and converged motion, safety, and IT traffic on the same copper or fiber, without the proprietary silicon of PROFINET IRT or EtherCAT. For a controls engineer, that means one switch infrastructure for drives, vision systems, historians, and office PCs, with guaranteed latency bounds for the motion traffic. The practical challenges are still significant. TSN requires every switch and endpoint in the time-critical path to support the same profile — and there are several competing profiles (IEC/IEEE 60802 for industrial, AVnu for pro-audio, 3GPP for 5G backhaul). Clock synchronization via PTP must be engineered carefully; a single non-TSN-aware switch dropped into the path silently breaks determinism. And from a security view, converging safety-rated motion traffic with general IT on one fabric puts a lot of eggs in the segmentation-and-authentication basket. Expect most greenfield projects to pilot TSN on a single cell or line first, with brownfield conversions lagging well into the late 2020s.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (risk assessment and system design) and 62443-4-2 (component requirements) continue to be the center of gravity for OT procurement language. One practical trend worth watching is that more end users are now writing 62443-4-2 Security Level 2 as a hard requirement into capital project specs, rather than treating it as aspirational. Vendors that cannot demonstrate certified components — not just a self-declared datasheet claim — are increasingly being excluded at the RFQ stage, especially in European pharma and in North American water utilities responding to EPA pressure. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the de facto reference for OT security program structure, and CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are being folded into sector-specific guidance for water, chemicals, and food & ag. If you're building an OT security roadmap, the pragmatic combination is still 62443 for engineering controls, NIST CSF 2.0 for governance language your CFO understands, and the CPGs as a prioritized checklist for the first 18 months. Expect ISA/IEC to publish further guidance on secure remote access patterns in the coming year — an area where today's Juniper and Mitel KEV entries are a timely reminder.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment intrusion — recently reframed by investigators as likely a misconfiguration or operator error rather than a deliberate attack, but the control-system lessons remain valid regardless of attribution. An operator at the plant saw his HMI mouse pointer move on its own and watched sodium hydroxide setpoints briefly change from roughly 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm before he reverted them. The access path was a shared TeamViewer instance on a Windows 7 engineering workstation with a reused password, reachable from the internet. Whether this was an intruder or a legitimate but confused remote user, the technical realities are the same: one workstation with persistent remote access, shared credentials, no MFA, and HMI software that would accept a four-orders-of-magnitude setpoint change without a secondary interlock. The three takeaways for controls engineers are evergreen. First, remote access tools belong behind a brokered jump host with MFA, session recording, and per-user accounts — not on the HMI itself. Second, safety-relevant setpoints need engineering limits enforced in the PLC, not just in the HMI faceplate, so that no single mouse click can exceed a chemically plausible range. Third, instrumentation downstream (in this case pH analyzers) is the last line of defense and must be independent of the control loop that could be manipulated.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a heavy slate of ICS advisories today. The headline is a 9.8 stack-based buffer overflow in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA refrigeration system managers (CVE-2026-129713) that allows arbitrary code execution with webserver privileges — notable because the same product family also received three new KEV entries covering older path traversal, command injection, and authentication flaws. Taken together, any internet-exposed AK-SM should be treated as presumed compromised until patched and reviewed. Schneider Electric also disclosed memory corruption issues in EcoStruxure Power Build Rapsody, affecting engineers who use the tool to import single-line diagrams and generate switchboard BOMs, while AutomationDirect C-More EA9 programming software (≤V6.80) has a file-parsing flaw that executes code when an engineer opens a malicious project file. On the embedded and open-source side, Autonomy Logic's OpenPLC Runtime (≤4.2.4) allows unauthenticated creation of admin accounts through a race condition — relevant to the growing number of labs, training rigs, and small production uses of OpenPLC. The libumqtt MQTT client library has an integer underflow that can crash or corrupt memory on MQTT brokers and gateways embedding it. Most striking is the Elecbrakes advisory covering EB2, VM1, and ELBC2000 electronic trailer brake controllers: attackers in Bluetooth range can take control of trailer brakes, disable brake lights, or push malicious firmware. It is a reminder that 'OT' now includes consumer-grade safety-critical electronics that share the road with us. In the broader IT-adjacent space, Juniper Session Smart Conductor (CVE-2026-103406) is under active attack with root RCE via a crafted salt minion ID — many OT sites use Session Smart as SD-WAN edge into remote substations and plants. Mitel MiCollab zero-days are being chained in what Accenture describes as AI-assisted extortion activity, and Drupal's incomplete installer patch is being exploited in the wild. 1Password confirmed a GitHub-based source code and customer data breach (vaults untouched), and FIFA disclosed a data incident of unspecified scope. None are OT-specific, but Juniper and Mitel frequently sit on the IT/OT boundary.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added an unusually large batch to the KEV catalog this week. The Danfoss AK-SM 800A cluster (CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2023-25914, CVE-2023-25915) is the most directly relevant to OT — these controllers are common in supermarket refrigeration and cold storage, and the flaws range from information disclosure through path traversal to full remote command execution. Combined with today's new 9.8 advisory on the 8xxA series, operators running Danfoss site managers should isolate them from the internet immediately, put them behind a VPN or jump host, and schedule firmware upgrades. The Mitel MiCollab additions (five CVEs in the AWV component, including XML injection and OS command injection) and Juniper Session Smart Conductor (CVE-2026-103406) matter because both products commonly terminate traffic at the enterprise edge adjacent to OT networks. Treat any exposed management interface as a priority patch target and review firewall rules between the IT DMZ and the OT DMZ. Broadcom VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890) auth bypass is also on the list — relevant because many plants run SCADA historians, engineering workstations, and HMI servers on virtualized infrastructure. If your vCenter is reachable from engineering VLANs, apply Broadcom's patch and audit directory service logs for anomalous bind attempts.Read more →
