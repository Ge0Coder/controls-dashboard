Today's Knowledge Bite

The ABB B&R Automation Studio advisory hinges on TLS trust handling for ANSL and OPC-UA — and OPC-UA's own security model was designed assuming the client validates the server certificate chain, but the spec permits 'trust on first use' in many SDK defaults. Surveys of deployed OPC-UA servers consistently find a majority running with self-signed certificates that nobody ever rotates, meaning the cryptographic protections are largely theatrical until someone configures a real PKI.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller pre-dates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass autopilots in the 1910s and Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 mathematical analysis of ship steering for the USS New Mexico established the three-term structure entirely in mechanical and hydraulic form. Minorsky derived the derivative term by watching how an experienced helmsman anticipated the ship's swing, not by solving differential equations first. That's why PID tuning still rewards intuition: the algorithm was reverse-engineered from human behaviour before it was ever a transfer function.

Tech Spotlight

Worth a closer look this week: deterministic OPC-UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking). The combination is the convergence point everyone has been promising for a decade — OPC-UA PubSub providing a vendor-neutral information model, and IEEE 802.1 TSN providing bounded latency and zero packet loss on standard Ethernet. In practice this means a single physical network can carry motion-control traffic that used to require Profinet IRT or EtherCAT, alongside MES, video, and engineering traffic, with guaranteed time slots reserved per stream. The practical implication for controls engineers is that the line between fieldbus and IT network keeps blurring. Siemens, B&R, Beckhoff, and Rockwell all have shipping TSN-capable controllers, and the IEC/IEEE 60802 industrial profile is finally stabilising the configuration model. That said, adoption is gated by switch maturity (you need TSN end-to-end, not just at the endpoints), tooling for stream reservation that engineers can actually use without a networking PhD, and the awkward reality that mixing TSN and non-TSN traffic exposes new failure modes — a misconfigured gPTP grandmaster can silently drift a whole cell. Pilot it on a greenfield cell before retrofitting, and budget for managed switches that are 2–3× the price of your current unmanaged plant gear.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle) continue to anchor most procurement conversations, but the activity worth watching is around 62443-2-1 — the recently revised edition redefining the Cybersecurity Management System for asset owners. The new structure aligns more closely with ISO 27001 control families while retaining OT-specific concepts like zones and conduits, which makes life easier for sites already running an ISMS but tightens the documentation expectations for asset inventory, patch management, and incident response. CISA's CI Fortify announcement today doesn't replace 62443 but layers a resilience lens on top — essentially asking 'can you operate degraded?' which maps to 62443's Foundational Requirement 7 (Resource Availability) but pushes harder on duration. NIST SP 800-82r3 remains the best free companion document for translating these controls into something a plant engineer can actually implement, and the NIST CSF 2.0 'Govern' function is increasingly what executives ask about during cyber insurance renewals. If you're scoping a 2026 program, the pragmatic stack is CSF 2.0 for governance language, 62443-2-1 for the OT management system, 62443-3-3 for system requirements, and 800-82r3 as the implementation guide.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's incident is the Mexican water utility intrusion documented by Dragos. According to the report, threat actors gained initial access through exposed remote-access infrastructure and then used Claude as an interactive assistant to interpret what they were seeing — querying it for help understanding network share contents, identifying which Windows hosts likely hosted SCADA components, and reasoning about how to move from IT into the OT segment. They reached engineering workstations and OT-adjacent assets before being contained. No process impact was reported, but the dwell time and depth of OT reconnaissance are what make this notable. What's instructive is the failure mode: it wasn't a novel exploit, it was an unsegmented path between a remote-access entry point and the OT environment, combined with engineering workstations that contained enough documentation and HMI shortcuts to make the OT layout legible to an outsider. The AI didn't break anything cryptographic — it just lowered the expertise barrier dramatically. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, assume your engineering workstations and their documents are intelligence goldmines, and treat their network reachability accordingly — no direct internet, no shared credentials with IT. Second, the obscurity of vendor-specific tooling (RSLogix, TIA Portal, Studio 5000, Automation Studio) is no longer a defence; document your detection rules around their process and network behaviour. Third, audit every remote-access path — vendor VPNs, jump hosts, ScreenConnect/SimpleHelp/TeamViewer agents — because that is still where these stories begin.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's headline for OT teams is Dragos's report on a water and drainage utility intrusion in Mexico where threat actors leveraged Anthropic's Claude AI to help navigate the victim's network and identify OT assets. The attackers reportedly used the LLM as a real-time advisor for reconnaissance and pivoting decisions — a meaningful escalation from prior 'AI-generated phishing' stories, because it shows adversaries closing knowledge gaps about unfamiliar SCADA and process environments on the fly. For utilities relying on the obscurity of legacy HMIs and vendor-specific protocols as a soft defence, that obscurity is eroding quickly. CISA also launched 'CI Fortify,' explicitly framing it around geopolitical cyber conflict and asking operators to design OT environments that can survive extended isolation from IT and the internet. The guidance reinforces themes controls engineers already know — local engineering workstations, offline backups of PLC programs, manual fallback procedures, and tested island-mode operation — but elevates them to a national resilience expectation. Expect auditors and insurers to start asking pointed questions about how long your plant can run cleanly disconnected. On the advisory front it was a heavy ABB day: B&R Automation Studio (TLS/OPC-UA trust masquerade), B&R Automation Runtime (denial of service via CVE-2025-11044 affecting versions below 6.5 and the R4.93 branch), B&R PVI client logging information disclosure, and Ability OPTIMAX with an Azure AD SSO authentication bypass on 6.1–6.3 prior to 6.3.1-251120. Hitachi Energy PCM600 has an integrity-impact flaw, and Johnson Controls CEM AC2000 (10.6, 11.0, 12.0) has a CVSS 8.7 local privilege escalation — relevant anywhere physical access control bridges into IT identity. In the wider threat picture, SecurityWeek reports an Iranian APT (likely MuddyWater) running an intrusion disguised as Chaos ransomware to muddy attribution, and autonomous offensive security firm XBOW pulling another $35M — a reminder that AI-driven offensive tooling is industrialising on both sides of the fence.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV additions over the last week skew heavily toward perimeter and remote-access infrastructure that frequently sits adjacent to OT networks. CVE-2026-0300 in Palo Alto PAN-OS is the standout: an unauthenticated out-of-bounds write in the User-ID/Captive Portal service giving root RCE on PA-Series and VM appliances. Many sites use these firewalls as the IT/OT boundary, so patching the perimeter device that enforces your Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ should be treated as urgent. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) and SimpleHelp (CVE-2024-57726/57728) re-appearing on KEV is a pointed reminder that vendor and integrator remote-support tools are still a top OT initial-access vector — audit which of yours are exposed, version-current, and behind MFA. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager picked up three KEV entries (CVE-2026-20122, 20128, 20133) covering privileged API misuse, credential storage in recoverable format, and information disclosure. If your sites use SD-WAN to backhaul plant traffic, treat the SD-WAN controller as a Tier-0 asset and patch on an emergency cycle. Apache ActiveMQ (CVE-2026-34197) is also worth flagging for anyone running it as a historian or MES message broker. Practically: prioritise PAN-OS, Cisco SD-WAN Manager, and any remote-support agents this week; verify those agents aren't dual-homed into engineering workstations; and confirm your jump hosts log every session to a write-once store independent of the tool itself.Read more →
