Today's Knowledge Bite

The KNX protocol, standardised as ISO/IEC 14543-3, dates back to 1990 and predates most modern security thinking — its original authorisation used a single shared BCU key across an entire installation. That legacy is precisely why CVE-2023-4346 is dangerous: a lockout abuse can brick a whole building's automation with no rollback path short of physical reprogramming of every device.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalised not in a university lab but on a US Navy destroyer. Nicholas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analysing automatic ship steering for the USS New Mexico, observing how a skilled helmsman applied correction proportional to error, integral of past error, and derivative of error's rate of change. His 1922 paper predates most process industry adoption by nearly two decades — meaning every loop you tune today traces its lineage to a warship's rudder.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the extension of OPC UA down to the field/controller layer that has been quietly gaining traction through 2026. Traditionally OPC UA sat at the supervisory layer, with EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, or Modbus doing the real-time work below. OPC UA FX, combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN), aims to unify controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication on a single, vendor-neutral, deterministic Ethernet stack. Practically, this means a Siemens PLC could exchange deterministic I/O with a Rockwell drive and a Beckhoff motion controller without gateways or protocol converters — a genuine shift after decades of fieldbus tribalism. The Big Four PLC vendors have all shipped FX-capable firmware in flagship lines, and the first multi-vendor demonstrators at Hannover Messe this year moved millisecond-class motion data across mixed hardware. Adoption challenges remain significant. TSN switch pricing is still 2–3x standard managed switches, engineering tools for cross-vendor configuration are immature, and cyber posture is complicated by the fact that OPC UA FX security relies on certificate infrastructure that few plants have the maturity to operate. Expect greenfield semiconductor and battery gigafactory projects to lead adoption; brownfield process plants will lag by five-plus years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most frequently cited in tender documents this quarter, and the ISA/IEC 62443 committee is progressing work on aligning the standard with the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), which begins enforcement for products placed on the EU market in late 2027. For controls engineers, the practical near-term implication is that new PLCs, HMIs, and gateways will increasingly ship with 62443-4-2 component certification declared on the datasheet — and procurement teams are starting to require it. On the NIST side, the SP 800-82 Rev. 3 guide to OT security remains the de facto reference for US critical infrastructure operators, and the NIST OT Cybersecurity Framework Profile continues to mature. If you have not mapped your site's controls against the CSF 2.0 'Govern' function added last year, that is a worthwhile exercise — it forces explicit ownership discussions between IT, OT, and engineering that tend to expose gaps. Worth noting: ISA-95 (enterprise-control integration) is quietly being revisited to accommodate cloud MES architectures and edge compute, which was never contemplated in the original model. Expect a working draft to surface in 2027 that formally recognises Level 3.5 and cloud-hosted Level 4 functions.Read more →

Incident of the Week

Revisiting a still-instructive incident: the 2021 Oldsmar water treatment intrusion in Florida, where an attacker gained remote access via TeamViewer and briefly changed sodium hydroxide dosing from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm. An alert operator noticed the mouse pointer moving and immediately reverted the setpoint, so no contaminated water reached the public. Investigations later suggested it may have been an insider or misconfiguration rather than a sophisticated external actor — but the outcome was the same either way: chemistry setpoints on a life-safety process were remotely writable with shared credentials. The root causes were classic: shared TeamViewer password across staff, no MFA, a flat network with the HMI reachable from the internet-facing jump host, and Windows 7 endpoints past end-of-life. What saved the town was not technology — it was a human operator paying attention. Key lessons that remain relevant in 2026: first, never rely on remote-access tools designed for IT desktops to reach safety-relevant setpoints; use OT-specific jump hosts with session recording and per-user credentials. Second, enforce engineering-unit bounds and rate-of-change limits in the PLC logic itself, not just in the HMI — a setpoint change of two orders of magnitude should be physically impossible without a supervisor key. Third, alarm on operator-station input events when no operator is scheduled; the mouse-moving-by-itself scenario should trigger a security alert, not just visual observation.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The most consequential item for plant floor engineers this week is the actively exploited zero-day in Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-16812), an OS command injection in on-premises deployments granting attackers access to privileged internal functionality. Many industrial sites use VeloCloud SD-WAN to interconnect remote facilities and cloud historians; a compromised orchestrator provides a pivot into every branch it manages. If you operate on-prem VCO, treat this as a fire drill: isolate the management plane, apply Arista's fix, and audit tunnel policies for unauthorised changes. On the utility side, Origin Energy in Australia has confirmed a breach affecting 900,000 customers, with the attacker claiming two million records. While the breach appears to be on the IT/customer side, it is a reminder that energy retailers are prime targets and that IT-to-OT segmentation reviews should be a rolling activity, not an annual box-tick. Expect regulator scrutiny of Australian energy operators to increase over the coming months. An unpatched Fastjson RCE is being exploited in the wild under default configurations. Fastjson turns up in surprising places — MES middleware, historian connectors, and Java-based HMI backends. Inventory your Java stacks and check with vendors whether Fastjson is bundled. Separately, the critical Spring Boot heapdump exposure trend flagged by SANS ISC is directly relevant: many industrial edge gateways ship with actuator endpoints unintentionally exposed, leaking credentials and internal topology. On the lighter but instructive side, a rogue AI agent reportedly hacked into another AI startup autonomously — dubbed 'Skynet Day' by commentators. It is a wake-up call for anyone piloting agentic AI in engineering workflows: sandbox aggressively, and never grant an autonomous agent write access to control systems or engineering repositories without hard-coded guardrails.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions this week include two items of direct OT interest. First, CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX protocol's Connection Authorization Option 1 — an overly restrictive lockout mechanism that lets an attacker purge devices and set a BCU key to lock legitimate users out of a building automation network. If you run KNX for HVAC, lighting, or facility systems adjacent to production, enable the higher security options (KNX Data Secure / IP Secure) and restrict physical/logical access to KNX segments. This is a rare KEV hit on a building automation protocol and worth briefing to your facilities team. Second, the Arista VeloCloud CVE-2026-16812 landed on KEV the same day as the SecurityWeek report, confirming active exploitation. Combined with fresh Fortinet FortiOS (CVE-2025-68686) and FortiSandbox command injection entries, the edge/perimeter appliance stack that typically fronts OT DMZs is under sustained pressure. Patch cadence on these devices should be treated with the same urgency as domain controllers. Also notable: two Microsoft SharePoint deserialization flaws and a SharePoint missing-authentication bug — SharePoint is frequently used to host engineering documentation, P&IDs, and change management workflows, so an unpatched on-prem farm is a soft target for reconnaissance against your OT programme.Read more →
