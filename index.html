Today's Knowledge Bite

The first documented use of a PID controller wasn't in a factory — it was on a ship. Nicholas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analyzing the automatic steering of the USS New Mexico, observing how a skilled helmsman didn't just react to heading error but also to its rate of change and accumulated drift. Nearly every temperature loop in your plant today is descended from a naval autopilot algorithm.

⚛

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The centrifugal flyball governor that James Watt fitted to his steam engine in 1788 is often called the first industrial feedback controller, but its stability wasn't understood mathematically until James Clerk Maxwell's 1868 paper 'On Governors' — a paper widely regarded as the founding document of control theory. Maxwell showed that a governor that seemed to work perfectly could be driven into oscillation by reducing friction, which is counterintuitive: making the mechanism 'better' made the loop unstable. That same phenomenon, now called derivative kick or under-damped response, is why every PID tuner learns to fear too much integral gain on a low-friction valve.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the extension of OPC UA down to controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication at the field level. Where classic OPC UA has lived mostly at the SCADA/MES boundary, FX defines standardized information models for machine-to-machine data exchange — asset, connection and offline engineering models — so that a Siemens PLC, a Beckhoff controller and a Rockwell drive can, in principle, exchange structured process data without a bespoke gateway. Combined with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) at the Ethernet layer, it aims to give deterministic, sub-millisecond field communication over the same network that carries diagnostics and engineering traffic. The practical implication is that the traditional split between a proprietary fieldbus (Profinet, EtherNet/IP, EtherCAT) and an overlay OPC UA telemetry layer starts to collapse. You get one semantic model from sensor to cloud, which drastically simplifies digital twin and analytics projects that currently choke on tag mapping. Adoption reality is more sober. Vendor interoperability profiles are still maturing, TSN switch support is uneven and expensive, and most brownfield plants have no appetite to rip out working Profinet or EtherNet/IP segments. Expect to see OPC UA FX first in new packaging, robotics and battery-cell manufacturing lines where OEMs from different ecosystems must integrate tightly, and treat it as a multi-year transition rather than a forklift upgrade.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues its steady expansion, with 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) now the two parts most commonly cited in procurement specifications. If you're writing a new RFQ for a PLC, RTU or drive, referencing a specific Security Level (SL-C 2 is typical for general process industries) is becoming the norm, and increasingly OEMs will refuse to bid without a stated target. The IEC's ongoing work to align 62443 with the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), which enforces security requirements on products sold into the EU from late 2027, is quietly reshaping vendor roadmaps. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the reference OT security guide and now maps cleanly onto the CSF 2.0 Govern function, giving asset owners a defensible way to tie plant-level controls back to enterprise governance. ISA is also progressing ISA/IEC 62443-2-2 (security program ratings) which will give a way to score an operating program, not just a product — expect auditors and insurers to start asking about it. For engineers, the practical move this quarter is to make sure your zone-and-conduit diagram, required by 62443-3-2, actually reflects the network as built rather than the network as designed three years ago. Most gaps found in 62443 assessments are not missing controls — they are undocumented conduits that appeared when someone added a vendor VPN or a historian replication link.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Arkansas City, Kansas water treatment plant incident, where operators detected a cyber intrusion and manually switched the facility to fully manual operation while isolating the SCADA network. No treatment disruption occurred and water quality stayed within spec, but the plant ran on paper and radio for several days while systems were rebuilt. Initial access is believed to have been through internet-exposed remote management on an HMI/SCADA component, consistent with the pattern CISA has flagged repeatedly for small water utilities. What makes this incident instructive is not the sophistication of the attacker — it wasn't sophisticated — but the maturity of the response. Staff had recently rehearsed manual operation, knew which valves and chemical feeds to run by hand, and had non-SCADA-dependent monitoring (grab samples, local gauges) they trusted. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, inventory every remote-access path into your control system yourself, because the one that gets you compromised will be the one nobody documented. Second, treat manual-operation procedures as a first-class engineering deliverable — write them, drill them, and keep printed copies in the control room. Third, ensure critical process measurements have at least one instrument path that does not depend on the SCADA or historian being alive, because in an incident you will lose confidence in your primary telemetry long before you lose the process.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The news cycle is dominated by AI-related security stories rather than pure ICS disclosures, but several items carry indirect weight for plant floor teams. Attackers are now abusing ChatGPT Custom GPTs to run ClickFix social-engineering campaigns that trick users into pasting PowerShell into Run boxes — a technique controls engineers should assume will reach engineering workstations via phishing to vendor portals, remote-support links, or fake HMI/driver download pages. The Macfinger ClickFix analysis from SANS ISC reinforces that the loader ecosystem behind these lures is maturing quickly. On the platform side, Apple shipped an emergency out-of-band patch for CVE-2026-86950 covering iOS 26, macOS 26 and macOS 15, with confirmation of in-the-wild exploitation. Any iPad-based operator interfaces, mobile alarm callouts, or engineer-issue MacBooks used for tunneling into OT networks should be patched this week. Wireshark 4.6.9 also landed with 19 CVE fixes; if you carry it on a jump host or laptop that sees plant traffic, update before your next capture session. On the enterprise IT front, the Pentagon DMDC breach affecting 3 million personnel records is a reminder that identity data from supply-chain and defense-adjacent employers is being aggregated for targeted spearphishing — relevant to any integrator or utility with cleared personnel. Funding rounds for Reco and Rig Security signal that agentic AI identity is being treated as its own attack surface, worth tracking as vendors start pushing AI copilots into historian and MES stacks. Finally, SANS observed fresh scanning for the Wordfence WAF endpoint (wordfence-waf.php) — trivially relevant only if your site or vendor extranet runs WordPress, but a good prompt to inventory any internet-facing WordPress that touches customer or operations content.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the last two weeks are heavy on perimeter and remote-access gear that frequently sits in front of OT networks. Two new Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway flaws (CVE-2026-88771 and 88772) allow unauthenticated command execution or RCE — if NetScaler fronts your remote engineering access or vendor VPN, treat this as an emergency patch. MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-67279) allows an unauthenticated exec session and is chainable; MikroTik is common in small water, wastewater and remote pumping sites, so audit your fleet for exposed WinBox/SSH/API interfaces. Two Check Point advisories (CVE-2026-93616 path traversal and CVE-2026-85102 improper cert validation on Site-to-Site and Remote Access VPN) round out the perimeter picture, along with F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127) and Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952). For remote support tooling, ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2026-84869) once again allows unauthorized file transfer and execution over an active session — this is exactly the tool many OEMs use to reach PLC and drive cabinets, so verify server version and disable unattended access where not required. Cisco ISE (CVE-2026-76460) and Cisco Secure Email Gateway (CVE-2026-76461, unauthenticated SQLi to root) are also on the list; ISE in particular is often the NAC gatekeeper between IT and OT zones. Practically, prioritise this week: patch or virtually-patch NetScaler, Check Point VPN, F5 APM, MikroTik, and ScreenConnect; verify that DMZ and jump-host paths into Purdue Level 3/3.5 are not reachable from an unpatched device; and check logs on these appliances back to early September for signs of successful exploitation before the KEV listings.Read more →
