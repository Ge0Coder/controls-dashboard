Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, still running on countless PLCs today, was designed by Modicon in 1979 for the Modicon 084 PLC and predates TCP/IP's standardization by two years. Its function codes and register model were never intended to cross a routed network — the reason Modbus/TCP has no authentication is not an oversight but a fossilized design decision from an era when "the network" meant a 9600-baud RS-485 twisted pair inside a locked panel.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term traces back to James Watt's 1788 centrifugal flyball governor, but the mathematics that made PID tunable came from a 1922 paper by Nicolas Minorsky, who derived three-term control by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico. Minorsky noticed that experienced helmsmen didn't just correct the current heading error — they anticipated based on how fast the ship was swinging and remembered how long it had been drifting. He formalized that human intuition into proportional, derivative, and integral action, meaning every PID loop tuned today is, in a sense, imitating a 1920s naval helmsman.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to move from trade-show demo to production reality, and it's worth understanding what it actually changes. Classical OPC UA is excellent for client/server data exchange but was never deterministic — jitter of tens of milliseconds is normal on shared Ethernet. TSN, a set of IEEE 802.1 standards, adds hardware-level time synchronization (802.1AS), traffic shaping (802.1Qbv scheduled traffic), and frame preemption (802.1Qbu) so that safety and motion traffic can share the same wire as camera streams and MES telemetry without missing a cycle. For controls engineers, the promise is one converged network from sensor to cloud, replacing the current layered mess of Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, EtherNet/IP, and a separate corporate uplink. The OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) specification, now maturing under the OPC Foundation with support from Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, and B&R, defines the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device profiles that ride on TSN. Adoption challenges are real. TSN requires switches, NICs, and end devices that all support the same profiles — mixing vendors still exposes interoperability gaps. Engineering tools for scheduling TSN streams are immature compared to configuring a Profinet network, and troubleshooting a missed schedule requires new skills and instruments. Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component requirements) remain the most-cited standards in current OT procurement language, and we're seeing more asset owners require vendors to demonstrate certified security levels (SL-C) rather than just claim conformance. If your organization writes control system specifications, adding an explicit SL-T (target security level) per zone — typically SL 2 for general process control and SL 3 for safety-adjacent zones — is now considered baseline good practice. NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3, finalized in 2023, continues to be the practical companion for teams that need to map IEC 62443 concepts onto the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 functions. The newer CSF 2.0 "Govern" function is particularly relevant for OT because it forces explicit ownership of risk decisions that historically fell between plant management and corporate IT — exactly the ambiguity the Honeywell survey highlighted this week. Worth watching: ISA is progressing work on 62443-2-2 (security program ratings) which will give asset owners a defensible way to score their own program maturity against an external benchmark — a useful counterweight to the self-reported "88% mature" statistic that likely overstates reality.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2021 Oldsmar, Florida water treatment intrusion, because the recent surge in remote-access CVEs makes its lessons freshly relevant. An attacker gained access to a plant HMI via a shared TeamViewer session on an unattended engineering workstation and briefly raised the sodium hydroxide dosing setpoint from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm. An operator watching the screen noticed the cursor move, reverted the change within minutes, and downstream pH monitoring would have caught the deviation before any water left the plant. The root causes were mundane: shared credentials on a remote-access tool, no MFA, Windows 7 endpoints, and a flat network that allowed the internet-exposed workstation to directly command the HMI. What saved the plant was human vigilance and defense-in-depth on the process side, not cybersecurity controls. Lessons for controls engineers: first, assume remote-access tools will be abused and enforce MFA plus session recording on every one of them — the ConnectWise ScreenConnect KEV entry this month is Oldsmar waiting to happen again. Second, engineer the process itself to survive a bad setpoint: interlocks, rate-of-change limits, and independent analytical alarms are cybersecurity controls even though they don't look like them. Third, don't rely on operators noticing a mouse cursor — but do make sure they know it counts as an incident when they do.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The Honeywell 2026 OT Cybersecurity Report headlines today's news and paints an uncomfortable picture: 88% of industrial security leaders describe their programs as mature, yet only 21% have a complete OT asset inventory. That gap between confidence and ground truth is exactly what bites plant floor teams during an incident, when responders discover legacy HMIs, engineering workstations, and dual-homed jump boxes that were never in the CMDB. AI adoption in OT security is accelerating for detection and triage, but fully autonomous response remains rare — and rightly so, given the safety implications of automated actions on live process control networks. Microsoft's disruption of the EvilTokens phishing-as-a-service platform is worth noting because these AI-driven kits are increasingly used to harvest credentials from engineering staff and contractors with remote access to OT systems. The platform reportedly used generative AI at every step, from target selection to lure crafting, making traditional "spot the typo" phishing training largely obsolete. Combined with the ClickFix and TerminalFix campaigns SANS is tracking, the initial-access threat to engineering laptops is intensifying. On the IT side, Adobe patched nine critical flaws in Connect and AEM Forms, and Chrome shipped 108 fixes including several memory corruption issues. Both matter for OT because engineering workstations and vendor portals frequently run outdated browsers and Adobe stacks well past corporate refresh cycles. If your site still runs Chrome or Acrobat on HMI-adjacent machines, this is a reminder to check patch status on that quiet corner of the network. Finally, funding news around Outerlimit's $16M raise to police rogue AI agents hints at where OT is heading: as vendors bolt agentic AI onto historians, MES, and asset performance tools, the question of what an autonomous agent is allowed to write back to a controller becomes a real engineering safety issue, not a theoretical one.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV catalog has been unusually busy over the past two weeks, and the pattern is clear: attackers are hammering the perimeter and remote-access gear that sits between corporate IT and OT DMZs. F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127, unauthenticated RCE via OAuth), Check Point Security Gateway and Management (CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-93616, cert-validation bypass and path traversal to RCE), Citrix NetScaler (CVE-2026-19490, auth bypass on AAA/Gateway), Fortinet FortiOS (CVE-2025-25249, heap overflow), and Cisco ISE (CVE-2026-76460, unauth privileged API abuse) are all being actively exploited. If any of these products front your OT remote access, engineering VPN, or Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ, treat patching as this week's priority. Equally relevant for controls environments: Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952) is used in SD-WAN deployments that increasingly connect remote substations and pump stations; MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-86060, CVE-2026-67277) is common in smaller utility and OEM skid deployments; and Zyxel GS1900 switches (CVE-2026-7273) show up in building automation and small industrial LANs. ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2026-84869) is a well-known vector for MSP-driven compromise of remote sites — verify no unauthorized ScreenConnect sessions are active on maintenance PCs. Practical remediation: prioritize edge devices reachable from untrusted networks, verify MFA on all remote-access paths, and, where patching windows are constrained, apply vendor workarounds (disable OAuth on affected APM virtual servers, restrict management interfaces to jump hosts, and enforce strict allowlists on IDMZ firewalls).
