Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, still ubiquitous 45 years after Modicon released it in 1979, has no concept of time. Every read and write is stateless and unordered, which is why controls engineers historically enforced sequencing through PLC logic rather than the wire. This same statelessness is why Modbus/TCP is so trivially spoofable — the protocol was designed for a serial world where physical access implied authorisation.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronic computing by decades — Elmer Sperry's ship autopilots in 1911 already used all three terms, and Nicolas Minorsky formalised the mathematics in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico. What Minorsky noticed was that good helmsmen didn't just correct for current heading error (proportional), they anticipated the ship's swing (derivative) and remembered persistent offsets like wind (integral). Every loop you tune today is essentially a mathematical description of an experienced sailor's intuition.

Tech Spotlight

Advanced Physical Layer (APL) Ethernet is quietly transitioning from pilot to production in process plants. APL delivers 10 Mbit/s Ethernet over a single twisted pair up to 1,000 metres, with intrinsic-safety options for Zone 0/Division 1 areas — meaning you can finally run true Ethernet down to a transmitter in a hazardous location without gateways, without HART bridging, and without segment length gymnastics. It rides on IEEE 802.3cg (10BASE-T1L) with a purpose-built process-industry profile developed jointly by FieldComm, ODVA, OPC Foundation, and PI. The practical payoff is enormous: PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, or OPC UA all the way to the sensor, richer diagnostics, faster commissioning, and no more analog/HART conversion losses. Vendors including Endress+Hauser, Pepperl+Fuchs, Emerson, and Phoenix Contact now ship APL field switches and instruments at reasonable price parity with 4-20 mA loops when you factor in cabinet real estate savings. Adoption challenges remain real, though. Existing brownfield sites have decades of home-run cabling that isn't easily reused; power budgeting on APL trunks requires new engineering discipline; and cybersecurity models must extend authentication down to instruments that historically had none. Expect APL to dominate greenfield process projects by 2028 while brownfield conversions lag another decade.Read more →

Standards Watch

ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment and system design) continues to be the most commonly cited section in RFPs this year, and the ISA99 committee's ongoing work on Part 2-2 (security program ratings) is producing the first real, auditable maturity model for IACS operators. Where 62443-2-1 tells you what to have in your CSMS, 2-2 aims to give assessors a repeatable way to score it — think of it as the OT equivalent of CMMI. Early adopters in oil and gas are already using draft 2-2 language in vendor contracts. NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3, published in 2023, remains the reference most US operators map against, particularly for its expanded coverage of cloud-hosted historians and remote engineering access. If you haven't revisited your zone and conduit model against Rev 3's updated overlays, it's worth an afternoon — several new recommendations around identity federation between IT and OT directories are directly relevant given the surge in identity-based attacks on Active Directory. On the horizon, IEC 62443-4-2 certification is increasingly a gating requirement for PLC and RTU procurement in European utility tenders, and the EU Cyber Resilience Act enforcement dates in late 2027 will effectively make it mandatory for anything sold with a digital element into EU markets. Start asking your PLC vendors now for their 4-2 certification roadmap — several tier-one suppliers are still catching up.Read more →

Incident of the Week

Revisiting the 2024 Frontier Software / Australian Government payroll incident offers useful lessons even though it wasn't strictly plant-floor. A ransomware compromise of a shared services provider cascaded into operational disruptions at multiple downstream state agencies, including some responsible for water and transport infrastructure oversight. The initial vector was an internet-exposed management interface protected by MFA that was bypassed via a session-token replay flaw — a scenario increasingly common as vendors move OT management planes to SaaS. The operational lesson for controls engineers is that your 'supply chain' now includes SaaS platforms your IT team may have signed for without OT consultation — CMMS, historian analytics, remote support, even PLC programming tools with cloud licence servers. When any of these is disrupted, engineering productivity halts even if the process is running fine. Three takeaways: first, inventory every cloud dependency your engineering team relies on and document the manual fallback for each. Second, insist that any remote engineering access solution supports phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn) rather than TOTP or push. Third, tabletop the scenario where your engineering laptops can access the PLC but cannot reach any vendor cloud service for a week — most sites discover surprising dependencies.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline item for controls engineers is the confirmed in-the-wild exploitation of a Microsoft SharePoint deserialization flaw (CVE-2026-50522) shortly after PoC release. SharePoint frequently underpins plant document repositories, MOC workflows, and P&ID libraries in the DMZ or corporate zone, so any bridge server touching Level 3.5 needs urgent patching and log review. Combined with two additional SharePoint CVEs on the KEV list within the last month, this is now a sustained campaign against a very common OT-adjacent platform. Ceva Logistics disclosed a cyberattack disrupting European contract-logistics operations across eight warehouses, causing shipment delays for multiple customers. While not a plant-floor incident, controls engineers should note the downstream impact: if your just-in-time raw-material feed or finished-goods dispatch relies on a third-party logistics provider, expect scheduling turbulence over the next fortnight, and confirm your MES/ERP interfaces can gracefully handle late or missing ASN data without triggering line stoppages. Microsoft's August Patch Tuesday delivered fixes for 418 vulnerabilities, including one exploited zero-day and critical remote code execution bugs in QUIC and the DNS server role. Intel and AMD collectively patched over 80 issues, several allowing privilege escalation on engineering workstations. Chipmaker firmware updates rarely make it into OT patch cycles quickly, but any engineering laptop that dual-purposes for internet browsing should be prioritised. On the emerging-threat side, a stealthy 'City-Forum' campaign is quietly exfiltrating data from Salesforce and ServiceNow via unauthenticated guest access. Many utilities and manufacturers now run ServiceNow for CMMS, change management, and asset records — a treasure trove for adversaries mapping your OT estate. CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added several high-impact entries this cycle. Most relevant to OT perimeter operators is CVE-2026-20349 in Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD, an unauthenticated remote DoS via heap inspection. Any ICS site running ASA/FTD as its IT/OT boundary firewall should schedule an urgent patch window; an unexpected reload during a batch or a turnaround could cascade badly. Pair this with the recently added CVE-2026-20316 hard-coded password flaw in Cisco Firewall Management Center — check for lingering default credentials on management appliances. SharePoint deserialization bugs (CVE-2026-50522 and CVE-2026-58644) are now confirmed exploited. If your site uses SharePoint for as-built drawings, procedure repositories, or PI Vision integration, treat these as priority-one. Progress LoadMaster (CVE-2026-8037) and Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-16812) command-injection flaws are also worth flagging — both appear in SD-WAN deployments increasingly used to link remote sites and skids back to central historians. On the endpoint side, the Windows AFD.sys use-after-free (CVE-2026-68820) is a local privilege escalation that adversaries will chain after any initial foothold. Engineering workstations and HMIs running standard Windows builds should be included in the next patch window rather than deferred to the annual outage.Read more →
