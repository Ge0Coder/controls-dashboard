Today's Knowledge Bite

The KNX protocol, now in CISA's KEV catalogue, dates back to 1990 and was one of the first field buses to be standardised as an international ISO/IEC standard (14543-3) for home and building electronic systems. Despite being over three decades old, its default installation still ships without encryption on the twisted-pair medium — KNX Secure was only introduced in 2017, and adoption remains under 10% of deployed installations globally.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — the first mathematical description of proportional-integral-derivative control was formalised by Nicolas Minorsky in 1922, based on observations of how helmsmen steered US Navy battleships. Minorsky noticed that experienced helmsmen didn't just react to the current heading error (proportional), but also to how long the error had persisted (integral) and how quickly it was changing (derivative). The three-term controller that runs billions of loops worldwide today was, quite literally, reverse-engineered from human intuition on a warship's bridge.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) continues its slow march into OT, and 2026 is looking like the year deterministic Ethernet finally becomes practical for motion control applications. The IEEE 802.1 TSN standards suite — particularly 802.1Qbv (time-aware shaper), 802.1AS (precision timing), and 802.1CB (frame replication) — allows standard Ethernet to guarantee microsecond-level latency and zero packet loss, historically the domain of purpose-built protocols like EtherCAT and Profinet IRT. What's changed recently is silicon availability. TSN-capable switch ASICs from Marvell, Microchip, and NXP are now shipping in volume, and major PLC vendors including Siemens, B&R, and Rockwell have TSN-ready CPUs on their roadmaps. OPC UA over TSN promises a converged network where safety, motion, standard I/O, and IT traffic share the same wire without the traditional airgap requirements between control levels. The practical challenges remain significant: TSN commissioning requires network engineering skills that most controls engineers do not yet possess, and stream reservation configuration is nowhere near as plug-and-play as vendor demos suggest. Expect the first mainstream deployments in automotive body shops and semiconductor fabs, with process industries following once the tooling matures.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to see growing regulatory pull, particularly following the EU NIS2 directive's enforcement phase. Increasingly, asset owners are being asked by insurers and auditors to produce zone and conduit diagrams with documented security level targets (SL-T) — a requirement most brownfield sites cannot satisfy without significant retrofit work. On the standards front worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 certification for embedded devices is becoming a de-facto procurement requirement in European utilities and increasingly in North American water and wastewater tenders following EPA guidance. If you're specifying new PLCs, RTUs, or protection relays, ask vendors for their 62443-4-2 SL capability statement in writing — many still confuse product certification with organisational (4-1) certification. NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3 remains the go-to reference for OT security programme structure in the US, and its alignment with the NIST CSF 2.0 "Govern" function has clarified board-level accountability expectations. Controls engineers increasingly find themselves supporting evidence collection for these frameworks — worth understanding at least the vocabulary even if governance isn't your remit.Read more →

Incident of the Week

The Stadler Rail ransomware extortion attempt referenced in this week's news is worth examining. Stadler, the Swiss rail rolling stock manufacturer, was targeted by an extortion group claiming to have exfiltrated design and manufacturing data. While Stadler stated production was not disrupted, the incident illustrates how attackers increasingly target the engineering and manufacturing IT layer — where PLC logic, HMI projects, and safety system configurations often live — rather than the production floor itself. What likely happened, based on similar rail-sector incidents, is initial access through either a phishing campaign or an exposed remote access service, followed by lateral movement into engineering file shares. The extortion model requires no ICS-specific tradecraft — attackers simply need to find and steal valuable engineering IP. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, treat your PLC project backup repository as crown-jewel data and encrypt it at rest with access logging. Second, air-gapping the plant floor does not protect your intellectual property if the engineering workstations that program those PLCs sit on the corporate network. Third, exercise your response playbook for the scenario where you must rebuild an HMI or PLC from a compromised-but-still-functional configuration — you may not be able to trust your own backups without integrity verification.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Rockwell Automation has released patches for code execution vulnerabilities in its Arena simulation software, a widely used discrete-event simulation tool in manufacturing and logistics planning. While Arena runs on engineering workstations rather than the plant floor directly, these systems often hold sensitive process models and network to production data historians — making them a viable pivot point into OT environments. Engineers should prioritise patching Arena installations on any workstations that also connect to control networks. Siemens ROX II industrial switch vulnerabilities were disclosed this week as part of a broader roundup, alongside a ransomware extortion attempt against Stadler Rail. ROX II switches are deployed heavily in substations, rail signalling, and outdoor industrial environments; any newly disclosed flaws in this platform warrant immediate review of Siemens ProductCERT advisories and firmware inventory. The Stadler Rail incident is a further reminder that rail operators remain a favoured target for extortion groups. Australia's Origin Energy has confirmed a data breach affecting up to 2 million customers. While the initial disclosure focuses on customer PII rather than OT systems, energy sector breaches often reveal the IT/OT boundary was less isolated than assumed. Expect regulatory scrutiny across the Australian NEM sector and a fresh push on network segmentation audits. The SANS ISC diary on "the autonomous attacker being your own AI model" is worth reading in full — it describes an incident where an internally-deployed AI agent was manipulated into performing attacker-directed actions. As controls organisations begin embedding LLMs into engineering assistants, work-order triage, and alarm summarisation, this represents a new class of insider risk that has no precedent in traditional ICS threat models.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The most notable OT-relevant addition to CISA's KEV catalogue is CVE-2023-4346 in the KNX protocol's Connection Authorization Option 1 — an overly restrictive account lockout mechanism that allows an attacker to purge devices and lock the BCU key. KNX is ubiquitous in building automation, HVAC, and increasingly in industrial facility management. If you operate KNX-based systems, verify that Option 2 (or equivalent stronger authentication) is enabled on all lines, and audit which devices retain default BCU keys. Several SharePoint deserialization and authentication-bypass CVEs (50522, 58644, 56164) are now actively exploited. SharePoint is heavily used in engineering document management, MOC workflows, and P&ID libraries — a compromise here often exposes the intellectual crown jewels of a plant. Fortinet FortiSandbox command injection flaws (25089, 39808) and SonicWall SMA1000 issues (15409, 15410) also warrant urgent attention, as these appliances frequently sit at IT/OT boundaries or provide remote engineering access. Check Point SmartConsole (CVE-2026-16232) allowing unauthenticated administrative token capture is particularly concerning for organisations using Check Point at the perimeter of OT DMZs. Patch or restrict management-plane access immediately.Read more →
