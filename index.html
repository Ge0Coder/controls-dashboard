Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Modbus protocol, published by Modicon in 1979, was designed to run over a 4-wire RS-485 bus at 19.2 kbps — slower than a 1990s dial-up modem. Yet it is still the lingua franca of industrial automation nearly five decades later, and the TCP variant preserves the same 256-byte PDU limit originally chosen to fit cheaply inside an 8-bit microcontroller's RAM. Few protocols in any field have had that kind of staying power.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was essentially reverse-engineered from the behaviour of experienced helmsmen. In the 1920s, Nicolas Minorsky was asked to automate steering on the USS New Mexico, and rather than invent a control law from scratch he watched how skilled sailors held course: they responded to the current error, to how long the error had persisted, and to how fast it was changing. That observation became proportional-integral-derivative control, and more than a century later it still runs the vast majority of loops in every refinery, boiler house and brewery on the planet.

Tech Spotlight

Advanced Physical Layer (APL) Ethernet is quietly reaching the point of serious plant adoption. APL is a 10BASE-T1L variant engineered specifically for process industries: a single twisted pair carrying both 10 Mbps full-duplex Ethernet and power, up to 1,000 metres, with intrinsic-safety options for Zone 0/Division 1 hazardous areas. In practical terms, it finally allows field instruments — pressure, temperature, flow, valve positioners — to speak native Ethernet (typically PROFINET or EtherNet/IP, often with OPC UA on top) all the way from the sensor diaphragm back to the control room, replacing 4–20 mA and HART. For controls engineers the implications are significant. You get bidirectional, high-bandwidth access to the full digital asset model of each instrument, enabling richer diagnostics, model-based maintenance, and simplified device replacement workflows. Loop checkouts can be done over the network, and marshalling cabinets shrink dramatically. Adoption challenges remain real: APL field switches are still relatively expensive, few plants have the Ethernet-fluent instrument technicians required, and cybersecurity suddenly extends down to the transmitter itself — a device that previously could only misreport a value can now, in principle, be a network participant. Expect brownfield rollouts to stay cautious and greenfield greenfield projects (particularly in chemicals and pharma) to lead.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component security requirements) continue to dominate OT procurement language, with an increasing number of DCS and PLC vendors advertising certified product variants at Security Level 2. If you are writing specifications, be careful: a certified component does not deliver a certified system, and the integrator is responsible for the 2-4 and 3-3 requirements that bind components into a secure architecture. The common gap we see is zone and conduit documentation that stops at a block diagram without actually enumerating data flows and the countermeasures assigned to each conduit. NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the most readable overview for mixed IT/OT teams and is worth revisiting if your last read was Rev. 2 — the revision substantially expanded coverage of cloud-connected OT, safety-security interaction, and incident response in control environments. Pair it with ISA TR84.00.09 if you have any SIS in scope; the technical report on cybersecurity for safety instrumented systems is still underused despite being directly actionable. For those tracking the European side, the NIS2 transposition deadlines have now passed in most member states, and enforcement activity is beginning. Operators of essential services in energy, water, and manufacturing should expect that 'we are working toward 62443' is no longer a sufficient answer to a regulator's questionnaire.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Tampa Bay-area water utility near-miss, in which attackers used exposed VNC on an engineering workstation to reach the HMI controlling chemical dosing. Unlike the better-known Oldsmar incident, this one was caught during the attempt because the operator noticed cursor movement on a shared screen and disconnected the session before any setpoint change took effect. Post-incident analysis showed the VNC instance had been installed years earlier by an integrator for after-hours support, with the same password reused across multiple sites and no MFA. The root cause was not a zero-day; it was accumulated operational debt. The remote access tool was undocumented, the credential was shared, and the firewall allowed inbound VNC from the internet because 'it had always been that way.' Three lessons stand out. First, inventory every remote-access path into your control system, including the ones your integrator installed and forgot — assume at least one exists that you do not know about. Second, replace any shared or vendor-default credentials with per-user accounts tied to an identity provider, and require MFA at the jump host. Third, operators are often the last and most effective line of defence; make sure they know they are authorised to pull the plug on any suspicious session without waiting for IT approval.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The security news flow today is largely IT-centric, but several items have knock-on effects for plant floor operations. The arrest and extradition of a Qilin ransomware suspect from Japan to Germany is a positive sign of international cooperation, but Qilin remains one of the most prolific groups targeting manufacturing and critical infrastructure — the arrest will not meaningfully reduce the affiliate ecosystem. Controls engineers should assume Qilin tooling and TTPs will continue circulating in the wild. Advantest's disclosure that its February 2026 ransomware incident involved data theft is a reminder that even specialised semiconductor test equipment vendors are being hit, and that disclosure often lags months behind the actual breach. If you operate test cells or receive calibration/firmware updates from Advantest, now is the time to validate the integrity of recently delivered files and confirm your supplier risk assessment is current. SANS ISC continues to flag abuse of Remote Monitoring and Management (RMM) tools by threat actors — ScreenConnect last week and another RMM this week. This matters directly to OT: engineering workstations and vendor remote-support jump hosts frequently have legitimate RMM agents installed, and attackers are increasingly using these same tools to blend in. Audit which RMM agents exist on your Level 2 and 3 systems, baseline their expected destinations, and alert on anything that deviates. Finally, Chrome 155 patched 247 vulnerabilities including four critical use-after-free bugs. Browser flaws are not SCADA flaws, but HMI thin clients, historians with web dashboards, and engineering laptops all run Chromium-based browsers inside the OT environment. CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the last two weeks are heavily weighted toward perimeter and remote-access infrastructure that commonly fronts OT networks. Three Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway CVEs (CVE-2026-88779, 88772, 88771) cover both DoS and unauthenticated RCE paths — NetScaler is a frequent choke point between corporate and DMZ segments feeding plant historians and remote engineering access, and these should be patched immediately. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-76504) allows unauthenticated admin access via URI hex encoding handling; if your sites are stitched together with Catalyst SD-WAN, this is a direct path into site-to-site transport. F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127) and two Check Point gateway/management flaws (CVE-2026-93616, 85102) round out a bad fortnight for VPN concentrators. On the network edge, MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-67279) and Zyxel GS1900 switches (CVE-2026-7273) have unauthenticated RCE paths. These devices show up surprisingly often in remote well pads, pump stations, and small manufacturing cells where they were installed as 'temporary' connectivity. Fortinet FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286) path traversal is less OT-relevant but affects the mail relays many utilities use for alarm notification. Practically: prioritise anything terminating remote access or site-to-site tunnels into OT, confirm management interfaces are not exposed to any untrusted network (including the corporate LAN), capture a current config backup before patching, and verify your Purdue Level 3.5 DMZ still enforces the zone boundary even if these devices are compromised.Read more →
