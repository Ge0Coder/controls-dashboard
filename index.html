Today's Knowledge Bite

The MQTT protocol, now ubiquitous in IIoT and found in the vulnerable libumqtt library, was originally invented in 1999 by Andy Stanford-Clark (IBM) and Arlen Nipper (then at Arcom) to monitor oil pipelines over satellite links where bandwidth cost real money per byte. Its deliberate minimalism — a fixed 2-byte header — is why it fits comfortably on an 8-bit microcontroller today, decades after the satellite constraint vanished. The protocol was donated to OASIS in 2013 and only standardised as ISO/IEC 20922 in 2016.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's 'integral windup' problem was first systematically characterised not in a factory but aboard the USS New Mexico in the 1920s, when Nicolas Minorsky watched the ship's automatic steering overshoot badly in heavy seas and worked out the mathematics to fix it. His 1922 paper 'Directional Stability of Automatically Steered Bodies' is arguably the first rigorous derivation of three-term control, predating the term 'PID' by decades. Every anti-windup clamp you've ever tuned on a flow loop is a direct descendant of a problem discovered trying to keep a battleship pointing straight.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) continues to be the quiet revolution underneath OPC UA FX (Field eXchange). TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 amendments that give standard Ethernet frames guaranteed latency bounds and zero jitter through mechanisms like IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic and 802.1AS-Rev precision time sync. When paired with OPC UA PubSub, it theoretically allows motion control, PLC-to-PLC peer messaging, and MES/historian traffic to coexist on the same cable with hard real-time guarantees — collapsing the historical split between fieldbus and enterprise networks. The practical reality in 2026 is messier. Silicon support in managed switches has matured (Cisco IE, Hirschmann, Siemens Scalance XCM-300, Belden) but multi-vendor interoperability profiles are still being hammered out through the Avnu Alliance and OPC Foundation FLC working groups. Gap-free configuration tooling is the real bottleneck: engineers need to compute gate-control lists by hand or rely on vendor-specific engineering tools that don't yet talk to each other. Expect early adoption in semiconductor, packaging machinery, and automotive body shops where motion determinism justifies the integration pain, with broader process-industry uptake still 3-5 years out.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-2-1:2024, the revised cybersecurity management system standard, continues to roll out across asset-owner programmes and is now the preferred reference over the withdrawn 2010 version. The key shift is tighter alignment with ISO 27001 structure while explicitly carving out OT-specific requirements: security zones, conduits, essential functions, and the role of the asset owner versus service provider. If your site is still citing 62443-2-1:2010 in procedure documents, now is the time to cross-walk. ISA/IEC are also progressing 62443-3-2 (risk assessment) adoption guidance and the 62443-4-2 component requirements continue to drive product certification. Several of this week's CISA advisories — AutomationDirect C-More, Schneider EcoStruxure, OpenPLC — highlight how engineering workstation and design-tool software still largely escapes 62443-4-1 secure-development-lifecycle scrutiny compared to the PLCs themselves. Expect regulators and insurers to begin pressing vendors on SDLC evidence for engineering tools, not just runtime firmware, through 2027.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's spotlight is the tanker cyberattack cluster referenced in SecurityWeek, in which multiple product tankers reportedly experienced GPS and navigation system interference during loading operations at Middle Eastern terminals. While details remain thin, investigators point to compromise of shore-to-ship data links used for cargo handling and ballast management — a reminder that maritime OT shares most of the pathologies of land-based process control, including flat networks, default credentials, and vendor remote-access tunnels left permanently open. The near-term lesson is that cargo-handling PLCs and loading-arm control systems are increasingly reachable from terminal IT networks via historian replication and emission-reporting feeds. Attackers don't need to touch the ship's bridge to cause an incident; manipulating the shore-side loading computer is enough to overfill a tank or mis-sequence valves. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, inventory every data diode or 'read-only' link — many are bidirectional in practice once a management channel is added. Second, treat GNSS as an untrusted input even for fixed infrastructure; add sanity checks in your control logic rather than trusting position/time from a single receiver. Third, make sure your terminal operating system (TOS) and your DCS have independently authenticated operator sessions — shared SSO is convenient until an attacker only needs one password.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a cluster of ICS advisories this week with direct implications for plant engineers. The headline is Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series (versions up to 5.4.0), which carries a CVSS 9.8 stack-based buffer overflow (CVE-2026-129713) allowing arbitrary code execution with webserver privileges. These controllers are widespread in supermarket and cold-chain refrigeration plant rooms and are frequently exposed to the internet for remote monitoring. Danfoss is simultaneously facing three new entries on CISA's KEV list for older AK-SM 800A flaws, so this family deserves immediate attention. Schneider Electric disclosed a memory-corruption flaw in EcoStruxure Power Build Rapsody, the design tool used to generate switchboard bills of material and single-line diagrams. AutomationDirect's C-More EA9 HMI programming software (<=V6.80) has a parallel file-parsing issue (CVE-2026-127280) that could lead to arbitrary code execution if an engineer opens a malicious project file. Both are classic engineering-workstation supply-chain risks: compromise the designer's PC, compromise everything downstream. Autonomy Logic's OpenPLC Runtime (<=4.2.4), popular in academic and pilot deployments, allows race-condition creation of unauthenticated admin accounts (CVE-2026-126088). The libumqtt advisory (CVE-2026-125132) matters because this MQTT client library is embedded in many IIoT gateways and edge devices; integer-underflow conditions can be triggered remotely by a malicious broker or man-in-the-middle. Finally, the Elecbrakes advisory is a sobering reminder that trailer brake controllers are now ICS — attackers could disable brakes or brake lights over-the-air. On the IT side with OT blast-radius: SAP confirmed a GitHub token theft with reported source-code exposure, Juniper Session Smart Conductor (CVE-2026-103406) is under active exploitation as root-RCE, and Broadcom VMware vCenter has an auth-bypass now on KEV. Anyone running vCenter to host historian, MES, or SCADA VMs needs to patch this weekend.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA added five Danfoss AK-SM 800A CVEs effectively (three this week — CVE-2025-41450, CVE-2023-25914, CVE-2023-25915) alongside the new 8xxA advisory, signalling that refrigeration controllers are being actively targeted. The exploited bugs range from path traversal and improper auth to arbitrary command execution via the XML interface. If you operate Danfoss AK-SM gear, isolate the management interfaces behind a VPN, disable XML endpoints where possible, upgrade firmware, and audit for internet exposure via Shodan/Censys queries on your public ranges. The Juniper Session Smart Conductor/Router flaw (CVE-2026-103406) is particularly dangerous for distributed OT WANs — it allows unauthenticated root command execution via a crafted salt minion ID and is already being used in attacks. Broadcom VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890) auth-bypass and five Mitel MiCollab bugs round out the high-risk set. For plant floors, the practical priority order is: patch any vCenter hosting OT workloads, patch Juniper SSR edge devices, then address Danfoss and any exposed Mitel conferencing infrastructure that could pivot into business networks adjacent to OT.Read more →
