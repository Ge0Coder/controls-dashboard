Today's Knowledge Bite

The MQTT protocol that underpins countless modern IIoT deployments was created in 1999 by Andy Stanford-Clark at IBM and Arlen Nipper at Arcom to monitor oil pipelines over satellite links where bandwidth cost real money per byte. Its deliberately tiny 2-byte fixed header is why it still outperforms heavier protocols on constrained field networks. The 'MQ' originally referred to IBM's MQ Series, but the spec is now fully open under OASIS and ISO/IEC 20922.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID control loop most of us tune daily traces its mathematical form to Nicholas Minorsky's 1922 paper on automatic ship steering for the US Navy, where he derived the three-term controller by watching how helmsmen actually steered the USS New Mexico — anticipating the ship's yaw rate rather than just reacting to heading error. The derivative term, in other words, was reverse-engineered from human intuition before it was formalised in mathematics. It took another two decades before Ziegler and Nichols published their tuning rules in 1942, meaning engineers ran PID loops for twenty years mostly by feel.

Tech Spotlight

Software-defined OT networking is quietly moving from vendor pitch decks into real projects. The idea is to apply the same SDN controllers and intent-based policies used in data centres — think Cisco Cyber Vision with ISE, or Nozomi integrating with Fortinet fabric — to dynamically segment PLCs, drives, and HMIs without rewiring. Instead of static VLANs and hand-maintained firewall rules, the controller learns device identities (often via DPI of EtherNet/IP, PROFINET, or Modbus) and enforces microsegmentation that follows the asset even if it moves ports. For controls engineers the practical appeal is reducing the blast radius of a compromised HMI or contractor laptop without re-architecting the plant network. The practical pain is that policy mistakes can cause real production outages: block the wrong CIP class and a line stops. Adoption challenges are mostly organisational — the networking team owns the controller, but only the controls team knows which traffic is legitimate. Successful deployments we've seen pair a 6–12 month 'monitor-only' phase with joint OT/IT change control before any enforcement is enabled, and keep a documented bypass procedure for emergency maintenance.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the practical anchor for new greenfield projects, but the emerging piece worth watching is IEC 62443-2-3, which addresses patch management in the IACS environment. With advisories like this week's Danfoss and Schneider engineering tool flaws, having a documented, vendor-coordinated patching process — not just a spreadsheet — is increasingly what auditors and insurers are asking to see. 62443-2-3 gives you the vocabulary (VPAs, compatibility statements, deployment windows) to formalise what most sites do ad-hoc. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3 remains the reference OT security guide and now aligns explicitly with the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function added in 2024. If you're updating an OT security program document in 2026, mapping your controls to both 62443-3-3 SRs and 800-82 Rev 3 gives you dual-framework coverage that satisfies most regulators from NERC CIP to the EU NIS2 implementing acts. ISA is also progressing work on 62443-4-2 Edition 2, which will tighten component-level requirements around secure boot and cryptographic key storage — relevant as more PLC vendors ship TPM-backed controllers.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, where the CyberAv3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers did not exploit a zero-day — the PLC was exposed to the internet with its default password of 1111, and they defaced the HMI with an anti-Israel message. Operators were able to switch to manual control and no water quality impact occurred, but the station was offline for days. The root causes were depressingly ordinary: internet-exposed OT, default credentials, and no network segmentation between the PLC and the public internet. The attackers scanned for the specific Unitronics PCOM protocol on TCP/20256 and hit anything that answered. Lessons for controls engineers: first, inventory every PLC, RTU, and gateway with a routable address and confirm none are reachable from the internet, even indirectly through a 4G modem; second, change every default credential on commissioning and document it in a password vault, not a drawing title block; third, make sure manual fallback procedures are current and that operators have actually drilled them, because that is what saved Aliquippa from a worse outcome.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's CISA ICS advisory load is heavy and spans both plant-floor and edge-device ecosystems. A critical stack-based buffer overflow in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA refrigeration system managers (CVE-2026-129713, CVSS 9.8) can give an attacker arbitrary code execution with webserver privileges — a serious concern for supermarket chains, cold storage, and food-processing operators who commonly expose these units for remote service. Schneider Electric's EcoStruxure Power Build Rapsody engineering tool has a memory-corruption flaw triggered by opening malicious project files, and AutomationDirect's C-More EA9 HMI programming software (CVE-2026-127280) carries the same file-parsing risk. Both reinforce that engineering workstations remain a prime target vector. Autonomy Logic's OpenPLC Runtime (<=4.2.4) has a race condition allowing creation of unauthenticated admin accounts (CVE-2026-126088), relevant to the growing number of academic, pilot, and small-scale deployments using open-source PLC stacks. The libumqtt advisory (CVE-2026-125132) affects an MQTT client library embedded in many IoT and gateway products — expect downstream advisories as vendors identify affected firmware. More unusually, CISA published an advisory on Elecbrakes electronic trailer brake controllers where exploitation could disable brake lights, modify brake behaviour, or flash firmware — a rare publicly disclosed transportation safety-system flaw. On the enterprise side, Broadcom pushed critical patches for VMware ESX and vCenter, including an authentication bypass (CVE-2026-123890) already on CISA's KEV list. Many OT environments run historians, HMIs, and engineering VMs on vSphere, so this is a priority for IT/OT boundary teams. Check Point confirmed a successful AI-assisted campaign against its Security Management Servers from 99 source IPs — notable because those appliances often sit at the IT/OT demarcation. KEPCO in South Korea disclosed a 1.5-million-record breach of customer and supplier portals, a reminder that utility-side IT compromises can cascade into OT supply-chain exposure. Finally, OpenAI and Cisco released Shirudo, an open-source tool that uses AI-generated shims to shield unpatched Linux systems from known exploits. It is explicitly not production-ready, but the concept is worth tracking for OT teams who routinely cannot patch field Linux appliances on vendor timelines.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The headline KEV addition for OT-adjacent environments is CVE-2026-123890, the Broadcom VMware vCenter authentication bypass in the Directory Service. Any vCenter reachable from a compromised IT segment can be taken over without credentials, and from there an attacker owns every VM including SCADA servers, historians, and jump hosts. Patch immediately, and verify vCenter is not reachable from general corporate or OT user VLANs. CVE-2026-123039 in Check Point's Gaia DynamicCLI daemon is a permissions flaw affecting firewalls, gateways, and management servers — exactly the devices many sites use as the Purdue Level 3.5 boundary. Combined with the active AI-assisted campaign reported this week, assume Check Point perimeter devices are being probed and prioritise the vendor hotfix. Also noteworthy: Cisco Expressway hard-coded credentials (CVE-2026-76466), SAP NetWeaver injection (CVE-2025-42884), and a Synology DSM flaw (CVE-2024-10441) now flagged as used by ransomware — relevant anywhere NAS units are used for OT backups or video storage. Several additions involve deserialisation and code-injection paths (Apache Roller, Apache Axis, Puppet Enterprise, Drupal Core, LiteLLM). Puppet Enterprise is the one controls engineers should flag with IT: a signed agent cert is enough to execute code as the Puppet service user on the server, which in many shops manages both corporate and OT-adjacent Linux hosts.Read more →
