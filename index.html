Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, designed by Modicon in 1979 for the 584 PLC, has no concept of session, encryption, or authentication — yet it still carries roughly 70% of legacy industrial traffic in some surveys. Its longevity is partly because the original spec fits on about 30 pages, making it trivial to implement on 8-bit microcontrollers and embedded gateways. The flip side, as today's ABB AC500 advisory shows, is that even confidentiality of past telegrams was never an explicit design goal.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term has an unexpected ancestor: James Clerk Maxwell's 1868 paper 'On Governors', written to analyse why Watt's centrifugal flyball governor hunted instead of settling. Maxwell's analysis — essentially the first stability proof in feedback control — predates the term 'control theory' by almost a century and was motivated not by industry but by curiosity about telescope drives. The same equations Maxwell scribbled to keep a steam engine steady are what your loop tuner is solving every scan today.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) layered with OPC UA continues its slow march from demo booths into real plants. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 standards that turn standard Ethernet into a deterministic medium by adding precise time synchronisation (802.1AS, derived from PTP), traffic scheduling (802.1Qbv), and frame preemption (802.1Qbu). Combined with OPC UA's pub/sub model, it offers the long-promised single-network architecture where motion control, safety, and information-layer traffic share the same wire without the determinism penalties of TCP/IP. The practical implications are significant: you can collapse multiple fieldbus segments (Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos III) into one converged Ethernet, simplify cabinet wiring, and feed cloud or edge analytics directly from the controller without a translation gateway. Vendors like B&R, Siemens, Rockwell and Beckhoff all ship TSN-capable hardware now. The adoption challenges remain real. TSN-capable switches are still 2–4× the cost of managed industrial switches, configuration tooling (CNC/CUC) is immature and largely vendor-specific despite 802.1Qcc, and most installed bases of HMIs, drives, and remote I/O cannot generate scheduled traffic. Expect TSN to land first in greenfield automotive, semiconductor, and packaging lines where the determinism-plus-convergence story justifies the price premium.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the most-cited standard in vendor advisories — including several of today's ABB CSAFs, which reference zone-and-conduit segmentation as the recommended compensating control. If you have not formally documented your zones and conduits, the ABB AC500 Modbus-fragment vulnerability is a textbook example of why: even when a controller leaks data, a properly defined conduit limits who can ever reach it to ask. On the NIST side, the long-promised update to SP 800-82 Rev 3 ('Guide to Operational Technology Security') is now the operative baseline for federal and federal-adjacent OT environments, and increasingly referenced by insurers. Notable shifts from Rev 2 include explicit coverage of cloud-connected OT, safety-instrumented system risk, and a tighter mapping to the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function. Practically, this means controls engineers should expect more questions from auditors about who owns risk decisions for OT, not just what controls are deployed. ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 component certification is also accelerating among PLC vendors. If you are specifying new controllers or RTUs in 2026, asking for SL-2 certified components against 62443-4-2 is now a reasonable contractual baseline, not a stretch requirement.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's spotlight is the LA Metro cyberattack, now attributed by SecurityWeek-cited researchers to Iranian state-sponsored infrastructure despite a hacktivist front group taking public credit. The attack disrupted customer-facing services and triggered fare and signage outages across several lines. While core train control was reportedly unaffected, the corporate and operational IT-OT boundary was where the incident propagated, and recovery required taking several station systems offline. The mechanics followed a familiar pattern: initial access via internet-exposed remote-management infrastructure, credential reuse to pivot into operational support systems, and a public-facing claim of responsibility designed to obscure attribution. Transit and rail operators are particularly exposed because passenger information systems, ticketing, and SCADA frequently share network segments or AD domains in ways that pre-date modern segmentation guidance. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, hacktivist claims should never end your investigation — assume state involvement until logs and infrastructure indicators say otherwise. Second, the IT/OT boundary is not a firewall, it is a set of identity, time, and patch dependencies; shared AD and shared jump hosts are where these incidents actually cross. Third, exercise your manual fallback procedures: LA Metro kept trains moving partly because operators could fall back to radio dispatch and manual signage. If your plant has no rehearsed degraded-mode operation, you do not have a recovery plan, you have a hope.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's docket is dominated by a six-pack of ABB/B&R advisories pushed through CISA. The most operationally significant is the AC500 V2 PLC issue (≤2.5.2, 2.5.3), where residual memory exposes fragments of previously transmitted Modbus telegrams — a confidentiality leak that could disclose process variables, setpoints, or addressing information to anyone able to query the controller. The Terra AC EV charger flaw is more severe, with potential heap corruption leading to firmware rewrite. Also covered: Ability Zenon Remote Transport allowing unauthenticated reboots, Ability Camera Connect bundling an outdated VLC 2.2.4, LVS MConfig leaking sensitive info on the LAN, and a B&R Automation Runtime SDM denial-of-service. Engineers running any ABB/B&R stack should pull the CSAFs and plan firmware windows. On the broader threat front, SecurityWeek attributes the LA Metro cyberattack to Iranian state-sponsored infrastructure despite a hacktivist front — a reminder that transportation OT remains a priority target for state actors, and that public-facing claims of responsibility frequently mask nation-state involvement. The FBI separately warned of Silent Ransom Group operatives physically visiting law firms to plant USB drives, an old-school vector that is equally viable against unattended HMIs, engineering workstations and panel PCs in plants. Supply-chain risk continues to escalate. The 'SymJack' technique weaponises AI coding agents via malicious repos and symlinks to silently install attacker-controlled MCP servers, and the TeamPCP campaign has now reached GitHub's internal codebase and trojanised an officially Microsoft-published Python SDK across three package ecosystems. Any controls team using Python-based historians, MES connectors, or AI copilots for code generation should review dependency provenance and pin versions tightly. Finally, CISA is urging immediate patching of an actively exploited LiteSpeed cPanel zero-day (CVE-2026-48172) granting root-level script execution — relevant if any remote-access or vendor portals in your environment sit behind cPanel-managed infrastructure.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalogue saw heavy additions over the last week. The standouts for OT-adjacent environments are CVE-2026-0300 in Palo Alto PAN-OS (unauthenticated root RCE in the User-ID Captive Portal — patch immediately on any IT/OT boundary firewall), CVE-2026-20182 in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (authentication bypass yielding admin), and CVE-2026-6973 in Ivanti EPMM (authenticated RCE) which is commonly deployed for mobile engineering devices. Trend Micro Apex One on-prem (CVE-2026-34926) is also flagged — directory traversal allowing malicious code to be pushed to agents, a meaningful concern where Apex One protects OT DMZ hosts. CISA also re-added a striking batch of legacy Microsoft vulnerabilities from 2008–2010 (MS08-067 Server Service, DirectShow, IE use-after-frees) on 20 May. These almost certainly reflect active exploitation against unsupported Windows systems still embedded in industrial estates — Windows XP, Server 2003, and unpatched 7 hosts running HMIs, vendor tools, or legacy historians. If you cannot remove these hosts, segment them ruthlessly and disable SMBv1, RPC where feasible, and outbound internet access. The LiteSpeed cPanel (CVE-2026-48172) and cPanel/WHM (CVE-2026-41940) bugs are being exploited in the wild, with the latter linked to known ransomware actors. Anywhere your organisation uses cPanel-hosted infrastructure for vendor portals, remote access, or contractor file exchange, treat as urgent.Read more →
