Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, first published by Modicon in 1979, was designed with no concept of session, authentication, or message integrity because it was originally intended to run over a short RS-485 cable between a single master and its slaves. Every modern Modbus/TCP vulnerability — including today's ABB AC500 telegram-fragment disclosure — ultimately traces back to that 1979 assumption that the wire itself was the security boundary. Forty-seven years later, we are still patching around it.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was largely formalised not by a control theorist but by Nicolas Minorsky in 1922, while analysing how helmsmen steered the USS New Mexico. He noticed that experienced helmsmen didn't just react to the current heading error (proportional), they also accounted for how fast it was changing (derivative) and how long the ship had been off course (integral) — and he wrote the math down. Almost every loop you tune today is, in essence, a formalisation of what a Navy helmsman was doing intuitively a century ago.

Tech Spotlight

Today's spotlight is on time-sensitive networking (TSN) for OPC UA, which is quietly moving from trade-show demo to real plant deployments in 2026. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 extensions that turn standard Ethernet into a deterministic transport — bounded latency, scheduled traffic windows, and frame preemption — so that a single physical network can carry motion control traffic alongside SCADA polling and even video, without the jitter that historically forced engineers to deploy parallel fieldbus networks like PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, or Sercos. For controls engineers the practical implication is significant: a converged OPC UA over TSN architecture means your drive-to-drive synchronisation, your PLC-to-PLC peer messaging, and your historian traffic can live on the same switches, dramatically simplifying cable plant and asset inventory. Major vendors (Siemens, B&R, Beckhoff, Rockwell via CIP Motion over TSN, Schneider) all now ship TSN-capable hardware, and the OPC Foundation's UA FX (Field eXchange) specification published the controller-to-controller profile that makes vendor interoperability realistic. The adoption challenges are real. TSN requires every switch in the deterministic path to be TSN-capable and centrally configured (typically via a CNC — Centralised Network Controller), which most brownfield sites cannot retrofit cheaply. Engineering tools for scheduling TSN streams are still vendor-specific and immature, and troubleshooting a misconfigured gate-control list is meaningfully harder than diagnosing a PROFINET topology. Expect TSN to land first in greenfield automotive, semiconductor, and packaging lines before it touches process industries.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component-level technical requirements) continue to dominate procurement language in 2026, and the ABB advisory cluster today is a useful reminder of why. Most ABB advisories now reference a 62443-4-2 security level the product targets, and increasingly end users are writing SL-2 minimums into capital project specifications. If you are scoping a new line, asking your integrator for the 62443-4-2 conformance evidence (not just the marketing claim) of every PLC, HMI, and gateway is now a defensible baseline. Worth watching: ISA/IEC is progressing work on 62443-2-2 (security program ratings) which aims to give asset owners a measurable maturity score for their operating security program — analogous to how 4-2 rates products. Early drafts suggest it will be more prescriptive than NIST CSF profiles, which should make it easier to benchmark sister sites within a corporation. NIST itself released SP 800-82r3 in 2023 and a companion implementation guide is in public comment, with revised guidance on cloud-connected ICS and remote engineering access that reflects post-COVID realities. For day-to-day work, the most actionable standards activity remains the ISAGCA's joint guidance with ISA on secure remote access architectures — particularly relevant given today's Zenon Remote Transport vulnerability, which is exactly the class of remote service the guidance addresses.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's instructive case is the ongoing Akira ransomware campaign, which SANS dissected yesterday in a kill-chain reconstruction. Akira affiliates have repeatedly compromised manufacturers and utilities through internet-exposed VPN appliances — typically unpatched SonicWall and Cisco ASA devices — then escalated to domain admin within 24–72 hours and pivoted laterally into OT networks where flat addressing and shared Active Directory let them encrypt engineering workstations, historians, and HMI servers alongside IT systems. What made the reconstruction valuable is the joining of perimeter logs with endpoint telemetry: the SANS analysts showed that the truly diagnostic events sit in the days before encryption — anomalous VPN authentications from unusual ASNs, followed by RDP fan-out and unusual SMB enumeration. By the time the ransom note appears, the OT impact is already locked in. In multiple recent cases, plants lost recipe servers and batch historians, forcing manual operation or full shutdown. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, treat your IT/OT boundary firewall logs as a safety-relevant data source and retain them at least 90 days. Second, ensure engineering workstations and HMI servers do not share a domain or local administrator credentials with the IT estate — credential reuse is what turns an IT ransomware event into an OT outage. Third, have an offline, validated restore path for your PLC programs, HMI projects, and historian databases; tested annually, not just documented.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's headline event for plant floor engineers is a coordinated drop of six CISA advisories covering ABB products — a noteworthy concentration that warrants attention across asset inventories. The AC500 V2 advisory (versions 2.5.2 and 2.5.3 and earlier) describes a buffer-related flaw that can let an attacker recover fragments of previously transmitted Modbus telegrams, which is meaningful where AC500s sit on shared OT segments carrying setpoint or recipe data. The B&R Automation Runtime advisory describes a denial-of-service in the System Diagnostics Manager that can halt the controller — a direct availability concern for any line running B&R PCC or X20 hardware. The Terra AC EV charger advisory is the most severe of the set: a heap memory corruption that potentially permits remote takeover and firmware modification. While not classical process control gear, Terra AC units increasingly sit on the same enterprise/OT boundaries as building and site infrastructure, and a firmware-altering foothold is a meaningful pivot point. The Ability Camera Connect and LVS MConfig advisories are lower-severity but reflect the persistent pattern of bundled third-party components (VLC 2.2.4 in this case) ageing inside vendor installation packages. The Ability Zenon Remote Transport flaw lets an unauthenticated attacker trigger OS reboots via the Remote Transport Service — a clean availability attack against HMI/SCADA servers if the service is network-reachable. On the broader cyber landscape, SANS published a useful Akira ransomware kill-chain reconstruction joining perimeter and endpoint logs — worth reading for OT defenders responsible for IT/OT boundary monitoring, since Akira affiliates have repeatedly pivoted from VPN appliances into flat plant networks. Google launched an 'AI Threat Defense' platform combining Mandiant, Wiz and Gemini telemetry, and the UK's cyber intelligence chief warned of intensifying Russian grey-zone activity — context worth carrying into your next risk review if you operate critical infrastructure in Europe. Practical takeaway: inventory your ABB footprint today. AC500 V2, B&R Automation Runtime, Terra AC, Zenon, Camera Connect, and MConfig all have patches or mitigations available — coordinate with ABB's PSIRT advisories and prioritise anything internet-adjacent or in safety-relevant zones.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The last 30 days of KEV additions are heavily IT-weighted but contain several entries OT teams should care about. CVE-2026-20182 (Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN authentication bypass yielding admin) is significant for any site using SD-WAN to backhaul OT traffic between plants — an attacker with controller access can re-route or mirror traffic invisibly. CVE-2026-0300 in Palo Alto PAN-OS (unauthenticated RCE as root in the User-ID Captive Portal) directly threatens the firewalls many sites use as their IT/OT boundary; confirm Captive Portal is disabled if not required and that PAN-OS is patched to current train. The Nx Console (CVE-2026-48027) and TanStack (CVE-2026-45321) supply-chain poisonings on npm reinforce a trend OT engineering teams shouldn't ignore: development laptops used to build HMI dashboards, Ignition projects, or Node-RED flows are increasingly the entry vector. If your integrators use npm-based tooling, audit for the affected versions. Also notable is CISA's re-listing of several legacy Microsoft and Adobe CVEs from 2008–2010 — a reminder that legacy engineering workstations still running Windows XP/7 in isolated cells remain exploitable by these decade-old bugs if removable media or transient laptops bridge the air gap. For remediation prioritisation: patch PAN-OS and Cisco SD-WAN this week, validate Trend Micro Apex One (CVE-2026-34926) on any engineering workstations, and ensure Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) is patched if used to manage field tablets or mobile HMIs.Read more →
