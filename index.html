Today's Knowledge Bite

The original Modbus protocol, published by Modicon in 1979, was designed to run over RS-232 with a maximum of 247 slave addresses — a limit chosen not for any technical reason but because the 8-bit address field reserved 0 for broadcast and 248-255 for future use that never materialised. Nearly five decades later, that same address space constrains modern Modbus TCP implementations, even though TCP/IP could trivially support millions of endpoints.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller we all take for granted was first described mathematically not by an engineer but by a Russian-American naval architect, Nicolas Minorsky, in 1922, while he was analysing how to automatically steer the USS New Mexico. Minorsky derived the three-term control law by watching helmsmen and noting that experienced sailors not only corrected for current heading error (proportional) but also anticipated based on the rate of change (derivative) and compensated for persistent crosswinds (integral). Ziegler and Nichols wouldn't publish their famous tuning rules for another 20 years — meaning the control law existed in rigorous form for a generation before anyone had a practical method to tune it.

Tech Spotlight

APL — Ethernet-Advanced Physical Layer — is finally moving from pilot to production in process plants, and it deserves attention from any engineer still specifying 4-20mA loops for new greenfield work. APL delivers 10 Mbit/s Ethernet and bus power over a single twisted pair up to 1,000 metres, is intrinsically safe for Zone 0/1 hazardous areas, and uses the same two-wire topology instrument techs already know. The result: a pressure transmitter can speak PROFINET or EtherNet/IP natively, carrying its primary variable, diagnostics, and configuration over one cable, with no HART gateway in the middle. The practical implication is a step-change in diagnostic data from the field. Instead of polling HART-over-4-20mA at a few updates per second through a multiplexer, APL devices stream rich asset-health telemetry continuously to the DCS and historian. For brownfield sites the challenge is that APL requires new field switches (power switches and field switches with 2-WISE output) and compatible instruments, so the economics only work when you're replacing a loop anyway or building new. Current adoption hurdles include a still-maturing device catalogue, limited multi-vendor interoperability testing, and the usual conservative instrument-approval cycles in oil & gas and pharma.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the most frequently cited standard in OT procurement specifications, and the IEC working groups are progressing updates to 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) to better align with the certification scheme ISASecure now runs for asset owners, not just products. If you're writing a cybersecurity requirements spec for a capital project, the current best practice is to require 62443-4-1 (secure development lifecycle) and 4-2 (component security) certification from suppliers at a defined Security Level, and to run your own 3-2 zone-and-conduit assessment before FAT. On the NIST side, the Cybersecurity Framework 2.0's new Govern function is quietly reshaping how OT security programs get funded, because it forces executive accountability conversations that used to stop at the CISO. For controls engineers that translates into more frequent requests from corporate security to produce asset inventories, data flow diagrams, and SBOMs for installed PLC firmware — artefacts that are often painful to produce retroactively. The practical move is to require SBOMs as a deliverable on every new PLC, drive, and HMI purchase order from here forward; retrofitting them later is far more expensive.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Tampa Bay-area water utility incident in which operators discovered that a contractor's remote-support laptop had been used to connect to the SCADA network over a cellular hotspot, bypassing the firewall entirely. No damage occurred, but forensics showed the laptop had been infected with an infostealer two months earlier, and credentials for the HMI had been exfiltrated. The utility learned about the exposure only when a threat-intel vendor spotted the credentials in a criminal marketplace. The mechanism was mundane: the contractor plugged into the control network switch to troubleshoot a radio link and tethered to a phone for internet access to download a driver. The site had a strong IT/OT firewall policy but no NAC enforcement on the plant switches, so an untrusted endpoint could obtain an IP and route to the HMI. Three lessons stand out. First, perimeter firewalls are irrelevant if a laptop can plug directly into an internal switch — port-level 802.1X or MAC-based NAC is the control that matters. Second, contractor laptops should be treated as hostile by default; provide hardened jump hosts rather than allowing BYOD on the OT LAN. Third, credential monitoring services are cheap and have repeatedly been the earliest warning signal for OT exposure, often beating EDR and SIEM alerts by weeks.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline deal today is IonQ's up-to-$1.3B all-stock acquisition of Elisity, a microsegmentation and least-privilege access vendor that has been quietly gaining traction in OT environments. Elisity's identity-based segmentation approach — overlaying policy on existing switches without new VLANs — has been pitched as an alternative to the traditional Purdue model enforcement. If the acquisition changes product direction or pricing, plant engineers currently piloting or deploying Elisity for cell/area segmentation should watch roadmap communications closely over the next quarter. On the broader security front, Canonical suffered its second DDoS of the year against Ubuntu's web properties. The outage didn't affect package repositories, but sites running Ubuntu-based HMIs, historians, or edge gateways should note that documentation and update portals were briefly unreachable — a reminder to maintain local mirrors of OS and driver repositories for air-gapped or constrained OT networks. Separately, SANS ISC flagged active scanning for CVE-2026-21589, a newly patched Atlassian arbitrary file read bug; many engineering teams run Confluence/Jira for change management and P&ID documentation, and those instances often hold sensitive network diagrams. The arrest of Edward Dubrovsky, a well-known Canadian incident response executive, in connection with the ShinyHunters FBI breach probe has rattled the IR community. For asset owners, the practical takeaway is to review which third-party responders hold persistent access or copies of your OT forensic data, and under what contractual protections. The iRhythm breach (360k patients) and the YMCO money-mule guilty plea round out a week dominated by cybercrime enforcement rather than nation-state activity. CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks lean heavily toward perimeter and remote-access infrastructure that typically sits between enterprise IT and the OT DMZ. Three Citrix NetScaler CVEs (2026-88779, 88772, 88771) cover memory corruption, RCE and unauthenticated command injection; Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-76504) allows unauthenticated admin access via URI encoding tricks; F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127) has a pre-auth heap overflow when OAuth is configured; and Fortinet FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286) permits unauthenticated arbitrary file write. Any of these appliances fronting a plant network should be patched this week — they are the exact pivot points attackers use to reach jump hosts and historians. Also notable for OT: MikroTik RouterOS (CVE-2026-67279) is widely deployed as cheap industrial routing in remote sites, pump stations, and skid equipment; the unauthenticated session-channel bug warrants an urgent inventory sweep. Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952) affects SD-WAN deployments common in multi-site manufacturing. Finally, the batch of older CVEs added on 8 October — BIND, Apache Struts, ProFTPD, ONLYOFFICE — suggests CISA is cleaning up long-tail exploitation seen in recent intrusions; legacy historians, engineering workstations, and vendor-maintained servers often still run these components unpatched.Read more →
