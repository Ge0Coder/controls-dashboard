Today's Knowledge Bite

The Ruggedcom ROX advisories include CVEs from 2019 — flaws in third-party components that have remained exploitable in shipping firmware for nearly seven years. This is a vivid reminder that the real lifecycle of an OT vulnerability is governed by the slowest vendor in the supply chain, not by the original disclosure date. When you build an SBOM for a substation device, expect to find libraries older than some of your junior engineers.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it owes its mathematical form to Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 analysis of automatic ship steering for the U.S. Navy battleship New Mexico — he derived the three-term control law by watching how a helmsman actually steered, noting that experienced sailors anticipated the ship's swing (derivative action) and corrected for steady-state offsets like wind (integral action). The algorithm we now drop into every loop controller was literally reverse-engineered from human intuition before it was ever called PID. It took another twenty years before Ziegler and Nichols published their tuning rules in 1942, and another forty before microprocessors made the math trivial.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is on OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the field-level extension to OPC UA that pushes the protocol down from the supervisory layer into controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communications. FX defines standardized information models for automation components (drives, I/O, robots) and uses OPC UA PubSub over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) to deliver deterministic, low-jitter messaging suitable for motion and safety-adjacent applications. The practical promise is significant: a single, vendor-neutral protocol from sensor to cloud, replacing the patchwork of PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, EtherCAT, and Modbus that most plants run today. Early demonstrators from Siemens, Rockwell, Beckhoff, and others have shown sub-millisecond cycle times with full security (X.509 certificates, signing, encryption) at the field level — something the legacy fieldbuses simply do not offer natively. The adoption brake is hardware. TSN requires switches and end devices with synchronized clocks (IEEE 802.1AS) and traffic shapers (802.1Qbv), and most installed plant networks aren't there yet. Expect FX to land first in greenfield machine-builder applications (packaging, semiconductors, EV battery lines) over the next 2-3 years before it filters into brownfield process plants. For now, the engineering takeaway is to ensure that any new managed switch purchases are at least TSN-capable so you don't have to rip and replace later.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most quoted in customer specifications this year, but the one worth watching is IEC 62443-2-1, which was substantially revised in 2024 and is now being adopted into asset-owner programs. The revised 2-1 aligns the IACS security program requirements with the zone-and-conduit and security-level concepts from -3-3, giving asset owners a cleaner mapping between their policies and the technical controls vendors are certified against. If your site's cybersecurity management system was written against the 2010 edition, it is now formally out of date. On the NIST side, the second public draft of SP 800-82 Rev. 3 has been the de-facto reference for OT security in U.S. regulated sectors, and CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) are increasingly being cited in insurance questionnaires. The CPGs are not a standard in the IEC sense, but they have become a practical baseline that procurement teams expect suppliers to meet. For those working with safety, keep an eye on the ongoing harmonization between IEC 61508/61511 functional safety and 62443 cybersecurity — TR 63069 remains the bridging document, and several notified bodies now expect a combined SIL/SL argument in new safety system assessments rather than two parallel documents.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's case study is the 2024 Arnold Clark-style attack pattern as applied to a European water utility, recently summarized in incident-response debriefs: attackers gained initial access via a compromised remote-support tool on an engineering laptop (similar to the SimpleHelp and ScreenConnect KEVs above), pivoted through a flat IT/OT network, and reached the historian and HMI segment within 48 hours. They did not manipulate the process — they exfiltrated engineering drawings, P&IDs, and PLC backup files, then issued an extortion demand. The root cause chain was depressingly familiar: an engineering laptop used for both corporate email and remote PLC programming, no MFA on the remote-support portal, and a 'temporary' firewall rule from 2019 that allowed RDP from the engineering VLAN directly to the historian. Detection came only when the historian's CPU usage spiked during off-hours archive compression by the attacker's exfil tool. Three lessons: first, engineering workstations are crown jewels and should never share a security posture with general office endpoints; treat them like domain controllers. Second, audit your firewall rule base annually and document the business owner for every rule — 'temporary' rules become permanent the day you forget who asked for them. Third, the threat model for OT now includes intellectual property theft, not just process disruption; your P&IDs and PLC source code have real black-market value to competitors and to nation-state actors building target packages.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's biggest signal for plant floor engineers is a wave of Siemens advisories pushed through CISA. Ruggedcom ROX is hit twice with serious issues: an authenticated remote command execution as root via the Scheduler, and an improper access control flaw allowing arbitrary file reads. Given Ruggedcom's prevalence in substations, rail, and other harsh-environment networks, anyone on ROX firmware below 2.17.1 should be planning an upgrade window now. A separate bundle of third-party CVEs (some dating back to 2019) is also rolled into the same ROX update, underlining how long known vulnerabilities can linger in field-deployed gear. On the HMI side, SIMATIC HMI Unified Comfort Panels before V21.0 expose a path to the underlying web browser via the help link in the Control Panel. If the panel is not locked down with the documented hardening steps, an unauthenticated local user can pivot into the browser and from there explore the device. Siemens Industrial Devices advisory also covers a denial-of-service issue across multiple product families, with countermeasures published where fixes aren't yet available. Teamcenter PLM users should review the V2312/V14 update guidance as those flaws hit confidentiality, integrity, and availability of engineering data. Outside the ICS-specific stream, two items deserve attention. Microsoft confirmed CVE-2026-42897, an Exchange Server zero-day already being exploited; many OT sites still run on-prem Exchange or hybrid mailflow, and OWA is a common pivot point. Public PoC code was also released for a critical NGINX vulnerability — relevant because NGINX is embedded in many vendor HMI gateways, historian web frontends, and reverse proxies in DMZs. Finally, the TanStack supply chain compromise that hit OpenAI and the public release of the Shai-Hulud worm source code by TeamPCP point to a continuing escalation in npm/JavaScript ecosystem attacks, which increasingly leak into engineering toolchains and vendor portals.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog added several entries this month that matter for OT operators even if they aren't ICS-labelled. CVE-2026-42897 (Exchange Server XSS, exploited in the wild) and CVE-2026-20182 (Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN auth bypass to admin) are the most urgent: both sit in the typical IT/OT boundary stack where compromise gives an attacker either credential theft or full control of the WAN underlay that carries SCADA replication and remote-site traffic. Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS CVE-2026-0300 (unauthenticated RCE as root in the Captive Portal) is similarly high-impact for sites using PAN appliances as their IT/OT firewall. For remote-access exposure, Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) and ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) are both back in scope — ScreenConnect in particular is frequently abused as an initial access vector into engineering workstations. SimpleHelp's two April additions (CVE-2024-57728 and CVE-2024-57726) round out a clear theme: remote support tooling continues to be a top entry point, with known ransomware association. Controls engineers should audit which remote-support products still run on jump hosts and engineering laptops, confirm patch levels, and verify MFA on all consoles.
