Today's Knowledge Bite

The IKEv1 protocol that just produced the Check Point KEV entry was standardized in 1998 and formally deprecated by the IETF in RFC 8247 back in 2017, yet remains enabled by default on a surprising number of OT-facing VPN concentrators because legacy PLC remote-access clients and older vendor laptops never moved to IKEv2. The cryptographic weaknesses in aggressive-mode IKEv1 PSK exchanges have been public since 1999 — meaning some plants are still running a key exchange older than the engineers maintaining it.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades — Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass-driven ship autopilots in the 1910s already implemented proportional-plus-derivative action mechanically, and Nicolas Minorsky published the formal three-term theory in 1922 based on observing how helmsmen actually steered the USS New Mexico. What we now teach as a tuning exercise was originally reverse-engineered human behavior. Even more curious: Ziegler and Nichols developed their famous tuning rules in 1942 at Taylor Instrument Companies using pneumatic controllers whose 'integral' action came from a deliberately leaky bellows, and the rules still work because the underlying math doesn't care whether the controller is air, op-amps, or a function block.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) is finally moving from trade-show demo to shipping product, with Siemens, B&R, Beckhoff, and Rockwell all having field-ready stacks in 2026. The promise is a single converged Ethernet fabric that carries deterministic motion-control traffic, standard OPC UA pub/sub telemetry, and regular IT traffic on the same wire, with bounded latency in the microseconds and no need for parallel fieldbus runs. For a controls engineer, that means the long-standing split between a deterministic fieldbus (Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos) and an information layer (OPC UA, MQTT) can collapse into one cabling plant and one switch fleet. The practical challenges are still considerable. TSN requires switches that support 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic, 802.1AS time sync, and often 802.1CB frame replication — and mixing vendors is rarely as plug-and-play as the marketing suggests. Engineering tools to compute and deploy the gate schedules across a brownfield network are immature, and most plants lack staff who understand both PTP-grade time distribution and traditional control engineering. Expect TSN to land first in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines where the cable savings justify the learning curve, and to creep into process industries only as switch prices fall and configuration tooling matures. For security, TSN does not change the threat model much — OPC UA still needs its certificate-based security profile enabled, which a worrying number of deployments skip — but the converged fabric does mean a single misconfigured VLAN or compromised switch can now reach both the motion loop and the MES. Network segmentation discipline becomes more, not less, important when the physical separation disappears.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component requirements) remain the most actively cited parts of the family in 2026 procurement specs, and several large EPCs are now refusing to release final payment to integrators who cannot demonstrate SL-2 capability on critical components. If you are writing or reviewing automation tenders, expect 62443-4-2 conformance claims to be backed by ISASecure CSA certificates rather than vendor self-attestation — the certification body's public registry is the easiest way to verify. NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 continues to be the de facto English-language reference for OT security program design, and its alignment with the NIST CSF 2.0 Govern function has made it more useful for plant managers trying to translate engineering controls into board-level reporting. The Govern function is where most asset owners are weakest — formal OT risk ownership, supplier risk management, and policy enforcement across plants — and auditors are starting to ask pointed questions about it. Worth watching: ISA-95 Part 2 work on object models for cross-site data exchange is converging with the emerging Asset Administration Shell (IEC 63278) from the Industry 4.0 community. If your roadmap includes a unified namespace or a cross-plant data lake, decisions made in the next 12 months about which information model to standardize on will be hard to reverse.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful incident to revisit this week is the 2024 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, which remains one of the cleanest public examples of how trivial OT exposure can be. Attackers affiliated with the CyberAv3ngers group reached an internet-exposed Unitronics Vision-series PLC at a booster station, defaced its HMI screen with a political message, and forced operators to revert to manual control of pressure regulation. No customer outage occurred, but the station was offline for hours and the incident triggered a CISA advisory and Congressional attention. The root cause was depressingly mundane: the Unitronics PLC was reachable from the public internet on TCP/20256 with the factory default password of 1111. There was no firewall, no VPN, and no monitoring on the OT segment. The attackers used Shodan-style scanning rather than any zero-day. The 'sophistication' was political branding, not technical. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, assume any device with a public IP will be found within hours — there is no security through obscurity at internet scale. Second, default credentials on field controllers are a board-level risk, not a nuisance: a written, audited credential standard for every PLC, HMI, and VFD model on site is cheap insurance. Third, manual fallback procedures actually saved this utility. If your process cannot run on local manual control for at least a shift, your operational resilience plan is incomplete regardless of how good your cyber controls are.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The week's most pressing item for plant-floor engineers is FortiBleed: roughly 86,000 internet-facing Fortinet firewalls and VPN concentrators had credentials stolen — about half of the publicly reachable Fortinet estate. Even if your OT firewalls are not directly exposed, many sites use Fortinet appliances as the IT/OT boundary or for vendor remote access, and stolen creds frequently enable lateral movement into Level 3.5 DMZs. Treat any FortiGate or FortiClient EMS with internet exposure as compromised until rotated, and audit VPN logs for anomalous logins, especially from third-party integrators. The Klue supply-chain breach hit several cybersecurity vendors via their Salesforce instances (Huntress and Recorded Future among them). While not directly OT, it is another reminder that SaaS integrations into your security stack are now a credible pivot — relevant if your SOC or MSSP relies on tools whose tenant data has been quietly exfiltrated. Separately, CryptoBandits malware is being seen routing C2 over Tor via a local SOCKS5 proxy, a TTP that historically defeats simple egress filtering and is worth a fresh look at your OT firewall outbound rules. On the policy side, Macron's call for democracies to coordinate on advanced AI regulation foreshadows further compliance burden on AI-enabled SCADA, vision, and anomaly-detection tools you may be piloting. Cisco's planned acquisition of WideField to bolster Splunk's agentic SOC is also worth tracking — agentic security operations on industrial telemetry is moving from pilot to product faster than most asset owners are prepared to govern. Finally, SANS ISC continues to report a steady drumbeat of phishing and initial-access tradecraft — IPv4-mapped IPv6 phishing URLs and malicious VHDX archives delivering Remcos — both of which bypass naive URL and attachment filters. Engineering workstations remain the soft underbelly of most OT networks; if your project laptops still mount arbitrary removable images or run unsigned email attachments, this is your reminder to push GPO hardening through change control.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

This month's KEV adds include two items with direct OT relevance. CVE-2026-50751 in Check Point Security Gateway is an IKEv1 authentication bypass allowing unauthenticated remote VPN access — known ransomware use. Many process plants terminate vendor remote access on Check Point gateways, so patching to the fixed train and disabling IKEv1 where IKEv2 is supported should be immediate. CVE-2026-0257 in Palo Alto PAN-OS is a similar authentication bypass enabling unauthorized VPN sessions; combined with FortiBleed, every major firewall vendor used at the IT/OT boundary now has an active credential or auth-bypass concern within a single 30-day window. For engineering-adjacent infrastructure, CVE-2026-20253 (Splunk Enterprise unauthenticated file creation/truncation via a PostgreSQL sidecar) and the two Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager flaws (CVE-2026-20262 path traversal and CVE-2026-20245 root command execution) are noteworthy — Catalyst SD-WAN is widely deployed to backhaul remote substations, pump stations, and unmanned facilities. CVE-2024-21182 in Oracle WebLogic (T3/IIOP) is a reminder that historian and MES front-ends often still expose legacy Java middleware. Practical remediation: prioritize the firewall and SD-WAN fixes this weekend, force credential rotation on any internet-reachable VPN, and verify that your OT historian's WebLogic tier is either patched or firewalled to a tight allowlist.Read more →
