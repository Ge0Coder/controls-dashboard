Today's Knowledge Bite

The CIP protocol used across Rockwell, ODVA, and EtherNet/IP devices has no built-in authentication in its classic form — CIP Security (introduced via ODVA in 2018) is an optional add-on using TLS/DTLS and certificates. Most installed CompactLogix and ControlLogix deployments still run unauthenticated CIP, which is precisely why a malformed CIP packet can take a controller to MNRF. Segmenting the EtherNet/IP layer is, in practice, still the primary control.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller's integral term has a much older lineage than most engineers realise: Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship autopilot in 1911 used what was effectively proportional-plus-integral action to hold a heading against wind and current, and Nicolas Minorsky formalised the maths in 1922 by studying how a skilled helmsman steered the USS New Mexico. Minorsky observed that experienced helmsmen did not just react to the current heading error, they also anticipated based on the rate of change and accumulated past error — three terms, derived from human behaviour, decades before digital control existed. Every PID block you tune today is, in a real sense, an automated helmsman.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow march from demo booth to plant floor. The promise is single-network convergence: deterministic, sub-millisecond cyclic I/O traffic sharing the same Ethernet infrastructure as MES, historian, and engineering traffic, with OPC UA PubSub providing a vendor-neutral data model on top. For controls engineers, that potentially means retiring parallel fieldbus runs (PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, EtherNet/IP CIP Motion) in favour of one IEEE 802.1 standards-based fabric. The practical reality in 2026 is still mixed. TSN-capable switches from Cisco, Hirschmann, Siemens and Moxa are widely available, and Siemens, Beckhoff, B&R and Rockwell have shipped TSN-capable controllers, but interoperability across vendors — particularly around the CNC (Centralised Network Configuration) model versus fully distributed configuration — remains the friction point. Time synchronisation via 802.1AS also requires every hop in the path to be TSN-aware; one legacy unmanaged switch breaks determinism silently. Most greenfield projects today are using TSN within one vendor's ecosystem and treating cross-vendor PubSub as a Level 2/3 data exchange rather than hard-real-time control.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-2 (component requirements) continue to be the most frequently cited parts in procurement specifications, and we are seeing more end users explicitly require SL-2 certification for new PLCs and remote I/O. Rockwell, Siemens, Schneider, and Phoenix Contact all now have certified product lines, which makes it realistic to write SL-2 into a tender and actually get compliant bids — something that was aspirational only three years ago. On the framework side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the practical companion document for US-regulated sectors, and it now aligns its control mappings to both NIST CSF 2.0 and 62443. For European sites, NIS2 enforcement is in full swing and national regulators are increasingly asking for evidence of zone-and-conduit documentation as proof of 'appropriate technical measures.' If you do not yet have a current 62443-3-2 zone/conduit drawing for your plant, that is the highest-leverage standards-driven document to produce this year — it underpins both audit responses and incident scoping.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Frontier Communications incident, where attackers pivoted from a corporate VPN appliance into operational support systems, and the 2023 Aliquippa, PA water authority attack where Iran-linked actors compromised a Unitronics Vision PLC because it was exposed to the internet with the default password '1111'. The Aliquippa intrusion did not cause a safety event — the operators switched to manual control — but it forced a multi-day degraded operation and triggered a CISA-wide alert on internet-exposed PLCs. The attack chain was almost embarrassingly simple: Shodan-style discovery of Unitronics devices on TCP/20256, default credential login, HMI defacement, and a stop command to the pressure-control logic. There was no zero-day, no custom malware, no supply chain compromise. Three lessons remain directly actionable. First, no PLC or HMI should be directly reachable from the public internet — if a vendor or integrator insists otherwise, the answer is a jump host with MFA, not a port forward. Second, default credentials on any field device must be treated as a commissioning defect, captured in your FAT/SAT checklist with a sign-off line. Third, manual fallback procedures matter: Aliquippa avoided a service impact because operators knew how to run the plant without the HMI. Walk that drill at least annually.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's CISA ICS releases are dominated by Rockwell Automation. CompactLogix 5370 (L1/L2/L3) and the broader Logix 5370/5570 family, including Compact GuardLogix, are vulnerable to denial-of-service conditions triggered via crafted CIP traffic, with the worst cases resulting in a major nonrecoverable fault (MNRF) requiring on-site intervention to recover. FLEX I/O EtherNet/IP adapters (1794-AENTR and 1794-AENTRXT at V2.012) carry two new CVEs allowing unauthorised access and account takeover, and RSLinx Classic up to 4.50.00 is exposed to a long-known third-party flaw (CVE-2020-13573) that hangs the application until manual restart. For plants standardised on ControlLogix/CompactLogix, the practical message is to verify firmware revisions, restrict CIP exposure to engineering subnets only, and accelerate patch planning where MNRF risk exists. On the application side, FactoryTalk Analytics PavilionX below 7.01 is affected by CVE-2025-14272, which allows privileged operations — relevant if you use PavilionX for model-predictive control or production optimisation, where attacker access could distort setpoints fed downstream to DCS/PLC layers. Separately, CISA flagged Yarbo's mobile app and cloud MQTT backend for hard-coded credentials and unauthenticated command issuance to a robot fleet; while consumer robotics, it is a clean case study of why MQTT brokers in any OT context need authentication, TLS, and topic-level ACLs. Outside ICS-specific advisories, F5 patched critical NGINX flaws that allow unauthenticated remote restart and possible RCE — worth checking if NGINX fronts your historian, MES, or remote-access portals. Cisco ISE received a critical command-execution patch (root escalation via input validation), and Splunk fixed an OS command injection in its AI Toolkit. Engineers using Splunk for OT log aggregation should review the AI Toolkit footprint specifically before next patch window.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalogue over the last 30 days has two entries that should jump out to OT teams: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-20262 path traversal and CVE-2026-20245 command injection as root) and Check Point Security Gateway (CVE-2026-50751, IKEv1 authentication bypass allowing unauthenticated VPN sessions, with confirmed ransomware use). Both sit squarely on the IT/OT boundary in many sites — SD-WAN often carries plant-to-enterprise traffic, and Check Point gateways frequently terminate vendor and remote-engineer VPNs. Treat these as priority patches and audit VPN logs for sessions without corresponding user authentication events. Also notable: Ivanti Sentry (CVE-2026-10520) unauthenticated root RCE, Oracle PeopleSoft (CVE-2026-35273, ransomware-linked) and an older Oracle WebLogic T3/IIOP flaw (CVE-2024-21182) — WebLogic still underpins some MES and quality systems, so confirm exposure is not via plant networks. The Linux kernel cgroups v1 escalation (CVE-2022-0492) being added now is a reminder that container hosts running historian or edge-analytics workloads need a fresh kernel review. Practically, none of these are PLC-resident bugs, but they all sit in the supporting infrastructure that controls engineers depend on for remote access, data flow, and identity. The remediation pattern is consistent: patch the perimeter and identity stack first, restrict management interfaces to jump hosts, and validate that vendor remote-access paths cannot bypass MFA via these gateway flaws.Read more →
