Today's Knowledge Bite

OPC-UA, often pitched as inherently secure, ships with security disabled in many vendor defaults — the spec mandates the option to use 'None' security policy so brownfield clients can connect. The B&R PPT30 advisory today is a reminder that even when transport security is enabled, the server's availability is a separate attack surface entirely. A well-architected OPC-UA deployment treats the server itself as a conduit-protected asset, not a public service.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronic computing by nearly a century. Elmer Sperry's gyroscopic ship-steering autopilot in 1911 implemented what we'd now recognise as proportional-integral-derivative control using purely mechanical linkages, springs, and pneumatic feedback — and Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 mathematical treatment of it, prompted by watching a helmsman steer the USS New Mexico, is the paper most modern control texts still trace their lineage to. The 'D' term, often the first one engineers detune to zero, was Minorsky's key insight: the helmsman wasn't just reacting to error, he was anticipating its rate of change.

Tech Spotlight

Today's spotlight is on OPC-UA FX (Field eXchange), the extension of OPC-UA down to the controller-to-controller and controller-to-device layer, running over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) on standard Ethernet. The promise is significant: a single, vendor-neutral information model from sensor to cloud, with deterministic timing comparable to Profinet IRT or EtherCAT, but on commodity switches that also carry your IT traffic in separate scheduled queues. For controls engineers, the practical implication is that the historical wall between fieldbus and enterprise networks could finally come down — one cable, one protocol, one semantic model. A drive, a PLC, and a SCADA historian could all consume the same companion specification without gateway translation. That said, adoption challenges are real. TSN requires switches that support IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduling and precision time (gPTP), and the tooling for configuring TSN streams across multi-vendor networks is still maturing. Most pilot deployments today are within a single vendor's ecosystem. From a security perspective, OPC-UA FX inherits OPC-UA's certificate-based authentication and signing, which is a step up from raw industrial Ethernet — but it also means certificate lifecycle management becomes a field-device problem. If you're evaluating FX for a greenfield line, plan your PKI before you plan your network.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security Risk Assessment for System Design) continues to be the standard most asset owners stumble on during certification programs. It's the document that formalises zone-and-conduit modelling and target security level (SL-T) assignment, and it's increasingly being called out by name in procurement specifications from European utilities and pharmaceutical operators. If you've ever been asked to produce a 'cyber risk assessment' for a new skid or line, 62443-3-2 is the methodology auditors expect you to have followed, whether or not the contract said so explicitly. Worth noting that ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 was substantially revised in 2024 and the industry is still absorbing it. The new edition aligns security program requirements much more closely with ISO 27001 structure, which makes life easier for sites already running an ISMS but harder for OT-only teams who now need to demonstrate policy, governance, and metrics in IT-style language. Expect this to drive more joint IT/OT governance committees over the next 12–18 months. On the NIST side, the CSF 2.0 'Govern' function is increasingly being mapped against 62443 by mid-market manufacturers as a lighter-weight alternative to full certification. It won't satisfy a NIS2 regulator on its own, but it's a defensible starting point for a small plant building a program from scratch.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's reflection is on the 2024 Frontier Software / Australian government payroll incident, which though primarily an IT breach, is instructive because the threat actor moved through a managed service provider's environment and accessed systems used by multiple critical-infrastructure agencies. The relevant detail for controls engineers: the initial foothold was not a sophisticated exploit but a compromised VPN appliance with an unpatched authentication flaw — strikingly similar in profile to today's Palo Alto and Cisco SD-WAN KEV entries. What happened in practical terms was that vendor remote access — the same kind your integrator uses to dial into a PLC for diagnostics — became the lateral path. Multi-factor authentication was either not enforced or was bypassed via session token theft. From there, the attacker pivoted into engineering jump hosts that had been provisioned for 'emergency' use and never decommissioned. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, every vendor remote-access account needs an expiry date on the calendar, not a hope. Second, jump hosts must log keystrokes and screen video, not just connection metadata — most plants discover too late that their logs show 'someone connected for four hours' but nothing about what they did. Third, the VPN appliance itself is OT-adjacent infrastructure; its patch cadence and configuration audits belong on the OT change-management board, not buried in an IT ticket queue.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA released a notable cluster of six ICS advisories today, with Hitachi Energy dominating the list. The MACH HiDraw advisory describes a buffer overflow that could yield denial-of-service or arbitrary code execution — significant given HiDraw's role in substation engineering workflows. The RTU500 advisory covers multiple flaws with primarily availability impact, which matters because RTU500 series gateways are widely deployed in transmission and distribution SCADA. A third Hitachi advisory affects the ITT600 SA Explorer testing tool with DoS conditions, though Hitachi notes IEC 61850 endpoints themselves are unaffected — the risk is to the engineering workstation, not the live bay. B&R disclosed CVE-2025-11482 in the PPT30 Operating System, where an attacker can render the OPC-UA server inaccessible. For sites running B&R panels as OPC-UA aggregators or HMI nodes, this is a straightforward availability concern worth tracking against your patch window. NAVTOR NavBox 4.16.1.20 carries hard-coded credentials (CVE-2026-21404) granting access to SOAP methods — relevant to maritime operators and any shore-side integrators handling chart and route data. The KMW CCTV advisory (CVSS 9.1, unverified password change) is a reminder that physical-security cameras on the OT network remain a soft underbelly: full feed and configuration takeover from an unauthenticated attacker. On the IT side that bleeds into OT, Cisco disclosed its seventh SD-WAN zero-day of 2026 (CVE-2026-20245), allowing root-level command execution with no patch yet. Many industrial sites use Cisco SD-WAN to connect remote plants, substations, and pipelines; compensating controls (management-plane ACLs, out-of-band access only) should be reviewed today. Five Eyes agencies also warned of Chinese intelligence officers posing as recruiters on LinkedIn-style platforms to target staff with access to classified or privileged systems — a social-engineering vector that applies equally to senior controls engineers at critical-infrastructure operators. Finally, SANS ISC continues to report on MSI-branded payloads embedded inside images delivered via WeTransfer links — a delivery technique increasingly used against engineering staff who routinely exchange large files (P&IDs, project archives) with integrators and OEMs.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog additions over the last two weeks lean heavily IT, but several have direct OT relevance. CVE-2026-0257 in Palo Alto PAN-OS is an authentication bypass allowing unauthorized VPN connections — a serious concern for any site using Palo Alto firewalls to terminate remote-engineer or vendor VPNs into Level 3/3.5. Oracle WebLogic CVE-2024-21182 (T3/IIOP unauthenticated compromise) matters because WebLogic still underpins MES and historian middleware at many large manufacturers; check your asset inventory before assuming you're unaffected. The supply-chain entries are the most strategically important. CVE-2026-48027 (Nx Console) and CVE-2026-45321 (TanStack) both involve malicious packages published to trusted registries that harvested credentials from developer machines — a reminder that the engineering laptop used to author HMI screens, OPC-UA clients, or PLC tooling is itself a high-value target. CISA also added a cluster of legacy Microsoft and Adobe vulnerabilities (2008–2010 vintage) to KEV, which sounds odd but reflects ongoing exploitation against unpatched embedded HMIs and panel PCs still running Windows XP/7 on plant floors. If you have any Windows endpoint that cannot be patched, segment it ruthlessly behind a conduit firewall and disable removable media.Read more →
