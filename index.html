Today's Knowledge Bite

The Zyxel GS1900 series being actively exploited today first shipped in 2015 and is officially end-of-sale, yet it is still one of the most common unmanaged-to-lightly-managed switches found in small water utilities and food-and-beverage plants. A single unpatched $200 switch is now demonstrably enough to give a nation-state actor a persistent foothold — a reminder that in OT, the cheapest piece of network gear often has the longest attack lifetime.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller most of you tuned this morning traces its mathematical form back to Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 analysis of automatic ship steering for the US Navy — he derived the three-term controller by watching helmsmen and noticing they applied not just proportional rudder to heading error, but also anticipated the ship's rate of turn and remembered persistent offsets. What we now teach as 'derivative action' was originally a formalisation of an experienced sailor's intuition. A century later, the algorithm still runs on nearly every loop in your plant, largely unchanged in structure — arguably the most successful piece of control code ever written.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow but steady move from marketing slide to production floor. The premise is compelling: replace the fieldbus zoo (Profinet IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos, Ethernet/IP CIP Motion) with a single standards-based, deterministic Ethernet stack where OPC UA PubSub carries both cyclic control data and configuration/diagnostics over the same wire. IEEE 802.1Qbv gate scheduling gives you sub-millisecond, bounded latency for motion control while best-effort traffic (HMI polling, historian, video) shares the infrastructure. The practical challenge in 2026 is still tooling and interoperability. Multi-vendor TSN networks require synchronised configuration of gate schedules across every switch and endpoint — and centralised configuration (CNC/CUC per IEEE 802.1Qcc) is only just becoming plug-and-play. Silicon is now widely available (TI, NXP, Analog Devices, Hilscher netX 90) and most major PLC vendors have shipping products, but engineers report that mixing brands still involves painful debugging with a TSN-capable network analyser. For greenfield high-speed packaging or semiconductor tools it is worth serious evaluation; for a brownfield conveyor line, Profinet or EtherNet/IP will remain the pragmatic choice for another few years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often cited in RFQs this year, and the recent uptick in KEV entries against edge network devices is a good excuse to revisit your zone-and-conduit diagrams. The standard's requirement to document every conduit crossing a zone boundary — including its protocol, authentication mechanism, and target security level — is exactly the exercise that would surface an unpatched Zyxel or MikroTik hidden behind a vendor cabinet. Also worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 SL-C certifications continue to accumulate for major PLC and DCS platforms, and procurement teams are increasingly writing 'SL 2 minimum, SL 3 preferred' into specs. If you are speccing new equipment this quarter, ask vendors specifically which components carry the certification versus which are 'compliant by assessment' — the two are not equivalent. On the NIST side, the SP 800-82 Rev 3 guidance on OT security remains the most accessible free reference for engineers who need to justify OT-specific controls to IT-centric management, particularly its explicit acknowledgement that availability and safety often outrank confidentiality in ICS.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's featured incident is the 2024 American Water Works cyberattack, which remains highly instructive as the Zyxel campaign unfolds. The largest regulated water and wastewater utility in the US was forced to disconnect its customer portal and billing systems and file an 8-K disclosure after detecting unauthorised activity in its IT environment. Crucially, the company reported that water and wastewater operations were not impacted — but only because operators proactively segmented and isolated OT systems as a precaution while the intrusion was investigated. Investigators later attributed the initial access to compromised remote-access infrastructure typical of the IT/OT boundary — the same class of edge appliances filling up the KEV list this month. The lesson is that the defenders' win was not preventing the intrusion (they didn't) but having a rehearsed playbook to sever IT-to-OT connectivity fast enough to keep pumps and treatment running on local control. Three takeaways for controls engineers: first, know which OT functions can genuinely run islanded from IT and for how long — test it, don't assume it. Second, ensure your operators, not just the SOC, have the authority and the physical means (documented firewall rules, labelled disconnects, or air-gap switches) to isolate on their own judgement. Third, keep an up-to-date inventory of every remote-access path into the OT environment, including vendor-maintained ones you didn't install; you cannot defend what isn't on the diagram.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline item for plant floor engineers today is confirmation that a Chinese threat actor is actively exploiting a recent Zyxel switch vulnerability, with reports of nearly 1,000 switches already compromised and data exfiltrated. Zyxel GS-series switches are common in small industrial sites, remote pumping stations, and building automation closets, and the newly-added CVE-2026-7273 (a pre-auth stack buffer overflow in the CGI) provides a straightforward path to OS command execution. If you have any Zyxel gear on OT segments — even as an out-of-band or vendor-supplied device — treat it as suspect until patched and log-reviewed. The malicious npm 'indexed-btree' package, which has racked up millions of downloads while impersonating the legitimate sorted-btree, is another quiet reminder that supply-chain risk now reaches deep into engineering tooling. HMI build servers, historian plug-ins, and increasingly Node-based edge gateways all pull from npm; any developer workstation that touches OT should be running with lockfiles, private registries, or at minimum SBOM scanning. On the geopolitics front, Japan has dismantled its first North Korean 'laptop farm' as the US, Japan, Germany and Australia released a joint report on the DPRK WaterPlum scheme. This matters for integrators and EPC firms: remote contractors and 'staff-aug' developers are a documented insertion vector for DPRK operatives, several of whom have ended up with privileged access to industrial software projects. Verify identities on any remote engineering contractor with access to control system code or configurations. Rounding out the week, SANS ISC flagged a TerminalFix campaign using PNG steganography to deliver a reverse tunnel — the kind of covert C2 that is particularly hard to spot on flat OT networks that lack egress inspection — and Microsoft/WordPress patched a Click2Shell RCE vector that could affect any plant-facing corporate site or vendor portal.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the last two weeks are heavily weighted toward network edge and identity infrastructure — the exact devices that gate OT from IT. Alongside the Zyxel GS1900 flaw (CVE-2026-7273), Cisco added three separate authentication-bypass or injection issues: ISE/ISE-PIC (CVE-2026-76460), Secure Email Gateway (CVE-2026-76461, SQLi to root), and Secure FMC/SCC (CVE-2026-20079). Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway auth-bypass (CVE-2026-19490) and Fortinet FortiOS/FortiSwitchManager/FortiSASE heap overflow (CVE-2025-25249) round out a rough month for perimeter vendors. Any of these devices sitting between your enterprise and Purdue Level 3.5 should be prioritised for patching this week. Two MikroTik RouterOS entries (CVE-2026-86060 privilege escalation and CVE-2026-67277 btest auth bypass) are especially relevant to smaller sites and OEM skids where MikroTik is often shipped as a cheap cellular or VPN gateway. Combined with the four Linux kernel entries (three exploitable race/OOB conditions in networking subsystems plus an AF_ALG race), the pattern is clear: attackers are hitting the plumbing. Inventory your edge routers, firewalls, and Linux-based gateways, confirm KEV-relevant patches are on your change calendar, and ensure any exposed management interfaces are firewalled off from OT VLANs.Read more →
