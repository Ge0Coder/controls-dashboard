Today's Knowledge Bite

The Hitachi Energy RTU500 lineage traces back through ABB and Brown Boveri to substation telecontrol equipment first deployed in the 1980s. Some installed RTU500 units have been in continuous service for over two decades, which is why availability-impacting vulnerabilities matter so much: a reboot during a fault event can mean a substation goes blind to SCADA precisely when operators need visibility most.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely — Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass-based ship autopilots in the 1910s implemented proportional-integral-derivative action mechanically, using gears, springs and dashpots. Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 mathematical analysis of the USS New Mexico's steering system formalised what Sperry had built by intuition, and Minorsky derived the value of derivative action by watching helmsmen anticipate the ship's swing rather than just reacting to current heading error. So the 'D' in your PID loop is, quite literally, an attempt to encode the judgment of an experienced human operator into mathematics.

Tech Spotlight

OPC-UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to mature as the leading candidate to replace the patchwork of deterministic fieldbuses — PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos III — with a single converged Ethernet stack. The idea is straightforward: OPC-UA PubSub handles the data model and security, while IEEE 802.1Qbv and friends handle deterministic scheduling at the switch layer. The result, in theory, is microsecond-class motion control coexisting on the same wire as MES traffic and engineering tools. In practice, adoption is still uneven. Silicon support is solid (TI, Intel, NXP, Renesas all ship TSN-capable MACs), and managed switches from Cisco, Hirschmann, Siemens and Moxa support the relevant Qbv/Qbu/Qcc profiles. But end-device integration is the bottleneck: most drives and remote I/O don't yet speak OPC-UA PubSub natively, and the tooling for engineering TSN schedules across multi-vendor networks remains immature. The IEC/IEEE 60802 industrial profile, which standardises the TSN feature set for OT use, is helping — but expect another two to three years before greenfield motion projects routinely specify it. For controls engineers today, the practical implication is to start asking vendors about TSN and OPC-UA PubSub roadmaps when specifying new I/O and drives, and to ensure any new managed switches purchased now are at least TSN-capable in hardware, even if you won't enable the features for years.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most often cited in tender documents, but the real action this year is around 62443-4-1 and 4-2 — the product-development and component requirements. More vendors are now shipping PLCs and HMIs with documented 62443-4-2 certifications at Security Level 2, and asset owners increasingly require this in procurement. If you're spec'ing replacement controllers, ask for the certification scope document, not just the marketing claim — the scope often excludes the very communication stacks where vulnerabilities like today's Hitachi and B&R advisories live. On the framework side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3 remains the de facto reference for OT security program design in North America, and it now aligns closely with the CSF 2.0 Govern function. For European engineers, NIS2 enforcement is driving tangible changes — operators of essential services in energy, water and manufacturing are being asked to demonstrate concrete OT segmentation, asset inventory and incident reporting capability, not just paper policies. ISA-TR84.00.09 (cybersecurity for safety systems) is also worth a fresh read if you operate SIS that share networks or engineering tools with the BPCS.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful one to revisit this week is the 2024 Frontier Communications breach, where attackers used a compromised contractor VPN credential to pivot from IT into operational provisioning systems, eventually impacting service to over 750,000 customers. While telecom rather than classic process industry, the attack pattern is the same one playing out across water and energy: third-party remote access, weak segmentation between corporate identity systems and operational systems, and insufficient monitoring of east-west traffic once inside. What made it instructive was the dwell time — attackers were inside for weeks before triggering noticeable operational impact, exfiltrating customer and network configuration data along the way. Detection finally came from anomalous data egress, not from any OT-side alerting. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, treat every vendor remote-access path as a likely initial vector and require per-session MFA plus session recording, not just a jump host. Second, your engineering workstations are the crown jewels — they hold project files, controller passwords, and network maps; isolate them aggressively. Third, build at least basic egress monitoring at the IT/OT boundary; many OT incidents are first detected by unusual outbound traffic, not by anything happening on the PLC.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a heavy slate of ICS advisories targeting electric-sector and maritime OT gear. Hitachi Energy MACH HiDraw has a buffer overflow that can crash the application or potentially allow code execution, while the RTU500 series — widely deployed as substation gateways and remote terminal units — carries vulnerabilities that primarily impact availability with secondary confidentiality/integrity risk. Substation engineers should review firmware inventories now and check whether their RTU500 communication interfaces are reachable from any engineering or corporate network segment. Hitachi's ITT600 SA Explorer testing tool is also vulnerable to DoS, though notably the flaw does not affect IEC 61850 endpoints themselves. B&R's PPT30 panel operating system has a flaw (CVE-2025-11482) that can render the embedded OPC-UA server inaccessible — a meaningful risk anywhere PPT30 HMIs serve as OPC-UA data sources to higher-level systems. Fixed in 1.8.0. The NAVTOR NavBox advisory (CVE-2026-21404) describes hard-coded credentials enabling local access to SOAP methods; relevant to fleet operators using NavBox for ECDIS and chart updates. KMW IP cameras (KM-IP521 and KM-IP421) carry a CVSS 9.1 unverified password change flaw that hands attackers full camera control — a recurring story for industrial CCTV often left on flat networks alongside PLCs. On the IT side, SolarWinds Serv-U is being actively exploited via crafted POST requests that crash the service unauthenticated (CVE-2026-28318, now on KEV). Many OT environments still use Serv-U for file transfer between IT and historian/engineering DMZs — patch or take it offline. Meta disclosed 20,000 Instagram accounts hijacked via abuse of an AI-driven account recovery tool, and Silent Ransom Group is leveraging DNS fast-flux to evade takedowns, a tactic worth flagging in DNS monitoring rules at the IT/OT boundary. For plant-floor engineers, the practical takeaway is to focus this week on substation and HMI firmware (Hitachi, B&R), audit any internet-facing file transfer servers, and verify camera systems are segmented away from process networks.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV adds for the past two weeks have a clear theme: edge and file-transfer services. CVE-2026-28318 in SolarWinds Serv-U is the headline — unauthenticated DoS via a deflate-encoded POST, already being exploited in the wild. If you run Serv-U as your IT/OT data exchange gateway (a common pattern for historian extracts and vendor file drops), patch immediately or restrict ingress at the firewall. Palo Alto PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257 is also notable: an authentication bypass allowing unauthorized VPN connections, which has obvious implications for remote access into engineering networks. Supply-chain compromises remain hot: malicious versions of Nx Console (CVE-2026-48027) and TanStack packages (CVE-2026-45321) were published to harvest credentials. Even if your PLC code doesn't touch npm, your engineering workstations and CI/CD pipelines for HMI builds increasingly do. Oracle WebLogic CVE-2024-21182 (T3/IIOP unauth compromise) is a reminder to audit MES and ERP-adjacent middleware that often bridges into manufacturing zones. The reappearance of very old Microsoft and Adobe entries (2008–2010 era) suggests CISA is back-filling KEV from active campaigns still hitting under-patched systems — a category that disproportionately includes legacy OT jump hosts.Read more →
