Today's Knowledge Bite

The Modbus protocol, still running on millions of devices today, was published by Modicon in 1979 for the 484 PLC — meaning Modbus RTU predates TCP/IP's standardization (1983) and the IBM PC (1981). Its function-code structure has barely changed in 46 years, which is why a modern PLC can still talk to a 1980s drive, but also why it carries zero authentication by design.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics: Nicolas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analyzing the automatic steering of the USS New Mexico, by watching how a skilled helmsman corrected the ship's heading — proportional to the error, integral of past error to remove offset, and derivative to anticipate. Every loop you tune in your DCS today is a direct descendant of a naval officer's observation of a human at a ship's wheel a century ago. The math didn't change; only the actuator did.

Tech Spotlight

Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers are quietly becoming the plumbing that connects LLM assistants to industrial data sources. An MCP server exposes a structured API — read a tag, query a historian, fetch a work order — that an AI assistant can call in a controlled way. Vendors are already shipping MCP connectors for OSIsoft/AVEVA PI, Ignition, and CMMS platforms, promising engineers a natural-language interface to ask questions like "why did line 3 trip at 03:14?" without writing SQL or SPL. The practical implication for controls engineers is that MCP is quickly becoming the new attack surface between IT-hosted AI and OT data. Today's SANS ISC report of active scanning for exposed MCP endpoints and cached AI credentials is the predictable result: these servers often hold long-lived API tokens with broad read access across historian, alarm, and maintenance systems. Adoption challenges are the usual OT ones — audit trails on AI actions, segmenting read from write, and preventing an assistant from becoming an unaudited path around change-management. Treat MCP endpoints like any other Level 3.5 broker: authenticated, allowlisted, logged, and never internet-facing.Read more →

Standards Watch

With CMMC Phase 2 suspended pending review, attention shifts back to the underlying controls in NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 3 and, for OT specifically, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 (Guide to Operational Technology Security), finalized in late 2023 and still the most useful government reference for aligning IT-centric frameworks to plant realities. If your organization was gearing up its CMMC assessment, the technical work — asset inventory, network segmentation, MFA on remote access, incident response tabletops — remains directly reusable for IEC 62443-2-1 and -3-3 compliance. On the IEC 62443 front, ISA is continuing work on 62443-2-2 (Security Program Ratings), which will give asset owners a scoring model to demonstrate program maturity to insurers and regulators — a likely successor conversation to CMMC-style attestation in critical-infrastructure sectors. Controls engineers should also watch the ongoing harmonization between IEC 62443-4-2 component requirements and product certification schemes like ISASecure CSA and the EU Cyber Resilience Act, which begins enforcement obligations for connected products in 2027. If you specify PLCs, RTUs, or industrial switches, expect procurement templates to start requiring CRA/62443-4-2 evidence within the next spec cycle.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2023 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority incident, in which Iran-linked CyberAv3ngers compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster station. The attackers didn't exploit a zero-day — the PLC was internet-exposed with the default password (1111) and the default TCP port 20256 open. They defaced the HMI with an anti-Israel message and forced a manual failover; no water quality impact occurred, but the operational disruption was real and the political overtones drew a CISA advisory and White House attention. The root cause was banal: a small utility, a vendor that shipped with weak defaults, and remote programming access left open for integrator convenience. Similar Unitronics exposures were subsequently found in dozens of U.S. water utilities within days of the initial disclosure. Three lessons carry forward. First, never trust that a small-footprint device with a niche vendor is invisible — Shodan indexes everything, and threat actors specifically target sector-typical brands. Second, default credentials and default ports together are effectively a public API to your PLC; change both, and put the device behind a VPN or jump host. Third, the incident showed the value of manual-operation resilience: the operators failed over to manual control in about two hours, which is what kept a defacement from becoming a public-health event. Test that muscle regularly.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline development is a supply-chain attack against multiple Jscrambler NPM package versions, dropping a cross-platform credential stealer. While Jscrambler is primarily a JavaScript obfuscation tool, its packages show up in HMI web front-ends, plant dashboards and third-party MES portals. Any engineering workstation or build server that pulls Node dependencies for such tooling should audit lockfiles and rotate credentials cached on developer laptops, CI runners and jump hosts. On the U.S. defense side, the Pentagon has suspended CMMC Phase 2 rollout while a new task force reviews the entire program. For controls engineers at defense-industrial-base fabricators and integrators, the immediate practical effect is a pause in contract flow-downs, but the technical baseline (NIST SP 800-171 alignment for CUI, and by extension OT segmentation of shared engineering environments) is not going away — treat it as a schedule slip, not a reprieve. SANS ISC reports active internet scanning for exposed MCP (Model Context Protocol) servers and AI-assistant credentials. This is directly relevant to sites experimenting with LLM-based copilots wired into historian, alarm or maintenance data. If you have piloted an MCP bridge to a PI server, Ignition, or a CMMS, assume it will be discovered within hours of internet exposure and put it behind the DMZ with strict allowlists. On the enterprise-IT side that inevitably touches OT: a RabbitMQ flaw lets unauthenticated attackers pull the broker's OAuth client secret, and Zimbra has patched a critical code-execution bug triggered by opening a crafted email. RabbitMQ is used by several MES and edge-orchestration stacks; check whether your data-diode or level-3.5 broker is affected. CISA Vulnerability Digest

Two entries in the recent KEV window stand out for OT teams. CVE-2026-12569 in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM is an unauthenticated remote code execution via improper input validation. Windchill is the PLM backbone at many discrete manufacturers and holds product BOMs, CAD, and engineering change data that feeds directly into MES and shop-floor build instructions — a compromise here is a design-integrity problem, not just an IT one. Patch, restrict external exposure, and audit service accounts that Windchill uses to reach downstream systems. CVE-2025-67038 in the Lantronix EDS5000 device server allows OS command injection as root through the username parameter. Lantronix serial-to-Ethernet gateways are common on legacy PLCs, meters, weighbridges and lab instruments. Because they typically sit on the process network with no EDR and rarely get firmware attention, any exposed management interface is a straight path to persistent OT access. Inventory your Lantronix fleet, restrict management-plane reachability to a jump host, and check for the vendor advisory. Also worth noting: Cisco Unified CM SSRF (CVE-2026-20230) and Catalyst SD-WAN Manager path traversal (CVE-2026-20262) both affect infrastructure commonly shared with plant networks, and SimpleHelp's OIDC signature-verification bypass (CVE-2026-48558) matters for anyone using it for remote vendor support into OT — a favorite ransomware vector.Read more →
