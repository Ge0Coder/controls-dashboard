Today's Knowledge Bite

PostgreSQL, the open-source database flagged in the ABB Symphony Plus advisory, is also embedded in Siemens, GE, Honeywell, and Emerson products — quietly making it one of the most widely deployed databases in process control. Its license permits redistribution without source disclosure, which is why it tends to appear silently in DCS engineering tools, historians, and MES platforms. When a PostgreSQL CVE drops, the real exposure is rarely the systems your DBA knows about.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics by decades. Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass autopilots in the 1910s already implemented proportional-plus-derivative steering for US Navy ships, and Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 mathematical analysis of the USS New Mexico's autopilot is the first formal description of three-term control — written entirely in terms of mechanical linkages and hydraulic feedback, with no concept of an op-amp or a microprocessor. The tuning rules Ziegler and Nichols published in 1942 were derived empirically on pneumatic controllers, and those rules still work today on digital DCS loops because the underlying math is indifferent to the implementation medium.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to inch toward production reality, and 2026 is shaping up as the year it finally appears in mainstream PLC line cards rather than just demo booths. The promise is straightforward: a single Ethernet network carrying both deterministic, sub-millisecond cyclic I/O traffic and conventional best-effort data, replacing the alphabet soup of PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos III, and POWERLINK with one IEEE-standard fabric. For a controls engineer this means motion control, safety, vision, and plant-historian traffic could all share infrastructure without the usual VLAN gymnastics. The practical catches remain real. TSN requires every switch in the path to support the relevant IEEE 802.1 features (Qbv scheduling, Qci ingress policing, AS gPTP for time sync), and very few brownfield managed switches qualify. Configuration tooling is also immature — most vendors still hand-craft schedules in proprietary engineering software rather than via the OPC UA FX (Field eXchange) profiles that are supposed to make multi-vendor integration tractable. Expect the first real deployments in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines, where the cost of dedicated TSN infrastructure pays back via reduced cabling and converged diagnostics, before it filters into process industries.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) remains the standard most controls engineers run into first, because it's increasingly being cited in tender documents from end users who want to push security accountability onto integrators. The risk-assessment workflow it prescribes — define the SUC, partition into zones and conduits, assess risk per zone, assign target security levels (SL-T) — is deceptively simple on paper but consistently underestimated in project schedules. Allow more time than you think for the zone-and-conduit drawing alone; it's typically the artifact that survives longest into operations. Worth watching: ISA/IEC 62443-2-1 was substantially revised in 2024 to align the operator-side cybersecurity management system with the more mature 4-x product standards, and asset owners are now starting to demand evidence of conformance during FAT and SAT. Meanwhile NIST SP 800-82r3 (the OT security guide) is being referenced in more US federal procurement language, and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 'Govern' function is appearing in audit checklists. If you support critical-infrastructure customers, expect to be asked for traceability between your project deliverables and specific 62443 requirements, not just generic 'we follow best practice' statements.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Tampa Bay-area water utility incident in which a contractor's compromised remote-support session was used to alter chemical setpoints on a treatment SCADA HMI. Investigators determined the initial access came through a remote-support tool installed on an engineering workstation — the same class of product (ScreenConnect / SimpleHelp) now appearing repeatedly in CISA's KEV. The attacker briefly drove a sodium hydroxide setpoint outside operational bounds before an operator noticed the alarm and reverted manually; physical chemical-feed limits prevented harm. The sequence is depressingly familiar: a vendor-installed remote tool with a shared admin password, no MFA, no session recording, and a flat path from the engineering workstation into the HMI. The saving graces were a vigilant operator and engineered physical limits on the dosing pumps. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, treat every remote-support tool as a potential ingress and insist on MFA, session recording, and time-boxed access — even when the vendor pushes back. Second, never rely solely on logical setpoint limits in HMI or PLC; engineer physical bounds (mechanical stops, orifice plates, dosing pump max strokes) so a malicious or fat-fingered setpoint cannot create a hazard. Third, train operators to question unexpected setpoint changes the same way they question unexpected alarms — the human in the loop remains the most reliable last line of defense.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

This week's ICS advisory traffic is dominated by ABB, with CISA publishing six concurrent advisories covering Ability OPTIMAX, AWIN Gateways, Edgenius Management Portal, Ability Symphony Plus Engineering, PCM600, and System 800xA / Symphony Plus IEC 61850. The Edgenius and PCM600 issues are the most serious for plant floor teams: both allow specially crafted messages to system nodes to trigger arbitrary code execution, and PCM600 is widely deployed for protection relay configuration in substation automation. The 800xA / Symphony Plus advisory is notable because it concerns ABB's MMS client implementation in the IEC 61850 stack — a protocol now deeply embedded in power, water, and increasingly process plants. The Symphony Plus Engineering advisory traces back to outdated PostgreSQL 13.11, a reminder that embedded third-party components in DCS engineering tools age faster than the DCS itself. The OPTIMAX issue is narrower but worth flagging: it specifically affects sites using Azure AD Single Sign-On integration, where authentication bypass is possible. Anyone running OPTIMAX 6.1, 6.2, or pre-6.3.1-251120 with Entra/Azure AD SSO should treat this as priority. The AWIN Gateway flaws permit unauthenticated reboot and configuration disclosure — low CVSS but high nuisance value if these gateways sit on field networks where availability matters more than confidentiality. On the broader OT-adjacent front, SecurityWeek noted CISA has published new zero-trust guidance specifically for OT environments — a useful read given the persistent industry debate over whether ZT principles can survive contact with deterministic control networks. Two US security professionals were sentenced to four years for assisting a ransomware gang, an uncomfortable insider-threat data point. A new Python-based 'Deep#Door' backdoor framework with Windows persistence has surfaced and is described as oriented toward espionage and disruption — relevant for engineering workstations that bridge IT and OT. Finally, the Bluekit phishing kit now ships with an AI assistant for automated domain registration and lure generation, lowering the skill floor for credential-harvesting campaigns aimed at remote-access portals and vendor support accounts that controls engineers routinely use.Read more →

The KEV catalog added twenty entries over the last 30 days, none of them ICS-specific but several with direct OT relevance. The PaperCut NG/MF flaw (CVE-2023-27351) is now confirmed exploited by ransomware actors and frequently sits on print servers shared between corporate and plant LANs. Also noteworthy: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager racked up three KEV entries (CVE-2026-20122, -20128, -20133), which matters for sites using SD-WAN to backhaul remote facility traffic. The Samsung MagicINFO server flaw (CVE-2024-7399) affects digital signage but those servers often live on corporate networks adjacent to OT DMZs. The Linux kernel privilege escalation (CVE-2026-31431) and a new Microsoft Windows Shell spoofing flaw (CVE-2026-32202) round out the high-priority items for engineering workstations and historians.
