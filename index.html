Today's Knowledge Bite

The original PID controller predates electronics: Elmer Sperry's 1911 gyroscopic ship autopilot implemented proportional, integral, and derivative action mechanically using flyweights, dashpots, and gear trains. Nicholas Minorsky formalised the maths in 1922 by watching helmsmen steer the USS New Mexico — the integral term came from observing that experienced helmsmen would hold extra rudder against a steady wind, something a pure proportional response could never do.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The reason most industrial pressure transmitters output 4–20 mA rather than 0–20 mA has a beautifully practical origin: the live-zero of 4 mA lets you distinguish a genuine zero-process reading from a broken wire, which would read 0 mA. The choice of 20 mA as the upper bound traces back to the 1950s Taylor Instrument and Foxboro pneumatic-to-electronic transition, where engineers wanted a current high enough to drive multiple receivers in series despite cable resistance, but low enough to be intrinsically safe in hazardous areas. That single design decision — a non-zero live zero — has probably prevented more silent instrument failures than any diagnostic protocol since.

Tech Spotlight

Deterministic Ethernet via TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) combined with OPC UA PubSub is finally moving from pilot to production in 2026, particularly in automotive body shops and packaging lines. The premise is straightforward: replace the patchwork of EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, and Ethernet/IP CIP Sync with a single IEEE 802.1 standard stack that delivers sub-microsecond synchronisation and bounded latency on commodity switches, while letting controllers, drives, and IT systems share the same wire. For a controls engineer, that means one cable plant, one diagnostic toolset, and direct publish/subscribe from a drive to a historian without a gateway. The practical reality is messier. TSN requires every switch in the path to support the relevant sub-standards — 802.1AS for time, 802.1Qbv for scheduled traffic, 802.1CB for frame replication — and not all vendor implementations interoperate cleanly. Configuration is also fundamentally different: instead of setting a cyclic rate on a master, you now author a network-wide schedule, often via a CNC (Centralised Network Configuration) tool that few engineers are trained on. Expect TSN to land first in greenfield lines from a single automation vendor, and to coexist awkwardly with legacy fieldbuses for another five years before anyone seriously rips out PROFINET.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) and 62443-4-1 (secure product development lifecycle) continue to harden into procurement requirements rather than aspirational guidance. Several major end users in oil and gas and pharma now mandate 62443-4-1 certification as a tender prerequisite, which is forcing PLC and HMI vendors to publish security development lifecycle artefacts they previously kept internal. If you are specifying new equipment this year, ask vendors for their 4-1 maturity level and the 4-2 component security level for each device — not just a marketing claim of being 'IEC 62443 compliant', which is meaningless without specifying the part and SL target. NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3 remains the most accessible free reference for OT security architecture, and its alignment with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (which added the 'Govern' function) gives engineers a useful vocabulary when talking to IT and audit. Worth watching is the ongoing work on ISA/IEC 62443-2-2 (security program ratings), which aims to give asset owners a defensible scoring method for their own operational security programme — useful ammunition when justifying segmentation projects or jump-host investments to finance.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful case to revisit this week is the 2024 Frostygoop malware incident in Lviv, Ukraine, where attackers used Modbus TCP commands to manipulate ENCO heat controllers and disrupt heating for over 600 apartment buildings in sub-zero conditions. The intrusion did not exploit a controller zero-day; attackers gained initial access months earlier through a vulnerable Mikrotik router, moved laterally, and then simply spoke Modbus to devices that, by design, accepted any well-formed command on TCP/502. The malware itself was small and largely a Modbus client wrapper. Three lessons travel well to any plant. First, Modbus, DNP3, and similar protocols have no authentication — your only control is who can reach TCP/502, so flat networks and 'temporary' VPN exceptions are the actual vulnerability. Second, the dwell time between IT compromise and OT impact was months; good network flow logging at the IT/OT boundary would likely have caught the reconnaissance. Third, the impact was achieved with legitimate protocol traffic, meaning signature-based IDS would not have flagged it — behavioural baselining of 'who normally talks Modbus to this device' is what catches this class of attack.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The dominant theme this week is not a direct ICS exploit but a wave of IT-side compromises with clear paths into OT. Fortinet has acknowledged the so-called FortiBleed credential-harvesting campaign, with attackers compiling a database of more than 86,000 confirmed working credentials against Fortinet appliances. For plant networks, Fortinet firewalls are very often the IT/OT boundary device, and validated credentials short-circuit most segmentation assumptions. If you operate FortiGate or FortiManager at your perimeter, treat every administrative and SSL-VPN credential as suspect, rotate them, and review logs for anomalous VPN sessions and configuration changes. Supply-chain attacks continue to escalate. Researchers attribute the malicious Mastra NPM package campaign to a North Korean cluster, with over 140 packages trojanised to fetch payloads targeting cryptocurrency browser extensions. While crypto wallets are not the controls engineer's concern, the same poisoned-dependency pattern has reached OT vendor tooling before — engineering workstations that pull JavaScript-based HMI, dashboarding, or build tooling from public registries are exposed. Pin versions, mirror to an internal registry, and review what your historian and dashboard vendors are pulling at install time. The ShinyHunters and Klue breach disclosures are a reminder that modern intrusions increasingly skip malware entirely, relying on stolen OAuth tokens, SaaS misconfiguration, and trust between vendors. Several major security firms — HackerOne, Huntress, Snyk, Tanium, Recorded Future — have now disclosed downstream impact from the Klue compromise, illustrating how a single SaaS vendor breach cascades. The implication for plant engineers: your remote-support, asset-inventory, and CMMS SaaS vendors sit upstream of your network, and their tokens to your environment deserve the same scrutiny you give a domain admin account. Finally, the Apple Usbliter8 boot-defence bypass and a Gravity SMTP WordPress flaw are getting press but are largely background noise for OT — unless corporate iPhones are used for HMI mirroring or remote alarming, in which case treat them as untrusted endpoints until Apple responds.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

CISA's KEV additions over the past two weeks are heavy on perimeter and identity systems that frequently front OT networks. Most consequential are Check Point Security Gateway CVE-2026-50751, an IKEv1 authentication bypass already linked to ransomware activity, and Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS CVE-2026-0257, an authentication bypass allowing unauthorised VPN sessions. Both are textbook initial-access vectors into flat or weakly-segmented OT environments. Patch or apply vendor mitigations urgently, and audit VPN session logs for sessions without corresponding MFA events. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager picks up two entries (CVE-2026-20262 path traversal and CVE-2026-20245 command injection as root), relevant for sites using SD-WAN to backhaul remote substations, pumping stations, or skid units. For server-side OT-adjacent infrastructure, the Splunk PostgreSQL sidecar flaw (CVE-2026-20253) enables unauthenticated file creation/truncation — a real concern if your SIEM ingests OT logs and sits in a shared zone. Oracle PeopleSoft (CVE-2026-35273) and Oracle WebLogic (CVE-2024-21182) are again under active exploitation, and many MES, LIMS, and ERP-to-OT integrations still ride on WebLogic. Ivanti Sentry CVE-2026-10520 (unauth root RCE) deserves immediate attention if you use it for mobile device gating into the corporate network. The Arista EOS decapsulation bug (CVE-2026-7473) is a quieter but interesting one for anyone running L3 leaf-spine fabrics in plant data centres — crafted tunnelled packets can bypass intended ACL boundaries. Practical priorities for this week: patch internet-facing Check Point and PAN-OS gateways, confirm Cisco SD-WAN Manager is not exposed beyond a jump host, inventory any Ivanti Sentry or WebLogic instances touching OT data flows, and verify Splunk sidecar services are not reachable from process networks.Read more →
