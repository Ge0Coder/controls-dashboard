Today's Knowledge Bite

The Hitachi Energy PCM600 advisory is a quiet reminder that protection and control engineering tools — the software you use to configure IEDs and protection relays — are themselves a privileged attack surface. A compromised PCM600 workstation can push altered protection settings to relays, and unlike a PLC logic change, a bad relay setting may sit dormant for months until a real fault occurs and the relay fails to trip (or trips wrongly), turning a cyber incident into a physical equipment-destruction event during the next grid disturbance.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively reverse-engineered from a helmsman, not derived from theory. Nicolas Minorsky's 1922 paper on automatic ship steering came from watching how experienced helmsmen on the USS New Mexico anticipated the ship's swing — applying rudder proportional to error, integral of past error, and derivative of how fast the heading was changing. The mathematics formalised an existing human skill, which is why PID still feels intuitive to tune: you are essentially encoding what a careful human operator would do, and why poorly-tuned loops often look like an anxious novice over-correcting at the wheel.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the IEC 62541-derived extension that pushes OPC UA down from the supervisory layer into controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication on the plant floor. FX adds standardised information models for automation components, peer-to-peer publish/subscribe over UDP multicast, and — critically — alignment with TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) for deterministic, sub-millisecond cyclic data exchange. The promise is that one protocol stack, with one security model (X.509 certificates, signed and encrypted sessions), spans from sensor to ERP, replacing the patchwork of PROFINET, EtherNet/IP, Modbus and proprietary backplane buses we live with today. For controls engineers the practical implication is significant: a future PLC-to-PLC interlock could be both deterministic and natively authenticated, eliminating the awkward pattern of running unauthenticated real-time fieldbus traffic alongside a separate OPC UA supervisory channel. The ABB B&R Automation Studio advisory this week — a TLS peer-impersonation flaw on the OPC UA / ANSL transport — illustrates why the security model has to be right: when one protocol carries everything, certificate validation bugs become safety-relevant. Adoption is still early. Demonstrators from Siemens, B&R, Beckhoff and Rockwell exist, but you'll struggle to buy a complete brownfield-friendly TSN switch fabric, and certificate lifecycle management at the device level (issuance, rotation, revocation across thousands of field devices) remains the unsolved operational problem. Expect pilot deployments in greenfield automotive and semiconductor lines through 2026–2027 before broader process-industry uptake.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-4-2 (technical security requirements for components) continues to be the standard most directly relevant to the vendor advisories landing this week. The ABB B&R Automation Studio TLS/OPC UA issue maps cleanly to CR 3.1 (communication integrity) and CR 1.5 (authenticator management); the Hitachi PCM600 integrity issue touches CR 3.4 (software and information integrity). When you're evaluating a vendor's response to a CVE, asking which 4-2 component requirement was violated — and what their secure-development-lifecycle process under 62443-4-1 missed — is a more productive conversation than chasing CVSS scores. On the regulatory side, the floated US 72-hour federal patch mandate is worth tracking because it will reshape contractual language for critical-infrastructure operators. NERC CIP-010-4 already requires 35-day patch evaluation cycles for medium/high-impact BES Cyber Systems; a 72-hour expectation creeping in via federal procurement could pressure utilities and their integrators to demonstrate faster paths to qualified patches. Realistically, a 72-hour cycle is incompatible with conventional FAT/SAT regression testing on a DCS, which is exactly the tension ISA TR84.00.09 (cybersecurity related to the safety lifecycle) tries to address. If you haven't looked at it recently, ISA/IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) remains the most useful starting point for justifying zone-and-conduit decisions to non-OT stakeholders — particularly when post-incident reviews like the Polish water case force rapid architectural changes.Read more →

Incident of the Week

The incident of the week is the Polish ICS breach across five water treatment plants, disclosed by the country's Internal Security Agency (ABW). According to the public reporting, attackers achieved more than network access — they reached systems where operational parameters of treatment processes (likely chemical dosing setpoints, pump schedules, or filtration cycle parameters) could be modified. No mass poisoning or service outage has been reported, suggesting either the access was detected before manipulation, or changes were within ranges that downstream safety instrumentation and manual sampling caught. While full technical detail isn't yet public, the pattern matches previous small-utility water incidents: internet-reachable HMIs or VNC, default or shared credentials, and flat networks where a single compromised device yields full process visibility. Small water utilities typically operate without dedicated OT security staff, rely on integrators for remote support, and run long-lifecycle Windows-based SCADA without segmentation between business IT and the control network. Three lessons for controls engineers: first, assume any remote-access path you didn't personally configure (vendor cellular routers, legacy dial-in modems repurposed over IP, 'temporary' TeamViewer installs) is reachable from the internet until proven otherwise — inventory and kill them. Second, put hard physical or logic-based limits on safety-critical setpoints (e.g., chlorine dosing ceilings enforced in PLC logic, not just on the HMI) so that an HMI compromise cannot directly translate into an unsafe process state. Third, ensure your independent verification — manual sampling, SIS, mechanical relief — is genuinely independent of the SCADA layer; if your only check on dosing is a trend on the same compromised HMI, you have no check at all.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

The headline event is Poland's internal security agency confirming that attackers breached ICS at five water treatment plants, gaining the ability to modify operational parameters of treatment equipment. This is not a reconnaissance or IT-only incident — it represents direct manipulation capability over public water supply, the same threat class as the Oldsmar and Aliquippa events. Initial reporting points to internet-exposed HMIs and weak remote-access hygiene on small-utility infrastructure. Plant engineers running similar small-municipal SCADA stacks should treat this as a forcing function to audit any cellular routers, VNC servers, or vendor remote-support tunnels still reachable from the public internet. CISA published a heavy slate of vendor advisories on Thursday and Friday. ABB's B&R portfolio alone accounts for three: Automation Studio (TLS/OPC-UA peer-impersonation flaw via certificate validation weakness in the ANSL transport), Automation Runtime (denial-of-service in versions <6.5 and selected R-branch builds, CVE-2025-11044 and a sibling CVE), and PVI client (sensitive data leakage in logs, mitigated by the default-disabled logging). Hitachi Energy PCM600 has an integrity-impacting vulnerability affecting protection-relay configuration workflows in transmission and distribution substations. Johnson Controls CEM AC2000 (physical access control, common in critical-infrastructure perimeters) carries a CVSS 8.7 local privilege escalation across versions 10.6, 11.0 and 12.0. MAXHUB Pivot (conferencing client, occasionally found on engineering-office laptops) rounds out the set. On the broader OT-adjacent front, a train hacker arrest tied to the long-running Polish railway radio-stop incidents was confirmed, and the US government floated a 72-hour patch mandate for federal systems — a cadence that will inevitably bleed into critical-infrastructure contracting language. SANS disclosed a second universal Linux LPE in two weeks, 'Dirty Frag,' following Copy Fail (CVE-2026-31431, now in KEV). Any Linux-based HMI, historian, edge gateway, or virtualisation host should be on your radar for kernel updates as distro patches land. Finally, a ransomware claim against Trellix and a breach at AI-ops vendor Braintrust are reminders that your security tooling and your AI/ML supply chain are themselves attack surfaces — relevant if you've started piping process data into cloud analytics or copilots.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

This week's KEV additions skew heavily toward edge and identity infrastructure that touches OT environments. Palo Alto PAN-OS CVE-2026-0300 is the most urgent: an unauthenticated out-of-bounds write in the User-ID Captive Portal yielding root RCE on PA-Series and VM firewalls. If your IT/OT DMZ relies on Palo Alto firewalls — extremely common at the Purdue Level 3.5 boundary — patch immediately and audit Captive Portal exposure. Ivanti EPMM (CVE-2026-6973) and Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (three separate CVEs) round out the network-edge concerns; SD-WAN Manager increasingly underpins multi-site manufacturing WANs. Linux kernel CVE-2026-31431 (Copy Fail) is now KEV-listed, and as noted above a second LPE (Dirty Frag) has just dropped. Privilege escalation on Linux-based engineering workstations, Ignition gateways, AVEVA/Wonderware historians on RHEL, or any containerised edge node should be assumed practical. Pair kernel patching with a review of who actually has shell access to those hosts. The ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2024-1708) and SimpleHelp (CVE-2024-57726/57728) entries deserve specific attention from controls teams: these remote-support tools are heavily used by integrators and OEMs to reach customer plants. If a vendor or SI uses either to support your DCS or PLCs, demand confirmation of patch status before allowing the next remote session.Read more →
