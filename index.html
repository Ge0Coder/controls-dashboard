Today's Knowledge Bite

The Lantronix serial-to-Ethernet server family, which just landed on the KEV list, traces its lineage to devices first shipped in the mid-1990s to network DEC VAX terminals. Many of those product lines are still in production and are functionally backward-compatible — which is why you can still find twenty-year-old units happily bridging RS-232 to Modbus/TCP on a live plant floor, often with the default admin password intact.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller predates electronics entirely — Elmer Sperry's gyrocompass-based ship autopilots in 1911 already implemented proportional, integral, and derivative action mechanically, using flyweights, dashpots, and gear trains. Nicholas Minorsky formalised the mathematics in 1922 after observing how helmsmen actually steered the USS New Mexico, noting they responded not just to heading error but to its rate of change and its accumulation. So the fundamental control algorithm running in billions of loops worldwide was reverse-engineered from watching sailors do their job — the human intuition came first, the maths second.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues to move from demo-booth to production, and 2026 is shaping up as the year it starts appearing in mainstream vendor roadmaps beyond Siemens, B&R, and Rockwell. The premise is straightforward: combine OPC UA's semantic, secure information model with deterministic Ethernet timing (IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic, 802.1AS time sync) so one converged network can carry motion-control-grade cyclic data alongside diagnostics, MES traffic, and video — replacing the traditional split between fieldbus and Ethernet. For controls engineers the practical implication is that the historic tradeoff between determinism and openness is genuinely dissolving. You can, in principle, run drive sync loops at sub-millisecond jitter across the same switches carrying your historian traffic. That eliminates protocol converters, simplifies cable plants, and makes secure-by-design more achievable because everything speaks OPC UA with its built-in authentication and encryption. The adoption challenges are real, though. TSN switches are still 2-3x the price of standard managed industrial switches, network engineering skills (stream reservation, gate control lists) are scarce, and multi-vendor interoperability remains fragile — testing at plugfests reveals mismatches in how vendors implement the same standard. Most greenfield projects still default to EtherCAT or PROFINET IRT for hard real-time and treat OPC UA/TSN as a supervisory layer. Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (Security risk assessment for system design) remains the standard most often misapplied in the field. It's the one that defines the zone-and-conduit methodology and requires you to assign a target security level (SL-T) to each zone based on risk assessment — yet many sites treat it as a documentation exercise done once at commissioning and never revisited. The 2020 edition is stable, but ISA99 working groups have been actively developing guidance on how SL-T assignment should tie into IEC 62443-3-3 system requirements and IEC 62443-4-2 component requirements, closing a gap that has frustrated integrators. Worth noting is the growing alignment between IEC 62443 and NIST's forthcoming updates to SP 800-82 Rev 3, which increasingly references 62443 constructs directly rather than defining parallel terminology. For engineers this means a single risk assessment framework can now credibly satisfy both a US federal audience and international customers — a meaningful simplification for global manufacturers. On the ISA side, the ISA/IEC 62443 cybersecurity certification programmes continue to gain traction with EPCs and system integrators. If you specify controls work, requiring bidders to hold at least ISA/IEC 62443 Fundamentals certification is now a reasonable minimum bar and costs bidders very little to comply with.Read more →

Incident of the Week

A useful recent case to revisit is the January 2024 Aliquippa, Pennsylvania water authority intrusion, in which the Cyber Av3ngers group compromised a Unitronics Vision-series PLC controlling a booster pump station. The attackers didn't exploit any sophisticated vulnerability — the PLC was internet-exposed on TCP/20256 with the factory default password (1111) still in place. They defaced the HMI with a political message and forced the pump station into manual operation; no water quality impact occurred, but the incident triggered CISA advisories and a wave of similar compromises across US and Irish water utilities. The how is uncomfortably mundane: Shodan-style scanning identified exposed Unitronics devices, and the default credential worked. Similar Unitronics compromises followed at municipal water sites in multiple states because the same install pattern — internet-reachable for integrator remote support, default password unchanged — was widespread. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, small water and wastewater sites, and by extension any small-footprint OT deployment, are being actively targeted precisely because they lack dedicated security staff — assume you are on the target list regardless of size. Second, integrator remote-access convenience is the single largest source of exposed OT; require a jump host with MFA, never direct PLC exposure. Third, change default credentials as part of commissioning acceptance testing, not as a follow-up task — if it isn't verified before sign-off, it never happens.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today's headlines are dominated by enterprise IT patch cycles, but several items deserve attention on the plant floor. Citrix disclosed six NetScaler vulnerabilities including a new HTTP/2 Bomb DoS technique and a CitrixBleed-style information disclosure — NetScaler often fronts remote-access portals used by vendors and integrators to reach engineering workstations and historians, so patching this appliance closes a well-worn OT ingress path. Adobe issued critical fixes (CVSS 10.0) for ColdFusion and Campaign Classic, both still surprisingly common on legacy MES-adjacent web front-ends. Apple's June bundle patched dozens of WebKit and kernel flaws across iOS, macOS, and Safari. That matters for the growing population of iPads used as HMI thin clients and for engineers doing remote access via mobile. Microsoft's Teams update, meanwhile, tightens control over external AI bots joining meetings — worth reviewing if your organisation has been experimenting with AI notetakers sitting in on incident bridges or vendor design reviews where control system architecture is discussed. On the automation side, no major PLC/DCS vendor advisories dropped today, but the CISA KEV additions (see below) include PTC Windchill/FlexPLM and Lantronix EDS5000 — both squarely in the OT/engineering domain. SANS ISC also published a useful piece on favicon-based host recon automation, a reminder that attackers are increasingly fingerprinting exposed engineering systems by asset signature rather than port scanning. The overall picture is one of steady erosion at the IT/OT boundary: VPN concentrators, PLM systems, terminal servers, and remote-access appliances are the vectors adversaries are actually walking through, not zero-days in the PLCs themselves. CISA Vulnerability Digest

Three KEV entries deserve immediate attention from OT teams. CVE-2026-12569 in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM is an unauthenticated RCE — Windchill holds product design data, BOMs, and often integrates with MES and ERP, so exposure here is both an IP-theft and a lateral-movement risk. CVE-2025-67038 in the Lantronix EDS5000 serial-to-Ethernet gateway allows root command injection via the username field; these devices sit directly on control networks bridging legacy serial equipment, and many are deployed and forgotten. CVE-2026-34908/34909/34910 covers a trio of Ubiquiti UniFi OS flaws (command injection, path traversal, improper access control) — UniFi is widely used in small-plant and remote-site networking. On the perimeter side, CVE-2026-50751 (Check Point Security Gateway IKEv1 authentication bypass, confirmed used in ransomware) and CVE-2026-10520 (Ivanti Sentry unauthenticated root RCE) are both high-priority for any site using these products for remote OT access. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager has two entries (CVE-2026-20262, CVE-2026-20245), and Splunk Enterprise's CVE-2026-20253 lets unauthenticated users create or truncate arbitrary files via a PostgreSQL sidecar — relevant if you rely on Splunk for OT log aggregation. Practical remediation: inventory Lantronix, Ubiquiti, PTC, Check Point, and Ivanti footprints against your asset register today; if patching is blocked by change windows, at minimum restrict management-plane access to a jump host and verify that these devices are not reachable from IT or the internet. The PTC and Lantronix entries in particular are the sort of assets that rarely make it into IT-driven vulnerability programmes.Read more →
