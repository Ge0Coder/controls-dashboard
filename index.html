Today's Knowledge Bite

The 'Dirty Frag' vulnerability class referenced in today's Hitachi CoreTec advisory stems from how some embedded TCP/IP stacks reassemble fragmented IP packets without properly validating overlapping fragment offsets — a bug pattern first documented in 1998's 'teardrop' attack. Nearly three decades later, the same mistake keeps resurfacing in new silicon and new firmware because fragment reassembly code is rarely rewritten; it is copy-pasted from reference implementations whose original authors have long since moved on.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalized not in a university lab but aboard US Navy ships in the 1920s, when Nicolas Minorsky analyzed the steering of USS New Mexico and realized a helmsman intuitively applied proportional, integral, and derivative action to hold a course against wind and waves. His 1922 paper essentially reverse-engineered human control behavior into math. Nearly every tuning rule you use today — Ziegler-Nichols, lambda tuning, IMC — ultimately traces back to the problem of keeping a battleship pointed in the right direction.

Tech Spotlight

Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) over standard Ethernet is quietly reaching a tipping point in process and discrete automation. TSN is a set of IEEE 802.1 standards (notably 802.1Qbv scheduled traffic, 802.1AS time sync, and 802.1CB frame replication) that allows deterministic, bounded-latency traffic to share the same wire as best-effort IT traffic. For controls engineers, this means a single converged network can carry motion control cycles, PROFINET IRT-class traffic, OPC UA PubSub updates, and plain video or MES data simultaneously — without the dedicated fieldbus hardware that historically forced vendor lock-in. The practical implication is huge: migration from proprietary industrial Ethernet variants (EtherCAT, PROFINET IRT, SERCOS III) toward OPC UA over TSN as a unified application-plus-transport stack. Early adopters in automotive and semiconductor fabs are already running mixed-vendor TSN cells. However, adoption challenges remain real — TSN requires every switch in the path to support the same profile (IEC/IEEE 60802 is still stabilizing), network engineering skills shift from 'plug and play' toward careful schedule computation, and troubleshooting tools are still catching up. If you are specifying a greenfield line in 2027, insist on TSN-capable switches and NICs even if you don't light up the features day one; retrofitting is painful.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues to see steady movement on part 2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners), with the 2024 edition now being referenced in procurement specs across European utilities and increasingly in US water sector guidance. The practical shift for controls engineers is that asset owners are being pushed to document a Cybersecurity Management System (CSMS) in operational terms — zone and conduit diagrams, patch SLAs by criticality, and documented acceptance of residual risk — rather than treating security as a one-time commissioning deliverable. If your site has an ISA/IEC 62443-3-3 security level target but no CSMS, auditors are starting to notice. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the touchstone for OT security guidance, and CISA's Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals (CPGs) have become the de facto baseline being referenced in TSA pipeline security directives and EPA water sector memoranda. Worth noting: the ISA Global Cybersecurity Alliance has been pushing ISASecure certification for components (CSA) and systems (SSA) as the conformance mechanism to 62443-4-2 — so when a vendor claims '62443 compliant,' ask specifically which part, which security level, and whether an accredited lab issued the certificate. Incident of the Week

A useful one to revisit this week is the 2024 Arnprior, Ontario water treatment plant intrusion, which quietly set a precedent for small-utility OT incidents. Attackers reached an internet-exposed HMI through a VPN appliance with default credentials and manipulated setpoints on a chemical dosing system. Operators noticed anomalous readings within hours, reverted to manual control, and no unsafe water was distributed — but the facility ran in degraded mode for days while forensics and rebuilds were completed. Mechanically, the attack chain was unremarkable: exposed remote access, weak authentication, flat network from the VPN concentrator straight to Level 2 HMIs, and no MFA. What made it instructive was the response — operators had a written manual-operation procedure and had drilled it, which is what prevented a safety event. The lessons for controls engineers are threefold: first, inventory every remote access path (including the ones vendors added 'temporarily' years ago) and put MFA on all of them; second, ensure HMI setpoint changes generate alarms visible to operators independently of the HMI itself (a separate historian trend or hardwired annunciator); and third, drill manual operations regularly, because the day you need to run the plant from the field is not the day to find out the written procedure is wrong.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a heavy slate of ICS advisories this morning that will land directly on controls engineers' desks. Hitachi Energy disclosed OS command injection and 'Dirty Frag' flaws in the CoreTec product line, and multiple RCE and information disclosure vulnerabilities in the TRO600 series wireless gateways commonly used in utility substations and remote telemetry. Siemens reported an authentication bypass in the Mendix SAML module via replayable IdP-initiated assertions, and a Secure Boot bypass in SIMATIC Industrial PCs tied to a leaked UEFI test key — a reminder that supply chain trust anchors can quietly erode platform integrity even when Secure Boot is 'enabled.' Mitsubishi Electric and Iconics issued a broad advisory covering GENESIS, GENESIS64, ICONICS Suite, Hyper Historian, AnalytiX, MobileHMI, IoTWorX, MC Works64, GENESIS32, and BizViz. The flaws (including CVE-2023-4863 WebP, and new auth bypass CVEs 2026-16436/16437) can enable arbitrary code execution, authentication bypass, and DoS across the HMI/historian stack. Given how widely GENESIS and Hyper Historian are deployed in water, pharma, and building automation, patch planning for this one should start today. EPICS Base (<=7.0.10) is also affected by four new memory corruption and DoS vulnerabilities. EPICS underpins large-scale physics facilities, accelerators, and some utility SCADA — environments where patch windows are measured in months, so compensating controls (strict CA network segmentation, channel access gateways with authentication) should be reviewed in the interim. On the broader security front, Chainalysis disclosed a breach executed through 'offensive agentic' AI techniques, and a malicious Claude Code extension was distributed via the Cursor marketplace that opened reverse SSH tunnels — both reinforcing that developer and engineering workstations (including those used for PLC programming) are increasingly targeted through the AI tool supply chain. SANS also flagged renewed scrutiny of Multipath TCP after a Linux kernel implementation flaw; MPTCP flows frequently bypass IDS inspection, something to validate on any OT DMZ running modern Linux-based jump hosts or firewalls.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalog added two chained Ivanti Connect Secure flaws (CVE-2026-118527 and CVE-2026-111357) that together permit authenticated file upload and local privilege escalation — relevant anywhere Connect Secure fronts remote vendor or engineer access to OT networks. Pair patching with a review of which accounts actually need Connect Secure access to control system jump hosts; most third-party integrators do not. Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD (CVE-2026-76418) was also added as an unauthenticated remote DoS via NULL pointer dereference, which matters for perimeter firewalls protecting Purdue Level 3/3.5 boundaries where availability is as critical as confidentiality. Siemens Software Center (CVE-2026-110936) made KEV as a DLL search-path hijack requiring local authenticated access — a classic engineering workstation threat, since Software Center is routinely used to deploy TIA Portal and other Siemens engineering tools. Treat engineering laptops as privileged assets: application allowlisting, removal of writable paths in the PATH variable, and signed-DLL enforcement go a long way. Also notable are the VMware ESX logging-bypass flaw (CVE-2026-41709) affecting virtualization hosts that increasingly run SCADA, historians, and HMI VMs — if your ESXi logs feed a SIEM, verify what admin actions are actually captured. Round out your review with the Fortinet path traversal (CVE-2025-61624) and BeyondTrust Remote Support authentication bypass (CVE-2026-102764); both products commonly sit at the IT/OT boundary and in third-party remote support paths. CISA's 21-day federal remediation clock is a reasonable yardstick for OT asset owners too, even if downtime windows push you longer.Read more →
