Today's Knowledge Bite

The IEC 61850 MMS protocol underlying today's ABB advisory was itself derived from the older ISO 9506 Manufacturing Message Specification, which dates back to General Motors' MAP (Manufacturing Automation Protocol) initiative in the 1980s. That 40-year-old object model — variables, domains, named lists — is still the lingua franca of substation automation worldwide, which is why a single client-stack flaw can echo across vendors.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was effectively reverse-engineered from human helmsmen. In the 1920s, Nicolas Minorsky watched US Navy quartermasters steer the USS New Mexico and noticed they didn't just react to heading error — they anticipated based on rate of change and remembered persistent offsets, exactly the D and I terms. His 1922 paper formalised what experienced sailors had been doing intuitively for centuries, which is why PID feels natural: it's literally a mathematical description of skilled human control behaviour.

Tech Spotlight

This week's spotlight is OPC UA FX (Field eXchange), the extension of OPC UA aimed squarely at controller-to-controller and controller-to-device communication on the plant floor — the territory historically owned by EtherNet/IP, PROFINET and Modbus. FX adds standardised information models for field-level assets, peer-to-peer communication patterns, and crucially, integration with Time-Sensitive Networking (TSN) for deterministic delivery on standard Ethernet. The promise is a single, secure, vendor-neutral protocol from sensor to cloud, with built-in certificate-based authentication and encryption. In practice, adoption is uneven. The big four (Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider, B&R/ABB) have all demonstrated FX, but production-grade controllers with full FX-over-TSN are still trickling out, and very few brownfield switches support the IEEE 802.1Qbv scheduling that makes TSN deterministic. The practical implication for controls engineers: don't rip out PROFINET or CIP yet, but when specifying new greenfield lines or doing major switch refreshes, ask vendors specifically about TSN profile support (IEC/IEEE 60802) and FX roadmap commitments. Standards Watch

IEC 62443 continues to be the gravitational centre of OT cyber standards, and the ongoing maintenance of 62443-2-1 (security program requirements for asset owners) is worth tracking — the revised edition restructures the original around a clearer set of security program elements (SPEs), making it considerably easier to map to an actual operating model rather than a checklist. If your last gap assessment used the 2010 edition, the new structure will feel quite different and aligns better with how 62443-3-3 system requirements are audited in practice. On the US side, NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the practical companion document, and CISA's newly mentioned zero-trust-in-OT guidance (referenced in this week's SecurityWeek roundup) is a useful bridge between the IT-centric NIST SP 800-207 zero trust architecture and the realities of Purdue-model networks. The guidance pragmatically acknowledges that you cannot apply per-request authentication to a safety PLC, and instead emphasises identity-aware brokers, micro-segmentation at conduit boundaries, and continuous monitoring — language that maps cleanly onto 62443 zones and conduits. Incident of the Week

This week's revisit is the 2024 Frontier Software/Frontier Communications and adjacent ScreenConnect-driven intrusions, made newly relevant by CVE-2024-1708 landing on KEV. In several documented cases through late 2024 and 2025, threat actors — including Black Basta and LockBit affiliates — abused unpatched ConnectWise ScreenConnect instances at managed service providers to pivot into customer environments, including manufacturing and water utility clients. The attackers used the path traversal flaw to plant rogue extensions, then leveraged the MSP's pre-authorised remote sessions to deploy ransomware tooling on engineering workstations and historian servers, in some cases reaching HMI nodes on flat networks. What made this so damaging in OT contexts was not the vulnerability itself but the trust relationship: the MSP's remote-support tool was explicitly allowlisted through firewalls and EDR exclusions because it was 'the support channel.' Once compromised, that channel became an unmonitored highway into the OT DMZ. Three lessons for controls engineers. First, treat every remote-support tool as a Tier-0 asset and demand evidence of patching from your integrators before each campaign — written, dated, with version numbers. Second, the support session itself should be brokered through a jump host that you control, with session recording and per-session credentials, not a persistent agent installed on the engineering workstation. Third, EDR and firewall allowlists for support tools should be time-boxed: open for the maintenance window, closed afterwards, audited monthly.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

Today is dominated by a coordinated wave of CISA ICS advisories covering ABB's product line. ABB Ability OPTIMAX 6.1 through early 6.3 builds carry an authentication bypass when integrated with Azure AD SSO, which is particularly relevant for power generation and industrial energy optimisation sites that have federated OPTIMAX into corporate identity. Separately, ABB AWIN gateways (GW100 rev.2 firmware 2.0-0/2.0-1) can be remotely rebooted and queried unauthenticated, exposing system configuration; these are common in building and utility telemetry. ABB Edgenius Management Portal 3.2.0.0/3.2.1.1 carries a remote code execution flaw allowing arbitrary install/uninstall and config modification on edge nodes — a serious concern given Edgenius typically sits at the IT/OT boundary aggregating field data. For the heavier process automation stack, ABB Symphony Plus Engineering inherits PostgreSQL 13.11-and-earlier vulnerabilities exploitable from the S+ Client/Server network, and PCM600 (used to configure protection relays in substations) versions 1.5 through 2.13 have a path traversal leading to arbitrary code execution when crafted project files are opened. Finally, an IEC 61850 MMS client stack vulnerability affects System 800xA and Symphony Plus, hitting the heart of substation automation interoperability. Engineers maintaining DCS or substation gear should treat this as a single coordinated patch cycle from ABB and start planning maintenance windows now. On the wider security front, SecurityWeek reports over 40,000 cPanel/WHM servers compromised via CVE-2026-41940, an authentication bypass that CISA added to KEV last week. While cPanel is rarely on the plant floor, many small integrators, OEM remote-support portals and vendor download sites run on it — worth checking your supply chain. Also of note: CISA has issued new guidance for zero trust in OT (mentioned in SecurityWeek's roundup), and the US DoD signed deals with seven hyperscalers and AI vendors to run AI on classified systems, a signal of where defence-grade OT/AI integration is heading. Housekeeping items worth flagging: Wireshark 4.6.5 fixes 43 vulnerabilities — update your engineering laptops, especially anything used for capturing on live process networks. SANS also reported a malvertising campaign pushing the MacSync stealer via fake Homebrew ads, a reminder that engineering workstations on macOS are increasingly targeted.Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

The KEV catalogue added a heavy slate over the last two weeks. Most relevant to OT-adjacent infrastructure are CVE-2024-1708 (ConnectWise ScreenConnect path traversal) and CVE-2024-57728/57726 (SimpleHelp) — both remote-support tools widely used by integrators and OEMs to reach customer sites. If your vendor uses either to dial into your DMZ jump host, confirm their patch status before granting another session. Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager picked up three KEV entries (CVE-2026-20122, 20133, 20128) covering file upload abuse, info disclosure and recoverable password storage; SD-WAN often underpins multi-site OT WAN consolidation, so this matters for distributed utilities, pipelines and manufacturing groups. Also notable: Apache ActiveMQ CVE-2026-34197 (code injection) — ActiveMQ shows up in MES, historian connectors and IIoT brokers more than people realise. Samsung MagicINFO 9 (CVE-2024-7399) is on KEV and frequently runs HMI-style digital signage in plant lobbies and control rooms on the same VLANs as production. PaperCut NG/MF (CVE-2023-27351) is back with confirmed ransomware use, and a 17-year-old Excel RCE (CVE-2009-0238) was added — a useful reminder that legacy engineering workstations still opening vendor-supplied .xls files are squarely in scope.
