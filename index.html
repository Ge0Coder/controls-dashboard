Today's Knowledge Bite

The Siemens SIMATIC IPC UEFI Secure Boot flaw stems from a 2012-era Microsoft test key that was never meant for production but ended up embedded in the firmware trust store of millions of devices. Once you understand that industrial PCs often inherit the same UEFI binaries as consumer motherboards, it becomes clear why 'Secure Boot enabled' on an HMI is not the same guarantee as 'Secure Boot trustworthy.' This is why defence-in-depth with measured boot and TPM attestation matters more than a single checkbox in BIOS.

Engineering Curiosity

⚛

The PID controller as we know it was formalised not in a lab but on a US Navy destroyer. Nicholas Minorsky derived the three-term control law in 1922 while analysing automatic ship steering on the USS New Mexico, by carefully watching how a skilled helmsman anticipated the ship's yaw rather than just reacting to it. His insight — that a good helmsman intuitively adds a derivative term to damp overshoot and an integral term to eliminate steady-state heading error — predates the first widespread industrial pneumatic PID controllers by more than a decade. Every loop you tune today traces its mathematical lineage to one man watching a sailor turn a wheel.

Tech Spotlight

OPC UA over TSN (Time-Sensitive Networking) continues its slow but steady move from standards documents into real plant deployments. The idea is to replace fieldbus-specific real-time networks (PROFINET IRT, EtherCAT, Sercos) with a single converged Ethernet layer where OPC UA PubSub carries both deterministic motion control traffic and traditional supervisory data on the same wire. TSN provides the sub-microsecond scheduling (IEEE 802.1Qbv) and frame preemption (802.1Qbu) that makes this possible, while OPC UA provides a semantic model that higher-level systems — MES, historians, digital twins — can consume natively. For controls engineers the practical implications are significant: fewer gateways, one cable plant for IT and OT, and a path to vendor-neutral motion control. The adoption challenges are equally significant. TSN requires end-to-end support in every switch, NIC, and device along the deterministic path — mixing a non-TSN switch into the chain breaks the guarantees. Configuration tooling is still fragmented across vendors, and few integrators have hands-on experience configuring Qbv gate schedules. Expect pilot deployments in greenfield automotive and semiconductor fabs through 2027 before broader process-industry adoption begins in earnest.Read more →

Standards Watch

IEC 62443-3-2 (security risk assessment for system design) continues to be the standard most frequently cited in procurement specifications this year, driven partly by NIS2 enforcement in the EU and partly by insurers asking for documented zone-and-conduit models before writing cyber policies. If you are specifying a new line or revamp, the practical implication is that your P&IDs now need a companion zone diagram showing security levels (SL-T) for each zone and the conduits between them — and your vendor needs to demonstrate SL-C capability for the components they deliver. ISA/IEC 62443-4-2 certification activity among PLC and RTU vendors has accelerated noticeably, with most major vendors now shipping at least SL 2 capable firmware on current platforms. This matters at purchase time: asking for the component certification report and the security hardening guide should be as routine as asking for the CE declaration. Note that 'certified' components still require 'secure by configuration' — the default-open settings on many devices will fail an audit even if the component itself is certified. On the NIST side, SP 800-82 Rev. 3 remains the most pragmatic reference for operators who need to map 62443 concepts to a US regulatory audience, and its guidance on remote access, patching, and incident response in OT environments is directly applicable to the Ivanti and PHP exposures highlighted in today's KEV additions.Read more →

Incident of the Week

This week's reflection is on the 2021 Oldsmar water treatment incident in Florida, which remains the clearest publicly documented near-miss for a safety-critical process. An attacker accessed the SCADA HMI through an inactive TeamViewer session installed on a shared Windows workstation and briefly raised the sodium hydroxide setpoint from 100 ppm to 11,100 ppm. An operator noticed the mouse cursor moving on-screen, reversed the change within minutes, and downstream pH alarms would likely have caught it anyway — but the incident revealed how fragile the controls were in practice. The root causes were depressingly ordinary: shared credentials across operators, an unmanaged remote access tool with no MFA, Windows 7 beyond end-of-life, and a flat network that let the HMI reach the internet. There was no engineering workstation separation, no change management on setpoint writes, and no alerting on out-of-band parameter changes. Three lessons remain current five years later. First, remote access tools proliferate quietly — audit everything installed on operator workstations quarterly, not just what IT thinks is there. Second, safety-critical setpoints should have engineered limits enforced in the PLC logic itself, not just in HMI faceplates, so even a compromised HMI cannot drive the process outside a safe envelope. Third, assume the operator will not be watching the screen; alarm on rate-of-change and absolute limits for any chemistry or safety parameter, and log every write with the originating session identity.Read more →

ICS / OT News Digest

CISA published a cluster of ICS advisories today that controls engineers should triage immediately. Hitachi Energy's CoreTec and TRO600 series (widely used in utility and substation communications) carry OS command injection and remote code execution flaws. Siemens issued fixes for a SAML authentication bypass in the Mendix module and a UEFI Secure Boot bypass affecting SIMATIC IPCs that ship with a leaked Microsoft test key — a particularly uncomfortable issue because it undermines a foundational trust boundary on industrial PC hardware commonly deployed as HMI, edge, and historian nodes. Mitsubishi Electric and ICONICS Digital Solutions disclosed four CVEs across the GENESIS, GENESIS64, Hyper Historian, MobileHMI, IoTWorX, and MC Works64 product families, including authentication bypass and arbitrary operation flaws. For sites standing up GENESIS SCADA or Hyper Historian in modern Purdue Level 2/3 roles, patching priority is high because these vulnerabilities enable both DoS and tampering with historian data — the sort of thing that corrupts regulatory records and quality traceability. Separately, EPICS Base ≤7.0.10 (ubiquitous in large-physics, accelerator, and research facility DCS deployments) has four memory corruption and DoS issues that research operators should schedule into their next shutdown window. On the broader IT-adjacent front, two unauthenticated PHP RCE flaws (CVE-2026-109618/109619) are being actively exploited via a 'PHP2Shell' campaign that is successfully rooting exposed Palo Alto firewalls — a reminder to audit any plant-floor PHP-based engineering web portals and vendor appliance management interfaces. Ivanti Connect Secure also picked up two new KEV entries used in a chained exploit, which matters wherever Connect Secure is the VPN into the OT DMZ. Finally, Wireshark 5.0.0rc1 shipped — worth grabbing for your protocol analysis workstation given the ongoing improvements to industrial dissectors (CIP, S7comm-plus, OPC UA).Read more →

CISA Vulnerability Digest

KEV additions this week lean heavily toward perimeter and remote-access infrastructure that typically fronts OT networks. The two Ivanti Connect Secure flaws (CVE-2026-118527 and CVE-2026-111357) chain with a prior CVE to allow a remote authenticated attacker to drop attacker-controlled files and then escalate privileges locally — a classic VPN-to-pivot pattern. If Connect Secure is your remote-access gateway for integrators or vendor support, patch immediately and audit recent session logs and uploaded files. Two unauthenticated PHP RCEs (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619) are being weaponized in the wild and have already been used to compromise exposed Palo Alto firewall management planes. Any OT-adjacent management portal, jump host, or engineering web app running PHP should be isolated, patched, and have egress tightly restricted. The Cisco ASA/FTD NULL pointer DoS (CVE-2026-76418) is relevant for firewalls segmenting IT/OT — expect reload loops under attack, so verify HA pair behaviour and failover timing. Also notable for OT: a Siemens Software Center DLL hijack (CVE-2026-110936) that affects engineering workstations where TIA Portal and related tooling are maintained — review workstation hardening and application allowlisting. BeyondTrust Remote Support auth bypass (CVE-2026-102764) deserves attention anywhere vendor support uses it to reach OT assets.Read more →
